## The exam and the constraint

Prelims is on Sunday 23 May 2027: GS Paper I (100 questions, 200 marks, one third negative) and CSAT Paper II (80 questions, 200 marks, qualifying at 33%, which is 66 marks). You confirmed eligibility on 4 October and will travel to India for the exam.

You are starting from zero and working full time. The committed hours are about 20 a week: 2 hours on weekdays, 5 hours a day on weekends. Over 33 weeks that is roughly 660 hours. A from-zero Prelims pass usually takes 800 to 1000, so the plan is cut by question weightage rather than stretched over the whole syllabus. Polity, Modern History, Geography, Economy and Environment get full depth. Ancient and Medieval history, Art and Culture, and Science and Technology are covered only through the chapters previous-year questions point to. If the weekly hours go up, the Sunday review widens the selective subjects first.

The goal is to clear with margin: 110 or more out of 200 on GS-I mocks by May, and 90 or more on CSAT. General-category GS-I cutoffs have been around 88 to 100 in recent years. 110 means about 85 attempts at 65% accuracy, or 75 attempts at 72%. Accuracy matters more than attempts, and both are tracked on every mock.

## Phases

### Build, 5 October to 20 December (11 weeks)

Foundation. One standard source per subject, nothing added. PYQs tagged from day one so the reading is aimed at how UPSC asks. One subject at a time on weekdays, starting with Polity because it carries the most questions and is the most learnable from zero. Weekends add a second subject.

As re-cut on 11 October, after Week 1 logged zero hours:

- Weeks 2 to 4: Polity (Laxmikanth: Constitution basics, Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles, Union and State executive and legislature, Judiciary, Centre-State relations, constitutional bodies). Weekends: Modern History (Spectrum, 1757 to 1857). The CSAT diagnostic moved to Sunday 11 October.
- Weeks 5 to 6: Polity, remaining PYQ-dense chapters only (Panchayats, Emergency, Amendments, Elections). Weekends: Modern History, 1857 to 1947.
- Weeks 7 to 8: Geography (NCERT Class 11 Fundamentals of Physical Geography and India Physical Environment, atlas work). Weekends: Economy (NCERT 11 and 12, selective).
- Weeks 9 to 10: Economy core (money and banking, inflation, fiscal policy, budget terms, external sector). Weekends: Environment (Shankar IAS: ecology, biodiversity, climate change).
- Week 11: Environment (protected areas, acts, conventions). The Saturday and Sunday of Week 11 are the only time given to Ancient and Medieval history plus Art and Culture, PYQ-pointed chapters only. Science and Technology basics move to the Converge phase through current affairs.
- Every day: 20 minutes of current affairs (from June 2026 onward), PYQ MCQs from the subject in hand. CSAT twice a week, Saturday and Sunday, 45 minutes each. Sunday is the weekly PYQ test on the week's chapters.

Exit criteria: every static subject read once with notes; PYQ corpus 2015 to 2026 tagged for Polity, History, Geography, Economy and Environment; CSAT diagnostic at 60 out of 200 or better.

### Converge, 21 December to 14 March (12 weeks)

Second reading at half the first-pass time, notes compressed. One coaching sectional test a week from the first week of January, reviewed the same day. Current affairs compiled from June 2026 to March 2027, each item mapped to a static anchor. Economic Survey and Budget 2027 in February, Prelims-relevant data only. The application window (13 January to 2 February) falls inside this phase.

Exit criteria: sectional tests at 55% accuracy or better; CSAT mock at 80 or better; PYQ corpus complete 2011 to 2026.

### Compress, 15 March to 8 May (8 weeks)

Two GS full-length mocks a week plus one CSAT mock, each followed by a two-hour error review (wrong because of a knowledge gap, a misread, an elimination error, or a guess). Revision cycles: the whole syllabus in four weeks, then two, then one. Current affairs consolidated into one document of 150 pages or fewer, revised twice. Schemes, reports and indices, species and protected areas, and maps as rapid-fire lists. No new sources.

Exit criteria: GS mock average at 100 or better with 20 or fewer wrong; CSAT at 90 or better.

### Lock, 9 to 22 May (2 weeks)

Only your own notes. One mock every two days until 18 May, none after 20 May. Travel to India. Sleep fixed to exam-day timing (9:30 am and 2:30 pm IST). Anything new in this phase costs more than it gives.

## The weekly rhythm (20 hours)

- Weekday, 2 hours: 20 minutes current affairs, 30 minutes spaced revision (items from 1, 3, 7, 21 and 60 days ago), 60 minutes new reading, 10 minutes of PYQs (10 questions).
- Saturday, 5 hours: 3 hours new reading, 45 minutes CSAT, 45 minutes revision, 30 minutes PYQs (20 questions).
- Sunday, 5 hours: 1 hour weekly PYQ test on the week's chapters, 1 hour error review, 2 hours new reading or catch-up, 45 minutes CSAT, 15 minutes to log the day and read the weekly review.

Three things are not negotiable: the revision queue comes before new material, the day gets logged, and PYQs happen every day.

## Sources (fixed)

Polity: Laxmikanth. Modern History: Spectrum. Ancient and Medieval: Tamil Nadu Class 11. Geography: NCERT 11 and 12 plus the Oxford atlas. Economy: NCERT 11 and 12 plus one concept book. ## Verification rule

Nothing enters permanent notes without a primary source (PIB, PRS, a ministry, UPSC, the RBI, a Supreme Court text) or two independent confirmations. Article numbers, dates, committee names and statistics from a model are not trusted until checked.

## How the plan changes

Only the Sunday review re-plans. If more than about a fifth of a week is undone, the next week absorbs the backlog by compressing lower-yield chapters, not by adding hours that the log says will not happen. If a second week in a row logs zero, the 20-hour assumption is wrong and the plan is cut to the real hours.
