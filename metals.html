    General market context

    What moves these prices

    Precious-metal prices react to several connected markets. These are the factors people commonly watch when reading a move.

    • US dollar directionA stronger dollar can make dollar-priced metals more expensive in other currencies.
    • Interest rates and real yieldsExpectations for inflation-adjusted returns can affect demand for assets that do not pay interest.
    • Inflation expectationsLonger-term concerns about purchasing power often shape interest in gold.
    • Central-bank demandOfficial-sector buying and reserve decisions can influence underlying gold demand.
    • Geopolitical uncertaintyPeriods of heightened uncertainty can change demand for perceived stores of value.
    • ETF and institutional flowsLarge fund inflows or outflows can add to short-term market demand or supply.
    • Industrial demand for silverSolar, electronics, and manufacturing demand give silver an industrial dimension.
    • Mining supply and costsProduction, energy, labour, and refining costs influence longer-run supply conditions.

    These factors are educational context, not a claim about the cause of today’s movement.

    A short guide

    Understanding these prices

    A spot quote is a market reference. The amount paid at a jeweller or bullion dealer will usually differ.

    Troy ounce, gram, tola, baht

    Metals trade in troy ounces (31.1035 g). A tola is 11.6638 g, a sovereign/pavan is taken as 8 g, and a Thai baht of gold is 15.244 g at 96.5% purity.

    24K and 22K gold

    24K is near-pure. 22K (916) is about 91.7% gold; the metal value scales with purity, before making charges, tax or dealer margin.

    Spot price and retail price

    Spot is the wholesale market reference. Coins, bars and jewellery trade above it because they are finished retail products.

    These are spot-market reference prices. Retail jewellery and bullion prices may include taxes, dealer premiums, fabrication costs, making charges and local market spreads.

    Transparency

    Methodology and data sources

    Live quotes and daily context come from different feeds and are kept separate, so nothing on the page looks more precise than the source data is.

    How prices and comparisons are calculated
    Current USD spot references come from Gold API. Currency conversion and daily historical references come from Frankfurter. Local prices multiply the USD quote by the latest available FX rate. Grams use 31.1034768 g per troy ounce; tola = 11.6638 g; sovereign = 8 g; baht = 15.244 g; 22K uses 22/24 of the metal value. History uses daily closes (weekly/monthly for long ranges). Calculator, holdings and alert data stay in your browser’s local storage and are never sent to a server. Seller estimates apply a typical premium band to spot and are never fetched per seller — confirm live prices on each seller’s site.
