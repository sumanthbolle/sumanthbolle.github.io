General market context

Market factors worth watching

Precious-metal prices are influenced by several connected markets. These are the factors investors commonly monitor when interpreting current movement.

  • US dollar directionA stronger dollar can make dollar-priced metals more expensive in other currencies.
  • Interest rates and real yieldsExpectations for inflation-adjusted returns can affect demand for assets that do not pay interest.
  • Inflation expectationsLonger-term concerns about purchasing power often shape interest in gold.
  • Central-bank demandOfficial-sector buying and reserve decisions can influence underlying gold demand.
  • Geopolitical uncertaintyPeriods of heightened uncertainty can change demand for perceived stores of value.
  • ETF and institutional flowsLarge fund inflows or outflows can add to short-term market demand or supply.
  • Industrial demand for silverSolar, electronics, and manufacturing demand give silver an industrial dimension.
  • Mining supply and costsProduction, energy, labour, and refining costs influence longer-run supply conditions.

These factors are educational context, not a claim about the cause of today’s movement. No live causal analysis is being presented.

A short guide

Understanding these prices

A spot quote is a market reference. The amount paid at a jeweller or bullion dealer will usually differ.

What is a troy ounce?

Precious metals are quoted in troy ounces. One troy ounce is 31.1035 grams, slightly heavier than the everyday ounce used for food and household goods.

24K and 22K gold

24K is the near-pure gold reference. A 22K metal-value estimate uses 22/24 of the 24K price. It does not include making charges, tax, or a dealer margin.

Spot price and retail price

Spot is the wholesale market reference for immediate settlement. Coins, bars, and jewellery may trade above it because they are finished retail products.

These are spot-market reference prices. Retail jewellery and bullion prices may include taxes, dealer premiums, fabrication costs, making charges and local market spreads.

Transparency

Methodology and data sources

Live quotes and daily context are kept separate so the interface does not imply more certainty than the source data supports.

How prices and comparisons are calculated
Current USD spot references come from Gold API. Currency conversion and daily historical references come from Frankfurter, an open-source service aggregating official and institutional exchange-rate sources. Local prices multiply the USD quote by the latest available FX rate. Grams use 31.1034768 grams per troy ounce; 22K uses 22/24 of the 24K metal value. One-, seven-, and thirty-day comparisons use the nearest available daily observation. “Strengthening” means the seven-day change is above 0.5%; “Weakening” means below −0.5%; values between those thresholds are “Stable.” Data may be delayed by source or cache intervals.
