          The filter

          An item survives on two of four. Keep this visible while you work.

          1. Syllabus linkage — a named topic, not vaguely “governance”.
          2. Static anchor — a concept that outlives the event. The most discriminating test.
          3. Debate content — two defensible positions exist.
          4. Durability — it still matters in six months.

          How to use this

          Forty-five minutes a day, eight to fifteen items retained. Not three hours and sixty forgotten ones. The loop below is the whole method; everything else on this page exists to serve it.

          1. Set your position

            Enter your stage and the date of your next paper. The tool derives the mode — build, converge, compress or lock — and says plainly when adding new material has stopped paying.

          2. Build the brief

            Daily covers the last 24 hours; weekly covers seven days and clusters the repeated anchors. Read the highlighted Use line first. If that line is not usable in an answer, skip the item entirely.

          3. Check the source before you trust the fact

            Green means the item came from a primary document. Amber means it came from coverage of one — open it and confirm the claim is actually there before it enters your notes. Models cite real URLs for claims those URLs do not make.

          4. Look up anchors, not headlines

            Search filters what is on screen. Press Enter, or use Look up, to fetch an exam-ready note on any concept: points, value-adds, both sides, three question stems, and the usual trap. Depth is capped on purpose.

          5. Save little, revise often

            Save only what has an anchor and a use. Each note joins the queue at day 1, 3, 7, 21 and 60. Prune anything you have never successfully retrieved — deleting is maintenance, not loss.

          6. Export at month end

            Copy or download your notes as Markdown, cluster them by anchor, and write one synthesis per anchor with three or more items. That synthesis is what you revise later, not the individual entries.

          What this tool will not do

          • It will not predict your paper. Scores rank items for revision priority; the anchor-frequency term is an editorial estimate from twenty years of recurring themes, not a tagged corpus of past questions, and every score says so.
          • It will not replace the primary document. Language models fabricate article numbers, committee names and statistics fluently. Nothing here is trustworthy as a fact until you have opened the source.
          • It will not add a source to your stack. If you are already behind on what you own, the answer to “should I read this too?” is no.
          • It is not affiliated with the Union Public Service Commission. Dates, eligibility and the syllabus come from upsc.gov.in, and where this page and the Commission disagree, the Commission is right.
