Karnataka Seeks Ninth Schedule Protection for SC/ST Quota Law

Karnataka government has recommended including its law enhancing SC/ST reservation in the Ninth Schedule of the Constitution to shield it from legal challenges. This move raises important constitutional questions on reservation laws and the scope of Ninth Schedule protection, relevant for UPSC aspirants studying social justice and federalism.

Karnataka's proposal to place its SC/ST quota enhancement law in the Ninth Schedule aims to protect it from judicial review, highlighting constitutional debates on affirmative action and federalism.

Why is this in the news?

On November 14, 2026, Karnataka's Chief Secretary wrote to the Union Home Secretary recommending the inclusion of the state's SC/ST reservation enhancement law in the Ninth Schedule of the Constitution.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is Karnataka's SC/ST quota enhancement law and its current status?

  • Law details. The Karnataka Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Reservation of Seats in Educational Institutions and of Appointments or Posts in the Services Under the State) Act, 2022, increases reservation for SCs from 15% to 17% and for STs from 3% to 7%.
  • Legislative approval. The law was passed in the Karnataka legislature's winter session in Belagavi in December 2022.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does Karnataka want to include the law in the Ninth Schedule?

  • Protection from legal challenges. Inclusion in the Ninth Schedule would fortify the law against judicial scrutiny and potential legal challenges.
  • Political context. The move comes ahead of the Assembly elections and follows criticism from the Congress party accusing the government of delaying the law's protection to attract SC/ST votes.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the significance of the Ninth Schedule in this context?

  • Ninth Schedule role. The Ninth Schedule of the Constitution protects certain laws from being challenged in courts, especially those related to social justice and affirmative action.
  • Implications for federalism. The state's request to the Centre to include the law in the Ninth Schedule highlights the interplay between state legislation and central constitutional provisions on reservation.

Back to basics: Ninth Schedule of the Constitution

The Ninth Schedule was added to the Constitution to protect certain laws, especially those related to land reforms and social justice, from judicial review. Laws placed in this schedule cannot be challenged in courts for violating fundamental rights, thus providing them immunity from legal scrutiny. This mechanism is often used to safeguard affirmative action laws from being struck down.
