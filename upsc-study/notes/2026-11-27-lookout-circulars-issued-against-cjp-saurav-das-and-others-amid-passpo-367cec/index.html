Lookout Circulars Against CJP Leaders Over Passport and Protest Issues

Lookout circulars were issued against Cockroach Janata Party leaders including Saurav Das due to an unpermitted protest and passport discrepancies. This raises questions on judicial independence and executive actions, relevant for understanding separation of powers.

Lookout circulars against CJP leaders highlight executive measures in law enforcement and their impact on judicial independence.

Why is this in the news?

Lookout circulars against CJP leaders, including Saurav Das, were issued by Mumbai Police and the Ministry of External Affairs in November 2026 amid passport and protest controversies.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary[2] The Indian Express · Secondary

What triggered the lookout circulars against CJP leaders?

  • Unpermitted protest. A lookout circular was issued following an FIR registered on October 3, 2026, over an unpermitted protest at Shivaji Park, Mumbai, on October 2, where CJP leaders gathered despite police refusal of permission.
  • Passport discrepancy. Saurav Das was also served a show-cause notice by the Chennai Regional Passport Office to clarify his place of birth, and was advised not to use his passport until he responded.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary[2] The Indian Express · Secondary

How do lookout circulars function and why were they issued here?

  • Function of lookout circulars. Lookout circulars are alerts sent through the Bureau of Immigration to stop persons from leaving the country when they are evading arrest or court appearance in cognisable offences.
  • Reason for issuance. The Mumbai Police issued lookout circulars against CJP leaders fearing they might leave the country amid ongoing investigations related to the protest.

[2] The Indian Express · Secondary

What legal provisions and authorities are involved in this case?

  • FIR charges. The FIR invokes sections related to unlawful assembly and disobedience of lawful orders under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, and provisions of the Maharashtra Police Act.
  • Authority for lookout circulars. Under Union Home Ministry guidelines, a designated officer at the level of Superintendent of Police or above approves lookout circulars upon police request.

[2] The Indian Express · Secondary

Why does this matter for judicial independence and separation of powers?

  • Executive action on judiciary members. The issuance of lookout circulars and passport scrutiny against a party co-convenor who is also a Chief Judicial Prosecutor raises constitutional questions on the independence of the judiciary from executive interference.
  • Court proceedings. Saurav Das challenged the travel restriction in the Delhi High Court, which is hearing the matter, highlighting tensions between judiciary and executive powers.

Back to basics: Lookout Circular

A lookout circular is an alert issued by law enforcement through immigration authorities to prevent a person from leaving the country. It is used when a person is wanted for investigation or court proceedings and is suspected of evading arrest or fleeing. The circular is authorized by senior police officers and helps enforce the law by restricting movement across borders.

Common mistake. Lookout circulars are not issued arbitrarily; they require police approval and are based on cognisable offences with legal grounds.
