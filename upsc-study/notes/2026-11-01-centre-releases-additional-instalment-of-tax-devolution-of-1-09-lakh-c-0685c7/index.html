Centre Releases ₹1.09 Lakh Crore Additional Tax Devolution to States

The Union Government released an additional instalment of ₹1,09,019 crore to states on 31 October 2026, beyond the regular monthly tax devolution. This move aims to support states' capital spending and welfare expenditure ahead of the festive season, highlighting fiscal federalism in practice.

The Centre's additional tax devolution of ₹1.09 lakh crore to states in October 2026 facilitates accelerated development and welfare spending, illustrating fiscal federalism.

Why is this in the news?

What is the recent tax devolution release by the Centre?

  • Additional instalment released. The Centre released an additional ₹1,09,019 crore to states on 31 October 2026, which is the second extra instalment in the financial year beyond the regular monthly devolution.
  • Purpose of release. This release aims to enable states to accelerate capital spending and finance development and welfare-related expenditure, especially in view of the upcoming festive season.
  • State-wise distribution. The amount was distributed among all states, with Uttar Pradesh receiving the highest share of ₹19,208 crore and smaller states like Sikkim receiving ₹365 crore.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

How does tax devolution work under fiscal federalism?

  • Tax devolution mechanism. The Centre devolves 41% of the taxes it collects to states in 14 instalments during a fiscal year, supporting states' financial autonomy.
  • Role in fiscal federalism. Tax devolution is a key fiscal federalism tool that balances the financial powers between the Union and states, enabling states to fund their development and welfare schemes.

Back to basics: Fiscal Federalism

Fiscal federalism refers to the financial relations between different levels of government, mainly the Union and state governments in India. It involves the distribution of taxation powers and expenditure responsibilities. Tax devolution is a key mechanism where the Centre shares a portion of its tax revenues with states to enable them to fund their functions and development activities.
