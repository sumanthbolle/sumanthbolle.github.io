India's Horticulture Production to Reach Record 386.9 Million Tonnes in 2025-26

India's total horticulture production is projected to increase by 4.36% to 386.9 million tonnes in 2025-26 despite a decline in cultivated area, reflecting diversification into high-value crops. This growth is significant for India's nutritional security, farmer incomes, and agricultural sector development.

India's horticulture production is set to reach a record 386.9 million tonnes in 2025-26, driven by diversification and productivity gains despite reduced cultivation area.

Why is this in the news?

On November 17, 2026, Union Agriculture Minister Shri Shivraj Singh Chouhan released the Third Advance Estimates projecting record horticulture production for 2025-26.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the key projections for horticulture production in 2025-26?

  • Overall production growth. India's total horticulture production is expected to reach 3,869.15 lakh tonnes (386.9 million tonnes) in 2025-26, a 4.36% increase over 3,707.38 lakh tonnes in 2024-25.
  • Decline in cultivated area. The total area under horticultural crops is estimated to decrease from 301.36 lakh hectares in 2024-25 to 297.21 lakh hectares in 2025-26, yet production is rising due to improved productivity.
  • Growth in sub-sectors. Fruit production is projected to increase by 4.60%, vegetable production by 4.35%, flower production by 14.45%, and plantation crops by 7.56%.
  • Notable crop increases. Potato production is expected to rise nearly 12% to 655.70 lakh tonnes, tomato production to 214.11 lakh tonnes, and spices production to 123.54 lakh tonnes.

Why does this horticulture growth matter for India’s agriculture and farmers?

  • Nutritional security. Increased horticulture production strengthens the country's nutritional security by providing diverse and nutritious food.
  • Farmer incomes. Diversification into fruits, vegetables, spices, and high-value crops creates new income opportunities for farmers, contributing to higher earnings.
  • Agricultural diversification. Farmers are moving beyond traditional crops to commercial and high-value crops, which supports self-reliance and sector growth.
  • Productivity gains. Production growth despite reduced area indicates improved productivity and efficient farming practices.

[1] Press Information Bureau[4] The Hindu · Secondary

What recent government initiatives support farmers and horticulture growth?

  • Financial support in Punjab. The Centre approved ₹254.20 crore under PM-RKVY and seven new projects worth ₹98.44 crore to boost Punjab’s agriculture.
  • Measures against black marketing. Strict action and imprisonment are planned against black marketing and forced tagging of fertilisers to protect farmers.
  • New pesticide law. A stringent new pesticide law is being introduced to curb fake fertilisers, seeds, and substandard pesticides.
  • Green ammonia trial. A nationwide trial of green ammonia as an alternative to urea is underway on 10,000 hectares to promote sustainable fertiliser use.
  • Crop diversification and integrated farming. The government promotes integrated farming models and crop diversification to increase small farmers’ incomes and soil health.
  • Market support schemes. The government will bear transportation costs for certain crops sold outside the state and provide compensation under the Market Intervention Scheme.

[2] Press Information Bureau[3] Press Information Bureau

How is the government addressing soil health and sustainability?

  • Soil testing promotion. Farmers are urged to test soil before applying fertilisers to avoid overuse and maintain soil fertility.
  • Natural farming encouragement. Farmers are encouraged to adopt natural farming on part of their land to improve soil health gradually.
  • Stubble management. Efforts to reduce stubble burning include promoting mechanisation and recognising stubble as beneficial for soil organic carbon.

Back to basics: Horticulture and Agricultural Diversification

Horticulture involves the cultivation of fruits, vegetables, flowers, spices, and plantation crops, which are often high-value and nutrient-rich. Agricultural diversification means shifting from traditional cereal crops to a wider variety of crops and allied activities to improve farmer incomes, nutritional security, and sustainability. Productivity gains can come from better farming practices, technology, and crop choices.

What happens next?

  • Implement new pesticide law. The government is preparing a stricter pesticide law to combat fake and substandard agricultural inputs, protecting farmers and productivity.
  • Promote integrated farming. Expanding integrated farming projects in the next budget aims to increase small farmers' incomes and reduce dependence on single crops.
  • Expand green ammonia trials. If successful, green ammonia fertiliser trials could reduce reliance on chemical fertilisers and improve sustainability.
