RBI Announces Results of Variable Rate Reverse Repo and State Government Securities Auctions

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) released the results of its 2-day Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auction and multiple State Government Securities auctions on November 17, 2026. Understanding these auctions is important for UPSC aspirants as they reflect RBI's liquidity management and government borrowing strategies, key topics in the Indian economy syllabus.

The RBI's VRRR and government securities auctions demonstrate its role in managing liquidity and financing state governments through market instruments.

Why is this in the news?

On November 17, 2026, the RBI released the results of its 2-day Variable Rate Reverse Repo auction and State Government Securities auctions, followed by an announcement of an overnight VRRR auction scheduled for November 18, 2026.

What is the Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auction and its recent outcome?

  • Definition and purpose. A VRRR auction is a monetary policy tool where the RBI absorbs excess liquidity from banks by borrowing funds at variable rates, helping to maintain overnight interest rates within the policy corridor.
  • Recent auction details. On November 17, 2026, the RBI conducted a 2-day VRRR auction with a notified amount of ₹50,000 crore, receiving offers worth ₹7,627 crore and accepting the entire amount at a cut-off and weighted average rate of 5.49%.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What were the key features of the State Government Securities auctions?

  • Auction scale and diversity. On November 17, 2026, the RBI auctioned State Government Securities totaling ₹23,300 crore across multiple states including Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Punjab, Rajasthan, Telangana, Tripura, and West Bengal with varying tenors from 2 to 26 years.
  • Bidding and acceptance. Competitive and non-competitive bids were received in large numbers, with partial allotments common; yields ranged approximately between 7.53% and 8.23%, reflecting market demand and state credit risk perceptions.
  • Notable exceptions. Goa did not accept any amount in the re-issue of its 7.80% SGS 2037 security, indicating no allotment in that particular auction.

[2] Reserve Bank of India

Why does the RBI conduct VRRR auctions and government securities auctions?

  • Liquidity management. VRRR auctions help the RBI absorb surplus liquidity from the banking system, stabilizing short-term interest rates and supporting monetary policy implementation.
  • Government financing. Auctions of State Government Securities enable states to raise funds from the market to finance their fiscal needs, reflecting the government's borrowing program.
  • Upcoming auction. Following the November 17 auctions, the RBI announced an overnight VRRR auction of ₹50,000 crore to be held on November 18, 2026, continuing its liquidity management operations.

Back to basics: Monetary Policy Tools and Government Securities

Monetary policy tools like the Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auction are used by central banks to manage liquidity in the banking system by borrowing excess funds from banks at variable interest rates. Government securities are debt instruments issued by the government (central or state) to raise funds from the market, with fixed or floating interest rates and specific maturities. These auctions determine the yield and price of such securities based on market demand and supply.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the RBI's recent auctions: 1. The 2-day Variable Rate Reverse Repo auction on November 17, 2026, had a notified amount of ₹50,000 crore but accepted only ₹7,627 crore at a cut-off rate of 5.49%. 2. The State Government Securities auction on the same day totaled ₹23,300 crore and included securities with tenors ranging from 2 to 26 years. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). Statement 1 might mislead by assuming the entire notified amount was accepted; only ₹7,627 crore was accepted. Statement 2 correctly states the total notified amount and tenor range.

Mains question (10 marks)

Explain the role of Variable Rate Reverse Repo auctions and government securities auctions in the Reserve Bank of India's monetary policy framework.

Explain: Explain how VRRR and government securities auctions function as tools for liquidity management and government financing within RBI's monetary policy.

Intro: The RBI uses auctions of VRRR and government securities as key instruments in its monetary policy toolkit.

  • VRRR auctions help absorb excess liquidity from banks, stabilizing short-term interest rates and supporting inflation control.
  • Government securities auctions enable states to raise funds for fiscal needs, reflecting the government's borrowing program and market confidence.
  • The auction results indicate market demand, interest rate expectations, and help RBI calibrate monetary policy actions.

Conclusion: Thus, these auctions are integral to RBI's efforts to maintain monetary and fiscal stability.
