El Nino Impact Spurs Calls for Paddy Crop Holiday and Water Conservation

Severe El Nino conditions have led farm scientists and officials in Telangana to recommend a crop holiday for paddy and promote cultivation of less water-intensive crops. This development highlights the need for adaptive agricultural practices and water management in response to climate variability, relevant for both Prelims and Mains exams.

Severe El Nino-induced water shortages have prompted Telangana to advise a paddy crop holiday and shift to less water-demanding crops to conserve water.

Why is this in the news?

In October 2026, Telangana faced severe water shortages due to El Nino, prompting farm scientists and government officials to recommend crop holidays for paddy and promote water conservation measures.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the impact of El Nino on agriculture and water resources?

  • Water shortage severity. El Nino caused a rainfall deficit of approximately 20% in Telangana, with inflows into Krishna and Godavari basins significantly below expectations, leading to limited water availability for farming.
  • Temperature rise and rainfall deficit. El Nino has caused deficient rainfall and a significant rise in temperatures, with conditions expected to worsen in November and December, and the northeast monsoon unlikely to improve the situation.
  • Electricity generation drop. Due to low reservoir water levels, electricity generation in Telangana fell to around 25% of last year's level, limiting power availability.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What measures are recommended to address the El Nino impact?

  • Paddy crop holiday advised. Farm scientists recommended declaring a crop holiday for paddy and advised farmers to avoid paddy and cotton cultivation during the Rabi season.
  • Promotion of less water-intensive crops. Farmers are encouraged to cultivate pulses like green gram, black gram, chickpea, groundnut, and oilseeds such as sunflower, sesame, and safflower, which require less water.
  • Water and energy conservation. Farmers were urged to use water and energy resources extremely judiciously, prioritizing drinking water needs over irrigation.
  • Government support and planning. The government plans to supply seeds for rain-fed and short-duration crops at subsidized rates and establish special war rooms to monitor El Nino conditions continuously.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for UPSC preparation?

  • Climate change adaptation. This case exemplifies how climate phenomena like El Nino affect agriculture and water resources, necessitating adaptive strategies relevant to GS3 topics on environment and agriculture.
  • Resource management challenges. The situation highlights the importance of water conservation and crop planning in drought-prone regions, linking to questions on resource mobilisation and sustainable development.
  • Policy response and governance. The coordinated government response, including advisories, subsidies, and monitoring mechanisms, illustrates governance in managing climate-induced crises.

Back to basics: El Nino and its impact on monsoon

El Nino is a climate phenomenon characterized by the warming of surface waters in the Pacific Ocean, which disrupts normal weather patterns globally. In India, it often leads to deficient monsoon rainfall and higher temperatures, affecting agriculture and water availability. Understanding El Nino helps explain seasonal variations and challenges in water resource management.
