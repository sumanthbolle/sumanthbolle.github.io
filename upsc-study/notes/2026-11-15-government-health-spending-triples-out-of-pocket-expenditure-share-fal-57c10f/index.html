Government Health Spending Triples; Out-of-Pocket Expenditure Falls Sharply

The National Health Accounts 2023–24 report shows that India’s government health expenditure has more than tripled in a decade, while the share of out-of-pocket expenditure by households has declined significantly. This shift indicates progress towards affordable and accessible healthcare, a key topic for UPSC exams on health policy and social sector governance.

India’s government health spending tripled from ₹1.30 lakh crore to ₹4.14 lakh crore between 2013-14 and 2023-24, reducing household out-of-pocket expenditure share from 64.2% to 42.7%.

Why is this in the news?

The Union Ministry of Health and Family Welfare released the National Health Accounts 2023–24 on 15 November 2026, highlighting major changes in India’s healthcare financing over the past decade.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the key changes in government health expenditure and household spending?

  • Tripling of government spending. Government health expenditure increased from ₹1.30 lakh crore in 2013-14 to ₹4.14 lakh crore in 2023-24, reflecting sustained public investment in healthcare.
  • Decline in out-of-pocket expenditure. Households’ out-of-pocket expenditure share fell sharply by 21.5 percentage points, from 64.2% in 2013-14 to 42.7% in 2023-24, indicating reduced financial burden on families.
  • Per capita spending increase. Per capita government health expenditure rose nearly threefold, from ₹1,042 to ₹2,959, showing increased public spending per citizen.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How has primary healthcare and social security financing evolved?

  • Boost to primary healthcare. Government investment in primary healthcare reached ₹1.53 lakh crore, supporting community-level services and facilities like Ayushman Arogya Mandirs.
  • Growth in social security expenditure. Social security expenditure on health nearly doubled as a share of total health expenditure, rising from 6% to 11%, strengthening financial protection through schemes like Ayushman Bharat.
  • Expansion of health insurance. Private health insurance expenditure increased from 3.4% to 10.2%, reflecting deeper insurance penetration and enhanced financial protection.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this matter for UPSC preparation?

  • Health financing and policy. Understanding the shift in healthcare financing is crucial for questions on government budgeting, social sector schemes, and health policy reforms.
  • Impact on public health outcomes. The reduction in out-of-pocket expenditure and increased public spending reflect progress towards Universal Health Coverage, a key government objective.
  • Link to flagship initiatives. Knowledge of schemes like Ayushman Bharat and Jan Aushadhi helps explain government efforts to make healthcare affordable and accessible.

Back to basics: Health Financing and Out-of-Pocket Expenditure

Health financing refers to how funds are collected, pooled, and spent to provide health services. Out-of-pocket expenditure is the direct payment made by individuals for healthcare services at the time of use, which can cause financial hardship. Reducing out-of-pocket expenditure through increased government spending and insurance schemes improves access and financial protection.

Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because out-of-pocket expenditure share actually declined sharply, not increased.
