India-Norway Collaboration on Sustainable Construction Using Industrial Waste

India and Norway held a dialogue on joint technologies to convert industrial waste into resources for sustainable construction, focusing on circular economy and clean technology. This cooperation aligns with their Green Strategic Partnership and highlights innovations in waste management relevant for environmental and economic development studies.

India and Norway are collaborating to develop technologies that turn industrial and construction waste into valuable construction materials, promoting a circular economy.

Why is this in the news?

On 10 November 2026, the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR), India, and SINTEF, Norway, organized a dialogue on sustainable waste-to-resource solutions in the cement and construction sector, following the elevation of India-Norway ties to a Green Strategic Partnership.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What was the India-Norway dialogue about?

  • Purpose of dialogue. The dialogue focused on exploring collaborative approaches for sustainable waste management and resource recovery in the cement and construction sectors.
  • Participants. Representatives from CSIR Institutes, SINTEF, regulatory authorities, and the cement and construction industries participated to discuss joint technology proposals.
  • Context. The event aligned with the Green Strategic Partnership between India and Norway, emphasizing circular economy, clean technology, and research cooperation.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What technologies and wastes were discussed?

  • Types of waste. Potential applications of construction and demolition waste, steel slag, fly ash, red mud, plastic waste, copper slag, and other industrial residues were discussed.
  • Technological focus. Discussions included processing technologies, standardisation, economic viability, market acceptance, and safe destruction of hazardous wastes like Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs), DDT, and PCB-contaminated materials.
  • Research highlights. CSIR-CRRI and CSIR-AMPRI showcased uses of industrial by-products in road construction, geopolymer concrete, specialised aggregates, and value-added construction products.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How does this collaboration promote sustainable construction?

  • Circular economy transition. The dialogue emphasized shifting from a linear to a circular economy by transforming construction and industrial waste into valuable resources.
  • Safe hazardous waste treatment. Cement kiln co-processing was explored as an environmentally sound method to destroy hazardous organic contaminants under controlled high-temperature conditions.
  • International best practices. Sessions highlighted recycling techniques, mobile recycling units, and improved impurity removal to enhance construction waste recycling.
  • Future collaboration. Priority areas include joint research proposals, pilot projects for hazardous waste treatment, technical guidelines for contaminated waste disposal, and strengthening industry-academia partnerships.

Back to basics: Circular Economy

A circular economy is an economic system aimed at eliminating waste and the continual use of resources by reusing, recycling, and recovering materials. It contrasts with a linear economy, which follows a 'take-make-dispose' model. In construction, this means converting waste like fly ash or demolition debris into useful materials, reducing environmental impact and conserving resources.

What happens next?

  • Pilot-scale projects. Implement pilot-scale demonstration projects for hazardous waste treatment to validate technologies and ensure regulatory compliance.
  • Technical guidelines. Develop technical guidelines for the disposal of DDT and PCB-contaminated waste to meet environmental safety standards.
  • Strengthen partnerships. Enhance cooperation between industry and academia to accelerate research translation into scalable solutions.
