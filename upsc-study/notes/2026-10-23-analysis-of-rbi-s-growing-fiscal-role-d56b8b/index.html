RBI's Expanding Fiscal Role and Its Implications

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has shown a growing fiscal role, highlighted by its record ₹2.87 lakh crore surplus transfer to the Centre. This development raises important questions about central bank independence, fiscal centralisation, and the impact on fiscal devolution to states, which are crucial for UPSC aspirants studying monetary and fiscal policy coordination.

The RBI's increasing surplus transfers to the government reflect its growing fiscal significance, influencing fiscal federalism and central bank autonomy.

Why is this in the news?

The Hindu reported on October 22, 2026, about RBI's record ₹2.87 lakh crore surplus transfer to the Centre, highlighting its growing fiscal role and related concerns.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is RBI's growing fiscal role?

  • Surplus transfer record. The RBI transferred a record surplus of ₹2.87 lakh crore to the Centre, reflecting its increasing fiscal significance.
  • Source of earnings. The surplus arises from RBI's earnings on reserve management and foreign assets, which support government finances.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does RBI's fiscal role matter for policy and federalism?

  • Central bank independence. The growing fiscal transfers raise concerns about the RBI's operational autonomy and its distance from government fiscal compulsions.
  • Fiscal centralisation. Large surplus transfers to the Centre may lead to fiscal centralisation, affecting the balance of fiscal powers between the Centre and States.
  • Fiscal devolution to States. Such transfers are excluded from fiscal devolution calculations, potentially reducing the resources available to States.

Back to basics: Central Bank and Fiscal Policy

A central bank manages monetary policy to control inflation and maintain currency stability, ideally operating independently from government fiscal decisions. Fiscal policy involves government spending and taxation to influence the economy. The central bank's independence ensures credibility and prevents fiscal dominance, where government borrowing pressures monetary policy.

Answer and trap
