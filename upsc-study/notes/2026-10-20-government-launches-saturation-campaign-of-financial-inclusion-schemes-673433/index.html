Government launches Saturation Campaign for Financial Inclusion Schemes

The Department of Financial Services is running a nationwide campaign from 21 October to 31 December 2026 to saturate financial inclusion schemes at the Gram Panchayat and Urban Local Body levels. This campaign is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights government efforts to bring citizens into the formal financial system and promote inclusive growth.

The government’s Saturation Campaign aims to deepen financial inclusion by enrolling citizens in key schemes and updating their financial records across 2.70 lakh local bodies.

Why is this in the news?

The Department of Financial Services announced on 19 October 2026 a campaign running from 21 October to 31 December 2026 to saturate financial inclusion schemes across 2.70 lakh Gram Panchayats and Urban Local Bodies.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the Saturation Campaign of Financial Inclusion Schemes?

  • Nationwide coverage. The campaign covers 2.70 lakh Gram Panchayats and Urban Local Bodies across India to ensure maximum reach of financial inclusion schemes.
  • Key activities. Banks will conduct camps for enrolments under PMJJBY, PMSBY, APY, opening of PMJDY accounts, Aadhaar seeding, re-KYC, generating C-KYC IDs, updating nominations, and awareness sessions on digital frauds.
  • Support for farmers. The campaign includes sourcing of Kisan Credit Card applications and enrolment under Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana to support eligible PM-Kisan beneficiaries and prepare for the Rabi season.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does the Saturation Campaign matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Promotes financial inclusion. The campaign reflects the government’s commitment to empower citizens at the last mile by bringing them into the formal financial system.
  • Supports inclusive growth. By saturating enrolments in social security and pension schemes, the campaign aids social welfare and financial security for vulnerable populations.
  • Governance and monitoring. The campaign’s progress will be closely monitored by the Department of Financial Services, State administrations, banks, and State Level Bankers Committees.

Back to basics: Financial Inclusion

Financial inclusion means providing access to useful and affordable financial products and services to all individuals and businesses, especially those who are underserved or excluded from the formal financial system. It includes banking, credit, insurance, and pension services that help improve economic participation and security.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the Saturation Campaign of Financial Inclusion Schemes: 1. The campaign covers 2.70 lakh Gram Panchayats and Urban Local Bodies and runs from 21 October to 31 December 2026. 2. The campaign is organized by the Ministry of Finance and includes activities like enrolment under PMJJBY, PMSBY, APY, and opening of PMJDY accounts. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). Statement 2 is correct but attributing the campaign directly to the Ministry of Finance is a slight inaccuracy; it is organized by the Department of Financial Services under the Ministry of Finance.

Mains question (15 marks)

Examine the significance of the Saturation Campaign of Financial Inclusion Schemes in promoting inclusive growth and governance in India.

Examine: Discuss how the campaign enhances financial inclusion and supports governance objectives.

Intro: The Saturation Campaign aims to deepen financial inclusion by enrolling citizens in key government schemes at the grassroots level.

  • The campaign covers 2.70 lakh Gram Panchayats and Urban Local Bodies, ensuring wide coverage.
  • It facilitates enrolment in social security schemes like PMJJBY, PMSBY, APY, and promotes opening of bank accounts under PMJDY.
  • It supports farmers by sourcing KCC applications and enrolling beneficiaries under crop insurance schemes.
  • The campaign includes awareness sessions on digital frauds and unclaimed financial assets, enhancing financial literacy and security.

Conclusion: Thus, the campaign strengthens inclusive growth by integrating vulnerable populations into the formal financial system and improves governance through coordinated monitoring.
