SEBI Examines Position Limits for Non-Agricultural Commodity Contracts

SEBI is reviewing position limits on non-agricultural commodity derivative contracts to enhance market liquidity and depth while maintaining risk controls. This matters for UPSC aspirants as it highlights regulatory efforts to improve commodity market efficiency and investor protection.

SEBI is working to boost liquidity in non-agricultural commodity derivatives by examining position limits and addressing structural market issues.

Why is this in the news?

On October 3, 2026, SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey announced that SEBI is examining position limits for non-agricultural contracts to improve liquidity and market depth without compromising risk controls.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is SEBI examining regarding position limits?

  • Reviewing position limits. SEBI is examining position limits for non-agricultural commodity derivative contracts to improve liquidity and market depth while ensuring risk controls remain effective.
  • Goal of reforms. The reforms aim to allow contracts to gain scale and improve price discovery by enabling deeper and more liquid cash markets with wider participation.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does SEBI plan to address structural issues in commodity markets?

  • Engaging on GST issues. SEBI is engaging with stakeholders on GST-related issues that affect participants who give or receive commodities through exchange platforms to reduce structural friction.
  • Technology and market needs. SEBI emphasizes that technology should meet the specific needs of commodity market participants such as producers, farmers, processors, and hedgers, while preserving fair access and market integrity.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What steps is SEBI taking to improve investor awareness and market integrity?

  • Project Jagrook. SEBI plans to strengthen efforts under Project Jagrook to increase awareness about commodity derivatives among farmers, farmer producer organisations, MSMEs, hedgers, and other market users.
  • Maintaining robust controls. SEBI stresses that simpler regulations should not weaken compliance; robust controls over client funds, margins, reporting, and supervision are essential to maintain trust and market integrity.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What recent approvals has SEBI made related to commodity derivatives?

  • FPI participation. In September 2026, SEBI approved allowing foreign portfolio investors to participate in physically settled, non-agricultural commodity derivative contracts, subject to safeguards.

Back to basics: Commodity Derivatives and Position Limits

Commodity derivatives are financial contracts whose value is derived from underlying physical commodities. Position limits restrict the maximum number of contracts a trader can hold to prevent market manipulation and excessive speculation. These limits help maintain market stability and protect investors by controlling risk exposure.
