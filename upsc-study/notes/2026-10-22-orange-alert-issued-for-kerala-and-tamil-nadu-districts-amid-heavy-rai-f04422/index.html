Orange Alert Issued for Kerala Districts Due to Heavy Rainfall

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) issued orange and yellow alerts for several districts in Kerala amid heavy rain and landslip warnings triggered by low-pressure systems over the Bay of Bengal. This is relevant for UPSC aspirants focusing on disaster management, monsoon climatology, and environmental challenges in southern India.

IMD issued orange and yellow alerts for multiple Kerala districts due to heavy rainfall caused by Bay of Bengal low-pressure systems.

Why is this in the news?

On October 21, 2026, the IMD issued orange alerts for several Kerala districts due to heavy rainfall warnings linked to low-pressure areas over the Bay of Bengal.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered the orange alert in Kerala districts?

  • Low-pressure systems. A low-pressure area formed over the southwest Bay of Bengal and adjoining Tamil Nadu coast, causing moisture influx and heavy thunderstorms in Kerala.
  • Cyclonic circulation effect. The cyclonic circulation associated with the low-pressure area supports isolated to very heavy thunderstorms along Kerala's ghat regions and southern and central parts.
  • Upcoming intensification. Another low-pressure area expected around October 23 is likely to intensify and increase rainfall activity across Kerala from October 23 to 25.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Which districts are affected and what are the alert levels?

  • Orange alert districts. On October 21, orange alerts were issued for Thiruvananthapuram, Kollam, Pathanamthitta, Alappuzha, and Kottayam due to very heavy rainfall.
  • Yellow alert districts. Idukki, Ernakulam, Thrissur, Palakkad, and Malappuram were placed under yellow alert for isolated heavy rainfall.
  • Next day alerts. On October 22, orange alerts were issued for Ernakulam, Thrissur, Malappuram, and Kozhikode, with the rest of Kerala under yellow alert.

Back to basics: Monsoon and Weather Alerts

Monsoon rainfall in India is influenced by low-pressure systems that form over the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea, bringing moisture and storms. The India Meteorological Department issues weather alerts such as orange and yellow to warn about heavy rainfall and potential hazards like landslips. Orange alerts indicate very heavy rainfall and higher risk, while yellow alerts indicate isolated heavy rain with moderate risk.

Explain the role of Bay of Bengal low-pressure systems in influencing monsoon rainfall and the importance of weather alerts in disaster management.

Explain: Explain how Bay of Bengal low-pressure systems affect monsoon rainfall and why weather alerts are crucial for managing disasters.

Intro: Bay of Bengal low-pressure systems are key drivers of monsoon rainfall in peninsular India.

  • These systems cause moisture influx leading to heavy rainfall and thunderstorms, especially in southern states like Kerala.
  • The India Meteorological Department issues alerts such as orange and yellow to warn about expected heavy rain and associated risks.
  • Such alerts enable timely government and public response to reduce damage from floods and landslips.
  • Understanding these weather patterns is essential for disaster preparedness and climate-related studies.

Conclusion: Thus, Bay of Bengal low-pressure systems and weather alerts play a critical role in monsoon dynamics and disaster management.
