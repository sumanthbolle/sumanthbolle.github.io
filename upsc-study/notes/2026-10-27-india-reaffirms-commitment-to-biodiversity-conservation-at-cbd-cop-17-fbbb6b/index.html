India Reaffirms Biodiversity Conservation Commitment at CBD COP-17

At the 17th Conference of Parties to the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD COP-17) in Yerevan, Armenia, India reiterated its dedication to biodiversity conservation and effective implementation of the Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework. This is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights India's environmental diplomacy, national biodiversity strategies, and multilateral cooperation efforts.

India reaffirmed its commitment to biodiversity conservation and the Global Biodiversity Framework at CBD COP-17, emphasizing national priorities and international cooperation.

Why is this in the news?

India's Environment Minister Shri Bhupender Yadav delivered the national statement at CBD COP-17 on 27 October 2026 in Yerevan, Armenia, reaffirming India's biodiversity commitments.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What commitments did India reaffirm at CBD COP-17?

  • Biodiversity conservation pledge. India reaffirmed its commitment to conserving biodiversity and strengthening collective efforts to live in harmony with nature.
  • Global Biodiversity Framework alignment. India emphasized that implementing the Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework must align with national priorities, circumstances, and legislation.
  • Support for finance and technology transfer. India called for adequate and predictable finance, capacity-building, and technology transfer from developed countries to support biodiversity governance.
  • Benefit-sharing on genetic resources. India supports benefit-sharing from digital sequence information on genetic resources through a multilateral mechanism, including the Cali Fund.
  • Science-based approach. India advocated a science-based, risk-proportionate, and precautionary approach to synthetic biology and biosafety.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How is India advancing biodiversity conservation nationally?

  • National Biodiversity Strategy. India’s updated National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan translates the Global Biodiversity Framework into 23 National Biodiversity Targets and 142 indicators.
  • Protected areas and heritage sites. India has 1,134 Protected Areas, 103 Ramsar sites, 57 Biodiversity Heritage Sites, and 18 Biosphere Reserves, including 13 under UNESCO’s Man and the Biosphere Network.
  • Conservation initiatives. Initiatives include the International Big Cat Alliance, Mission LiFE, Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam, MISHTI for mangrove restoration, Amrit Dharohar for wetland conservation, and the Soil Health Card Scheme.
  • Legislative alignment and local participation. India has aligned its legislation with the Convention and established village-level committees for decentralized access and benefit sharing, enabling local participation.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does India’s role at CBD COP-17 matter for international cooperation?

  • Multilateral cooperation. India reaffirmed its commitment to work with all Parties to advance the Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework under the spirit of 'One Earth, One Family, One Future.'
  • Hosting appreciation. India appreciated the Government of Armenia for hosting the 17th meeting of the CBD, reflecting diplomatic engagement in global environmental governance.

Back to basics: Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)

The CBD is a global treaty aimed at conserving biological diversity, promoting sustainable use of its components, and ensuring fair benefit-sharing from genetic resources. It convenes Conferences of Parties (COP) where member countries negotiate frameworks and commitments to protect biodiversity. National strategies translate these global frameworks into country-specific targets and actions.
