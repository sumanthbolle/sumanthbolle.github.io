NBA Disburses Rs 1.19 Crore to State Biodiversity Boards via ABS Mechanism

The National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) distributed Rs 1.19 crore to 29 State Biodiversity Boards and one UT Biodiversity Council under the Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) mechanism to support biodiversity conservation and community welfare. This highlights the practical implementation of biodiversity governance and benefit sharing under India's Biological Diversity Act.

The NBA's ABS fund distribution supports biodiversity conservation and local community welfare through regulated sharing of benefits from agricultural biological resources.

Why is this in the news?

On 17 October 2026, the NBA disbursed Rs 1.19 crore under the ABS mechanism to State Biodiversity Boards and a UT Biodiversity Council, reflecting ongoing efforts in biodiversity governance.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) mechanism?

  • Definition and purpose. The ABS mechanism facilitates sharing of benefits arising from the use of biological resources, ensuring conservation, sustainable use, and community welfare.
  • Role of NBA. The National Biodiversity Authority regulates ABS contributions from companies using agricultural biological resources for research and commercial purposes.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How was the Rs 1.19 crore distributed among States?

  • Distribution basis. Allocations were made proportionally based on estimated cultivation areas of biological resources, using official agricultural statistics.
  • Top recipients. Maharashtra received Rs 19,46,149, Rajasthan Rs 13,97,525, and Madhya Pradesh Rs 13,32,250 among others.
  • Resources covered. The funds relate to maize, rice, pearl millet, and cotton varieties used by companies for research and commercial utilization.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this matter for biodiversity conservation and governance?

  • Supporting conservation. Funds support activities like biodiversity documentation, conservation, sustainable use, and strengthening local biodiversity governance.
  • Community welfare. Benefit sharing promotes enhancement of community livelihoods and capacity building under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002.
  • Agriculture sector role. The agriculture sector is emerging as a key contributor to ABS, demonstrating how agricultural biodiversity can generate conservation benefits.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the broader significance of this disbursement?

  • Cumulative impact. NBA has disbursed over Rs 203 crore so far, with Rs 86 crore from the agriculture sector, supporting biodiversity and local communities.
  • International context. This effort aligns with India's commitment ahead of the 17th Conference of the Parties to the Convention on Biological Diversity (COP 17) in October 2026.

Back to basics: Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) Mechanism

Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) is a system under the Biological Diversity Act that ensures benefits arising from the use of biological resources are shared fairly with local communities and stakeholders. It promotes conservation and sustainable use of biodiversity by regulating commercial and research use of biological resources and distributing benefits to support local biodiversity governance and livelihoods.
