Expert Urges Temporary Renewal of India-Bangladesh Ganga Water Treaty

The 30-year-old Ganga Water Treaty between India and Bangladesh is set to expire soon, and an expert has urged political leaders of both countries to support its temporary renewal for three to five years. This is important for UPSC aspirants to understand the challenges in transboundary water treaties and their impact on bilateral relations and resource management.

The Ganga Water Treaty, crucial for predictable water sharing between India and Bangladesh, needs temporary renewal to avoid disruption in water supply affecting Bangladesh's agriculture and ecology.

Why is this in the news?

On November 15, 2026, former Chairman of Bangladesh's National River Conservation Commission, Manjur Ahmed Chowdhury, highlighted the impossibility of revising the Ganga Water Treaty before its expiry and called for its temporary renewal.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current status of the Ganga Water Treaty?

  • Treaty expiry. The 30-year-old Ganga Water Treaty between India and Bangladesh is about to expire with only four weeks left for renewal or revision.
  • Temporary renewal proposal. Manjur Ahmed Chowdhury suggested a temporary renewal of the treaty for three to five years as a full revision is not feasible within the limited time.
  • Political clearance needed. Such a renewal requires approval from the highest political leadership of both India and Bangladesh.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the treaty matter for India-Bangladesh relations and water management?

  • Ensures predictable water supply. The treaty provides a legally binding framework for sharing Ganga waters, ensuring predictability in water flow, which Bangladesh depended on after suffering unpredictability between 1988 and 1996.
  • Agricultural and ecological impact. Without the treaty, India has no obligation to release water regularly, which could severely affect Bangladesh's agriculture sector, economy, and ecology, especially during the February agricultural season.
  • Water sharing formula. The treaty specifies water sharing at Farakka based on ten-day periods from January 1 to May 31, with detailed division formulas depending on water availability.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What challenges affect the treaty's revision and implementation?

  • Need for updated treaty. The treaty should be revised to address issues like sedimentation and river changes over the last three decades, but lack of meetings and weak Joint River Commission have delayed this.
  • Foreign policy disconnect. Bangladesh's foreign policy has not effectively managed river water diplomacy, leading to challenges in securing water rights.
  • Urgent political agreement required. An immediate agreement between the political leaderships of both countries is necessary to keep the treaty operational and avoid reverting to past unpredictability.

Back to basics: Transboundary Water Treaty

A transboundary water treaty is an agreement between two or more countries sharing a river or water resource to regulate its use and management. Such treaties ensure predictable water sharing, prevent conflicts, and promote cooperation on ecological and economic issues related to the river basin.
