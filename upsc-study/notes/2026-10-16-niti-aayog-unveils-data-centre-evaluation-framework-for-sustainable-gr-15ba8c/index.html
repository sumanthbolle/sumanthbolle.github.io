NITI Aayog's Data Centre Evaluation Framework for Sustainable Growth

NITI Aayog released a Data Centre Evaluation Framework on 14 October 2026 to help Indian States plan sustainable growth of data centres by assessing infrastructure readiness and resource sustainability. This framework is important for UPSC aspirants as it links digital infrastructure development with environmental and economic planning, a key theme in sustainable development and resource management.

The framework guides States to evaluate infrastructure and resource availability for sustainable data centre investments, balancing economic growth with environmental safeguards.

Why is this in the news?

NITI Aayog’s Frontier Tech Hub unveiled the Data Centre Evaluation Framework on 14 October 2026 to support States in sustainable planning of data centres amid rapid sector growth.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the Data Centre Evaluation Framework?

  • Purpose. The framework helps States move from merely attracting data-centre investments to building investment-ready infrastructure that protects critical resources and maximises long-term economic and strategic value.
  • Development. It was developed with inputs from industry, Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, Ministry of Power, and Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade.
  • Structure. The framework is based on five pillars: Power and Energy; Water and Cooling; Siting and Environmental Sustainability; Economic and Strategic Value; and Policy, Governance and Implementation.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does sustainable planning of data centres matter?

  • Sector growth. India’s data-centre capacity grew from about 375 MW in 2020 to approximately 1.5 GW in 2026, driven by AI, cloud computing, and data-intensive applications.
  • Resource demands. Data centres require substantial electricity, water, land, and connectivity, which can strain local infrastructure and resources if not planned sustainably.
  • Environmental concerns. Local opposition has arisen due to concerns over water use, electricity demand, and ecological impacts, highlighting the need for careful resource assessment.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

How does the framework guide States in planning?

  • Resource assessment. States are advised to prepare 20-30 year seasonal assessments of water availability, assess grid readiness, and evaluate basin-level water stress and public supply impacts.
  • Economic evaluation. States must evaluate economic benefits against public support like land and utility concessions, linking incentives to measurable outcomes such as employment and renewable energy use.
  • Implementation support. The framework promotes time-bound approvals, clear accountability, monitoring of project commitments, and encourages Make in India initiatives across IT and non-IT infrastructure.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the strategic implications for India?

  • Technological capability. The framework supports leveraging data-centre investments to strengthen domestic R&D, industry-academia collaboration, and Indian participation in the data-centre technology value chain.
  • Sustainable competitiveness. It aims to build sustainable capacity that enhances India’s competitiveness and delivers lasting economic value while addressing environmental sustainability.
  • Governance. A central audit mechanism with the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology as nodal ministry is proposed to oversee environmental safeguards, climate risks, and governance.

Back to basics: Sustainable Infrastructure Planning

Sustainable infrastructure planning involves designing and developing facilities that meet current needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs. It requires balancing economic growth with environmental protection and resource conservation. For data centres, this means assessing resource availability like power and water, minimizing environmental impact, and ensuring long-term viability of infrastructure investments.

What happens next?

  • Central audit mechanism. The framework proposes a central audit mechanism with the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology as the nodal ministry to ensure environmental safeguards and governance.
  • State-level planning. States need to enhance their capacity for long-term resource assessment and link incentives to measurable outcomes to ensure sustainable data-centre growth.
