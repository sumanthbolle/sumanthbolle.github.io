Maharashtra Declares Two Wildlife Sanctuaries and Approves 14 Conservation Reserves

Maharashtra's State Board for Wildlife approved declaring two reserved forest areas as wildlife sanctuaries and sanctioned 14 new conservation reserves across multiple districts. This development is relevant for UPSC aspirants studying environment conservation, biodiversity protection, and forest governance.

Maharashtra expanded its protected areas by declaring two reserved forests as wildlife sanctuaries and approving 14 conservation reserves to enhance biodiversity conservation.

Why is this in the news?

On October 26, 2026, Maharashtra's State Board for Wildlife approved the declaration of two reserved forest areas as wildlife sanctuaries and sanctioned 14 conservation reserves in various districts.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What new protected areas were approved in Maharashtra?

  • Wildlife sanctuaries declared. The Kolamarka Conservation Reserve in Gadchiroli district was approved to become a wildlife sanctuary due to its wild buffalo population, and the newly expanded Tansa Wildlife Sanctuary was also declared a wildlife sanctuary.
  • Expansion of Tansa Sanctuary. The Board approved including 588.767 hectares of revenue and private land from six villages into the newly expanded Tansa Wildlife Sanctuary to make it free of human habitation.
  • Conservation reserves approved. Fourteen conservation reserves were approved in Pune, Raigad, Nashik, Nandurbar, Thane, Palghar, and Chandrapur districts, including areas like Velhe-Mulshi, Bhor-Girigad, Dindori, and others.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for forest governance and biodiversity?

  • Enhanced protection for wildlife. Declaring reserved forests as wildlife sanctuaries provides stronger legal protection to habitats and species, such as wild buffaloes in Kolamarka.
  • Human habitation management. Including private and revenue land from villages into sanctuaries aims to reduce human-wildlife conflict by making sanctuaries free of human habitation.
  • Integrated management plans. Routine works like dam repairs and rehabilitation within sanctuaries will be incorporated into management plans with standard operating procedures to streamline approvals.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What other conservation and infrastructure measures were approved?

  • Critical wildlife habitats notified. Proposals were approved to declare parts of Koka Wildlife Sanctuary and Jayakwadi Bird Wildlife Sanctuary as critical wildlife habitats.
  • Infrastructure in protected areas. Approvals were given for irrigation, healthcare, water supply, bridges, roads, optical fibre cables, and electricity lines within protected areas, eco-sensitive zones, and tiger corridors.
  • Policy on human-wildlife conflict and rehabilitation. The Board discussed declaring human-wildlife conflict a natural disaster and formulating rehabilitation policies for villages in tiger reserve buffer zones.

Back to basics: Wildlife Sanctuaries and Conservation Reserves

Wildlife sanctuaries are protected areas established to conserve wildlife and their habitats, where human activities are regulated to prevent disturbance. Conservation reserves are areas adjacent to national parks or sanctuaries that act as buffer zones with community involvement in protection. Both are part of India's strategy to preserve biodiversity and manage forest resources sustainably.
