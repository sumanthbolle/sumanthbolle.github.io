RBI’s Standing Advisory Committee Reviews MSME Credit Flow

The Reserve Bank of India held the 31st meeting of its Standing Advisory Committee on November 27, 2026, to review credit flow to the MSME sector. This meeting is important for understanding RBI’s role in promoting MSME financing and financial inclusion, relevant for both Prelims and Mains.

RBI’s Standing Advisory Committee meeting focused on enhancing credit availability and reducing collateral dependence for MSMEs to support economic growth.

Why is this in the news?

The 31st meeting of RBI’s Standing Advisory Committee on MSME credit flow was held on November 27, 2026, chaired by RBI’s Deputy Governor.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What happened at the RBI Standing Advisory Committee meeting on MSME credit?

  • Meeting details. The 31st meeting of the Standing Advisory Committee to review MSME credit was held in Kolkata on November 27, 2026, chaired by RBI Deputy Governor Shri Swaminathan J.
  • Participants. Attendees included senior officials from the Ministry of MSME, Ministry of Finance, select banks, SIDBI, NABARD, CGTMSE, NCGTC, KVIC, Indian Banks’ Association, and MSME associations.
  • Key discussions. The committee reviewed working capital and investment credit, lending to first-time borrowers, credit guarantee coverage, and higher collateral-free lending limits for micro and small enterprises.
  • Policy focus. RBI urged banks to expedite credit sanctions, reduce reliance on collateral by using cash-flow-based lending through ULI and digital channels, and promote receivables financing via TReDS.
  • Industry engagement. Industry associations were invited to participate in RBI’s outreach events like town halls and district-level MSME SAMVAAD meetings to raise concerns and improve awareness.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Why does RBI’s advisory on MSME credit matter for the exam?

  • MSME sector importance. MSMEs contribute significantly to India’s economy and development goals, making credit flow to this sector crucial for inclusive growth.
  • Financial inclusion. RBI’s push for collateral-free lending and digital credit channels supports financial inclusion by making credit more accessible to MSMEs.
  • Policy implementation. Understanding RBI’s role in credit policy and coordination with ministries and financial institutions helps in answering questions on economic governance and MSME development.

Back to basics: Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs)

MSMEs are enterprises classified based on investment and turnover limits and are vital for employment and economic growth. They often face challenges in accessing formal credit due to lack of collateral, making policy measures like collateral-free lending and credit guarantees important to support their financing needs.
