RBI Releases September 2026 Data on India's International Trade in Services

The Reserve Bank of India published provisional data on India's exports and imports of services for September 2026, showing growth compared to the previous year. This data is crucial for understanding India's economic engagement in global services trade, relevant for both Prelims and Mains preparation.

India's international trade in services for September 2026 showed exports of US$39,305 million and imports of US$20,097 million, reflecting growth over the previous year.

Why is this in the news?

The Reserve Bank of India released the monthly data on India's international trade in services for September 2026 on October 30, 2026.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What are the key figures in India's trade in services for September 2026?

  • Exports and imports values. India's exports (receipts) of services in September 2026 were US$39,305 million, while imports (payments) were US$20,097 million.
  • Growth rates. Exports grew by 7.2% and imports grew by 12.6% compared to September of the previous year.
  • Data provisional status. The data for July to September 2026 are provisional and have been revised based on balance of payments statistics.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Why does this data matter for UPSC preparation?

  • Economic health indicator. Trade in services data reflects India's economic health and its integration with the global economy.
  • Relevance to economy syllabus. Understanding the trends in services exports and imports helps in analyzing India's growth, resource mobilisation, and trade policies.
  • Prelims and Mains utility. The data can be used to answer questions on India's external sector in Prelims and to discuss economic growth and trade in Mains.

Back to basics: International Trade in Services

International trade in services involves the exchange of intangible products such as IT services, financial services, tourism, and transportation between countries. It is a key component of a country's balance of payments and reflects its economic engagement with the world. Tracking exports and imports of services helps understand economic growth, competitiveness, and global integration.
