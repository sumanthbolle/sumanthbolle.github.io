India implications

  • Rural water governance may become more decentralised if Gram Panchayats prepare annual O&M plans, mobilise resources and manage service delivery.
  • Better coordination among Rural Water Supply Departments, Panchayati Raj Institutions and Rural Development Departments could reduce fragmented implementation.
  • Prioritising maintenance and source sustainability can protect public investment and improve the continuity and safety of rural drinking-water services.
  • Capacity-building under RGSA and translation of O&M manuals into local languages can improve the ability of Panchayats and local functionaries to use funds correctly.

Way forward

  • Prepare annual Gram Panchayat-level O&M plans covering repairs, energy, staffing, water quality, leak management and source sustainability.
  • Align Finance Commission grant utilisation with permissible activities and local plans, while using convergence to reduce dependence on one funding stream.
  • Build Panchayat and local-functionary capacity through RGSA and disseminate O&M manuals in local languages.
  • Use transparent reporting, community participation, service-level indicators and audit mechanisms to connect expenditure with actual water reliability.
