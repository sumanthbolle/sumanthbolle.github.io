India implications

  • Urban local bodies can use outcome-oriented assessment to identify service gaps in collection, segregation, used-water management and sanitation access.
  • Greater attention to sanitation-worker welfare and grievance redressal links municipal performance with dignity, inclusion and accountable governance.
  • Coverage of urban river fronts, coastlines, water bodies, tourist and heritage hotspots and school behaviour-change initiatives broadens cleanliness policy beyond visible streets.
  • The model can support cooperative and competitive federalism, but implementation should adapt indicators to local conditions and ensure that rankings do not substitute for basic municipal capacity.

Way forward

  • Treat rankings as signals for corrective action rather than as the sole measure of municipal success.
  • Combine citizen feedback with independently verifiable field evidence and transparent grievance resolution.
  • Use mentor-city learning alongside financing, technical support and institutional strengthening for weaker urban local bodies.
  • Retain explicit measures for sanitation-worker welfare, used-water management, source segregation and behaviour change.
