Background

  • Other Effective Area-Based Conservation Measures (OECMs) are areas outside legally protected or conserved areas that deliver positive and sustained long-term outcomes for in-situ biodiversity conservation.
  • The Guidelines were prepared by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change in partnership with the National Biodiversity Authority.
  • Illustrative areas mentioned in the official record include biodiversity parks, sacred groves, community-managed irrigation tanks, seagrass and seaweed beds, and fisheries seasonal-ban areas.
  • The framework supports implementation of Target 3 (30x30) of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework and National Biodiversity Target 3 under the updated National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan, 2024–2030.

India implications

  • Can broaden India’s conservation estate without automatically converting recognised sites into protected areas.
  • May strengthen community-linked and locally governed conservation where Biodiversity Management Committees, Panchayats and other recognised institutions already manage ecological resources.
  • Creates a coordination role for NBA, State Biodiversity Boards, UT Biodiversity Councils, MoEFCC and UNEP-WCMC.
  • Requires ecological indicators, periodic assessment and transparent records to ensure that area-based reporting reflects actual conservation outcomes.

Way forward

  • Use site-specific ecological indicators rather than relying only on area reported.
  • Build the capacity of Biodiversity Management Committees, Panchayats, State Biodiversity Boards and UT Biodiversity Councils.
  • Maintain transparent records and conduct the required periodic assessments.
  • Prevent double counting through defined boundaries and systematic overlap checks.

Use in an answer

Use I-OECMs to frame biodiversity conservation as outcome-based, landscape-inclusive and institutionally coordinated, while distinguishing recognition from legal protection and insisting on ecological monitoring.

Recall card

I-OECMs recognise defined, non-protected areas delivering sustained in-situ biodiversity outcomes. India’s 2026 Guidelines require recognised governance, non-overlap, unchanged legal status and measurable monitoring at least every five years; NBA and MoEFCC lead recognition and reporting.
