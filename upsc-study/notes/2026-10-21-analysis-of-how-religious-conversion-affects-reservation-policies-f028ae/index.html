Impact of Religious Conversion on Reservation Eligibility in India

The Supreme Court upheld the invalidation of Tamil Nadu's 2024 order allowing persons converting to Islam from Scheduled Castes and Backward Classes to claim BC Muslim reservation. This ruling clarifies that conversion does not automatically change a person's notified community status, but social disadvantage requires evidence-based examination.

Religious conversion alone does not entitle a person to reservation benefits of the new community; social and legal criteria must be met.

Why is this in the news?

On October 15, 2026, the Supreme Court upheld the Madras High Court judgment invalidating Tamil Nadu Government Order dated March 9, 2024, which allowed converts from SC and BC communities to Islam to claim BC Muslim reservation.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What was the Tamil Nadu Government Order of 2024 about?

  • Order details. The order enabled persons converting to Islam from Backward Classes, Most Backward Classes, Denotified Communities, and Scheduled Castes to obtain Backward Class Muslim certificates and access the State’s 3.5% reservation for BC Muslims.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court rule regarding conversion and reservation?

  • Invalidation of the order. The Supreme Court upheld the Madras High Court’s judgment invalidating the Tamil Nadu order, emphasizing that conversion alone does not automatically transfer a person into a different notified community for reservation purposes.
  • Distinction between communities. The ruling highlights the legal distinction between Scheduled Caste status and backward-class classification, which are not interchangeable by mere religious conversion.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for reservation policy and social justice?

  • Need for evidence-based inquiry. While conversion does not grant automatic reservation rights, continuing social disadvantage faced by converts requires an evidence-based inquiry to determine eligibility.
  • Implications for affirmative action. The judgment clarifies the constitutional and legal framework governing reservation benefits, ensuring that affirmative action is based on social and educational backwardness rather than religion alone.

Back to basics: Reservation Policy and Social Backwardness

Reservation in India is a system of affirmative action that provides quotas in education, employment, and legislature to socially and educationally backward classes. These classes are notified based on historical disadvantage, not solely on religion. Conversion to another religion does not automatically change a person's eligibility for reservation benefits, as the criteria include social and educational backwardness within the notified community.
