Over 6.5 Lakh Voters Excluded in Karnataka’s Largest Electoral District Belagavi

The Election Commission of India's Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls led to the exclusion of 6,58,862 voters in Belagavi district, about 15.8% of its electorate. This raises important questions on electoral roll accuracy and voter disenfranchisement relevant for UPSC exams on electoral reforms and democratic participation.

Belagavi district saw over 6.5 lakh voters deleted from rolls after SIR, highlighting challenges in electoral roll management and potential voter disenfranchisement.

Why is this in the news?

On October 30, 2026, The Hindu reported that over 6.5 lakh voters were left out of Belagavi district’s electoral rolls following the Election Commission of India's Special Intensive Revision process.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What happened in Belagavi’s electoral roll revision?

  • Mass deletions. The Special Intensive Revision (SIR) reduced Belagavi’s voter count from 41,53,775 in January 2025 to 34,94,913, excluding 6,58,862 voters or 15.8% of the electorate.
  • Higher than state average. Belagavi’s deletion percentage is higher than Karnataka’s average of around 12%, though lower than Bengaluru’s 43% deletion rate.
  • Constituency-wise impact. All 18 Assembly seats in Belagavi saw an average deletion of 36,603 votes each, with some constituencies like Belgaum Dakshin and Yamkanamardi experiencing tens of thousands of deletions.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Who were affected and why does it matter?

  • Muslim voter exclusions. Among the deleted voters, approximately 1,03,839 were Muslims, with significant numbers in constituencies like Belgaum Uttar and Kudachi.
  • Political implications. Deletions in some constituencies exceeded winning margins by multiple times, raising concerns about the impact on electoral outcomes and fairness.
  • Allegations of misuse. Complaints surfaced about misuse of Form 7 applications, including document fudging and intimidation, with political leaders from BJP and Congress accusing each other of violating rules.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) process?

  • Purpose of SIR. The SIR is conducted by the Election Commission of India to update and clean electoral rolls by removing duplicate, dead, shifted, absent, and otherwise invalid entries.
  • Deletion categories. Voters are categorized as Absent, Shifted, Dead, Duplicate, and Others (ASDDO) for deletion during the SIR.
  • Use of Form 7. Third-party objections to voter names are filed using Form 7, which has been a source of controversy due to alleged misuse.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Electoral roll accuracy. Understanding the SIR process and its challenges is key to grasping issues of voter disenfranchisement and electoral integrity.
  • Democratic participation. Large-scale deletions can affect voter representation and raise questions about fairness in elections.
  • Electoral reforms. The controversy highlights the need for reforms to ensure transparent and accurate voter registration.

Back to basics: Electoral Rolls and Voter Registration

Electoral rolls are official lists of eligible voters in a constituency. They are periodically updated to add new voters and remove names of those who have died, moved away, or are otherwise ineligible. The Election Commission of India conducts revisions like the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) to maintain accurate voter lists, which is essential for free and fair elections.
