Ministry of Mines Launches Ninth Tranche of Critical Mineral Block Auctions with Reforms

The Ministry of Mines launched the ninth tranche of e-auction for critical and strategic mineral blocks and the third tranche of exploration licence auctions on 5 November 2026. This move, along with reforms in auction rules, aims to strengthen India's mineral security and promote private sector participation in exploration and mining.

The Ministry of Mines launched the ninth tranche of critical mineral block auctions with reforms to improve auction outcomes and promote responsible mining.

Why is this in the news?

On 5 November 2026, the Ministry of Mines launched the ninth tranche of critical and strategic mineral block auctions and the third tranche of exploration licence auctions, unveiling reforms to improve auction participation and outcomes.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What did the Ministry of Mines launch in November 2026?

  • Ninth tranche of mineral block auctions. The Ministry launched the ninth tranche of e-auction for 20 critical and strategic mineral blocks under the Mineral (Auction) Rules.
  • Third tranche of exploration licence auctions. Alongside, the third tranche of auctions for Exploration Licences (EL) was launched, allowing private investment in exploration of 29 critical and deep-seated minerals.
  • Auction participation. Previously, 100 unique critical and strategic mineral blocks were offered in eight tranches, with 64 successfully auctioned, a success rate of 64%.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Which minerals and states are involved in the auctions?

  • Minerals in critical blocks. The 20 blocks include minerals such as graphite, glauconite, vanadium, gallium, titanium, rare earth elements (REE), potash, halite, tungsten, phosphorite-limestone, bauxite, aluminous laterite, scandium, and rare metals.
  • States with auctioned blocks. Blocks are located in Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Telangana, West Bengal, and Gujarat (for EL blocks).
  • Exploration licence blocks. The third tranche of EL auctions covers copper, potash, halite, vanadium, and rare earth elements among others in Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, West Bengal, Rajasthan, Telangana, and Gujarat.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What reforms were introduced to improve auction outcomes?

  • Reduced bidder requirement. The Mineral (Auction) Third Amendment Rules, 2026 reduced the minimum number of technically qualified bidders for the second round from three to two for Part D minerals, addressing a key reason for unsold blocks.
  • Automatic issuance of Letters of Intent. Letters of Intent will now be issued automatically through the Unified Mining Portal to streamline the process.
  • Auction formats. Critical mineral blocks are auctioned through a two-stage ascending forward auction where the highest revenue share wins, while exploration licence blocks use a two-stage descending reverse auction where the lowest revenue share wins.
  • Earlier reforms. These reforms build on earlier changes such as the Mineral (Auction) Second Amendment Rules, 2025 and Insurance Surety Bonds to improve ease of doing business and investor confidence.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why do these auctions and reforms matter?

  • Strengthening mineral security. The auctions aim to secure domestic availability of critical and strategic minerals essential for sectors like clean energy, electric mobility, defence, aerospace, and electronics.
  • Encouraging private sector participation. Allowing private investment in exploration and mining promotes responsible mining and builds a resilient, self-reliant, and globally competitive mineral ecosystem.
  • Supporting energy transition. Access to critical minerals supports India's energy transition and industrial growth by ensuring supply for advanced manufacturing and strategic industries.

Back to basics: Mineral Auction and Exploration Licence

The Mineral (Auction) Rules govern the auction of mineral blocks in India to allocate mining rights transparently. Exploration Licences allow private entities to invest in exploring mineral deposits before mining starts, promoting early discovery and development. Auctions use competitive bidding to determine revenue share, ensuring fair value for mineral resources.
