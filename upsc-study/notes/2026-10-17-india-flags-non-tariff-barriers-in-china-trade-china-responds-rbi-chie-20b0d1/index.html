India Flags Non-Tariff Barriers in China Trade; US Trade Deal Progress

Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman highlighted non-tariff barriers as the biggest hurdle in India-China trade and expressed hope for an early conclusion of the India-US trade agreement. This matters for UPSC aspirants studying India's trade relations and economic diplomacy.

India identifies non-tariff barriers as key challenges in trade with China while progressing towards a trade pact with the US.

Why is this in the news?

On October 16, 2026, Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman discussed trade challenges with China and the progress of the India-US trade agreement during the IMF-World Bank meetings.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the challenges in India-China trade?

  • Non-tariff barriers. India faces significant non-tariff barriers from China, including phytosanitary measures and other restrictions that limit market access for Indian products like generic medicines, bovine meat, and vegetables.
  • Trade imbalance. India's trade deficit with China has crossed $100 billion in 2026, reflecting persistent restrictions on Indian exports despite years of engagement.
  • India's approach. Instead of imposing punitive tariffs to address trade imbalances, India prefers negotiation and dialogue, even if the process is time-consuming.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the status of the India-US trade agreement?

  • Progress in negotiations. The first phase of the India-US trade pact has been finalized and is undergoing legal drafting as of October 2026.
  • Government optimism. Union Finance Minister Sitharaman expressed hope that the agreement will be concluded soon to benefit global trade and bilateral economic ties.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for India's economy and trade policy?

  • Trade relations impact. Addressing non-tariff barriers and concluding trade agreements are crucial for improving India's export potential and reducing trade deficits.
  • Economic growth factors. India's sustained strong growth is supported by inclusive growth, fiscal prudence, and public capital expenditure, which are influenced by trade dynamics.
  • BRICS cooperation. India views BRICS as a partnership to improve welfare and economic opportunities rather than as a platform to counter other countries.

Back to basics: Non-Tariff Barriers

Non-tariff barriers are trade restrictions that do not involve tariffs but can include quotas, import licensing, sanitary regulations, and other measures that restrict imports or exports. They can limit market access and affect trade balances by making it harder for foreign goods to compete. Understanding these barriers is essential for analyzing international trade relations and negotiations.
