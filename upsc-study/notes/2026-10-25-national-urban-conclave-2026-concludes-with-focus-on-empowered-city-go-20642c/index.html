National Urban Conclave 2026 Emphasises Empowered City Governments

The National Urban Conclave 2026 held in Bhubaneswar focused on reforms in urban governance, planning, and financing to support India's urbanisation and economic growth goals. Empowered city governments with devolved functions and financial autonomy were highlighted as essential for effective service delivery and citizen participation.

Empowered city governments with devolved functions and financial autonomy are crucial for India's urban growth and sustainable development.

Why is this in the news?

The 2nd National Urban Conclave was held on 24-25 October 2026 in Bhubaneswar, Odisha, focusing on urban governance reforms and sustainable urban development.

What were the key themes of the National Urban Conclave 2026?

  • Focus on urban systems and delivery. The Conclave focused on reimagining urban systems, institutions, and implementation mechanisms to improve how cities function, with over 1,200 participants including Ministers, Mayors, and urban local bodies.
  • Seven thematic deliberations. Day-one sessions covered themes such as Right to Walk, Water and Waste Efficiency, Affordable Housing, Integrated Urban Development, Urban Service Delivery, Model Building Bye-Laws, and Model Municipal Law.
  • Launch of initiatives. Several initiatives were launched, including HUDCO's Urban Invest Window (UiWIN), the Samarthya Platform for capacity building, the ANKUR Handbook, and policies on public spaces and waste management.

[2] Press Information Bureau

Why is empowering city governments important for India's urban growth?

  • Urban population growth. India's urban population is expected to grow from around 50 crore currently to 85 crore by 2050, requiring fundamental changes in urban planning, governance, and financing.
  • Need for local governance. Cities cannot be effectively governed from state capitals; empowered city governments with authority and institutional capacity are essential for responsive service delivery and citizen participation.
  • Devolution of functions. States must devolve all 18 functions to urban local bodies as per the 74th Amendment, including master planning, water supply, fire services, and traffic management.
  • Financial autonomy. Municipal revenues are low; improving property tax and user-charge recovery and ensuring adequate fiscal transfers from states to urban local bodies are critical.

[3] Press Information Bureau

What urban infrastructure and policy developments were highlighted?

  • Urban transport progress. Since December 2025, nine Metro projects covering 174 kilometres costing over ₹59,000 crore were approved, with 102 kilometres becoming operational, expanding the Metro network to 1,185 kilometres across 26 cities.
  • Electric bus operations. Under the PM e-Bus Sewa Scheme, 1,100 electric buses operate in 28 cities, carrying about 2.5 lakh passengers daily.
  • Transit-Oriented Development policy. Delhi’s TOD Policy 2026 promotes planned development and affordable housing across 207 square kilometres along Metro, RRTS, and railway corridors.
  • Urban Challenge Fund and investments. The ₹1 lakh crore Urban Challenge Fund and ₹5,000 crore allocations for city economic regions support urban growth hubs and infrastructure.

[1] Press Information Bureau[3] Press Information Bureau

What reforms are needed in urban planning and housing?

  • Planning paradigm overhaul. Reforms are needed to remove restrictive Floor Area Ratios and building norms that limit land supply and increase costs for housing and commercial use.
  • Zoning and land use. Adoption of mixed-use zoning, development around transit hubs, and land pooling can increase land supply and reduce costs.
  • Affordable rental housing. Affordable rental housing is vital for migrant workers, industrial labour, students, and young professionals; States are urged to adopt the Model Tenancy Act to increase rental housing supply.

Back to basics: Urban Local Bodies and 74th Amendment

Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) are institutions of local self-government in urban areas responsible for civic administration and infrastructure. The 74th Amendment to the Constitution mandates the devolution of specific functions, powers, and finances to ULBs to promote decentralised governance and empower cities to manage their affairs effectively. This includes functions like urban planning, water supply, public health, and roads. Effective urban governance requires ULBs to have adequate authority, financial resources, and institutional capacity.

What happens next?

  • Implement municipal governance reforms. States should enact new Municipal Acts to enable directly elected mayors, Mayor-in-Council systems, and timely municipal elections to strengthen city governance.
  • Enhance fiscal transfers. State Finance Commissions must be regularly constituted to ensure adequate and timely fiscal transfers to urban local bodies for improved service delivery.
  • Adopt urban planning reforms. Reforms to remove restrictive building norms and promote mixed-use zoning and land pooling are needed to increase land supply and reduce housing costs.
Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about urban governance reforms discussed at the National Urban Conclave 2026: 1. The 74th Amendment mandates devolution of 18 functions to urban local bodies, including master planning and water supply. 2. The Urban Challenge Fund announced in 2025-26 budget allocates ₹5,000 crore for seven city economic regions over five years. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 incorrectly states the total Urban Challenge Fund amount; the ₹5,000 crore is the allocation per city economic region, not the total fund.
