Supreme Court Seeks Response on MLAs Abandoning Seats to Shift Loyalty

The Supreme Court issued notice to the Centre on a plea challenging MLAs who resign and switch parties, causing by-elections funded by the public. This raises important questions on anti-defection laws and the integrity of representative democracy, relevant for both Prelims and Mains.

The Supreme Court is examining whether MLAs can resign and defect to another party, triggering costly by-elections that burden the public exchequer and undermine democratic trust.

Why is this in the news?

On October 26, 2026, the Supreme Court issued notice to the Union government on a plea challenging MLAs resigning and switching parties to avoid disqualification under the anti-defection law, causing by-elections at public expense.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the issue raised in the Supreme Court plea?

  • Plea against MLAs' resignation. The plea challenges MLAs who resign their seats to switch parties, triggering by-elections funded by taxpayers, arguing this breaks the trust of the electorate and undermines representative democracy.
  • Context of the plea. The plea was filed against a Madras High Court judgment that dismissed a petition challenging AIADMK MLAs resigning and joining the ruling Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam soon after the 2026 Tamil Nadu elections.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for anti-defection law and democracy?

  • Avoiding disqualification through resignation. MLAs facing disqualification petitions under the Tenth Schedule resigned before the Speaker could decide, thus circumventing anti-defection provisions.
  • Public cost of by-elections. By-elections caused by these resignations are termed 'luxury elections' since they are unnecessary, costly, and funded by public money without any legal compulsion.
  • Impact on representation. The resignations muted the voice of voters from six constituencies during the Budget session, violating the principle that elected representatives serve a full five-year term.
  • Threat to legislative stability. In a hung Assembly, such resignations and defections can destabilize government formation and render the anti-defection law ineffective.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Who are the parties involved in this case?

  • Petitioner and respondents. The appeal was filed by advocate K. Suthan against AIADMK MLAs who resigned and joined the ruling party, with notices issued to the Centre, Election Commission, Tamil Nadu Chief Electoral Officer, State Assembly Secretary, and six former MLAs.
  • Legal representation. Advocate Merlyn Rachel J. represented the petitioner, and Advocate Ram Sankar appeared on caveat for the MLAs.

Back to basics: Anti-defection Law

The anti-defection law aims to prevent elected legislators from switching parties after elections, which can destabilize governments. It provides grounds for disqualification if an MLA voluntarily gives up membership or disobeys party directives. The law seeks to maintain stability and respect the mandate given by voters for a full term.
