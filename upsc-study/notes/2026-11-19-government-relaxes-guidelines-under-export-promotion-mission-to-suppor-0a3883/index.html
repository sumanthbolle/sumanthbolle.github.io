Government Revises Export Promotion Mission Guidelines to Boost MSME Exporters

On 17 November 2026, the Department of Commerce amended guidelines under four interventions of the Export Promotion Mission to enhance support for MSME exporters. These changes aim to improve market access, compliance assistance, and logistics support, especially for exporters in low export-intensity areas.

The government expanded financial and non-financial support under the Export Promotion Mission to strengthen MSME exporters’ participation in global trade and address logistical challenges.

Why is this in the news?

The Department of Commerce amended guidelines on 17 November 2026 under the Export Promotion Mission to support MSME exporters by easing eligibility criteria and expanding coverage of key interventions.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What changes were made under the Export Promotion Mission?

  • Market Access Support (MAS). The maximum eligible export turnover for airfare reimbursement increased from ₹75 lakh to ₹50 crore, with airfare reimbursement raised to 95% for micro and small exporters and 80% for others; the mandatory Export Promotion Council membership period was reduced from six to three months.
  • Trade Regulations, Accreditation & Compliance Enablement (TRACE). The list of certificates eligible for reimbursement expanded from 462 to 600, helping MSMEs meet international quality, safety, and technical standards.
  • Logistics Interventions for Freight and Transport (LIFT). Coverage of low export-intensity districts expanded from 13 to 34 States/UTs; eligible ports now include Land Customs Stations and Integrated Check Posts; waterways added as a transport mode; air freight support extended to Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh; minimum distance criterion reduced from 200 km to 100 km; support extended to all exportable merchandise except those in a negative list.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why do these changes matter for MSME exporters?

  • Enhanced financial support. Higher airfare reimbursements and increased turnover limits reduce cost barriers for MSMEs to participate in international trade events.
  • Improved compliance assistance. Expanding eligible certificates helps MSMEs meet global standards, facilitating smoother market entry and competitiveness.
  • Addressing logistical challenges. Expanding geographic coverage and transport modes lowers freight costs and improves export feasibility for MSMEs in remote or low export-intensity areas.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Export Promotion Mission and its scope?

  • Mission approval and funding. The Union Cabinet approved the Export Promotion Mission on 12 November 2025 with an outlay of ₹25,060 crore for FY 2025-26 to FY 2030-31.
  • Mission objectives. It aims to strengthen India's export competitiveness via financial and non-financial support, focusing on MSMEs, first-time exporters, and under-represented regions.
  • Sub-schemes and interventions. The Mission operates through two sub-schemes: Niryat Protsahan (trade finance access) and Niryat Disha (market access and non-financial barriers), with 11 interventions operational so far.

Back to basics: Export Promotion Mission

An Export Promotion Mission is a government initiative designed to enhance a country's export competitiveness by providing financial and non-financial support to exporters. It typically includes interventions to improve market access, compliance with international standards, and logistics infrastructure, especially targeting micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs). Such missions help exporters overcome barriers to international trade and expand into new markets.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the recent amendments under the Export Promotion Mission: 1. The maximum eligible export turnover for airfare reimbursement under Market Access Support was increased from ₹75 lakh to ₹50 crore. 2. The Logistics Interventions for Freight and Transport (LIFT) now includes waterways as a mode of transport and extends air freight support to Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). A common trap is to confuse the previous turnover limit (₹75 lakh) with the new limit (₹50 crore) or to overlook the extension of air freight support to Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh under LIFT.

Mains question (15 marks)

  • Discuss the widened geographical and logistical support under LIFT to reduce freight costs and address locational challenges.
  • Analyze how these changes collectively improve MSME exporters' ability to participate in global trade.

Conclusion: These amendments strengthen MSME exporters by reducing financial and logistical barriers, thus promoting inclusive export growth.
