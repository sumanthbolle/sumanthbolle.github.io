IOC Plans Own Crude and LPG Ships Amid West Asia Shipping Risks

Indian Oil Corporation (IOC) is acquiring crude oil and LPG carriers to secure its energy supply chain amid rising geopolitical tensions in West Asia that have disrupted shipping near the Strait of Hormuz. This move highlights India's strategic response to shipping vulnerabilities affecting its oil imports, relevant for understanding energy security and infrastructure resilience.

IOC is buying crude and LPG vessels to reduce shipping risks caused by West Asia tensions and to support domestic shipbuilding.

Why is this in the news?

IOC's board approved vessel purchases in response to West Asia geopolitical tensions disrupting shipping and increasing freight and insurance costs, announced during its Q2 earnings call in November 2026.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is IOC buying crude and LPG ships now?

  • Geopolitical tensions. Rising conflicts in West Asia have disrupted commercial shipping near the Strait of Hormuz, increasing freight rates and war-risk insurance costs.
  • Supply chain control. IOC aims to strengthen control over its energy supply chain by owning vessels rather than relying solely on chartered ships.
  • Board approval and tenders. The company's board has approved purchasing vessels, and tenders were issued several months prior to the announcement.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How is IOC changing its shipping strategy?

  • From chartering to ownership. IOC traditionally used time-charter and voyage-chartered vessels but is now exploring owning ships and supporting domestic shipbuilding.
  • Make in India initiative. IOC is considering building ships in India to align with the government's Make in India program.
  • Current fleet and cost savings. IOC currently charters three crude oil vessels and nine LPG vessels, which have saved costs during the crisis compared to spot-market rates.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the broader context of IOC’s procurement and inventory strategy?

  • Diversified crude sourcing. IOC sourced 55% of crude from Russia, 24% from the Middle East, and smaller shares from Africa, Brazil, the US, and Venezuela in the first half of the financial year.
  • Spot purchases increase. Spot imports accounted for about 87% of crude sourcing in the first half of FY27, up from 50% in the previous year, allowing negotiation of discounts amid volatile prices.
  • Inventory levels. IOC increased crude inventories to about 39 days from the usual 33-35 days and holds 20 days of LPG and 23-25 days of petrol and diesel stocks to manage supply disruptions.

Back to basics: Energy security and shipping logistics

Energy security means ensuring reliable and affordable access to energy resources. For countries like India, which import most of their crude oil, securing shipping routes and vessels is critical to avoid supply disruptions. Shipping logistics involves managing the transport of crude oil and petroleum products via chartered or owned vessels, with risks including geopolitical tensions and fluctuating freight costs.
