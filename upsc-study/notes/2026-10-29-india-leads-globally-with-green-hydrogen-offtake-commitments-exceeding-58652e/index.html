India Leads Globally with Over 5.5 Lakh MTPA Green Hydrogen Offtake

Indian companies have signed green hydrogen offtake commitments exceeding 5.5 lakh metric tonnes per annum (MTPA) as of October 2026, positioning India as the global leader in this sector. This development is crucial for UPSC aspirants to understand India's clean energy transition, industrial decarbonisation, and international energy partnerships.

India has become the global leader in green hydrogen offtake commitments, surpassing 5.5 lakh MTPA with major export-oriented contracts and domestic production under the National Green Hydrogen Mission.

Why is this in the news?

As of October 2026, Indian companies signed green hydrogen offtake agreements totaling over 5.5 lakh MTPA, making India the global leader in green hydrogen supply commitments under the National Green Hydrogen Mission.

[2] Press Information Bureau

What is green hydrogen and why is it important?

  • Definition and production. Green hydrogen is produced by splitting water using renewable electricity, resulting in minimal emissions compared to fossil-based hydrogen production.
  • Environmental benefits. It emits no carbon dioxide when used, helping reduce emissions in hard-to-abate sectors like fertiliser production, petroleum refining, steel manufacturing, heavy transport, and shipping.
  • India's resource advantage. India's abundant solar and wind resources make domestic green hydrogen production feasible, supporting energy security and net-zero emission goals.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are India's green hydrogen offtake commitments and their significance?

  • Offtake volume. Indian companies have signed green hydrogen offtake agreements exceeding 5.5 lakh MTPA as of October 2026, accounting for about 11% of the National Green Hydrogen Mission's target.
  • Export orientation. Over 70% of these commitments are earmarked for exports, strengthening India's position in global markets such as Europe, Japan, South Korea, and Germany.
  • Investment scale. These projects are expected to attract investments of around ₹90,000 crore (approximately USD 9.5 billion), indicating large-scale economic and industrial impact.

[2] Press Information Bureau

How is the National Green Hydrogen Mission supporting this growth?

  • Mission objectives. Launched in 2023 with an outlay of ₹19,744 crore, the Mission aims to produce at least 5 million metric tonnes of green hydrogen annually by 2030.
  • Strategic interventions. The SIGHT programme incentivises domestic electrolyser manufacturing and green hydrogen production, with 15 companies awarded capacity for 3,000 MW electrolyser manufacturing and 8,62,000 tonnes per annum of green hydrogen production.
  • Pilot projects and hubs. Pilot projects in steel, mobility, and shipping sectors test technology and infrastructure, while designated hydrogen hubs and innovation clusters improve project viability and economies of scale.
  • Policy and standards. The Mission includes waivers on interstate transmission charges, open access to renewable electricity, and India's Green Hydrogen Standard limiting emissions to 2 kg CO₂ equivalent per kg hydrogen.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What are the key international partnerships and contracts?

  • Major agreements. Notable contracts include ACME Group's supply of 4,88,000 MTPA green ammonia to Japan, Reliance Industries' $3 billion green ammonia deal with South Korea, and AM Green's agreements with Germany and Japan.
  • Export markets. India supplies green ammonia and methanol to European and Japanese markets, emerging as a competitive global supplier with clean bunkering fuels for shipping.

Back to basics: Green Hydrogen and Clean Energy Transition

Green hydrogen is hydrogen produced by electrolysis of water using renewable energy sources like solar and wind, resulting in low carbon emissions. It serves as an energy carrier that can replace fossil fuels in sectors where direct electrification is difficult. The clean energy transition involves shifting from fossil fuels to renewable energy and low-carbon technologies to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and combat climate change.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about India's green hydrogen sector: 1. India has signed green hydrogen offtake commitments exceeding 5.5 lakh MTPA as of October 2026. 2. The National Green Hydrogen Mission aims to produce at least 5 million metric tonnes of green hydrogen annually by 2030 with an initial outlay of ₹19,744 crore. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). A trap could be confusing the offtake commitments figure with the production target or the Mission's outlay, but both statements are supported by the evidence.

Mains question (15 marks)
