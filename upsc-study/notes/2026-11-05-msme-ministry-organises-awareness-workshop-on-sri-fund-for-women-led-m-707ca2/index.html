MSME Ministry's Workshop on SRI Fund for Women-Led Enterprises

The Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) held an awareness workshop on the Self-Reliant India (SRI) Fund to support women-led MSMEs, highlighting its role in mobilising growth capital. This event is important for UPSC aspirants as it illustrates government efforts to promote women entrepreneurship and MSME sector growth through innovative funding mechanisms.

The SRI Fund provides equity support to women-led MSMEs, leveraging ₹48,000 crore in private investment to help scale enterprises beyond traditional debt financing.

Why is this in the news?

On 4 November 2026, the MSME Ministry organised a workshop to promote the SRI Fund for supporting women-led MSMEs under the Yashashvini Campaign.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the SRI Fund and its purpose?

  • Fund structure. The SRI Fund was created with a ₹10,000 crore commitment from the Government of India, expecting private investors to contribute four times that amount, to provide equity support to MSMEs beyond traditional debt.
  • Investment mechanism. The Mother Fund invests only through 72 empanelled Daughter Funds, which make commercial investment decisions in MSMEs based on viability, growth trajectory, and business plans.
  • Impact on MSMEs. Investee enterprises have grown turnover three to four times within two to three years, with 824 MSMEs supported so far, including 74% micro enterprises.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does the SRI Fund matter for women-led MSMEs?

  • Women entrepreneurship focus. The workshop targeted women entrepreneurs, noting that 37.6% of registered MSMEs are women-owned, with a goal to increase this to 50%.
  • Growth capital importance. Women-led MSMEs, especially early-stage ones, require smaller investment tickets, investment-readiness programmes, and flexible support due to lack of collateral or long financial histories.
  • Linking capital and market access. Capital support is linked with market access through platforms like Government e-Marketplace (GeM), ONDC, and exports to enhance growth opportunities.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How does the government support MSMEs through the SRI Fund?

  • Financial commitments. ₹8,305 crore commitments have been made against the ₹10,000 crore corpus, leveraging nearly ₹48,000 crore from private venture capital and equity funds.
  • Additional funding and schemes. An extra ₹2,000 crore was allocated to the SRI Fund in the 2026-27 Union Budget for micro enterprises, alongside a new ₹10,000 crore SME Growth Fund for direct investment in small and medium enterprises.
  • Credit and equity synergy. MSME credit outstanding grew from ₹10 lakh crore in 2014 to over ₹40 lakh crore, with non-performing assets falling to about 3.5%, highlighting the need for credit and equity to work together.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What support ecosystem complements the SRI Fund?

  • Ecosystem partners. Support is provided through incubation, mentorship, market access, information on government schemes, and compliance assistance by partners like Atal Incubation Centre ALEAP Women Entrepreneurs Hub and Women Entrepreneurship Platform of NITI Aayog.
  • Investment readiness. Daughter Funds advise on investment criteria including market strength, team capability, scalable models, audited financials, governance readiness, and legal structure.
  • Entrepreneur guidance. Entrepreneurs are guided on steps for equity funding such as screening, due diligence, and documentation to improve access to growth capital.

Back to basics: Equity funding for MSMEs

Equity funding involves investors providing capital to enterprises in exchange for ownership stakes, which helps businesses grow without the burden of debt repayment. For MSMEs, equity funding is crucial as it offers growth capital, strategic mentorship, and improves access to further credit, especially when collateral is limited.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the SRI Fund for Women-Led MSMEs: 1. The Government of India committed ₹10,000 crore to the SRI Fund expecting private investors to contribute four times that amount. 2. The SRI Fund invests directly in MSMEs without intermediaries. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the SRI Fund invests through Daughter Funds, not directly in MSMEs.
