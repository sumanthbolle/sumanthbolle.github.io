Madras High Court Orders FIR in AI-Generated Book Purchase Scam

The Madras High Court directed the Directorate of Vigilance and Anti Corruption (DVAC) to register a First Information Report (FIR) regarding alleged irregularities in procuring AI-generated books for the Periyar Arivulagam library. This case highlights judicial intervention in governance and anti-corruption, relevant for understanding accountability mechanisms in India.

The Madras High Court mandated FIR registration to investigate the purchase of dubious AI-generated books in a Tamil Nadu public library.

Why is this in the news?

On November 11, 2026, the Madras High Court directed the DVAC to register an FIR following a writ petition by DMK leader I. Paranthamen concerning alleged corruption in book procurement for Periyar Arivulagam library.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered the Madras High Court's intervention?

  • Complaint and petition. DMK Legal Wing joint secretary I. Paranthamen filed a writ petition seeking a direction for DVAC to register an FIR based on his complaint about irregularities in book procurement for Periyar Arivulagam.
  • Allegations of AI-generated books. The complaint alleged that books supplied were AI-generated with fictitious authors and contained incorrect historical and factual information, raising doubts about authenticity.
  • Delay in FIR registration. Despite Paranthamen lodging the complaint on November 7, 2026, DVAC had not registered an FIR or informed him about any preliminary inquiry, prompting judicial action.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the background of the Periyar Arivulagam book purchase controversy?

  • Library establishment and funding. Periyar Arivulagam, a knowledge centre in Coimbatore, was established by the Tamil Nadu government at a cost of ₹300 crore, including ₹50 crore for book procurement.
  • Book selection process. A 112-member book recommendation committee was formed in November 2025, with meetings held in January 2026 and purchase orders issued in February 2026.
  • Public controversy. After the library's inauguration in October 2026, acclaimed author and committee member Perumal Murugan exposed the presence of AI-generated books with dubious content and pricing.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this case matter for governance and anti-corruption?

  • Judicial oversight. The High Court's direction to register an FIR exemplifies judicial intervention to ensure accountability and prompt investigation in alleged corruption cases.
  • Role of vigilance agencies. The case highlights the importance of vigilance bodies like DVAC in investigating irregularities and the need for timely action on complaints.
  • Implications for public procurement. The controversy raises concerns about transparency and quality control in government procurement processes, especially involving new technologies like AI.

Back to basics: Judicial intervention and anti-corruption mechanisms

The judiciary in India can direct investigative agencies to register FIRs and ensure accountability in governance. Vigilance agencies like the Directorate of Vigilance and Anti Corruption (DVAC) investigate corruption allegations. Public procurement involves government processes to acquire goods or services, requiring transparency to prevent misuse of funds.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements regarding the AI-generated book purchase scam case in Tamil Nadu: 1. The Madras High Court directed the DVAC to register an FIR based on a writ petition by DMK leader I. Paranthamen. 2. The Periyar Arivulagam library was established with a budget of ₹300 crore, including ₹50 crore for book procurement. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). A trap is confusing the petitioner or the court's role; the writ petition was filed by DMK leader I. Paranthamen, and the court directed DVAC to act, not the other way around.
