Centre Considers Law Against Child Pornography, Informs Supreme Court

The Union government told the Supreme Court on October 27, 2026, that it is contemplating a statutory legislative framework to address child pornography and non-consensual explicit content involving adults. This development is important for understanding the evolving legal measures on child protection and digital crime enforcement.

The Centre is planning a law to tackle child pornography and non-consensual explicit content, which may include holding intermediaries liable to report such content to authorities.

Why is this in the news?

The Supreme Court heard a plea by NGO Prajwala on October 27, 2026, regarding the circulation of non-consensual intimate images and child pornography, prompting the Centre to inform the court about its plan for a legislative framework.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Centre inform the Supreme Court about the new law?

  • Legislative framework under consideration. The Centre stated it is contemplating a statutory legislative mechanism to tackle child pornography and the circulation of non-consensual explicit content involving adults.
  • Liability on intermediaries. The proposed law might impose liability on intermediaries not only to take down offensive content but also to notify authorities about its uploading.
  • Third-party complaints. The policy framework may allow bona fide complaints from third parties, not just the affected persons.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for child protection and digital crime laws?

  • Addressing gaps in current safeguards. NGO Prajwala highlighted before the Supreme Court the absence of adequate safeguards for adults and the circulation of sexual assault videos, pointing to the need for stronger legal measures.
  • Non-consensual content categories. The court was informed that there are two categories of concern: child pornography and non-consensual intimate images and videos involving adults.
  • Downloading child pornography is already an offence. Existing laws criminalize downloading child pornography, but the new law aims to broaden the framework to cover circulation and intermediary responsibilities.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How will the law be developed and implemented?

  • Stakeholder consultation. The Centre indicated that suggestions would be invited from all stakeholders before finalizing the legislative framework.
  • Parliamentary process awaited. The Solicitor General stated that details cannot be disclosed until the law is presented before Parliament.

Back to basics: Child Protection Laws and Digital Crime

Child protection laws aim to safeguard children from exploitation, abuse, and trafficking, including digital forms such as child pornography. Digital crime laws regulate the use of technology and internet platforms to prevent and penalize offenses like non-consensual sharing of explicit content. Intermediaries such as online platforms have responsibilities under law to remove illegal content and report it to authorities.
