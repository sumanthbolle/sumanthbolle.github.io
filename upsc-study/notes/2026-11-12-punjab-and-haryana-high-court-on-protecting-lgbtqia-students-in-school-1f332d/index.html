Punjab and Haryana HC on Protecting LGBTQIA+ Students in Schools

The Punjab and Haryana High Court directed Haryana education authorities and CBSE to implement Supreme Court guidelines on student mental health and suicide prevention following a petition about bullying and assault of an LGBTQIA+ student. This ruling highlights judicial intervention in safeguarding vulnerable students' rights in educational institutions.

The Punjab and Haryana High Court mandated adherence to Supreme Court guidelines to protect LGBTQIA+ students from bullying and harassment in schools.

Why is this in the news?

On October 31, 2026, the Punjab and Haryana High Court directed Haryana education department and CBSE to ensure compliance with Supreme Court guidelines on student mental health and suicide prevention after a petition by a mother whose 16-year-old LGBTQIA+ son died by suicide following bullying and assault at school.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Punjab and Haryana High Court direct regarding LGBTQIA+ students?

  • Supreme Court guidelines enforcement. The High Court ordered Haryana education authorities and CBSE to ensure schools follow the Supreme Court’s Sukdeb Saha guidelines on student mental health and suicide prevention.
  • Context of the petition. The petition was filed by the mother of a 16-year-old boy who died by suicide in 2022 after prolonged bullying, harassment, and sexual assault at school, partly due to his artistic inclination being misunderstood as effeminate.

Why does this matter for social justice and education?

  • Protection of vulnerable students. The ruling emphasizes the need for schools to protect LGBTQIA+ students from bullying and harassment, ensuring their mental health and safety.
  • Judiciary’s role. It illustrates the judiciary’s intervention in enforcing rights and protections for marginalized groups within educational institutions.

Back to basics: Judicial Protection of Vulnerable Groups

The judiciary can direct government bodies and institutions to uphold rights and protections for vulnerable groups, such as LGBTQIA+ students, especially when their safety and mental health are at risk. Courts may enforce guidelines or laws to prevent discrimination, bullying, and harassment in schools to ensure an inclusive and safe learning environment.
