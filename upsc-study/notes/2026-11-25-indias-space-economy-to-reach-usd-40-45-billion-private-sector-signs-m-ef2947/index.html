India’s Space Economy to Reach USD 40-45 Billion with Private Sector Growth

India’s space economy is set to grow from USD 9 billion to USD 40-45 billion in less than a decade, driven by private sector expansion and policy reforms. This growth highlights the increasing role of private companies in satellite technology and space applications across various sectors.

India’s space economy is rapidly expanding due to private sector participation and technological reforms, aiming for USD 40-45 billion valuation by 2036.

Why is this in the news?

On November 24, 2026, the Indian government announced that the space economy is projected to grow to USD 40-45 billion, with private companies signing major satellite deals at the 5th India International Space Conclave.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the key drivers of India’s space economy growth?

  • Policy reforms. Reforms under Prime Minister Narendra Modi, including the establishment of New Space India Limited (NSIL) and IN-SPACe, have opened the space sector to private participation, breaking the government monopoly.
  • Private sector expansion. India’s space ecosystem has grown from single-digit startups to nearly 400-500 companies, with private firms developing launch vehicles, propulsion systems, and satellite imaging technologies.
  • Technological advancements. Companies like Skyroot and Agnikul have achieved milestones such as maiden launch vehicle flights and development of advanced propulsion systems, showcasing indigenous technological capabilities.
  • Cross-sector applications. Space technology is increasingly used beyond satellite launches, powering infrastructure planning, railways, defence, education, healthcare, and flagship government programmes.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What major satellite deals have private Indian companies signed?

  • GalaxEye-Bellatrix deal. GalaxEye and Bellatrix Aerospace signed a multi-year contract for Bellatrix to supply in-space propulsion systems for 30 GalaxEye satellites, marking the largest business-to-business order in India’s private space sector.
  • OptoSAR technology. GalaxEye is building the world’s first OptoSAR satellite constellation, which can image Earth through clouds, smoke, and darkness, with propulsion systems already flight-tested on ISRO missions.
  • Government support. The Department of Science and Technology committed ₹63.84 crore from the Research, Development and Innovation Fund to GalaxEye for its next multi-sensor satellite targeting imagery sharper than 0.5 metres.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does India’s space economy growth matter for the economy and technology?

  • Economic impact. The space economy’s growth includes not only commercial revenues but also savings, efficiencies, and improved outcomes across sectors, contributing to national development.
  • Technological sovereignty. India aims to strengthen indigenous capabilities in space technology while remaining open to international collaborations, balancing sovereignty with global partnerships.
  • Research and innovation. Government initiatives like the Anusandhan National Research Foundation and RDI Fund encourage private-sector R&D, with calls for increased CSR support and scientific philanthropy.
  • Interconnected technologies. Space technology development is linked with advances in quantum communication, semiconductors, and nuclear science, offering broad opportunities for technological and commercial growth.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What challenges and future steps are indicated for India’s space sector?

  • Demand generation. While capacity and supply are growing, there is a need to create greater demand for space products and services to sustain the ecosystem.
  • Policy stability. Long-term perseverance and stable government policies are essential since space sector investments have longer payback periods.
  • International cooperation. Exploring institutionalised international space innovation alliances and global accelerator programmes can strengthen India’s global role.
  • Expansion and inclusion. Efforts to expand the space ecosystem to more states and encourage participation of students and young innovators are ongoing.

Back to basics: Space Economy and Private Sector Participation

The space economy includes all activities related to manufacturing, launching, and using satellites and space technologies. Private sector participation means companies outside the government contribute to building and operating space systems, which can increase innovation, investment, and commercial opportunities. Policy reforms can open government-controlled sectors to private players, enabling faster growth and technological advancement.

What happens next?

  • Increase demand for space products. India needs to focus on generating greater demand for space-based products and services to sustain the growing private space ecosystem.
  • Maintain policy stability. Long-term stable policies are required to support investments in the space sector, which have longer payback periods.
  • Explore international alliances. Institutionalising wider international space cooperation through innovation alliances and accelerator programmes can enhance India’s global space role.
  • Expand ecosystem inclusivity. Efforts to include more states and encourage youth participation in the space sector are important for broad-based growth.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only

Answer and trap

Answer: (b). Statement 1 incorrectly attributes the growth mainly to government agencies, whereas the evidence highlights private sector driving the growth.
