Background

  • Commercial white LEDs are energy-efficient, but the source record identifies dependence on expensive rare-earth elements, complex high-temperature manufacturing and fixed light tone as limitations.
  • All-inorganic halide perovskite nanocrystals use the formula CsPbX₃, where X may be chlorine, bromine or iodine, and can emit bright colours.
  • When differently composed nanocrystals come into close contact, halide ions may migrate and exchange, causing intended colours to blend into an unwanted intermediate colour.
  • The reported strategy combines lead-selenide treatment to reduce halide vacancies with an ultrathin alumina coating deposited by atomic layer deposition to impede ion diffusion.
