UN Nuclear Watchdog Urges Iran to Allow Nuclear Inspections

The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) has called on Iran to permit inspectors to access key nuclear facilities, a demand intensified by recent conflicts and lack of cooperation. This issue is crucial for UPSC aspirants studying international security and nuclear non-proliferation, as it impacts global diplomacy and regional stability.

The IAEA urges Iran to take incremental steps to restore nuclear inspections amid concerns over nuclear material diversion and geopolitical tensions.

Why is this in the news?

On November 13, 2026, the IAEA released a report urging Iran to allow access to nuclear facilities after conflicts and strikes disrupted inspections.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current status of nuclear inspections in Iran?

  • Restricted access. Since June 2025, following a 12-day conflict involving Israel and the U.S. that included strikes on nuclear sites, the IAEA has been denied access to key nuclear facilities in Iran.
  • Recent conflict impact. The latest war starting February 28, 2026, also damaged nuclear facilities, further complicating inspection efforts.
  • Limited inspections. IAEA inspectors visited Iran's Bushehr nuclear plant in September 2026 but have not accessed other critical sites.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the IAEA want Iran to cooperate on inspections?

  • Verification critical. The IAEA considers access to nuclear sites critical to verify that nuclear material is not diverted from peaceful activities.
  • Proliferation concerns. Due to lack of cooperation, the agency cannot confirm the absence of nuclear material diversion, raising serious proliferation concerns.
  • Stockpile uncertainty. Before June 2025, Iran had about 440 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60%, close to weapons-grade levels, but the current status of this stockpile is unknown.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What diplomatic and security actions have followed Iran's non-cooperation?

  • UN Security Council referral. In September 2026, the IAEA Board referred Iran to the UN Security Council for failing to comply with nuclear reporting obligations, a first in 20 years.
  • US reward for information. The US announced a $20 million reward for information on Iran's nuclear program, which Iran condemned and urged the IAEA and UN to denounce.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What steps has the IAEA proposed for Iran?

  • Incremental cooperation. The IAEA director general encourages Iran to consider technical and gradual measures to enable the return of inspectors.
  • Confidentiality assurance. The IAEA assured Iran it would protect all confidential information related to its nuclear program.

Back to basics: Nuclear Non-Proliferation and IAEA Inspections

The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) monitors nuclear programs to ensure peaceful use of nuclear energy and prevent weapon development. It conducts inspections of nuclear facilities to verify compliance with agreements. Non-cooperation by a country raises concerns about possible diversion of nuclear material for weapons. The UN Security Council can take action if a country violates its obligations.

  1. 1 only

Answer and trap

Answer: (c). Statement 2 is correct but some may confuse the year or think the referral happened earlier; statement 1 is correct as per the conflict timeline.
