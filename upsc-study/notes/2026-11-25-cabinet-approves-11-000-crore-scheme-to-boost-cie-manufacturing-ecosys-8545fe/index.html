Cabinet approves ₹11,000-crore scheme to boost CIE manufacturing ecosystem

The Union Cabinet approved an ₹11,000-crore scheme to enhance the domestic manufacturing ecosystem for Construction and Infrastructure Equipment (CIE) over eight years starting FY2026-27. This scheme aims to reduce import dependence and position India as a global player in advanced CIE manufacturing, a key topic for UPSC economy and industrial policy syllabus.

The ₹11,000-crore CIE scheme incentivizes domestic manufacturing to reduce 45% import dependence and expand India's global market share.

Why is this in the news?

On November 25, 2026, the Union Cabinet approved a major ₹11,000-crore scheme to boost the domestic CIE manufacturing ecosystem, aiming to build indigenous capacity and reduce import reliance.

What is the new CIE manufacturing scheme about?

  • Scheme overview. The ₹11,000-crore scheme approved by the Cabinet will run for eight years from FY2026-27 to FY2033-34 to develop indigenous manufacturing capacity in Construction and Infrastructure Equipment (CIE).
  • Implementation and timeline. It includes a two-year gestation period (FY2026-27 to FY2027-28) for setting up manufacturing facilities, a five-year incentive disbursement period (FY2028-29 to FY2032-33), and a final year to settle incentives for incremental sales.
  • Scheme components. The scheme has two components: sales-linked incentives for eligible OEMs and component manufacturers, and a grant of up to ₹375 crore for establishing a dedicated CIE testing facility.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the CIE scheme matter for India’s economy and manufacturing?

  • Market size and import dependence. India's CIE industry is the world’s third-largest with a market size of ₹97,000 crore in FY2024-25, but about 45% of it depends on imports.
  • Growth potential. Driven by infrastructure and housing growth, the CIE market is projected to more than double to ₹2,28,100 crore by FY2029-30.
  • Strategic importance. The scheme aims to enhance self-reliance by reducing import dependence on key equipment like tunnel boring machines and cranes, especially after recent export controls by China on such machinery.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How will the scheme incentivize domestic manufacturing?

  • Sales-linked incentives. Incentives ranging from 8% to 13% of incremental sales value will be provided over five years to approved OEMs and component manufacturers to boost production.
  • Testing facility grant. A grant of up to ₹375 crore, similar to Viability Gap Funding, will support setting up a state-of-the-art dedicated CIE testing facility in India.

Back to basics: Industrial policy and manufacturing incentives

Industrial policy involves government measures to promote growth and competitiveness of manufacturing sectors. Incentive schemes like sales-linked incentives encourage companies to increase domestic production by providing financial rewards based on incremental sales. Testing facilities support quality assurance and innovation in manufacturing.

Consider the following statements about the ₹11,000-crore CIE manufacturing scheme: 1. The scheme includes a two-year period to set up manufacturing facilities followed by a five-year incentive disbursement period. 2. The scheme provides incentives only to Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs), excluding component manufacturers. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the scheme provides incentives to both OEMs and component manufacturers.
