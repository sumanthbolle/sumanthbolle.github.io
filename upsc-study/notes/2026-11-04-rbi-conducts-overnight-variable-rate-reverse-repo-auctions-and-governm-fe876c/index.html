RBI Conducts Overnight Variable Rate Reverse Repo and Government Securities Auctions

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) conducted an Overnight Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auction on November 3, 2026, to manage liquidity conditions. Understanding these operations is important for UPSC aspirants as they illustrate key monetary policy tools affecting interest rates and liquidity in the economy.

RBI uses VRRR auctions under the Liquidity Adjustment Facility to absorb excess liquidity and influence short-term interest rates.

Why is this in the news?

What is the Overnight Variable Rate Reverse Repo auction?

  • Definition. The Overnight Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auction is a monetary policy tool used by RBI under the Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) to absorb excess liquidity from the banking system by borrowing funds overnight at variable rates.
  • Purpose. It helps RBI manage short-term liquidity conditions and influence overnight interest rates, ensuring they remain within the policy corridor.
  • Recent auction details. On November 3, 2026, RBI conducted a VRRR auction with a notified amount of ₹50,000 crore for a one-day tenor, with the auction window from 10:45 AM to 11:15 AM and settlement on November 4, 2026.

How do RBI's money market operations reflect liquidity management?

  • Liquidity absorption. RBI's operations on November 2 and 3, 2026, show net liquidity absorption of ₹1,87,840 crore and ₹1,92,952 crore respectively, indicating RBI is actively managing surplus liquidity in the banking system.
  • Interest rates in money markets. Weighted average rates in overnight segments ranged between 4.80% and 5.95% on November 2 and between 0.01% and 5.90% on November 3, reflecting the influence of RBI’s liquidity operations.
  • Use of multiple instruments. RBI uses various instruments like repo, reverse repo, Marginal Standing Facility (MSF), and Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) to fine-tune liquidity and maintain monetary stability.

Back to basics: Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF)

The Liquidity Adjustment Facility is a monetary policy tool used by RBI to manage liquidity in the banking system. It involves repo operations, where RBI lends money to banks, and reverse repo operations, where RBI borrows money from banks. The variable rate reverse repo auction allows RBI to absorb excess liquidity by borrowing funds overnight at variable interest rates, helping to regulate short-term interest rates and maintain monetary stability.
