Madras High Court Appoints Board to Manage Chidambaram Sabanayagar Temple

The Madras High Court ruled that the Podhu Dikshidars maladministered the Chidambaram Sabanayagar Temple and appointed a Board of Management led by retired judges to restore proper administration. This judicial intervention highlights the role of courts in overseeing religious institution governance, relevant for UPSC's polity and society syllabus.

The Madras High Court intervened to address maladministration in the Chidambaram Sabanayagar Temple by appointing a Board of Management headed by retired judges.

Why is this in the news?

On October 30, 2026, the Madras High Court ruled that the Podhu Dikshidars had maladministered the Chidambaram Sabanayagar Temple and appointed a Board of Management to restore its administration.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Madras High Court rule regarding the temple administration?

  • Maladministration found. The Court held that the Podhu Dikshidars, a hereditary priest community, had mismanaged the temple since 2014, causing damage to the ancient structure and failing to maintain proper accounts.
  • Judicial intervention. The Court appointed a Board of Management chaired by retired High Court judges V. Bharathidasan and R. Suresh Kumar to oversee temple administration and restoration.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the responsibilities and powers of the Board of Management?

  • Restoration and maintenance. The Board must remove unauthorized constructions, renovate the temple without altering heritage structures, and conduct consecration ceremonies as per religious norms.
  • Property and finance management. It is tasked with inventorying temple properties, securing valuables, recovering dues, maintaining proper accounts, and augmenting income from temple assets.
  • Ensuring fair access. The Board must ensure devotees have equal opportunity for darshan and hymn recitation without violating customary practices.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Judiciary's role in governance. This case exemplifies judicial oversight in religious institution management, illustrating separation of powers and accountability mechanisms.
  • Temple administration as a public issue. It highlights challenges in managing heritage religious sites and the state's role in protecting cultural assets.

Back to basics: Judicial Intervention in Religious Institution Management

Religious institutions in India are often managed by hereditary or community groups, but courts can intervene when maladministration harms heritage or public interest. Judicial oversight ensures accountability, proper maintenance, and protection of cultural assets while respecting religious customs.
