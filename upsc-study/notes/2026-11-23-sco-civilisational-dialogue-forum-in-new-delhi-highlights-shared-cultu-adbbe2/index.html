SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum in New Delhi Highlights Shared Cultural Heritage

The First SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum was held in New Delhi from November 20 to 22, 2026, bringing together delegates from 22 countries to discuss shared cultural heritage, art, architecture, and historical trade relations. This event is important for UPSC aspirants as it showcases India's role in regional cultural diplomacy and the SCO's evolving focus beyond security to include civilisational cooperation.

The SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum in New Delhi reaffirmed shared cultural heritage among member states, promoting dialogue and cooperation through historical and artistic exchanges.

Why is this in the news?

India hosted the First SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum in New Delhi from November 20 to 22, 2026, with participation from 116 delegates representing 22 countries, including ministers and the SCO Secretary General.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What was the purpose and structure of the SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum?

  • Forum objective. The Forum aimed to strengthen cultural ties among SCO member states by promoting mutual understanding and cooperation through shared civilisational heritage and creating opportunities for cultural dialogue.
  • Participants and sessions. It brought together ministers, senior representatives, academicians, historians, and cultural experts from SCO member states and dialogue partners, featuring sessions on civilisational expressions, art and architecture, historical trade relations, language, literature, and religious traditions.
  • Knowledge partner and organisers. The event was jointly organised by India's Ministry of Culture and Ministry of External Affairs, with the Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts (IGNCA) as the Knowledge Partner.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

How does the Forum reflect India's role in SCO and regional cultural diplomacy?

  • India's leadership. India hosted and inaugurated the Forum, with Prime Minister Narendra Modi emphasizing civilisational dialogue as a means to foster peace, mutual understanding, and trust among nations.
  • Showcasing shared heritage. The Forum highlighted India's historical cultural connections with Central Asia, China, Southeast Asia, and the wider Eurasian region through trade, scholarship, spiritual journeys, and the movement of ideas.
  • Promoting contemporary cooperation. India proposed initiatives such as digitisation of manuscripts, collaborative research, joint exhibitions, academic exchanges, cultural tourism, and youth engagement to build on the shared legacy.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What were the key themes and outcomes of the Forum?

  • Themes discussed. Sessions covered civilisational and cultural expressions, shared artistic and architectural traditions, historical trade networks, language and manuscript preservation, and religious and philosophical traditions.
  • Emphasis on diversity and dialogue. Delegates, including Pakistan’s Federal Minister for Climate Change, highlighted the importance of recognising distinct identities and fostering dialogue amid diversity.
  • Moving beyond security focus. The SCO’s National Coordinator noted the need for the organisation to expand its agenda beyond security to include cultural networks, climate, youth, digitalisation, and joint projects.
  • Indraprastha Communiqué. The Forum concluded with the Indraprastha Communiqué, capturing key themes and convergence areas to guide future cultural cooperation among SCO countries.

Back to basics: Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO)

The SCO is a regional intergovernmental organisation focused on political, economic, and security cooperation among member states primarily in Eurasia. Originally security-centric, it is expanding its agenda to include cultural cooperation and civilisational dialogue to strengthen mutual understanding and regional integration.
