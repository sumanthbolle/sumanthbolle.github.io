Kerala High Court Quashes Suspension of Sanskrit University Students

The Kerala High Court overturned the suspension of 16 students from Sree Sankaracharya University of Sanskrit and nullified the inquiry report against them. This case highlights judicial oversight in educational institutions and the protection of student rights, relevant for understanding judiciary's role in governance.

The Kerala High Court quashed the suspension and inquiry report against 16 Sanskrit University students, directing the university to allow their exams and attendance.

Why is this in the news?

On November 23, 2026, the Kerala High Court quashed the suspension of 16 students of Sree Sankaracharya University of Sanskrit and set aside the inquiry report against them.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Kerala High Court decide regarding the suspended students?

  • Suspension quashed. The High Court quashed the suspension order against 16 students affiliated with the Student Federation of India at the Sanskrit University.
  • Inquiry report set aside. The Court set aside the Enquiry Committee report that had found the students guilty of misconduct.
  • Examinations ordered. The Court directed the University to conduct a separate semester examination for these students within six weeks.
  • Campus access restored. The students were permitted to enter the campus and attend regular classes.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for students’ rights and university governance?

  • Protection during inquiry. The Court clarified that students cannot be suspended pending a fresh inquiry if initiated.
  • Suspension period counted. The 84 days of suspension already served must be counted towards any punishment if the students are found guilty after a fresh inquiry.
  • Exams not to be withheld. A fresh inquiry cannot be used as a reason to withhold examinations from the students.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered the legal dispute and what were the allegations?

  • Allegations against students. The students were accused of staging a mock cremation of the vice-chancellor, manhandling her, and attempting to damage university property.
  • Students’ denial. The students fully denied the allegations made against them.
  • Supreme Court involvement. The dispute escalated to the Supreme Court, which referred the matter back to the Kerala High Court with instructions to hold a separate exam if the High Court ruled in favor of the students.

Back to basics: Judicial Review and Student Rights

Judicial review allows courts to examine the legality of decisions made by educational institutions, ensuring they comply with principles of natural justice and fairness. Students have rights to education and fair treatment, including protection from arbitrary suspension and the right to appear for examinations during inquiries. Courts can intervene to uphold these rights and ensure accountability in institutional governance.
