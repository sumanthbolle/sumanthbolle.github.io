Pakistan Revises Down GDP Growth Estimate for Fiscal Year 2026 to 3.65%

Pakistan's National Accounts Committee lowered the GDP growth forecast for FY26 to 3.65%, citing slowdowns in industry and services sectors. This revision reflects a sluggish economic recovery and has implications for regional economic stability and policy analysis.

Pakistan's GDP growth for FY26 was revised down to 3.65% due to slower industrial and service sector growth, indicating a modest economic recovery.

Why is this in the news?

On October 19, 2026, Pakistan's National Accounts Committee revised the GDP growth estimate for FY26 downward to 3.65%, reflecting slower growth in key sectors.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What caused Pakistan to revise its GDP growth estimate for FY26?

  • Slowdown in key sectors. The revision to 3.65% GDP growth was due to a mild slowdown in the industrial and services sectors compared to earlier projections.
  • Sector-wise growth changes. Agriculture growth was revised to 3.50%, industry to 3.34%, and services to 3.81%, all showing slight decreases from previous estimates.
  • Impact on per capita income. Per capita income increased slightly to $1,896 in FY26 but remains the lowest in the region, indicating limited improvement in living standards.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does this revision compare with other economic forecasts?

  • Alignment with multilateral lenders. Pakistan's revised GDP growth estimate aligns broadly with projections by the IMF (3.6%), World Bank (3.7%), Asian Development Bank (3.7%), and the State Bank of Pakistan (around 3.7%).
  • Quarterly growth trends. Quarterly GDP growth for FY26 showed a decline in the fourth quarter to 2%, after stronger growth in earlier quarters.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Regional economic context. Understanding Pakistan's economic performance helps analyze South Asia's economic dynamics and India's neighborhood policy.
  • Economic challenges. The sluggish growth and low per capita income highlight challenges like inflationary pressures and limited income growth, relevant for economic and social studies.

Back to basics: Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and Economic Growth

GDP measures the total value of goods and services produced within a country in a specific period, reflecting economic activity. Economic growth is the increase in GDP over time, indicating improvements in production and income levels. Sectoral growth rates show contributions from agriculture, industry, and services, which together determine overall GDP growth.
