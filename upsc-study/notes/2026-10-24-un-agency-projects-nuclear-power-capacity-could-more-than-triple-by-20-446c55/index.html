UN Agency Projects Nuclear Power Capacity Could More Than Triple by 2060

The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) projects that global nuclear power capacity could more than triple by 2060, driven by electrification and demand for reliable, carbon-free energy. This projection is important for UPSC aspirants as it relates to energy security, climate change mitigation, and international cooperation on nuclear energy.

The IAEA forecasts a significant increase in nuclear power capacity by 2060, highlighting its role in global energy security and climate goals.

Why is this in the news?

The UN's International Atomic Energy Agency released its annual report on October 23, 2026, projecting that nuclear power capacity could more than triple by 2060.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the IAEA's projections for nuclear power growth?

  • Capacity increase forecast. The IAEA projects that nuclear power capacity could more than triple by 2060, driven by electrification and the demand for reliable, carbon-free power from technology companies.
  • Current nuclear power status. In 2025, over 400 operating reactors in more than 30 countries produced 10% of global electricity and a quarter of its low-carbon electricity.
  • New reactors under construction. More than 60 new nuclear reactors are currently under construction worldwide, indicating a significant upward shift in future nuclear capacity projections.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does nuclear power matter for energy security and climate change?

  • Reliable and carbon-free energy. Nuclear energy is considered on par with solar energy and safer than other options, providing reliable, carbon-free power essential for electrification and data centers powered by artificial intelligence.
  • Energy security. A growing number of countries view nuclear energy as key to improving their energy security, reducing dependence on fossil fuels.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the geopolitical and safety concerns related to nuclear energy?

  • Nuclear non-proliferation challenges. The IAEA highlighted concerns about nuclear programmes in Iran and North Korea, with North Korea's programme lacking IAEA oversight and violating UN Security Council resolutions.
  • Safety risks in conflict zones. The Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant in Ukraine remains in a precarious position due to military activity, power losses, and limited backup supplies, posing risks to safety.
  • Progress in Syria. The IAEA has made significant progress in clarifying Syria's nuclear activities after nearly two decades, following engagement with the interim government.

Back to basics: Nuclear Power and Its Role in Energy

Nuclear power is generated by splitting atoms in a reactor to produce heat, which is then used to generate electricity. It is a low-carbon energy source that provides a stable and reliable supply of electricity, unlike some renewable sources that depend on weather conditions. Nuclear energy plays a role in reducing greenhouse gas emissions and enhancing energy security by diversifying energy sources.
