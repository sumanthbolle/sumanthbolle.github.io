Sri Lanka’s Human Rights Commission Flags Militarisation in Tamil Areas

The Human Rights Commission of Sri Lanka reported persistent militarisation in Tamil-majority northern and eastern provinces 17 years after the civil war ended. This matters for UPSC aspirants studying India’s neighbourhood relations and human rights issues in South Asia.

Militarisation remains entrenched in Sri Lanka’s Tamil-majority regions, affecting governance, land rights, and reconciliation efforts.

Why is this in the news?

The Human Rights Commission of Sri Lanka released a report in October 2026 highlighting ongoing military presence and land appropriation in Tamil-majority areas despite government assurances.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current situation of militarisation in Sri Lanka’s Tamil-majority areas?

  • Persistent military presence. Seventeen years after the civil war, the military continues to control land and influence governance in the northern and eastern provinces, especially in Tamil-majority districts.
  • Land appropriation and livelihoods. The military has appropriated lands, denied access to traditional livelihoods, and intimidated local populations, impeding economic revival and reconciliation.
  • Military involvement in civilian sectors. Military personnel are involved in economic activities such as agriculture and pre-school teaching, administered through the Civil Security Department.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the government’s actions and challenges regarding militarisation?

  • Government assurances and directives. The government, including the Defence Minister and President, has repeatedly assured and directed the military to return occupied lands and reduce its footprint.
  • Slow pace of land return. Despite claims that over 90% of military-held lands have been returned, local Tamil representatives argue that many lands remain under military or other state agency control, with ownership documentation being a challenge.
  • Lack of comprehensive reform. Sri Lanka currently lacks a national security sector reform plan to reduce military presence and restore civilian administration in the affected regions.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this militarisation matter for India and human rights?

  • Impact on post-war reconciliation. The military’s continued presence and land control hinder reconciliation between communities and economic progress in Tamil-majority areas.
  • Human rights concerns. The Human Rights Commission highlights intimidation, denial of property rights, and the use of national security as a justification for land acquisition affecting minority communities.
  • Regional security and India’s neighbourhood policy. Militarisation and human rights issues in Sri Lanka’s Tamil regions affect India’s relations with its neighbour and its approach to regional stability and minority rights.

Back to basics: Militarisation and Human Rights

Militarisation refers to the presence and influence of military forces in civilian areas, often involving control over land and economic activities. It can affect governance, restrict civil liberties, and impede post-conflict reconciliation. Human rights bodies monitor such situations to ensure protection of property rights, freedom from intimidation, and restoration of civilian control.
