Supreme Court Refers RTI Amendment Privacy Issue to Constitution Bench

The Supreme Court of India has referred petitions challenging the amendment to Section 8(1)(j) of the RTI Act by the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, to a Constitution Bench due to its constitutional sensitivity. This development is crucial for UPSC aspirants as it highlights the ongoing tension between transparency and privacy in governance, a key theme in Indian polity and governance.

The Supreme Court is examining how the RTI Act amendment affects the balance between citizens' right to information and personal data privacy.

Why is this in the news?

On October 13, 2026, the Supreme Court referred petitions challenging the amendment to Section 8(1)(j) of the RTI Act by the DPDP Act, 2023, to a Constitution Bench, recognizing its constitutional sensitivity.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What changes did the RTI Act amendment introduce?

  • Amendment details. The DPDP Act, 2023, amended Section 8(1)(j) of the RTI Act to remove the 'public interest override' that allowed disclosure of personal information if justified by larger public interest.
  • Effect of amendment. The amendment prohibits disclosure of any information related to personal information, creating a blanket ban that can block access to officials' details, procurement records, audit reports, and public spending information.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this amendment matter for governance and transparency?

  • Impact on state accountability. The RTI Act was designed to reduce information asymmetry between the state and citizens, ensuring government accountability; the amendment dilutes this by restricting access to information.
  • Citizen vs state data rights. While the DPDP Act allows the state to process personal data without consent, the RTI amendment denies citizens the ability to scrutinize the state using similar principles, creating an imbalance.
  • Chilling effect on journalism. Journalists may be classified as 'data fiduciaries' under the DPDP Act, facing heavy fines for non-compliance, which risks reducing investigative journalism to mere government press releases.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What constitutional issues does the Supreme Court need to address?

  • Definition of personal information. The Supreme Court may have to define what constitutes 'personal information' to balance privacy rights with transparency.
  • Public interest override principle. The Court must consider the precedent that personal information should remain private unless disclosure is necessary for the larger public interest, as held in the 2019 Central Public Information Officer judgment.

Back to basics: Right to Information Act and Transparency

The Right to Information Act, 2005, empowers citizens to access information from public authorities to promote transparency and accountability in governance. It includes provisions that allow withholding information only in specific cases, such as protecting personal privacy, but permits disclosure if the larger public interest justifies it. Transparency reduces information asymmetry between the government and citizens, essential for a functioning democracy.
