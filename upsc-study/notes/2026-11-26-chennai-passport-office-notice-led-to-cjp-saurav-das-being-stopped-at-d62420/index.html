Chennai Passport Office Notice Stops CJP Saurav Das at Delhi Airport

Saurav Das, co-convenor of the Cockroach Janata Party, was stopped from boarding a flight at Delhi airport due to a show-cause notice from the Chennai Regional Passport Office alleging discrepancies in his passport documents. This incident raises questions on passport verification procedures and administrative actions affecting judicial figures, relevant for understanding separation of powers and administrative justice.

The Chennai Passport Office's notice alleging document discrepancies led to Chief Justice of Punjab & Haryana High Court Saurav Das being stopped at Delhi airport, highlighting administrative and judicial procedural issues.

Why is this in the news?

On November 23, 2026, Saurav Das was stopped at Delhi airport after receiving a show-cause notice from the Chennai Regional Passport Office alleging discrepancies in his passport application and documents.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What happened with Saurav Das at the Delhi airport?

  • Notice timing. Saurav Das received an email notice at 7:30 p.m. on November 23, 2026, just hours before his scheduled 1 a.m. flight to London, about alleged discrepancies in his passport.
  • Reason for stopping. The Chennai Regional Passport Office alleged that particulars such as place of birth in his passport application were incorrect and that the passport was obtained by material suppression of facts.
  • Outcome. Das was stopped from boarding the flight at the immigration counter due to failure to respond satisfactorily to the notice.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for administrative justice and legal rights?

  • Due process concerns. Das argued that the notice was received only hours before travel and that no details of the alleged discrepancies were disclosed before stopping him, raising questions on procedural fairness.
  • Judicial implications. Das is the Chief Justice of Punjab & Haryana High Court, and the incident followed a Madras High Court order on a PIL seeking investigation into his nationality and passport validity.
  • Freedom of movement. Stopping a judicial figure from international travel on such grounds touches upon rights related to freedom of movement and administrative accountability.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What background legal actions led to this notice?

  • Madras High Court PIL. A Public Interest Litigation petition filed in Madras High Court sought a probe by the Ministry of Home Affairs into Das's nationality and requested cancellation of his passport and Aadhaar if found not Indian.
  • Court order. The Madras High Court ordered notice to Das and directed investigation into alleged discrepancies in his documents.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the political and social implications?

  • Political context. Das is associated with the Cockroach Janata Party, a youth movement critical of the government, which he alleges is using administrative tactics to suppress dissent.
  • Allegations of censorship. Das claimed government attempts to stop him from speaking at prestigious universities abroad and alleged mass censorship on social media platforms.

Back to basics: Passport issuance and administrative justice

A passport is an official document issued by the government certifying a person's identity and citizenship, allowing international travel. The Regional Passport Office verifies documents before issuing passports. Administrative justice requires that any action such as impounding a passport must follow due process, including clear communication of allegations and reasonable time for response. The judiciary plays a role in reviewing such administrative actions to ensure fairness and legality.

Common mistake. The notice and stopping of travel were not routine passport verification issues but linked to a court-ordered probe and political context involving the individual.
