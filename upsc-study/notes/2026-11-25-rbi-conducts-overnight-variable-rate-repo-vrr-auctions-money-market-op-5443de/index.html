RBI's Overnight Variable Rate Repo Auction and Money Market Operations Update

The Reserve Bank of India conducted an Overnight Variable Rate Repo (VRR) auction on November 25, 2026, allotting ₹50,007 crore at a cut-off rate of 5.66%. This update is essential for understanding RBI's liquidity management and monetary policy tools.

RBI uses VRR auctions to manage short-term liquidity by borrowing funds from banks at variable rates, influencing money market conditions.

What is the Overnight Variable Rate Repo (VRR) auction?

  • Definition and purpose. The VRR auction is a tool used by RBI to borrow funds from banks for a short tenor of one day at variable interest rates, helping manage liquidity in the banking system.
  • Recent auction details. On November 25, 2026, RBI notified an amount of ₹50,000 crore for the VRR auction, received bids worth ₹1,62,017 crore, and allotted ₹50,007 crore at a cut-off rate of 5.66%.
  • Partial allotment. Only about 50.55% of the bids received at the cut-off rate were allotted, indicating RBI's selective absorption of liquidity.

[3] Reserve Bank of India

How does RBI manage liquidity through money market operations?

  • Money market segments. RBI monitors various segments like call money, triparty repo, market repo, and repo in corporate bonds to gauge liquidity and interest rates.
  • Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF). RBI conducts repo and reverse repo operations under LAF to inject or absorb liquidity; for example, on November 23, 2026, RBI conducted fixed and variable rate repo operations totaling over ₹1 lakh crore.
  • Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) and Marginal Standing Facility (MSF). SDF and MSF are additional tools used by RBI to absorb or provide liquidity; on November 23 and 24, 2026, RBI absorbed liquidity through these facilities at rates of 5.25% and 5.75% respectively.
  • Net liquidity position. On November 23, 2026, RBI's net liquidity injection including outstanding operations was ₹6,401.26 crore, while on November 24, 2026, it was ₹12,825.26 crore, showing active liquidity management.

Back to basics: Monetary Policy and Liquidity Management

Monetary policy involves controlling money supply and interest rates to maintain price stability and support economic growth. Liquidity management is the process by which the central bank ensures adequate funds in the banking system, using tools like repo, reverse repo, and variable rate repo auctions to inject or absorb money as needed.
