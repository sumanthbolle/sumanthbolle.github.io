RBI Amends Foreign Exchange Management Regulations for Export-Import

The Reserve Bank of India issued the Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) (Second Amendment) Regulations, 2026, effective retrospectively from October 1, 2026. These amendments aim to promote ease of doing business and empower Authorised Dealers to provide faster services to exporters and importers.

The RBI amended the 2026 Foreign Exchange Management Regulations to simplify export-import procedures and enhance service efficiency.

Why is this in the news?

On November 20, 2026, the RBI issued amendments to the Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026, following stakeholder feedback.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What changes did the RBI make to the Foreign Exchange Management Regulations?

  • Superseding earlier regulations. The 2015 Foreign Exchange Management (Export of Goods and Services) Regulations were replaced by the 2026 Regulations issued on January 13, 2026, effective from October 1, 2026.
  • Amendments issued. Based on feedback from exporters and importers, the RBI issued Second Amendment Regulations on November 2, 2026, which apply retrospectively from October 1, 2026.
  • Focus on ease of business. The new regulations are principle-based and designed to promote ease of doing business, especially for small exporters and importers.
  • Empowering Authorised Dealers. The amendments empower Authorised Dealers to provide quicker and more efficient services to their customers.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Why do these amendments matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Impact on external trade. Changes in FEMA regulations directly affect India's external trade policies and foreign exchange management.
  • Relevance to economic governance. Understanding these amendments helps in analysing the regulatory framework governing India's export-import sector.
  • Link to ease of doing business. The amendments reflect the government's efforts to simplify procedures and support small traders, a key economic policy focus.

Back to basics: Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) and Authorised Dealers

FEMA regulates foreign exchange transactions in India to facilitate external trade and payments. Authorised Dealers are banks or entities authorised by the RBI to deal in foreign exchange and provide services to exporters and importers, ensuring compliance with FEMA rules.
