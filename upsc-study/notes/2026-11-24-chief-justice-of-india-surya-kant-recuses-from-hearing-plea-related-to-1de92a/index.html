CJI Surya Kant Recuses from Hearing Plea on Supertech Projects

Chief Justice of India Surya Kant recused himself from a Supreme Court hearing on November 23, 2026, concerning stalled housing projects of Supertech Limited due to a personal acquaintance with one applicant. This development highlights judicial ethics and the ongoing legal process to resolve homebuyers' disputes in major urban real estate cases.

CJI Surya Kant recused from the Supertech plea hearing due to a personal connection with an applicant, underscoring judicial propriety in a high-profile real estate dispute.

Why is this in the news?

On November 23, 2026, CJI Surya Kant recused himself from hearing a plea related to Supertech insolvency and homebuyers' disputes, citing a personal acquaintance with one of the applicants.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why did CJI Surya Kant recuse himself from the Supertech plea?

  • Reason for recusal. CJI Surya Kant recused himself because one of the applicants in the case was personally known to him, though not a client or relative, to avoid any perception of bias.
  • Action after recusal. He stated he would constitute a special bench to hear the matter instead.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the background of the Supertech projects case?

  • Case context. The case involves stalled housing projects of Supertech Limited, a debt-ridden realty firm, affecting around 27,000 homebuyers awaiting delivery.
  • Role of NBCC. The National Buildings Construction Corporation (NBCC), a public sector undertaking, was directed by the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) and upheld by the Supreme Court to complete 16 stalled projects.
  • Court orders. The Supreme Court exercised its power under Article 142 to uphold NCLAT's December 12, 2024 order directing NBCC to take over and complete the projects expeditiously.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for judicial ethics and governance?

  • Judicial conduct. The recusal demonstrates adherence to judicial ethics by avoiding conflicts of interest or perceived bias in high-stakes cases.
  • Impact on homebuyers. Ensuring impartial adjudication is critical in cases affecting thousands of homebuyers and the completion of large urban housing projects.

Back to basics: Judicial Recusal and Ethics

Judicial recusal is when a judge abstains from hearing a case due to potential conflicts of interest or personal connections to parties involved, ensuring impartiality and fairness. This practice upholds public confidence in the judiciary by preventing bias or the appearance of bias in judicial proceedings.
