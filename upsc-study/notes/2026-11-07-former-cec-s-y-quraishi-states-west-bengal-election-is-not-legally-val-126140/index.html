Former CEC S.Y. Quraishi questions legal validity of West Bengal election

Former Chief Election Commissioner S.Y. Quraishi stated that the West Bengal elections were not legally valid, raising concerns about the Election Commission of India's decision-making process and the special intensive revision (SIR) process. This issue is important for UPSC aspirants as it touches upon constitutional provisions related to elections, the role of the Election Commission, and electoral governance.

Former CEC S.Y. Quraishi challenged the legal validity of the West Bengal elections citing procedural irregularities and decision-making concerns within the Election Commission of India.

Why is this in the news?

On November 6, 2026, former Chief Election Commissioner S.Y. Quraishi publicly stated that the West Bengal elections were not legally valid, highlighting issues with the Election Commission's decision-making and the voter revision process.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did S.Y. Quraishi say about the West Bengal election?

  • Election validity questioned. S.Y. Quraishi stated that the West Bengal elections were not legally valid, emphasizing that some parts might be valid but others, especially where majority Muslim voters were allegedly removed, require rechecking and possibly re-polls.
  • Decision-making in ECI. He clarified that decisions in the Election Commission of India should be based on majority among the three members, not solely by the Chief Election Commissioner.
  • Concerns over SIR process. Quraishi criticized the special intensive revision (SIR) process as neither smooth nor transparent, alleging it was used for backdoor citizenship checks.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for electoral governance and constitutional law?

  • Role of Election Commission. The Election Commission is a constitutional body tasked with conducting free and fair elections, and its decisions must follow collective majority to maintain legitimacy.
  • Voter rights and inclusion. Traditionally, voter cards were delivered to voters' homes to ensure easy access to voting rights, but the recent process required voters to actively negotiate inclusion, raising concerns about disenfranchisement.
  • Legal scrutiny pending. The Supreme Court is currently hearing questions raised about the election process, which will clarify what went wrong and what was correct.

What reactions followed Quraishi’s statements?

  • Government response. Minister Dilip Ghosh responded by saying the government has been in power for 6-7 months and people are happy, warning that those opposing the election outcome face a difficult path.

Back to basics: Election Commission of India and electoral process

The Election Commission of India is a constitutional authority responsible for administering elections to ensure they are free, fair, and transparent. It consists of a Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners who make decisions collectively, usually by majority. The voter list revision process updates the electoral rolls to include eligible voters and remove ineligible ones, which must be done transparently to protect voting rights.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the Election Commission of India (ECI) and the West Bengal elections: 1. The Chief Election Commissioner alone can make final decisions in the ECI. 2. Former CEC S.Y. Quraishi alleged that the West Bengal elections were not legally valid due to irregularities in the voter revision process. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (b). Statement 1 is incorrect because decisions in the ECI are made by majority among all members, not solely by the Chief Election Commissioner.
