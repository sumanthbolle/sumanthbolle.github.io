GST Collections Rise 23% to ₹2.31 Lakh Crore in October 2026

Gross GST collections in October 2026 surged 23% year-on-year to ₹2.31 lakh crore, driven by festive demand and strong domestic and import revenues. This data reflects the resilience of the Indian economy and improved tax compliance, important for understanding fiscal policy and indirect taxation.

GST collections in October 2026 grew 23% to ₹2.31 lakh crore, indicating strong economic activity and tax compliance.

Why is this in the news?

Government data released on November 1, 2026, showed GST collections for October 2026 rising sharply due to festive demand and economic activity.

What were the GST collection figures for October 2026?

  • Gross collection amount. Gross GST collections reached over ₹2.31 lakh crore in October 2026, marking the second-highest monthly collection ever.
  • Growth rate. This represents a 23% year-on-year increase, the fastest growth pace in the current fiscal year.
  • Domestic and import revenues. Domestic sales contributed over ₹1.59 lakh crore (16% growth), while import-related GST rose 42% to ₹72,246 crore.
  • Components of GST. The total includes Central GST of ₹42,935 crore, State GST of ₹51,340 crore, and Integrated GST of over ₹1.37 lakh crore.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why did GST collections increase significantly in October 2026?

  • Festive season impact. Festive demand boosted consumer spending and retail activity, driving higher GST collections.
  • Economic resilience. The rise reflects strong underlying consumption and improving compliance despite global economic challenges.
  • State-level performance. Key states like Maharashtra, Karnataka, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, and Madhya Pradesh showed continued strength in GST revenues.

What does this mean for fiscal policy and GST reforms?

  • Sustaining revenue growth. Focus is on maintaining domestic revenue growth and ensuring GST 2.0 benefits like smoother input tax credit and faster refunds.
  • GST Council reforms. The GST Council is advancing reforms aimed at simplification, reducing taxpayer friction, and enhancing compliance.
  • Economic formalisation. GST increasingly reflects economic formalisation and business activity beyond being just a revenue tool.

Back to basics: Goods and Services Tax (GST)

GST is an indirect tax levied on the supply of goods and services across India, replacing multiple indirect taxes to create a unified tax structure. It is collected at each stage of the supply chain, with input tax credits available to avoid cascading taxes. GST revenues are shared between the Centre and States, and the GST Council governs rates and rules to ensure uniformity and compliance.
