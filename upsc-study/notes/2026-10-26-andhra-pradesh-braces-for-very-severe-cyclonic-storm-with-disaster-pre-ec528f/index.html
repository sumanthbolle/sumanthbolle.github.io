Andhra Pradesh Prepares for Very Severe Cyclonic Storm Landfall

A very severe cyclonic storm is expected to make landfall on Andhra Pradesh's coast between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam on October 28, 2026. This note explains the storm's characteristics, the state's preparedness measures, and their relevance for disaster management studies.

Andhra Pradesh is bracing for a very severe cyclonic storm with wind speeds up to 130 kmph, prompting red alerts and evacuation plans for vulnerable districts.

Why is this in the news?

The India Meteorological Department issued a red alert on October 25, 2026, as a very severe cyclonic storm formed over the Bay of Bengal and was forecast to hit Andhra Pradesh between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam on October 28.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the nature and trajectory of the cyclonic storm?

  • Storm classification. The storm is classified as a very severe cyclonic storm, the second highest intensity category, with wind speeds between 118 kmph and 221 kmph.
  • Current wind speeds. Wind speeds are expected to reach up to 130 kmph near landfall, higher than last year's severe cyclonic storm Montha, which had 65 kmph winds at Machilipatnam.
  • Storm path. The storm formed over the east-central Bay of Bengal and is forecast to intensify and move northwest, making landfall on the Andhra Pradesh coast between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam on October 28.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What disaster preparedness measures are in place in Andhra Pradesh?

  • Red alerts issued. The IMD issued red alerts for 13 districts including Krishna, Bapatla, Visakhapatnam, and East and West Godavari for October 27 and 28.
  • Evacuation plans. People living in low-lying and vulnerable coastal areas are being evacuated to safer locations ahead of the storm's landfall.
  • Government directives. Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu directed officials to remain alert, implement flood management plans, ensure water conservation, and keep emergency response teams ready.
  • Public advisories. Fishermen have been advised not to venture into the sea due to strong winds and rough conditions, and toll-free numbers have been set up for emergency assistance.

  • Governance and administration. The state's response showcases coordination between meteorological agencies and government for public safety, useful for governance and federalism studies.

Back to basics: Cyclone classification and disaster preparedness

Cyclones are classified by wind speed into categories such as depression, cyclonic storm, severe cyclonic storm, and very severe cyclonic storm. Disaster preparedness involves issuing alerts, evacuating vulnerable populations, and mobilizing government resources to reduce damage and loss of life during such natural events.
