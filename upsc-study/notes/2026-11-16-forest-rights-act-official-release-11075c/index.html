Forest Rights Act 2006: Recognising Tribal and Forest Dweller Rights

The Forest Rights Act (FRA), 2006 legally recognises individual and community rights of Scheduled Tribes and other traditional forest dwellers on forest land. It aims to secure land tenure, livelihood, and food security while promoting sustainable forest conservation, making it crucial for tribal rights and environmental governance in India.

The FRA 2006 grants legal titles to forest-dwelling communities, enabling them to claim rights over forest land and resources with a focus on sustainable use and conservation.

Why is this in the news?

The Ministry of Tribal Affairs released updated data and launched the National FRA Digital Platform in November 2026 to streamline forest rights claims and title issuance.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the Forest Rights Act and its objectives?

  • Legal recognition. The FRA, 2006 recognises rights of Scheduled Tribes and other traditional forest dwellers on forest land through official titles recorded in revenue records.
  • Objectives. It aims to recognise unrecorded forest rights, provide secure land tenure and livelihood to forest communities, and strengthen forest conservation by involving rights holders in sustainable management.
  • Implementation. The Ministry of Tribal Affairs is the nodal agency, with implementation in 20 States and one Union Territory, Jammu and Kashmir.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Who can claim rights and what rights are protected?

  • Eligible groups. Forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes and other traditional forest dwellers who have lived in forests for at least three generations before 13 December 2005 can claim rights.
  • Types of rights. Rights include individual titles for land up to four hectares, community forest rights for shared resources like grazing and minor forest produce, and community forest resource rights for managing customary forest areas.
  • Protection from eviction. No eligible member can be evicted from forest land occupied before the cut-off date until rights recognition and verification are complete.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How are claims processed and titles issued?

  • Role of Gram Sabha. The Gram Sabha initiates claims, verifies them, maps land, and passes resolutions with full participation including women.
  • Committee structure. Claims pass through Sub-Divisional Level Committee and District Level Committee, which include government officers and elected representatives, with the DLC’s decision being final.
  • Title issuance and records. Titles are signed by three district officers and entered into revenue and forest records; they are inheritable but cannot be sold or transferred.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the progress and support for implementation?

  • Claims and titles distributed. As of 31 August 2026, about 54 lakh claims were received, 43.56 lakh disposed of, and 25.42 lakh titles distributed covering nearly 238 lakh acres.
  • State-wise distribution. Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Madhya Pradesh, Telangana, and Andhra Pradesh are the top five States in titles distributed.
  • Digital and institutional support. The National FRA Digital Platform was launched to digitise claims; FRA cells at State and district levels assist in claim settlement; States link FRA records with revenue and banking systems.

Back to basics: Forest Rights and Community Rights

Forest rights refer to the legal entitlements of forest-dwelling communities to land and resources they have traditionally used. Community rights allow villages to collectively manage and conserve forest resources within customary boundaries, ensuring sustainable use and biodiversity conservation.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the Forest Rights Act (FRA), 2006: 1. The Act recognises rights of Scheduled Tribes and other traditional forest dwellers on forest land through official titles. 2. The District Level Committee's decision on claims is advisory and can be overruled by the State Government. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the District Level Committee's decision is final and binding, not merely advisory.

Mains question (15 marks)

Discuss how the Forest Rights Act, 2006 balances tribal rights with forest conservation objectives in India.

Discuss: Explain the provisions of the FRA that secure forest-dwelling communities' rights while promoting sustainable forest management.

Intro: The Forest Rights Act, 2006 recognises and secures forest rights of tribal and traditional forest dwellers alongside conservation goals.

  • The Act grants individual and community titles to forest land and resources, legally recognising customary rights.
  • It prevents eviction of forest dwellers until rights are verified, ensuring livelihood and food security.
  • The Act empowers Gram Sabhas to manage community forest resources sustainably, linking rights with conservation responsibilities.
  • Implementation involves government and community participation through committees ensuring rights recognition and forest protection.

Conclusion: Thus, the FRA integrates social justice for forest communities with ecological sustainability.
