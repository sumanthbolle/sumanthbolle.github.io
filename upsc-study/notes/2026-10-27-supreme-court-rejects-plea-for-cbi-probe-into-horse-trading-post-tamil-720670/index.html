Supreme Court rejects plea for CBI probe into Tamil Nadu post-poll horse-trading

The Supreme Court dismissed a petition seeking a CBI investigation into alleged horse-trading by Tamil Nadu MLAs after the 2026 Assembly elections. This ruling highlights the judiciary's stance on electoral malpractice and the limits of court intervention in political matters.

The Supreme Court refused to order a CBI probe into allegations of horse-trading by Tamil Nadu MLAs following the 2026 Assembly polls.

Why is this in the news?

On October 26, 2026, the Supreme Court rejected a plea for a CBI probe into alleged horse-trading by Tamil Nadu MLAs after the Assembly elections.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court decide regarding the CBI probe?

  • Rejection of CBI probe. The Supreme Court dismissed the petition seeking a Central Bureau of Investigation probe into allegations of horse-trading involving Tamil Nadu Minister N. Anand and four former AIADMK MLAs.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Who were the accused in the horse-trading allegations?

  • Accused MLAs and Minister. The petition accused Tamil Nadu Minister N. Anand and four former AIADMK MLAs—K. Maragatham Kumaravel, S. Jayakumar, P. Sathyabama, and E. Subaya—of shifting political allegiance to secure a majority.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What were the petitioners' demands besides the CBI probe?

  • Additional reliefs sought. The petition sought court directions to frame rules preventing disqualified or resigned MLAs from contesting bypolls without valid reasons and to recover election expenses from such MLAs.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for electoral integrity and governance?

  • Impact on democratic processes. The case highlights challenges in addressing alleged political horse-trading and the judiciary's cautious approach to intervening in electoral disputes and political realignments.

Back to basics: Horse-trading in politics

Horse-trading refers to the unethical practice where elected representatives switch political allegiance, often for personal gain, to influence the formation or stability of a government. It undermines the democratic mandate and can lead to political instability. The judiciary and election authorities play roles in curbing such practices to maintain electoral integrity.
