India and New Zealand Sign MoC on Electronic Origin Data Exchange under FTA

India and New Zealand signed a Memorandum of Cooperation (MoC) on Electronic Origin Data Exchange to facilitate bilateral trade under their Free Trade Agreement (FTA) effective from October 20, 2026. This MoC aims to deepen customs cooperation and improve trade facilitation by enabling advance electronic exchange of origin data for goods traded between the two countries.

The MoC on electronic origin data exchange under the India-New Zealand FTA enhances customs cooperation and trade facilitation by enabling secure and efficient digital information sharing.

Why is this in the news?

The MoC was signed on October 19, 2026, by the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) Chairman and the New Zealand Customs Service Chief Executive, shortly after the India-New Zealand FTA came into force on October 20, 2026.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the MoC on Electronic Origin Data Exchange?

  • Purpose of MoC. The MoC facilitates the electronic exchange of origin data on trade in goods between India and New Zealand to strengthen customs cooperation and support trade facilitation.
  • Signatories and Date. Signed on October 19, 2026, by Shri Vivek Chaturvedi, Chairman of CBIC, and Ms. Christine Stevenson, Comptroller and Chief Executive of New Zealand Customs Service.
  • Relation to FTA. The MoC is part of the India-New Zealand Free Trade Agreement which came into effect on October 20, 2026.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How does the MoC impact bilateral trade?

  • Digitalisation and Efficiency. It leverages digitalisation and enhanced information exchange to make cross-border trade more efficient, secure, and predictable.
  • Trade Facilitation. Advance electronic exchange of origin data reduces information gaps and improves operational efficiency, benefiting traders engaged in bilateral trade.
  • Utilisation of Trade Preferences. Supports effective use of trade preferences under the FTA, ensuring benefits reach eligible businesses more efficiently.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the key features of the India-New Zealand Free Trade Agreement?

  • Duty-Free Access. India gains duty-free access to 100% of New Zealand’s tariff lines, a unique feature among India’s trade deals.
  • Sectoral Benefits. Tariffs on sectors like textiles, apparel, leather, engineering goods, plastics, rubber, electronics, pharmaceuticals, agriculture, marine products, and chemicals have been reduced or eliminated.
  • Investment Promotion. New Zealand commits to promoting foreign direct investment (FDI) into India with a target of increasing investment by USD 20 billion within 15 years.
  • Mobility Provisions. Includes new Temporary Employment Entry visa categories, expanded post-study work rights for Indian graduates, guaranteed work rights during study, and a working holiday scheme for Indian nationals.
  • New Zealand Market Access. India grants phased market access on 70% of tariff lines to New Zealand, excluding sensitive sectors like dairy and certain agricultural products.

Back to basics: Free Trade Agreement and Trade Facilitation

A Free Trade Agreement (FTA) is a pact between countries to reduce or eliminate tariffs and trade barriers to encourage trade. Trade facilitation involves simplifying and harmonizing international trade procedures, often through digitalisation, to make cross-border trade faster, cheaper, and more predictable. Electronic exchange of origin data helps verify the country of origin of goods, enabling preferential tariff treatment under FTAs.

Consider the following statements about the India-New Zealand Free Trade Agreement and the related MoC: 1. The MoC on Electronic Origin Data Exchange was signed after the India-New Zealand FTA came into force. 2. India has granted duty-free access to 100% of New Zealand’s tariff lines under the FTA.

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). Statement 1 is correct as the MoC was signed on October 19, 2026, and the FTA came into force on October 20, 2026. Statement 2 is correct because India gains duty-free access to 100% of New Zealand’s tariff lines, not the other way around.
