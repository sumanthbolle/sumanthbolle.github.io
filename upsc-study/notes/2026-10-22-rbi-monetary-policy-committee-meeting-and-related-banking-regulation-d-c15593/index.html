RBI MPC Raises Repo Rate, Issues Directions to Puducherry Co-op Bank

The Reserve Bank of India’s Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) increased the policy repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% in October 2026, shifting the stance to calibrated tightening amid rising inflation pressures. Concurrently, RBI imposed operational restrictions on Puducherry Co-operative Urban Bank due to liquidity concerns, highlighting regulatory oversight under the Banking Regulation Act.

The RBI raised the repo rate to manage inflation and issued directions restricting operations of Puducherry Co-operative Urban Bank to protect depositors.

Why is this in the news?

The RBI released the minutes of its Monetary Policy Committee meeting held on October 5-7, 2026, and issued directions to Puducherry Co-operative Urban Bank on October 21, 2026.

What decisions did the RBI Monetary Policy Committee take in October 2026?

  • Repo rate increase. The MPC unanimously voted to raise the policy repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50%, with the standing deposit facility rate at 5.25% and the marginal standing facility and bank rate at 5.75%.
  • Stance changed to calibrated tightening. The MPC shifted the monetary policy stance from neutral to calibrated tightening, signaling that rate cuts are unlikely in the near term and future actions will be rate hikes or pauses based on evolving conditions.
  • Growth and inflation outlook. The Indian economy showed resilience with projected real GDP growth of 7.1% for 2026-27, but inflation pressures widened, with CPI inflation projected at 5.2% and core inflation at 4.4%.
  • Dissenting views. Two external MPC members preferred retaining the neutral stance, citing geopolitical and trade uncertainties and concerns about growth-supportive policies.

What are the reasons behind the RBI’s monetary policy decisions?

  • Global and domestic inflation pressures. Rising crude oil prices, geopolitical tensions in West Asia, and supply-side shocks like deficient monsoon and El Niño conditions contributed to broadening inflation pressures.
  • Inflation expectations and price pressures. Core inflation and diffusion indices indicate some generalisation of inflation, with inflation expectations rising, necessitating monetary tightening to anchor expectations.
  • Economic resilience. Despite global headwinds, strong private consumption, investment, and export growth support sustained economic momentum.
  • Balancing growth and inflation. The MPC aims to manage inflation without derailing growth, with the extent and duration of rate hikes dependent on evolving economic data.

[2] Reserve Bank of India[3] The Hindu · Secondary

What directions did RBI issue to Puducherry Co-operative Urban Bank and why?

  • Operational restrictions imposed. RBI directed the bank not to grant or renew loans, make investments, incur liabilities, or disburse payments without prior RBI approval from October 21, 2026.
  • Withdrawal limits set. Depositors are allowed to withdraw up to ₹5,000 from their accounts, with loans allowed to be set off against deposits under specified conditions.
  • Reasons for directions. The bank’s poor liquidity position and lack of concrete efforts to address supervisory concerns led RBI to impose these measures to protect depositors.
  • Deposit insurance coverage. Depositors are entitled to insurance claims up to ₹5,00,000 under the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation Act, 1961.

Back to basics: Monetary Policy Committee and Monetary Policy

The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) is a statutory body of the Reserve Bank of India responsible for setting the policy interest rates to control inflation and support growth. The key policy rate is the repo rate, which influences borrowing costs in the economy. The MPC meets periodically to assess economic conditions and decide on rate changes and policy stance, balancing inflation control with economic growth.
