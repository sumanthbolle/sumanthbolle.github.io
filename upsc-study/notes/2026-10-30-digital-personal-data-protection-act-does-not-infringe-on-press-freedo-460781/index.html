Digital Personal Data Protection Act and Press Freedom: Government's Stand

IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw stated on October 28, 2026, that the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act does not infringe on press freedom, affirming constitutional guarantees for journalistic independence. This clarification is crucial amid concerns from news publishers about the law's impact on investigative journalism and data handling.

The DPDP Act protects personal data without undermining journalistic freedom, with the government open to amendments to explicitly safeguard media rights.

Why is this in the news?

On October 28, 2026, IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw addressed media concerns regarding the DPDP Act's impact on press freedom, inviting dialogue and assuring constitutional protection for journalism.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the government's position on the DPDP Act and press freedom?

  • Constitutional guarantee. The government asserts that journalistic freedom is guaranteed by the Constitution and the DPDP Act does not intend to affect this freedom.
  • Invitation for dialogue. The IT Minister invited editors and media professionals to engage in open dialogue with the government to discuss concerns about the law.
  • Open to amendments. The government is willing to amend the DPDP Act to explicitly safeguard journalistic activities if necessary.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why do news publishers have concerns about the DPDP Act?

  • Impact on investigative reporting. Publishers fear the absence of a specific exemption for journalism could hinder reporting on public interest matters involving personal data collected without consent.
  • Retention of information. Concerns exist about the ability to retain information necessary for investigative reporting under the new law.
  • Protection of sources. There is apprehension about safeguarding confidential sources under the DPDP Act.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How was the DPDP Act formulated and what does it aim to achieve?

  • Supreme Court influence. The Act was introduced following a Supreme Court judgment recognizing the right to privacy as a fundamental right.
  • Stakeholder consultation. The law was drafted after over a year of consultations with stakeholders including media and IT industry representatives.
  • Objective of the Act. The DPDP Act aims to protect personal data without undermining media freedom.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Back to basics: Right to Privacy and Press Freedom

The right to privacy is a fundamental right recognized by the Supreme Court, protecting individuals' personal information. Press freedom is also constitutionally guaranteed, allowing journalists to operate independently. Laws like the DPDP Act must balance protecting personal data with ensuring that journalistic activities are not hindered.

What happens next?

  • Potential legislative amendment. The government is open to amending the DPDP Act to explicitly protect journalistic freedom if required, ensuring clarity and addressing media concerns.
Common mistake. The DPDP Act does not intend to restrict journalistic freedom; concerns arise from the absence of explicit exemptions which the government is willing to address.
