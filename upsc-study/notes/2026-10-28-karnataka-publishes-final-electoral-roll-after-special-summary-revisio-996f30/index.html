Karnataka Publishes Final Electoral Roll After Special Summary Revision

Karnataka completed a Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of its electoral rolls, deleting over one crore names and finalizing a list of around 4.54 crore voters. This exercise highlights challenges in maintaining accurate voter lists, a key issue for electoral integrity and democratic governance.

Karnataka's SIR exercise removed over one crore names from the electoral roll, finalizing a list of 4.54 crore voters to improve voter list accuracy.

Why is this in the news?

Karnataka's Chief Electoral Officer published the final electoral roll on October 27, 2026, after a Special Intensive Revision exercise that deleted over one crore names.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What was the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) exercise in Karnataka?

  • Purpose of SIR. The SIR aimed to ensure no eligible voter was excluded and no ineligible voter remained on the electoral rolls.
  • Duration and scale. The exercise lasted over three months and began with 5.54 crore voters on the rolls.
  • Outcome. It led to the deletion of 1.07 crore names, finalizing the electoral roll at around 4.54 crore voters.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What categories of deletions occurred during the SIR?

  • Dead voters. 16.39 lakh names were deleted because the voters were identified as dead.
  • Duplicate entries. Over 7 lakh names were removed as duplicates.
  • Others category. 3.83 lakh names were deleted under an 'Others' category, which includes people who refused to sign and is uncommon in other states.
  • Shifted or untraceable voters. The largest deletion category, 80.62 lakh names, comprised voters who had shifted residence or were not found at their addresses.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the SIR matter for electoral integrity and governance?

  • Accuracy of voter lists. The exercise attempts to maintain accurate voter rolls by removing ineligible or duplicate voters.
  • Impact on voter inclusion. Voters who shifted residence had to apply through Form 6 to be restored, but only 7,87,534 applications were received, including new voters.
  • Challenges in restoration. The scale of deletions was not offset by additions, raising concerns about potential disenfranchisement.

Back to basics: Electoral Roll Revision

Electoral rolls are official lists of eligible voters maintained by the Election Commission. Periodic revisions, such as the Special Intensive Revision, are conducted to update these lists by adding new voters and removing ineligible or duplicate entries. This process aims to ensure free and fair elections by maintaining accurate voter data.

Intro: Electoral roll revisions are essential to maintain accurate voter lists for free and fair elections.

  • They remove ineligible, duplicate, and deceased voters to prevent electoral fraud.
  • They ensure eligible voters are included, but challenges exist in restoring shifted voters.
  • The balance between deletion and inclusion impacts voter participation and democratic legitimacy.

Conclusion: Periodic electoral roll revisions are vital for electoral integrity but require careful implementation to avoid disenfranchisement.
