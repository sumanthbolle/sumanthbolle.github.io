India and New Zealand Sign MoC on Electronic Origin Data Exchange Under FTA

India and New Zealand signed a Memorandum of Cooperation (MoC) on electronic origin data exchange to facilitate bilateral trade under their Free Trade Agreement (FTA). This step enhances customs cooperation and trade facilitation, important for understanding India's trade diplomacy and digital trade mechanisms.

The MoC on electronic origin data exchange under the India-New Zealand FTA strengthens customs cooperation and supports efficient bilateral trade.

Why is this in the news?

On 19 October 2026, India and New Zealand signed a MoC on electronic origin data exchange under the India-New Zealand FTA, which came into force on 20 October 2026.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the MoC on Electronic Origin Data Exchange?

  • Purpose of MoC. The MoC facilitates the advance electronic exchange of origin data on trade in goods between India and New Zealand, aiming to deepen customs cooperation and support trade facilitation measures.
  • Signatories and Date. Signed on 19 October 2026 by Shri Vivek Chaturvedi, Chairman of CBIC, and Ms. Christine Stevenson, Comptroller and Chief Executive of New Zealand Customs Service.
  • Digitalisation Focus. The MoC reflects a shared commitment to leverage digitalisation and enhanced information exchange for more efficient, secure, and predictable cross-border trade.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How does the MoC support the India-New Zealand Free Trade Agreement?

  • Trade Facilitation. The electronic exchange of origin data improves operational efficiency, reduces information gaps, and provides greater certainty to traders engaged in bilateral trade.
  • Utilisation of Trade Preferences. It supports effective utilisation of trade preferences under the FTA, ensuring benefits negotiated under the Agreement reach eligible businesses more efficiently.
  • Alignment with International Practices. The initiative aligns bilateral customs cooperation with evolving international practices in digitalisation, information exchange, and trade facilitation.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the key features of the India-New Zealand Free Trade Agreement?

  • Duty-Free Access. India gains duty-free access to 100% of New Zealand’s tariff lines, a unique feature compared to other Indian FTAs.
  • Sectoral Benefits. Sectors like textiles, apparel, leather, footwear, engineering goods, plastics, rubber, electronics, pharmaceuticals, agriculture, marine products, and chemicals benefit from tariff reductions or eliminations.
  • Investment Promotion. New Zealand commits to promoting foreign direct investment (FDI) into India aiming to increase it by US$ 20 billion within 15 years, subject to India’s economic growth.
  • Mobility Provisions. The FTA includes new Temporary Employment Entry visa categories, enhanced post-study work rights for Indian graduates, work rights during study, and a working holiday scheme for Indian nationals.
  • Protection of Sensitive Sectors. India excludes 30% of its tariff lines to protect sensitive and strategic sectors, while granting zero-duty imports on 30% of tariff lines from New Zealand.

Back to basics: Free Trade Agreement (FTA) and Trade Facilitation

A Free Trade Agreement (FTA) is a pact between two or more countries to reduce or eliminate tariffs, import quotas, and preferences on most goods and services traded between them. Trade facilitation refers to simplifying and harmonizing international trade procedures, often through digitalisation and information exchange, to reduce costs and delays in cross-border trade.
