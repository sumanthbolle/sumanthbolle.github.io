Indian Navy’s First Training Squadron Visits Colombo, Sri Lanka

The Indian Navy’s First Training Squadron, comprising INS Tir, INS Sujata, and ICGS Sarathi, arrived at Colombo on 26 October 2026 for a port call. This visit enhances professional naval cooperation between India and Sri Lanka and supports India’s regional maritime security vision.

The Indian Navy’s First Training Squadron’s visit to Colombo strengthens bilateral naval ties and advances India’s maritime security cooperation in the Indian Ocean region.

Why is this in the news?

The Indian Navy’s First Training Squadron arrived at Colombo, Sri Lanka, on 26 October 2026, marking a significant step in maritime cooperation between the two countries.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What happened during the Indian Navy’s First Training Squadron visit to Colombo?

  • Arrival and welcome. INS Tir, INS Sujata, and ICGS Sarathi arrived at Colombo on 26 October 2026 and were ceremonially welcomed by the Sri Lanka Navy with a naval band.
  • Professional interaction. The visit enabled professional engagement between the two navies, including interaction between Sri Lanka Navy officials and Indian sea trainees onboard.
  • Training and exercises. Personnel from both navies conducted cross-training visits and a Passage Exercise (PASSEX) to exchange knowledge and improve coordination.
  • Community outreach. Public ship visits and community engagement activities were held to familiarize locals with Indian Navy training and ships.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this visit matter for India’s maritime strategy and regional security?

  • Strengthening maritime partnership. The port call reflects the enduring maritime partnership between India and Sri Lanka and their commitment to naval cooperation.
  • Enhancing regional security. The visit supports India’s vision of MAHASAGAR, which promotes mutual cooperation for security and growth across the Indian Ocean region.
  • Professional naval training. Engagements during the visit help improve professional naval training and operational coordination between the two navies.

Back to basics: Maritime Diplomacy

Maritime diplomacy involves naval interactions and cooperation between countries to promote security, build trust, and enhance regional stability. It includes port calls, joint exercises, training exchanges, and community engagement to strengthen bilateral and multilateral relations in the maritime domain.

Mains question (15 marks)

Examine how India’s naval engagements with neighbouring countries like Sri Lanka contribute to regional maritime security and diplomacy.

Examine: Explain the role of naval cooperation in enhancing regional security and diplomatic relations using India-Sri Lanka naval interactions as an example.

Intro: Naval engagements are a key component of India’s maritime diplomacy and regional security strategy.

  • India’s naval visits and joint exercises with neighbours build trust and interoperability.
  • Such cooperation supports India’s vision of regional security and economic growth in the Indian Ocean.
  • Professional exchanges improve naval capabilities and coordination among friendly navies.
  • Community outreach during visits strengthens people-to-people ties and maritime awareness.

Conclusion: India’s naval diplomacy with neighbours like Sri Lanka advances mutual security and regional stability.
