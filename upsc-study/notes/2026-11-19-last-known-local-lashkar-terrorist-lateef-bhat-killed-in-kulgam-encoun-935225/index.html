Last Known Local Lashkar Terrorist Lateef Bhat Killed in Kulgam Encounter

Lashkar-e-Taiba's last known active local terrorist in Kashmir, Lateef Bhat, was neutralised in a joint operation by J&K Police, Army, CRPF, and intelligence agencies in Kulgam in November 2026. This marks a significant development in India's counter-terrorism efforts in Jammu and Kashmir, relevant for understanding internal security dynamics and government strategies against terrorism.

The killing of Lashkar-e-Taiba's last known local terrorist in Kashmir highlights the coordinated counter-terrorism efforts by Indian security forces in Jammu and Kashmir.

Why is this in the news?

The killing of Lashkar-e-Taiba's last known active local terrorist, Lateef Bhat, in Kulgam in November 2026 triggered official statements and highlighted ongoing counter-terrorism operations in Jammu and Kashmir.

Who was Lateef Bhat and what happened in the encounter?

  • Identity and background. Lateef Ahmad Bhat was the last known active local Lashkar-e-Taiba terrorist in Kashmir, involved in multiple attacks including the November 11, 2026 attack on CRPF personnel in Kulgam.
  • Encounter details. In a joint operation by J&K Police, Indian Army, CRPF, and intelligence agencies, Bhat was killed in a gunfight in Kulgam on November 18, 2026, after refusing to surrender despite a video call urging him to do so.
  • Associated terrorist neutralised. One of Bhat's associates, Umar Nisar Dar, was neutralised in Kulgam a day before the encounter with Bhat.

[2] Press Information Bureau[3] The Indian Express · Secondary[4] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for India's internal security?

  • End of local active terrorists. With Bhat's killing, for the first time since militancy began in 1989, there is no listed active local terrorist in Kashmir Valley, marking a milestone in counter-terrorism.
  • Ongoing threat from foreign operatives. Despite the elimination of local terrorists, around 30 Pakistani operatives remain active in Kashmir, indicating continued cross-border terrorism threats.
  • Government's coordinated approach. The operation demonstrated high synergy among security forces, reflecting the 'Whole of Government approach' emphasised in national counter-terrorism policy.

[1] Press Information Bureau[3] The Indian Express · Secondary[2] Press Information Bureau

What challenges and concerns arose during the operation?

  • Civilian hardships. Prolonged security operations in civilian areas of Kulgam led to restrictions affecting local populations, drawing criticism from political parties about collective punishment and hardships.
  • Sanctity of religious places. Security forces displayed restraint to preserve the sanctity of the mosque where Bhat was hiding during the encounter.

Back to basics: Counter-terrorism operations in Jammu and Kashmir

Counter-terrorism operations in Jammu and Kashmir involve coordinated efforts by multiple security agencies including the police, army, paramilitary forces, and intelligence agencies. These operations aim to neutralise terrorists, prevent attacks, and dismantle terror networks while balancing security with civilian safety. The 'Whole of Government approach' ensures synergy among all stakeholders to effectively address terrorism and related threats.

What happens next?

  • Balancing security and civilian rights. Addressing concerns about prolonged restrictions and hardships on civilians during anti-terror operations is necessary to prevent alienation and maintain public support.
