Himalayas Agenda Excluded from COP31 Formal List Due to Lack of Major Bloc Support

At the 31st UN Climate Change Conference (COP31) in 2026, the Himalayas agenda proposed by Nepal, Bhutan, and Kyrgyzstan was not included in the formal discussion list after developed nations withheld support. This development highlights challenges in advancing regional climate issues within global negotiations, relevant for understanding international climate diplomacy.

The Himalayas agenda was excluded from COP31's formal list as major developed countries did not support it, reflecting the complexities of climate negotiations involving regional environmental concerns.

Why is this in the news?

Nepal's Foreign Minister announced on November 10, 2026, at COP31 in Antalya, Turkiye, that the Himalayas agenda was excluded from the formal list due to lack of support from major developed countries.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why was the Himalayas agenda excluded from COP31's formal list?

  • Lack of major bloc support. The agenda proposed by Nepal, Bhutan, and Kyrgyzstan did not receive backing from developed nations, notably the European Union, which led to its exclusion from the formal list of topics at COP31.
  • Mixed reactions from countries. While several Least Developed Countries (LDCs) and mountain nations supported the proposal, major developed countries remained reluctant to adopt a new agenda item.
  • Negotiation dynamics. The exclusion resulted from complex negotiations where different major blocs had varying positions, making it difficult for the Himalayas agenda to pass.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the implications of excluding the Himalayas agenda at COP31?

  • Delayed formal recognition. Without formal inclusion, the specific challenges of the Himalayas region in climate change discussions face delays in receiving global attention and support.
  • Continued advocacy expected. Nepal and its partners plan to continue pushing for recognition and expect a high-level dialogue on mountain-related issues in upcoming COP31 sessions.
  • Focus on research and financial aid. Nepal seeks research assistance to understand mountain climate changes and financial support for adaptation, mitigation, and disaster recovery.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the Himalayas agenda matter for climate change and India’s neighbourhood?

  • Climate justice concerns. Nepal, contributing negligibly to global emissions, faces severe climate impacts like glacial floods and mountain collapses, highlighting the need for climate justice.
  • Regional environmental impact. The Himalayas sustain millions of lives and livelihoods, and their rapid melting poses risks to water security and disaster vulnerability in South Asia.
  • International cooperation challenges. The agenda’s exclusion reflects difficulties in addressing regional environmental issues within global frameworks, affecting India and neighbouring countries.

Back to basics: UN Climate Change Conferences (COP)

The Conference of Parties (COP) is an annual meeting of countries under the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change to negotiate and advance global climate action. Each COP sets formal agenda items for discussion, which determine the focus areas for negotiations and decisions. Inclusion in the formal agenda is crucial for raising specific issues and securing international commitments.
