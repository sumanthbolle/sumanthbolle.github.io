India Administers Over 1 Crore HPV Vaccine Doses in Under Nine Months

India has achieved a milestone by administering more than 1 crore doses of the HPV vaccine to 14-year-old girls under a national campaign launched in February 2026. This progress is significant for cervical cancer prevention and reflects strong government coordination and public confidence in preventive healthcare.

India's National HPV Vaccination Campaign has administered over 1 crore doses in less than nine months, covering nearly 87% of the target cohort of 14-year-old girls to prevent cervical cancer.

Why is this in the news?

The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare announced on November 21, 2026, that India crossed the milestone of administering over 1 crore HPV vaccine doses in under nine months since the campaign launch in February 2026.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the National HPV Vaccination Campaign and its coverage?

  • Campaign launch and target. Launched by Prime Minister Narendra Modi in February 2026, the campaign provides free, safe, voluntary single-dose HPV vaccination to 14-year-old girls at government health facilities with parental consent.
  • Coverage achieved. The campaign has reached nearly 87% of the annual target cohort of approximately 1.2 crore girls, administering over 1 crore doses in less than nine months.
  • State-wise performance. Six states—Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Mizoram, Madhya Pradesh, and Bihar—have achieved 100% coverage of their target populations, with Uttar Pradesh vaccinating over 29.33 lakh girls.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

How is the campaign implemented and monitored?

  • Centre-State coordination. Strong coordination between the Union Ministry of Health and Family Welfare and state governments supports tailored approaches to reach rural, urban, and remote areas.
  • Community engagement. Healthcare professionals, school teachers, ASHAs, ANMs, and frontline workers actively engage parents and communities to build awareness and counter misinformation.
  • Digital monitoring. The U-WIN digital platform enables real-time monitoring, beneficiary tracking, vaccination slot booking, and issuance of digital certificates, enhancing transparency and accessibility.
  • Safety measures. Vaccination sessions are supervised by trained medical officers with 24×7 healthcare arrangements to manage rare adverse events following immunization (AEFI).

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does HPV vaccination matter for cervical cancer prevention?

  • Cause of cervical cancer. Persistent infection with high-risk types of Human Papillomavirus is responsible for the majority of cervical cancer cases.
  • Effectiveness of vaccination. HPV vaccination is a proven preventive intervention that reduces the risk of HPV infections leading to cervical cancer.
  • Timing of vaccination. Vaccinating girls at 14 years offers protection before potential exposure to HPV, contributing to long-term cervical cancer prevention.

Back to basics: HPV Vaccination and Cervical Cancer Prevention

Human Papillomavirus (HPV) is a virus that can cause cervical cancer in women. Vaccination against HPV is a preventive measure that protects individuals before they are exposed to the virus. Vaccines are typically given to adolescent girls to build immunity early. National immunisation campaigns use government health facilities and frontline workers to deliver vaccines safely and monitor adverse events. Digital platforms can support tracking and transparency in vaccination programs.
