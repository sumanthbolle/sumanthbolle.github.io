Jharkhand Finance Minister Moves Resolution Against Special Intensive Revision

Jharkhand Assembly held a special session on November 25, 2026, where the Finance Minister moved a resolution against the ongoing Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls citing 27% suspicious voters. This issue highlights electoral roll revision challenges and federal concerns over Election Commission functioning, relevant for UPSC exams on electoral reforms and Centre-State relations.

Jharkhand's Finance Minister opposed the SIR exercise due to 27% suspicious voters, urging its halt and transparency reforms.

Why is this in the news?

On November 25, 2026, Jharkhand Assembly's special session debated the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls and the amended mines law, with the Finance Minister moving a resolution against SIR citing procedural issues and suspicious voters.

What is the Jharkhand Assembly's resolution against SIR about?

  • Resolution moved. Jharkhand Finance Minister Radha Krishna Kishore moved a resolution seeking an immediate halt to the ongoing Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls.
  • Reason for opposition. The resolution cited procedural complexities and technical shortcomings in the voter list revision process, with 27% of Jharkhand's 2.21 crore voters deemed suspicious and sent notices.
  • Allegation against Election Commission. The minister alleged that the Election Commission of India was acting as a puppet of the Centre and called for removal of the Chief Election Commissioner to protect the Constitution.
  • Next steps. The Assembly planned to discuss challenges related to SIR and vote on the resolution, which if passed, would be sent to the Centre and other authorities.

Back to basics: Electoral Roll Revision

Electoral roll revision is the process of updating the list of eligible voters to ensure accuracy and remove duplicates or ineligible entries. Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is a focused exercise to clean the electoral rolls comprehensively. The Election Commission of India oversees this process to maintain free and fair elections, but it must balance transparency, accuracy, and federal cooperation.
