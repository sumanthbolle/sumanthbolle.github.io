India Leads Regional Health Cooperation at First BIMSTEC Health Ministers’ Meeting

The inaugural BIMSTEC Health Ministers’ Meeting in Colombo saw unanimous adoption of a Joint Ministerial Statement and a Revised Plan of Action on Public Health (2026–2030). This marks a key step in regional health collaboration, with India offering expertise and hosting a Centre of Excellence in Traditional Medicine.

India advanced regional health security and cooperation through BIMSTEC by adopting a joint statement, sharing expertise, and deepening bilateral healthcare partnerships.

Why is this in the news?

The first BIMSTEC Health Ministers’ Meeting held on 11 November 2026 in Colombo adopted a joint ministerial statement and a revised public health action plan for 2026–2030, with India playing a leading role.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What were the key outcomes of the first BIMSTEC Health Ministers’ Meeting?

  • Joint Ministerial Statement. The meeting unanimously adopted a Joint Ministerial Statement reaffirming commitment to accessible, affordable, quality healthcare and regional health security across BIMSTEC countries.
  • Revised Plan of Action. The Revised BIMSTEC Plan of Action on Public Health (2026–2030) was adopted, outlining cooperation on disease surveillance, pandemic preparedness, digital health, traditional medicine, One Health, and healthcare workforce development.
  • Regional Centres of Excellence. The meeting welcomed initiatives including a BIMSTEC Centre of Excellence for Public Health in Sri Lanka, a Centre of Excellence on Tropical Medicine in Thailand, and India's commitment to host a Centre of Excellence in Traditional Medicine at ITRA, Jamnagar.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How is India contributing to regional health cooperation under BIMSTEC?

  • Leadership and Expertise Sharing. India highlighted its healthcare transformation and offered to share expertise in primary healthcare, digital health systems, and public health infrastructure with BIMSTEC countries.
  • Healthcare Infrastructure Support to Sri Lanka. India exchanged an MoU to supply 100 fully-equipped ambulances to Sri Lanka under a grant assistance of approximately USD 5.28 million, enhancing emergency medical response.
  • Support for Primary Healthcare in Sri Lanka. India announced support for 50 Medical Kiosks/Health ATMs to strengthen Sri Lanka’s primary healthcare services, integrating point-of-care diagnostics with telemedicine.
  • Bilateral Engagements. India held bilateral meetings with Sri Lanka and Nepal to deepen healthcare cooperation, reflecting strong historical and cultural ties and the Neighbourhood First policy.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this regional health cooperation matter for India and the BIMSTEC region?

  • Strengthening Regional Health Security. The cooperation enhances disease surveillance, emergency preparedness, and coordinated responses to cross-border health threats, crucial for regional stability.
  • Advancing Universal Health Coverage. The joint commitment supports Sustainable Development Goal 3 by improving access to quality health services and building resilient health systems.
  • Promoting Traditional Medicine. India’s hosting of the Centre of Excellence in Traditional Medicine fosters collaborative research and preservation of traditional knowledge across the region.
  • Digital Health Innovation. The meeting envisaged a BIMSTEC Digital Health Framework to promote telemedicine, interoperable electronic health records, and AI in diagnostics.

Back to basics: Regional Health Cooperation

Regional health cooperation involves countries working together to improve public health outcomes by sharing resources, expertise, and coordinated responses to health challenges. It helps in disease surveillance, pandemic preparedness, and building resilient health systems. Such cooperation can include joint action plans, shared digital health initiatives, and collaborative research in traditional medicine, enhancing healthcare access and security across borders.
