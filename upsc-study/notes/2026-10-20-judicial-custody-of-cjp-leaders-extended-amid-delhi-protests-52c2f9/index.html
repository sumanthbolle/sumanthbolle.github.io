Judicial Custody of CJP Leaders Extended Amid Delhi Protests

The judicial custody of Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) activists Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka was extended by two days by a Special Executive Magistrate court in Delhi due to concerns over potential public disorder. This development is significant for UPSC aspirants studying law enforcement, judicial processes, and the balance between civil liberties and public order.

The Delhi court extended the judicial custody of CJP leaders citing their conduct as disrespectful to law and a threat to public peace during protests.

Why is this in the news?

On October 19, 2026, a Special Executive Magistrate court in Delhi extended the judicial custody of CJP leaders Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka amid ongoing protests.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why was the judicial custody of CJP leaders extended?

  • Court's observation. The Special Executive Magistrate observed that the conduct of Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka showed no respect for the law and raised concerns about their potential to disturb public peace.
  • Concerns over protests. The court noted their habitual calling of protests at de-notified areas and found sufficient material in the investigating officer’s application to consider the threat to public order.
  • Surety rejection. The sureties submitted on behalf of the activists were rejected due to discrepancies in identity documents and lack of control over the activists to ensure their court appearance.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What led to the arrest and judicial custody of the CJP leaders?

  • Protest at Jantar Mantar. The activists were detained on October 13 for entering the barricaded Jantar Mantar, which had been de-notified as a protest site, by climbing over its gates.
  • Violation of undertaking. Police alleged that the activists violated an undertaking by calling people to gather at Jantar Mantar and used social media to encourage demonstrations at unauthorized locations.
  • Context of protests. The CJP, along with other groups, has been protesting alleged irregularities in the special intensive revision of electoral rolls and demanding the resignation of the Chief Election Commissioner.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What does this case illustrate about law enforcement and civil liberties?

  • Balancing act. The extension of judicial custody highlights the challenge of balancing the right to protest with maintaining public order and respecting the law.
  • Judicial scrutiny. The court’s role in assessing the conduct of protest leaders and the adequacy of sureties reflects judicial oversight in law enforcement actions.
  • Use of social media. The case shows how authorities monitor social media activities related to protests to assess potential threats to public peace.

Back to basics: Judicial Custody

Judicial custody refers to the detention of an accused person in a jail or prison under the authority of a court, usually during the investigation or trial period. It is ordered when the court believes that releasing the accused might lead to tampering with evidence, influencing witnesses, or disturbing public order. The judiciary balances the rights of the accused with the need to maintain law and order while ensuring fair trial procedures.
