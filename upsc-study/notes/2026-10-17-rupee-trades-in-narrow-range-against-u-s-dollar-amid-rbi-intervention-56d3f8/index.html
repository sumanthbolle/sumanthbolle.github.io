Rupee Trades in Narrow Range Against U.S. Dollar Amid RBI Intervention

The Indian rupee hovered around the 96 mark against the U.S. dollar in early October 2026, pressured by high crude oil prices and foreign fund outflows. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) intervened in the forex market to reduce volatility and prevent sharper losses.

The RBI's active intervention helped stabilize the rupee's exchange rate despite external pressures from oil prices and foreign portfolio selling.

Why is this in the news?

The rupee's narrow trading range against the U.S. dollar in early October 2026 and RBI's forex market interventions amid macroeconomic pressures triggered this report.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What caused the rupee's narrow trading range against the U.S. dollar?

  • External pressures. Elevated crude oil prices and sustained foreign fund outflows exerted downward pressure on the rupee.
  • RBI intervention. The Reserve Bank of India periodically intervened in the forex market to smooth excessive intraday volatility and cap sharper losses.
  • Macro environment. An overall macro environment of elevated import bills and persistent strength of the U.S. dollar weighed on investor sentiment.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How did the RBI's actions affect the rupee's stability?

  • Market smoothing. RBI's dollar interventions helped maintain the rupee within a narrow trading band and prevented sharper depreciation.
  • Reserve changes. India's forex reserves fell by $18.343 billion to $747.557 billion during the week ended September 25, 2026, reflecting the scale of RBI's market defence.
  • Monetary policy context. The Monetary Policy Committee was expected to raise the policy rate by 25 basis points to 5.50%, indicating tightening to manage inflation and currency pressures.

Back to basics: Monetary Policy and Exchange Rate Management

Monetary policy involves the central bank regulating interest rates and money supply to control inflation and stabilize the economy. Exchange rate management is the central bank's intervention in the foreign exchange market to prevent excessive volatility in the currency's value. This can include buying or selling foreign currency to influence the rupee's exchange rate against other currencies like the U.S. dollar.
