IndiaAI Mission Launches Indigenous AI Foundation Models EKA-1 and Dhīr

The IndiaAI Mission under MeitY released three indigenous foundation AI models: EKA-1 by Soket and Dhīr 2B and Dhīr 5B by ZenteiQ. These models mark a milestone in India's homegrown AI capabilities, supporting multiple Indian languages and sectors like banking, cybersecurity, and government services.

IndiaAI Mission's release of EKA-1 and Dhīr models demonstrates India's progress in developing indigenous AI technology tailored to national needs.

Why is this in the news?

On November 3, 2026, the Ministry of Electronics and IT released indigenous AI foundation models EKA-1 and Dhīr 2B & 5B under the IndiaAI Innovation Centre pillar.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the newly released indigenous AI models?

  • EKA-1 by Soket. EKA-1 is a foundation model with 26.3 billion parameters trained on approximately 7.5 trillion tokens across all 22 scheduled Indian languages and English, designed for math, code, reasoning, and agentic use.
  • Dhīr 2B and Dhīr 5B by ZenteiQ. Dhīr 2B and Dhīr 5B are open-source multilingual assistants supporting English and 10 Indian languages, deployable as secure, on-premise, offline-capable solutions for critical sectors like defence, government, and education.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

How do these models support India's AI ecosystem?

  • Technological autonomy. The development of these indigenous models strengthens India's technological and strategic autonomy in artificial intelligence.
  • Sector-specific applications. EKA-1 is tailored for India's banking, financial services, and cybersecurity sectors, while Dhīr models enable offline automation and knowledge retrieval in low-connectivity or sensitive environments.
  • Accessibility and innovation. Through platforms like AIKosh, these models are accessible to researchers, students, and innovators, encouraging experimentation and wider adoption.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What role does the IndiaAI Mission play in this development?

  • Mission pillars. The IndiaAI Mission operates across seven pillars including Compute Capacity, Innovation Centre, Datasets Platform (AIKosh), Application Development, FutureSkills, Startup Financing, and Safe & Trusted AI.
  • Support infrastructure. Access to subsidized national AI compute infrastructure and research support under the mission enabled the development of these models, showcasing how public AI infrastructure accelerates homegrown innovation.
  • Vision and approval. Approved by the Union Cabinet on March 7, 2024, the mission aims to democratize AI use, address India-centric challenges, and position India as a global AI leader.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the technical features and deployment advantages of these models?

  • EKA-1 capabilities. EKA-1 is an open, sparse hybrid language model capable of performing inferencing locally on devices like smartphones without internet connectivity.
  • Dhīr models' flexibility. Dhīr 2B and Dhīr 5B can run on various computing platforms including GPUs and TPUs, and their lightweight architecture supports on-device deployment on mobile phones without cloud dependency.
  • Privacy and security. Local deployment of Dhīr models enhances privacy and security by keeping data on users' devices, crucial for sensitive sectors.

Back to basics: Foundation AI Models

Foundation AI models are large-scale machine learning models trained on vast amounts of data to perform multiple tasks such as language understanding, reasoning, and code generation. They serve as base models that can be fine-tuned for specific applications or sectors. Indigenous foundation models are developed domestically to ensure technological autonomy, data privacy, and relevance to local languages and contexts.
