World Bank Reports Historic Decline in India's Extreme Poverty

Prime Minister Modi highlighted a World Bank report showing a sharp fall in India's extreme poverty from 27.1% in 2011 to 2.6% in 2023, reflecting a major socio-economic shift. This matters for UPSC aspirants as it relates to poverty alleviation, economic inequality, and employment growth, key topics in GS3 Economy and GS1 Social Issues.

India's extreme poverty rate fell from 27.1% in 2011 to 2.6% in 2023, with 49.6 crore people moving out of poverty, according to the World Bank.

Why is this in the news?

On November 1, 2026, Prime Minister Narendra Modi cited a World Bank report highlighting India's rapid decline in extreme poverty between 2011 and 2023.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What does the World Bank report say about India's poverty decline?

  • Extreme poverty rate drop. India's extreme poverty rate fell from 27.1% in 2011 to 2.6% in 2023, based on the $3 per day poverty line at 2021 PPP.
  • People lifted out of poverty. Between 2011 and 2023, 49.6 crore people moved out of extreme poverty in India.
  • Measurement thresholds. The report uses the lower-middle-income poverty threshold of $4.20 per day and the extreme poverty line of $3 per day at 2021 PPP to estimate poverty.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this poverty decline matter for India’s economy and society?

  • Employment growth. Employment increased by 13.4 crore in six years, nearly twice the growth of the working-age population, indicating improved job opportunities.
  • Reduction in inequality. India's Gini Index, a measure of economic inequality, fell by over 20% to 23.0, showing a significant decrease in income disparity.
  • Multidimensional poverty reduction. The report highlights progress not only in monetary poverty but also in non-monetary indicators, reflecting broader socio-economic development.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How has the government positioned this poverty decline?

  • Government commitment. Prime Minister Modi stated that eradicating poverty was a steadfast commitment of his government, not just a slogan.
  • Political context. The Prime Minister contrasted this achievement with the Congress party's decades-old 'Garibi Hatao' campaign, implying sustained efforts led to real results.
  • Public messaging. The Prime Minister used a popular Bollywood song symbolizing perseverance to emphasize the determination behind poverty reduction efforts.

Back to basics: Poverty and Inequality Measurement

Poverty is often measured by income thresholds such as the extreme poverty line, which is the minimum income needed to meet basic needs. The World Bank uses international poverty lines like $3 per day at purchasing power parity to compare poverty across countries. Inequality is measured by indices like the Gini Index, where a lower value indicates more equal income distribution. Understanding these measures helps evaluate socio-economic progress.

Answer and trap
