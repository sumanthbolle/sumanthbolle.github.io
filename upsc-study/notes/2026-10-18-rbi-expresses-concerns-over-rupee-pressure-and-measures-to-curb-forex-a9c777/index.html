RBI Measures to Curb Rupee Volatility and Forex Market Risks

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) introduced regulatory measures on October 10, 2026, to manage rupee depreciation and reduce volatility in the foreign exchange market. These steps are crucial for maintaining market discipline, managing inflation, and ensuring financial stability amid external pressures.

RBI's new forex regulations aim to curb speculative trading, protect forex reserves, and stabilize the rupee amid global economic challenges.

Why is this in the news?

On October 10, 2026, the RBI issued circulars introducing measures to address rupee depreciation and forex market volatility amid geopolitical tensions and fluctuating oil prices.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What measures did RBI introduce to manage rupee volatility?

  • Derivative contract restrictions. RBI barred rebooking of cancelled foreign exchange derivative contracts involving INR to curb speculative activity, while allowing rollovers on maturity.
  • Lowered exposure threshold. The threshold for undertaking foreign exchange derivative transactions without underlying exposure was reduced from $100 million to $5 million across all authorised dealers and stock exchanges.
  • Additional documentation. Authorised dealers must obtain undertakings confirming that the same underlying exposure is not hedged with multiple dealers.
  • Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve (FERR). For derivative contracts exceeding $2 million, dealers must maintain a 20% cash reserve with RBI to manage risk.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is the rupee under pressure and why does it matter?

  • External factors driving depreciation. Persistent US dollar strength, high crude oil prices, geopolitical tensions, and foreign portfolio outflows are causing sustained rupee depreciation.
  • Impact on inflation and economy. A depreciating rupee raises import costs, especially for crude oil and essential goods, feeding into domestic inflation and increasing corporate foreign debt servicing risks.
  • Current account deficit and reserves. High import bills widen the current account deficit and pressure foreign exchange reserves, which the RBI actively manages to maintain stability.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How do RBI’s other interventions support forex stability?

  • Dedicated Dollar Facility for OMCs. RBI supplies dollars directly to oil marketing companies to reduce their demand in the interbank market, moderating rupee pressure.
  • Liquidity management tools. RBI uses sell-buy swaps and limits on banks’ net open positions to control speculative forex trading and manage liquidity.
  • Mobilising foreign capital. Instruments like Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) deposits and concessional dollar swaps help bring foreign capital into the banking system without immediate spot market depletion.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the outlook and challenges for the rupee?

  • Short-term stabilization. RBI’s measures are expected to arrest the rupee’s fall in the short term, with potential appreciation towards 95.5 to 96 against the dollar.
  • Medium to long-term pressures. Structural issues like geopolitical risks, crude oil prices, and interest rate differentials will continue to keep the rupee under pressure.
  • Risk of speculative activity. Strict adherence to new exposure limits and documentation is needed to prevent speculative bets that could destabilize the currency.

Back to basics: Foreign Exchange Market and Currency Management

The foreign exchange market is where currencies are traded and their values determined. Central banks like the RBI intervene to stabilize their currency by managing liquidity, regulating derivative contracts, and controlling speculative trading. Currency depreciation can increase import costs and inflation, so managing volatility is crucial for economic stability.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about RBI's recent measures to curb rupee volatility: 1. RBI reduced the threshold for foreign exchange derivative transactions without underlying exposure from $100 million to $5 million. 2. RBI requires a Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve of 20% for derivative contracts involving INR exceeding $5 million. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 incorrectly states the threshold for Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve, which applies to contracts exceeding $2 million, not $5 million.
