World Bank Highlights India's Remarkable Poverty Reduction Progress

The World Bank's October 2026 report shows India achieved one of the fastest poverty declines globally, with extreme poverty falling from 27.1% in 2011 to 2.6% in 2023. This matters for UPSC aspirants as it reflects the impact of government policies on poverty alleviation and inclusive growth.

India's poverty rate drastically declined between 2011 and 2023, driven by rising employment, especially among rural women, and expanded social transfers.

Why is this in the news?

The World Bank released its October 2026 Poverty and Equity Brief on October 30, 2026, acknowledging India's rapid reduction in poverty over the last decade.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What progress has India made in reducing poverty?

  • Monetary poverty decline. Between 2011 and 2023, the share of the population living below the Lower Middle-Income Country line ($4.2/day) fell from 57.7% to 16.1%, lifting an estimated 496 million people out of poverty.
  • Extreme poverty reduction. Extreme poverty, measured at $3.00/day, declined sharply from 27.1% in 2011 to 2.6% in 2023.
  • Non-monetary poverty improvement. Using the Multidimensional Poverty Index, poverty fell from 53.8% in 2005-06 to 16.4% in 2019-21, with the World Bank MPI at 15.5% in 2022-23.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What factors contributed to this poverty reduction?

  • Employment growth. Employment increased by about 134 million people between 2017 and 2023, nearly twice the growth of the working-age population, with rural women accounting for much of this gain.
  • Social transfers and subsidies. Expanded social transfers, including food subsidies, played a significant role in reducing poverty.
  • Improved consumption and welfare. Strong consumption growth and welfare gains were observed in both rural and urban households, with rural India narrowing the rural-urban welfare gap.
  • Infrastructure access. Access to electricity reached 99% of the population by 2022, supporting improved living standards.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What challenges and disparities remain in poverty reduction?

  • Concentration of poverty. Poverty is increasingly concentrated among households with lower education, fewer assets, limited stable employment, and higher dependency ratios.
  • Geographical concentration. About 57% of India's poor population live in a contiguous belt from Uttar Pradesh to West Bengal.
  • Wage disparities. Median earnings among the top 10% of workers were about 13 times those of the bottom 10% in 2023.
  • Youth unemployment. Youth unemployment rose from 14.6% to 15.9% between Q1 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027, particularly among rural young women.
  • Employment quality. Real earnings remained broadly unchanged as employment growth was concentrated in relatively low-productivity activities.

Back to basics: Poverty and its measurement

Poverty is commonly measured by income thresholds such as the international poverty lines defined by the World Bank, including extreme poverty at $3.00/day and lower middle-income poverty at $4.20/day. Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) measures poverty beyond income, considering factors like education, health, and living standards to capture non-monetary deprivation.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about India's poverty reduction as per the World Bank's 2026 report: 1. Extreme poverty in India declined from 27.1% in 2011 to 2.6% in 2023. 2. Employment growth between 2017 and 2023 was mainly driven by urban men entering paid work. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the employment growth was largely driven by rural women entering paid work, not urban men.

Mains question (15 marks)

Examine the factors behind India's rapid poverty reduction in the last decade and discuss the challenges that remain.

Examine: Analyze the causes of India's poverty decline and the persisting issues that need attention.

Intro: India has achieved one of the fastest poverty reductions globally in the last decade.

  • Discuss the role of rising employment, especially among rural women, and expanded social transfers in reducing poverty.
  • Explain improvements in non-monetary dimensions such as access to electricity and welfare gains.
  • Highlight remaining challenges including wage disparities, geographic concentration of poverty, and rising youth unemployment.
  • Suggest the need for addressing employment quality and targeted policies for vulnerable groups.

Conclusion: While India’s poverty reduction is notable, addressing structural disparities and employment quality is crucial for sustained inclusive growth.
