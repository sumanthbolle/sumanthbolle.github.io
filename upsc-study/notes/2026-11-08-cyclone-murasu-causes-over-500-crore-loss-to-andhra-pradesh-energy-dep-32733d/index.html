Cyclone Murasu Causes Over ₹500 Crore Loss to Andhra Pradesh Energy Department

Cyclones Arnab and Murasu caused preliminary losses exceeding ₹500 crore to Andhra Pradesh's Energy Department, damaging over 7,500 power poles. This event highlights the challenges in disaster management and infrastructure resilience, important for UPSC preparation on disaster impact and government response.

Cyclone Murasu inflicted over ₹500 crore damage to Andhra Pradesh's power infrastructure, prompting urgent restoration efforts.

Why is this in the news?

On November 6, 2026, Andhra Pradesh's Energy Minister reported that cyclones Arnab and Murasu caused over ₹500 crore in losses to the state's Energy Department.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What damage did Cyclone Murasu cause to Andhra Pradesh's energy infrastructure?

  • Extent of damage. Cyclone Murasu alone damaged more than 7,500 electricity poles along with electricity lines, conductors, and distribution transformers.
  • Financial loss. Preliminary losses to the Andhra Pradesh Energy Department from cyclones Arnab and Murasu exceeded ₹500 crore.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How is the government responding to the cyclone damage?

  • Restoration efforts. Restoration work is progressing on a war footing with a target to restore power supply in all affected areas by Saturday evening.
  • Manpower deployment. More than 3,000 electricity personnel from Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and Telangana have been deployed to assist in restoration.
  • Prioritization. Officials have been directed to prioritize restoring domestic connections first, followed by agricultural and aquaculture connections.
  • Interstate coordination. The Chief Minister coordinated with counterparts in neighboring states to mobilize additional manpower and equipment.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What other damages were reported due to the cyclones?

  • Agricultural impact. Crops such as maize, banana, papaya, amla, mango, and tobacco suffered damage, along with curing barns in affected mandals.
  • Infrastructure damage. Houses, cattle sheds, mango orchards, and nurseries were also damaged in the cyclone-affected areas.
  • Assessment directives. The Energy Minister directed district and agriculture officials to complete a speedy and transparent assessment of crop losses.

Back to basics: Disaster Management and Energy Infrastructure

Disaster management involves preparing for, responding to, and recovering from natural calamities like cyclones. Energy infrastructure, including power poles, lines, and transformers, is vulnerable to such events, affecting electricity supply. Efficient restoration and inter-state coordination are critical to minimize disruption and support affected populations.
