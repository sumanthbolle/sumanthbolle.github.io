National Conference on Gender Data and Statistics by MoSPI and UN Women

On 2nd November 2026, the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) in collaboration with UN Women organised a national conference to discuss gender data and its use in policymaking. This event is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights the role of gender-disaggregated data in evidence-based policy formulation for gender equality and social sector reforms.

The conference emphasised strengthening gender statistics to inform policies that address disparities in health, education, employment, and financial inclusion.

Why is this in the news?

MoSPI and UN Women organised a national conference on 2nd November 2026 to deliberate on gender data and statistics for evidence-based policymaking.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What was the purpose of the National Conference on Gender Data and Statistics?

  • Conference objective. The conference aimed to deepen understanding of the findings from the Women and Men in India 2025 publication and to strengthen the use of gender statistics for policymaking.
  • Participants involved. It brought together policymakers, statisticians, researchers, representatives from central and state governments, academic institutions, technical experts, and UN agencies.
  • Focus on evidence-based policy. The event focused on bridging the gap between data producers and users to promote evidence-based policies addressing gender disparities.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What key themes were discussed at the conference?

  • Health and survival. Discussions recognised progress in maternal healthcare and essential health services while highlighting nutritional and adolescent health challenges.
  • Girls’ education and transition. The session noted improvements in enrolment but emphasised gaps in learning outcomes, STEM participation, and employment pathways.
  • Women’s work and wages. Rising female labour-force participation was acknowledged alongside challenges in job quality, wage disparities, and unpaid care work.
  • Livelihoods and financial inclusion. Progress in entrepreneurship and financial inclusion was recognised, with a need for better access to credit, markets, and sustainable livelihoods.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does gender-disaggregated data matter for policymaking?

  • Identifying gender gaps. Gender data helps identify disparities in health, education, employment, and financial inclusion to inform targeted policies.
  • Improving policy quality. Stronger gender statistics enable better state-level analysis and guide reforms for welfare delivery and women’s financial empowerment.
  • Supporting inclusive development. Translating gender-disaggregated data into actionable insights supports inclusive development and advances gender equality.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How will the conference outcomes influence future gender statistics and policies?

  • Future editions of data reports. Inputs from the conference will improve measurement of gender gaps in future editions of Women and Men in India.
  • Inter-ministerial coordination. The event underscored the need for strengthened coordination among ministries for evidence-based policymaking.
  • Collaboration for data ecosystem. It reaffirmed the importance of collaboration among government institutions, statistical agencies, researchers, and development partners.

Back to basics: Gender-disaggregated Data

Gender-disaggregated data is information collected and presented separately for women and men. It helps reveal differences in outcomes and experiences between genders, which is essential for designing policies that address specific needs and promote equality. Such data supports evidence-based policymaking by providing a clear picture of gender gaps in areas like health, education, employment, and financial inclusion.

What happens next?

  • Improved measurement. The conference highlighted the need for better measurement of gender gaps in future data reports to enhance policy relevance.
  • Strengthened coordination. It called for stronger inter-ministerial coordination to effectively use gender statistics for policymaking.
