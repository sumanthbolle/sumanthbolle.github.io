Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas Observed with PM Honouring Bhagwan Birsa Munda

The Ministry of Railways and the Prime Minister observed Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas to honour tribal freedom fighter Bhagwan Birsa Munda and launched development projects for tribal empowerment. This event highlights tribal contributions to India's freedom struggle and ongoing government efforts to improve tribal welfare, relevant for understanding tribal identity and constitutional protections.

Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas commemorates tribal freedom fighters like Bhagwan Birsa Munda and showcases government initiatives for tribal development.

Why is this in the news?

The Ministry of Railways observed Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas on 13 November 2026 with tributes to Bhagwan Birsa Munda, followed by the Prime Minister's participation on 15 November 2026 to inaugurate tribal development projects.

What is Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas and why is it observed?

  • Purpose of the day. Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas is observed to honour the contributions of tribal freedom fighters, especially Bhagwan Birsa Munda, who resisted British colonial rule and worked to protect tribal rights and dignity.
  • Ministry of Railways observance. On 13 November 2026, the Ministry of Railways paid floral tributes to Bhagwan Birsa Munda at Rail Bhawan, New Delhi, recalling his role in India's freedom struggle and tribal welfare.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What were the key activities during the 2026 Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas?

  • Prime Minister's participation. On 15 November 2026, the Prime Minister participated via video conferencing from Gadchiroli, Maharashtra, addressing the 151st birth anniversary of Bhagwan Birsa Munda.
  • Development projects inaugurated. The Prime Minister inaugurated and laid foundation stones for 246 projects worth around ₹3,000 crore aimed at tribal empowerment, including education, healthcare, livelihoods, and community infrastructure.
  • Key projects. Projects included 38 Eklavya Model Residential Schools for tribal students, 100 Multi-Purpose Centres under PM-JANMAN, the Tribal Research Institute building in Arunachal Pradesh, and the Ropuiliani Tribal Freedom Fighter Museum in Mizoram.
  • Significance of the museum. The museum commemorates Mizo tribal freedom fighters and is named after Ropuiliani, a 19th-century Mizo chieftainess who resisted British rule, symbolizing tribal resistance and sacrifice.

[2] Press Information Bureau

Why does Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas matter for tribal identity and welfare?

  • Recognition of tribal contributions. The day reinforces national pride and respect for India's tribal heritage by remembering tribal leaders' courage and sacrifices during the freedom struggle.
  • Focus on tribal empowerment. The launch of projects under schemes like PM-JANMAN and DAJGUA aims to strengthen education, healthcare, livelihoods, and infrastructure, addressing tribal community needs.
  • Promotion of tribal culture. Institutions like the Tribal Research Institute and museums help preserve and showcase tribal culture, history, and heritage.

Back to basics: Scheduled Tribes and Tribal Welfare

Scheduled Tribes are indigenous communities recognized by the Constitution for special protection and development. Tribal welfare schemes focus on improving education, healthcare, livelihoods, and preserving cultural heritage to address historical disadvantages and promote social inclusion.
