ED Attaches ₹10.63 Crore Properties in PMEGP Misuse Case in Kashmir

The Enforcement Directorate (ED) attached immovable properties worth ₹10.63 crore in Jammu and Kashmir for alleged misuse of the Prime Minister Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP) loans. This case highlights governance and accountability challenges in the implementation of central welfare schemes.

The ED attached properties worth ₹10.63 crore in Kashmir over alleged criminal misuse of PMEGP loans involving fake borrowers and bank officials.

Why is this in the news?

On November 9, 2026, the ED attached properties worth ₹10.63 crore in Kashmir under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act due to alleged misuse of PMEGP loans.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What happened in the PMEGP misuse case in Kashmir?

  • Property attachment. The ED attached immovable properties worth ₹10.63 crore under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act in connection with alleged misuse of PMEGP loans.
  • Misappropriation details. Loans under the PMEGP scheme were misappropriated through a criminal conspiracy involving fake borrowers, fake invoices of non-existent firms, bank officials from Sumbal and Gamud Gadkhud branches of J&K Bank, and private brokers.
  • Diversion of funds. The proceeds from the offences were diverted through multiple bank transactions and partly used for asset construction, renovation, and personal enrichment.
  • Investigation status. The ED traced proceeds of crime amounting to ₹11.37 crore and investigations are ongoing; the CBI and J&K Anti-Corruption Bureau also registered cases in June 2026.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for governance and welfare schemes?

  • Scheme misuse impact. Misuse of PMEGP loans undermines the scheme’s goal of promoting employment and entrepreneurship among the youth.
  • Accountability challenges. The involvement of bank officials and brokers in the conspiracy reveals gaps in monitoring and accountability in scheme implementation.
  • Anti-corruption enforcement. The ED’s action under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act demonstrates the government’s efforts to curb financial crimes linked to welfare schemes.

Back to basics: Prime Minister Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP)

PMEGP is a central government scheme aimed at generating employment by providing financial assistance to micro-enterprises. It involves bank loans and subsidies to beneficiaries to start or expand businesses. Effective monitoring is essential to prevent misuse and ensure the scheme benefits intended recipients.
