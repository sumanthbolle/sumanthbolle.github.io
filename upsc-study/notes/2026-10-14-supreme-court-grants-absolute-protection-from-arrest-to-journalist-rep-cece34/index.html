Supreme Court grants absolute protection from arrest to journalist in Ayodhya temple theft case

The Supreme Court made absolute the interim protection from arrest for journalist Abhishek Upadhyay, who reported alleged theft of donations at the Ayodhya Ram Temple. This ruling is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights judicial safeguards for press freedom and digital privacy rights.

The Supreme Court granted absolute protection from arrest to journalist Abhishek Upadhyay in a road rage case linked to his investigative reporting on Ayodhya temple donation theft allegations.

Why is this in the news?

On October 14, 2026, the Supreme Court made absolute the protection from arrest for journalist Abhishek Upadhyay, who reported on alleged misappropriation of donations at the Ayodhya Ram Temple.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What protection did the Supreme Court grant to the journalist?

  • Absolute protection from arrest. The Supreme Court made absolute the interim protection from arrest granted to Abhishek Upadhyay in a road rage case and any subsequent FIRs.
  • Stay on arrest. The court ordered that the arrest of the journalist shall remain stayed until further notice.
  • Quashing police notice. The Court quashed a police notice to social media platform X seeking Upadhyay’s digital data generated three months prior to the incident, calling the notice an 'overkill'.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this ruling matter for press freedom and digital privacy?

  • Protection of journalistic freedom. The Court allowed the journalist to continue his investigative reporting and to challenge the FIR on any grounds, reinforcing press freedom.
  • Safeguarding digital privacy. By quashing the police notice for digital data, the Court protected the journalist’s digital privacy and limited police overreach.
  • Judicial oversight on police actions. The ruling shows the judiciary’s role in checking police actions that may threaten fundamental rights of journalists.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the implications for legal and journalistic practice?

  • Right to challenge FIRs. The journalist can legally challenge the road rage FIR, demonstrating the right to defend against potentially malicious cases.
  • Limits on coercive police measures. The judgment restricts police from using coercive measures like arrest or digital data requisition without proper justification.
  • Potential for guidelines on digital privacy. The Court permitted Upadhyay to file a separate petition seeking guidelines to protect digital privacy and journalistic freedoms.

Back to basics: Press freedom and judicial protection

Press freedom allows journalists to report without fear of arbitrary arrest or harassment. Judicial protection ensures that courts can intervene to prevent misuse of police powers against journalists, safeguarding their rights to free speech and privacy. Digital privacy involves protecting individuals’ data from unwarranted access, which is critical for journalists working in sensitive areas.
