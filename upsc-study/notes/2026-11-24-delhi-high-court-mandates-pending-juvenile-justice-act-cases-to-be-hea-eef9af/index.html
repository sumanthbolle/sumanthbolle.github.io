Delhi High Court mandates Children’s Courts for Juvenile Justice Act cases

The Delhi High Court ruled on November 23, 2026, that all pending cases under the Juvenile Justice Act must be tried in Children’s Courts, even if trials began before the 2022 amendment. This clarifies procedural jurisdiction and safeguards child rights, relevant for understanding juvenile justice reforms and court procedures.

The Delhi High Court ruled that all offences under the Juvenile Justice Act must be tried exclusively by Children’s Courts, applying the 2022 amendment retrospectively.

Why is this in the news?

On November 23, 2026, the Delhi High Court issued a ruling on the trial jurisdiction of cases under the Juvenile Justice Act following the 2022 amendment.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Delhi High Court rule about Juvenile Justice Act cases?

  • Mandatory Children’s Courts. The Court ruled that all cases involving offences under the Juvenile Justice Act must be tried by Children’s Courts, including those pending before other courts when the law was amended in 2022.
  • Retrospective application. The Court held that the change in trial forum is procedural and thus the 2022 amendment mandating Children’s Courts applies retrospectively.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Does every case with a minor victim go to Children’s Court?

  • No automatic transfer. The Court clarified that only offences under the Juvenile Justice Act must be tried in Children’s Courts; ordinary criminal cases with minor victims do not require transfer.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does this ruling affect appeals and trial rights?

  • Change in appeal forum. Since the trial forum changes to Children’s Courts, appeals will now lie before the High Court instead of the Court of Sessions.
  • No impact on rights. The Court noted that this procedural change does not affect the accused’s or State’s rights to appeal.

Back to basics: Juvenile Justice Act and Children’s Courts

The Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act provides a legal framework for handling offences involving children. Children’s Courts are specialized courts designated to try offences under this Act, ensuring child-sensitive procedures and protections. The 2022 amendment mandated that all offences under the Act be tried exclusively by Children’s Courts, centralizing jurisdiction to better protect child rights.

Common mistake. Not all criminal cases involving minor victims must be tried in Children’s Courts; only offences under the Juvenile Justice Act require this.
