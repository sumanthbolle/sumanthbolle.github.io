Supreme Court Upholds Tribal Land Rights, Quashes Odisha Mining Acquisition

The Supreme Court ruled that prior consent of the Gram Sabha is mandatory for land acquisition in Scheduled Areas under the 2013 land acquisition law, setting aside Odisha's acquisition for a mining project. This judgment reinforces tribal land rights and impacts land acquisition and environmental governance policies.

The Supreme Court invalidated Odisha’s mining land acquisition for lacking mandatory Gram Sabha consent, affirming tribal land protections under the 2013 Act.

Why is this in the news?

On November 19, 2026, the Supreme Court quashed Odisha's land acquisition for Dalmia Cement’s mining project, emphasizing mandatory Gram Sabha consent under the 2013 Act.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court rule regarding tribal land acquisition?

  • Mandatory Gram Sabha consent. The Court ruled that prior consent of the concerned Gram Sabha is mandatory for land acquisition in Scheduled Areas under the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013.
  • Set aside Odisha acquisition. It set aside the land acquisition notifications and orders related to Odisha’s Sundargarh district mining project by Dalmia Cement Bharat Limited as unsustainable.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this ruling matter for tribal rights and land laws?

  • Affirms tribal land protections. The judgment reinforces tribal communities' control over their land by requiring Gram Sabha approval before acquisition, strengthening protections in Scheduled Areas.
  • Clarifies legal requirements. It clarifies that prior consent of at least 80% of affected families is mandatory and must be obtained alongside the Social Impact Assessment study, not as a mere formality.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What was the background of the Odisha land acquisition case?

  • Mining lease and land acquisition. Odisha government granted a mining lease to Dalmia Cement for 893.55 hectares, extended until 2040, and sought to acquire 273.57 hectares of land across three villages for surface rights.
  • High Court ruling and challenge. The Orissa High Court upheld the acquisition without prior consent, reasoning it was a State acquisition and consent was a continuing process, but this was challenged in the Supreme Court.

Back to basics: Land Acquisition and Gram Sabha Consent

The Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013 mandates that land acquisition in Scheduled Areas requires prior consent of the Gram Sabha, the village assembly of tribal communities. This protects tribal land rights by ensuring community approval before acquisition, especially for projects affecting their habitat and livelihood.
