RBI Conducts Open Market Operation Sale of Government Securities

The Reserve Bank of India conducted an Open Market Operation (OMO) sale of government securities on October 13, 2026, accepting ₹25,000 crore in bids. This auction influences liquidity and interest rates, making it important for understanding monetary policy and fiscal management in the Indian economy.

RBI's OMO sale of ₹25,000 crore government securities on October 13, 2026, is a key monetary policy tool affecting liquidity and interest rates.

Why is this in the news?

RBI conducted an OMO sale of government securities on October 13, 2026, accepting ₹25,000 crore to manage liquidity and influence interest rates.

What is the RBI's Open Market Operation sale?

  • Definition. Open Market Operation (OMO) is when RBI sells government securities to absorb liquidity from the banking system.
  • Recent auction details. On October 13, 2026, RBI notified an aggregate amount of ₹25,000 crore for OMO sale and accepted bids worth ₹25,000 crore from participants.

[1] Reserve Bank of India[2] Reserve Bank of India

Which securities were sold and what were the yields?

  • Securities sold. The auction included multiple government securities with maturities from 2030 to 2034, such as 7.88% GS 2030 and 7.10% GS 2034.
  • Cut-off yields. The cut-off yields ranged from 7.1428% for 7.88% GS 2030 to 7.3947% for 7.18% GS 2033.

[1] Reserve Bank of India[2] Reserve Bank of India

How does the OMO sale impact the economy?

  • Liquidity management. By selling government securities, RBI absorbs excess liquidity from the banking system, which can tighten money supply.
  • Interest rates influence. OMO sales can push up yields on government securities, influencing overall interest rates in the economy.
  • Fiscal and monetary coordination. OMO is a tool to manage government borrowing and liquidity without disrupting fiscal policy.

[1] Reserve Bank of India[2] Reserve Bank of India

What was the participation and acceptance in the auction?

  • Bids received. Participants bid for ₹55,518 crore (face value) against the notified ₹25,000 crore, showing strong demand.
  • Acceptance. RBI accepted the full notified amount of ₹25,000 crore, partially allotting bids at cut-off prices.

Back to basics: Open Market Operations

Open Market Operations are a monetary policy tool used by the central bank to regulate liquidity in the economy by buying or selling government securities. Selling securities absorbs liquidity, which can increase interest rates, while buying injects liquidity, lowering interest rates. This helps the central bank control inflation and stabilize the economy.
