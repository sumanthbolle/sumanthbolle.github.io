Cyclone Akvan Forecasted to Hit Andhra Coast as Very Severe Storm

Cyclone Akvan is expected to make landfall between Nellore and Tuni in Andhra Pradesh by noon on November 20, 2026, as a very severe cyclonic storm with wind speeds of 120-130 kmph gusting to 145 kmph. This forecast is crucial for understanding disaster preparedness and regional climate impact for UPSC aspirants.

Cyclone Akvan is predicted to intensify and strike Andhra Pradesh's coast between Nellore and Tuni as a very severe storm on November 20, 2026.

Why is this in the news?

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) forecast on November 16, 2026, warned that Cyclone Akvan will intensify and make landfall on the Andhra coast as a very severe cyclonic storm on November 20.

What is the forecast for Cyclone Akvan's landfall?

  • Landfall location. Cyclone Akvan is expected to cross the Andhra Pradesh coast between Nellore and Tuni by noon on November 20, 2026.
  • Storm intensity. The cyclone will be a very severe cyclonic storm with maximum sustained wind speeds of 120-130 kmph, gusting to 145 kmph at landfall.
  • Movement and intensification. The system is moving north-northwestwards over the Bay of Bengal, intensifying from a deep depression to a very severe cyclonic storm between November 16 and November 20.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary[2] The Indian Express · Secondary

What are the expected weather impacts and warnings?

  • Rainfall and wind. Extremely heavy rainfall exceeding 204 millimetres in 24 hours is expected over coastal Andhra Pradesh starting November 19, with squally winds of 50-60 kmph increasing to gale force as the cyclone intensifies.
  • Sea conditions. Sea conditions are forecast to be rough to very rough until November 17, worsening thereafter as the cyclone approaches the coast.
  • Pre-cyclone watch. The IMD has issued a pre-cyclone watch for Andhra Pradesh and adjoining south Odisha coasts, advising district administrations to be on high alert.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary[2] The Indian Express · Secondary

Why does Cyclone Akvan matter for disaster management and regional preparedness?

  • Fishermen advisories. Fishermen have been advised to stay ashore until November 20 to avoid risks from the cyclone, especially as previous cyclones caused significant damage to fisheries.
  • Historical context. The Krishna district, including Machilipatnam, has experienced devastating cyclones in the past, making preparedness critical.
  • Economic impact. Cyclone Arnab caused ₹307 lakh in damages to fisheries in Visakhapatnam district, highlighting the economic vulnerability of coastal communities.

Back to basics: Cyclone and Disaster Management

A cyclone is a large-scale air mass that rotates around a strong center of low atmospheric pressure, often causing heavy rain and strong winds. Disaster management involves preparing for, responding to, and recovering from such natural calamities to minimize damage to life, property, and livelihoods. Early warnings and coordinated evacuation plans are key components of effective disaster management.
