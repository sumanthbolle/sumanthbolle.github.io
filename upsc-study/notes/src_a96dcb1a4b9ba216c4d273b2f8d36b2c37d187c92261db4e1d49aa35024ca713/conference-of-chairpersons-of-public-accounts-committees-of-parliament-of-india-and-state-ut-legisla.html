A Public Accounts Committee is a legislative committee that scrutinises public expenditure and audit findings, examines whether expenditure conforms to authorised purposes, and reviews departmental responses and follow-up. CAG reports are referred to PACs after being tabled in the legislature.[3]

Background

  • The PAC examines public expenditure and audit-related matters through legislative scrutiny. CAG audit reports, once tabled, are referred to PACs, which examine selected findings and recommendations and seek departmental responses.[3]
  • The resolution emerged from the 3–4 November 2026 conference of chairpersons and members of Parliament and State/UT PACs, whose deliberations focused on audit, accountability and good governance.
  • The proposed mechanism is a follow-up chain: audit observation → PAC examination → recommendation → Action Taken Note → review of pending compliance. The resolution does not itself establish a binding statutory enforcement regime.

India implications

  • A common digital portal could facilitate exchange of procedural innovations between Union and State/UT PACs, supporting cooperative legislative oversight.
  • State/UT PACs may require stronger secretariat capacity, specialised training and analytical tools to examine complex technical and financial issues.
  • Closer collaboration with the C&AG may help PACs focus legislative attention on high-risk governance areas, while preserving legislative due process.
  • Standardised timelines for Action Taken Notes could make audit follow-up more visible and reduce accumulation of pending observations, subject to implementation.

Way forward

  • Set realistic, publicly trackable timelines for departmental Action Taken Notes while preserving committee discretion in complex cases.
  • Operationalise interoperable digital tracking with audit trails, role-based access and protection of confidential legislative material.
  • Strengthen PAC Secretariats through specialised training, research support and analytical tools.
  • Use risk-based selection of audit observations so limited committee time targets financially and administratively significant issues.
