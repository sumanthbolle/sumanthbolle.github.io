SC Allows Petition on Pre-Loaded Caste Menu for Census 2027

The Supreme Court permitted the OBC Federation of India to approach the Registrar General for including a pre-loaded drop-down menu of OBC castes in Census 2027 digital tools. This development highlights constitutional and policy debates on caste enumeration methods relevant for social justice and governance.

The Supreme Court allowed a petition seeking a pre-loaded OBC caste menu in Census 2027 to improve accuracy in caste data collection.

Why is this in the news?

On October 28, 2026, the Supreme Court allowed the OBC Federation of India to petition the Registrar General for a pre-loaded drop-down caste menu in Census 2027 digital tools.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court decide regarding the caste menu in Census 2027?

  • Permission to petition. The Supreme Court allowed the OBC Federation of India to approach the Registrar General and Census Commissioner with a request for a pre-loaded drop-down menu of recognised OBC castes in the digital Census 2027 tools.
  • Caste enumeration phase. Caste data is collected in the second phase of the Census, and the petition was filed as this phase was about to commence.
  • Court's stance on policy. The Court observed that decisions on granular Census operations are part of policy-making and not constitutional mandates.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is the pre-loaded drop-down menu for caste important?

  • Constitutional recognition. Article 342A recognises Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs), which include OBCs, alongside Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
  • Budget and scale. Over ₹11,000 crore has been budgeted for Census 2027, highlighting the scale of the operation where accurate caste data is crucial.
  • Current data entry method. Currently, except for SCs and STs, caste details are entered manually in an open text field, which can cause inaccuracies.
  • Past enumeration issues. The petition cited inaccuracies in caste enumeration during the 2011 Socio-Economic and Caste Census due to open-ended data entry.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the implications for policy and governance?

  • Policy vs constitutional mandate. The Court noted that caste enumeration details are governed by policy decisions rather than constitutional provisions.
  • Representation to Census authorities. The Court disposed of the case by directing that the petition be considered as a representation by Census authorities.

Back to basics: Census and Caste Enumeration

The Census of India collects demographic data including caste to aid policy formulation and social justice measures. Caste enumeration is typically done in phases, with Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes having predefined lists, while Other Backward Classes (OBCs) are recognised under constitutional provisions. Accurate caste data helps in targeted welfare schemes and affirmative action. The use of digital tools and data entry methods affects the reliability of this data.
