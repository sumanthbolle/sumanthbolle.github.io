Supreme Court Collegium Recommends Four Judges as High Court Chief Justices

The Supreme Court Collegium has proposed four judges for appointment as Chief Justices of the High Courts of Gujarat, Delhi, Telangana, and Uttarakhand. This update is relevant for understanding the judicial appointment process and the separation of powers in India.

The Supreme Court Collegium recommended four judges for High Court Chief Justiceships, reflecting the judiciary's role in appointments under the Constitution.

Why is this in the news?

On October 26, 2026, the Supreme Court Collegium recommended four judges for appointment as Chief Justices of various High Courts.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What appointments did the Supreme Court Collegium propose?

  • Four Chief Justices recommended. The Collegium proposed Justice Manash Ranjan Pathak for Gujarat High Court, Justice P. Sam Koshy for Delhi High Court, Justice Tapabrata Chakraborty for Telangana High Court, and Justice Sudhir Singh for Uttarakhand High Court.
  • Current and parent courts. Justice Pathak is currently a judge at Gujarat High Court with Gauhati as his parent court; Justice Koshy serves at Telangana High Court with Chhattisgarh as his parent court; Justices Chakraborty and Singh belong to Calcutta and Patna High Courts, respectively.
  • Additional appointments. The Collegium also approved the appointment of Ajay Sharma and advocate Vaibhav Anant Sugdare as judges of the Bombay High Court.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for the judiciary and UPSC exams?

  • Judicial appointments process. The Supreme Court Collegium plays a key role in recommending judges for High Court Chief Justiceships, illustrating the judiciary's independence in appointments.
  • Separation of powers. These appointments highlight the balance of power between the judiciary and the executive, a critical aspect of constitutional governance.
  • Exam relevance. Understanding the Collegium system and judicial appointments is essential for questions on judiciary structure, appointment mechanisms, and constitutional provisions.

Back to basics: Supreme Court Collegium and Judicial Appointments

The Supreme Court Collegium is a body of senior Supreme Court judges responsible for recommending appointments and transfers of judges in the higher judiciary. It ensures judicial independence by limiting executive interference in appointments. The Collegium system is a key feature of the separation of powers in India's constitutional framework.
