Supreme Court Assures No Prosecution for Deleted Voters Filing Form 6

The Supreme Court orally assured that voters deleted during Maharashtra's Special Intensive Revision (SIR) will not be prosecuted for submitting Form 6 to re-register. This clarification is important for understanding judicial safeguards in electoral processes and voter protection.

The Supreme Court assured that deleted voters filing Form 6 for re-registration will not face prosecution.

Why is this in the news?

On October 26, 2026, the Supreme Court orally assured no prosecution for deleted voters submitting Form 6 during a hearing on a plea challenging Maharashtra's voter deletion in the Special Intensive Revision.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the issue with deleted voters in Maharashtra's SIR?

  • Deleted voters in SIR. Thousands of voters in Mumbai and Pune were marked absent and deleted from the electoral roll due to home redevelopment projects shifting residents out.
  • Effect of redevelopment. About 3,500 redevelopment projects in Mumbai and 1,500 in Pune caused many voters to be deleted during the Special Intensive Revision.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why are deleted voters submitting Form 6 and what is the concern?

  • Form 6 submission. Deleted voters are compelled to submit Form 6, which is an application to register as a new voter, effectively representing themselves as fresh voters.
  • Legal concern. Representing oneself as a fresh voter when previously registered is an offence and could lead to prosecution.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court assure regarding prosecution?

  • No prosecution assurance. The Supreme Court orally assured that no voter deleted during the SIR will be prosecuted merely for applying for re-registration by filing Form 6.
  • Judicial response. Chief Justice Surya Kant responded to concerns by stating, 'Do not worry. Nobody will be prosecuted.'

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the suggestions for improving the process?

  • New form for deleted voters. Senior advocate Arvind Datar suggested notifying a new form exclusively for deleted voters in the SIR to avoid misrepresentation as fresh voters.
  • Urgency of hearing. The court agreed to urgently take up the plea challenging voter deletion on November 5.

Back to basics: Electoral Rolls and Form 6

Electoral rolls are official lists of voters maintained by the Election Commission. Form 6 is the application used by citizens to register as new voters or to re-register if their names have been deleted or omitted. The Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is a process to update electoral rolls comprehensively. Filing Form 6 is essential for ensuring voter inclusion but must be managed to prevent offences like duplicate registration.
