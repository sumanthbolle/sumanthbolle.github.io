DRI Foils Wildlife Trafficking Bid in Assam Recovering Elephant Tusks

The Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI) intercepted an illegal wildlife trafficking attempt near Guwahati, Assam, recovering two elephant tusks weighing 3.7 kg. This incident highlights the enforcement of wildlife protection laws and the ongoing challenges in curbing illegal trade in endangered species.

DRI seized two elephant tusks in Assam under the Wildlife Protection Act, demonstrating India's commitment to combating wildlife trafficking.

Why is this in the news?

On 5th November 2026, DRI officers intercepted suspects near Sonapur Toll Gate, Assam, recovering two elephant tusks and preventing illegal wildlife trafficking.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What happened during the wildlife trafficking interception?

  • DRI operation details. On 5th November 2026 near Sonapur Toll Gate, about 27 km from Guwahati, DRI officers acting on intelligence intercepted five suspects on two two-wheelers carrying a bag suspected to contain elephant tusks.
  • Suspects' response. When intercepted, the suspects tried to flee while brandishing a Khukri and a Dao, but abandoned the bag and escaped into a nearby village.
  • Recovery and seizure. The abandoned bag contained two elephant tusks measuring 29 inches and 30 inches in length, weighing approximately 3.7 kg, which were seized under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this matter for wildlife protection and law enforcement?

  • Legal protection of elephant tusks. Elephant tusks are protected under Schedule I of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, and Appendix I of CITES, prohibiting trade and commercial dealings in such articles.
  • Role of DRI. DRI uses intelligence-led enforcement and coordination with other agencies to combat wildlife trafficking and illegal trade in protected species.
  • Conservation significance. Preventing trafficking of elephant tusks helps protect endangered species and supports biodiversity conservation efforts.

Back to basics: Wildlife Protection Laws in India

The Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, is the primary legislation that protects wild animals, birds, and plants in India. It prohibits hunting and trade of endangered species listed under its schedules. The Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora (CITES) is an international agreement regulating trade in endangered species to ensure their survival.
