Delhi SIR: BJP BLAs’ Voter Deletion Applications Focused on Three Neighbourhoods

In the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of Delhi’s electoral rolls, 796 voter deletion applications filed by BJP-appointed Booth Level Agents (BLAs) targeted mainly three Muslim-majority neighbourhoods in Mustafabad. This raises important questions on electoral roll management and the integrity of voter lists, relevant for both Prelims and Mains.

During Delhi’s SIR, BJP BLAs filed concentrated voter deletion requests against Muslim electors in three Mustafabad neighbourhoods, highlighting electoral roll scrutiny issues.

Why is this in the news?

On November 10, 2026, The Hindu reported that 796 voter deletion applications filed by BJP BLAs in Mustafabad were concentrated in three Muslim-majority neighbourhoods during Delhi’s Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What happened in the Delhi SIR voter deletion applications?

  • Concentrated applications. 796 Form 7 applications seeking deletion of voter names were filed by 20 BJP-appointed BLAs, mostly focused on 25 polling booths in Mustafabad Assembly constituency.
  • Targeted neighbourhoods. The applications were concentrated in three neighbourhoods—Nehru Vihar, Dayalpur, and Chandu Nagar—chosen for their sizeable Muslim populations.
  • Focus on Muslim electors. All deletion requests targeted Muslim electors, raising concerns about selective scrutiny of voters.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for electoral integrity and governance?

  • Electoral roll management. The concentration of deletion applications in specific areas questions the impartiality and fairness in updating electoral rolls during the SIR process.
  • Potential communal bias. Targeting Muslim-majority neighbourhoods for voter deletion applications may affect the representation and voting rights of a community, raising constitutional and democratic concerns.
  • Role of BLAs. BJP-appointed BLAs filed the applications, but the party claimed it did not direct these actions, highlighting issues in party accountability and election oversight.

Back to basics: Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of Electoral Rolls

The Special Intensive Revision is a process conducted by the Election Commission to update and verify electoral rolls before elections, ensuring accuracy in voter lists. It involves scrutiny of entries, deletion of ineligible voters, and inclusion of new eligible voters to maintain electoral integrity.

Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the BJP claimed it did not direct the filing of these deletion applications, despite them being submitted by BJP-appointed BLAs.
