Supreme Court mandates swift aid and rehabilitation for bonded labourers

The Supreme Court of India ordered the Centre and States to ensure financial assistance is given within seven days to rescued bonded labourers and mandated uniform procedures for their rehabilitation. This judicial directive highlights the continuing constitutional obligation to eradicate bonded labour and rehabilitate survivors, a key issue in social justice and labour rights.

The Supreme Court directed time-bound financial aid and a revised rehabilitation process for rescued bonded labourers, emphasizing the State's ongoing responsibility beyond their release.

Why is this in the news?

On November 2, 2026, the Supreme Court issued directions to the Centre and States for time-bound financial aid and rehabilitation of rescued bonded labourers, addressing delays and institutional gaps.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court order regarding bonded labourers?

  • Time-bound financial aid. The Court ordered that financial assistance must be disbursed within seven days of a bonded labourer's release to ensure immediate support.
  • Revised rehabilitation scheme. It directed the Centre to revise the existing rehabilitation scheme within three months to increase financial aid and provide subsistence support during delays.
  • Uniform procedure and SOP. A revised standard operating procedure (SOP) formulated on May 14 must be implemented by all States and Union Territories within six months, with supplementary guidelines from the Centre in consultation with the NHRC.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for bonded labour eradication?

  • Continuing constitutional obligation. The Court emphasized that the State's responsibility does not end with release; identification, release, and rehabilitation are interconnected stages requiring effective enforcement.
  • Addressing institutional shortcomings. The ruling highlights delays in financial assistance and gaps in identification, rescue, and rehabilitation processes, including inter-State coordination and language barriers.
  • Strengthening enforcement mechanisms. States must designate nodal officers, establish coordination mechanisms, and constitute vigilance committees at district and sub-divisional levels to monitor and enforce the Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How will the Supreme Court monitor implementation?

  • Compliance timelines. The Centre must file a compliance affidavit within four months, while States and Union Territories have six months to comply and two additional weeks to submit their affidavits.
  • Ongoing judicial oversight. The petitions remain pending for further consideration after seven months to ensure enforcement of the Court’s directions.
  • Accountability for delays. Any unjustified failure to meet statutory duties or timelines will be subject to scrutiny and legal action by supervisory authorities.

Back to basics: Bonded Labour and Rehabilitation

Bonded labour is a form of forced labour where a person is compelled to work to repay a debt, often under exploitative conditions. The Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976, prohibits this practice and mandates identification, release, and rehabilitation of bonded labourers. Rehabilitation includes financial aid, livelihood support, and social integration to ensure survivors can live with dignity and avoid re-exploitation.
