Calls for Judicious Use of Water and Electricity Amid El Nino Concerns

Telangana Deputy Chief Minister Bhatti Vikramarka urged collective efforts to conserve water and electricity due to dry weather and rising power demand linked to El Nino. This highlights the challenges of resource management during climate variability, relevant for UPSC environment and geography topics.

El Nino-related dry conditions in Telangana have led to calls for water and electricity conservation to manage rising demand and declining reservoir levels.

Why is this in the news?

On October 24, 2026, Telangana's Deputy Chief Minister Bhatti Vikramarka called for judicious use of water and electricity amid concerns over El Nino-induced dry weather and increasing power demand.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the current resource challenges due to El Nino in Telangana?

  • Rising power demand. Peak power demand in Telangana has increased from 6,648 MW in 2014 to 20,018 MW now, with daily electricity consumption rising about 37% from 245.35 million units last October to 335.05 million units this October.
  • Declining water reservoirs. The state's major reservoirs with a combined capacity of 470.22 TMC currently hold only 178.60 TMC of water, and nearly 40% of its 44,034 tanks have less than one-fourth of their capacity filled.
  • Groundwater shortages. Approximately 1.5 million out of three million agricultural pump sets in Telangana face groundwater shortages, impacting irrigation and farming.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What measures are being urged to manage these challenges?

  • Conservation appeals. Deputy Chief Minister Bhatti Vikramarka called for collective efforts to conserve water and electricity, urging farmers in groundwater-deficient areas to avoid operating motors to prevent damage.
  • Government response. The Telangana government, led by Chief Minister A. Revanth Reddy, has formed a Cabinet Sub-Committee to address El Nino-related challenges and is expanding transmission infrastructure and building new substations to manage power demand.
  • Balancing livelihoods and conservation. The government plans to prioritise water conservation while protecting the livelihoods of farmers, labourers, and rural communities.

Back to basics: El Nino and its impact on Indian climate

El Nino is a periodic climatic phenomenon characterized by the warming of sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern Pacific Ocean, which affects global weather patterns. In India, El Nino often leads to weaker monsoon rains, causing droughts and water shortages. This impacts agriculture, water reservoirs, and power generation, making resource management critical during such events.
