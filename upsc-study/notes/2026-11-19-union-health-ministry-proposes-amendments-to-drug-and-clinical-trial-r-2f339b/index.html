Union Health Ministry Proposes Amendments to Drug and Clinical Trial Rules

The Union Ministry of Health and Family Welfare has proposed amendments to the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019 and the Drugs Rules, 1945 to strengthen regulatory oversight of antimicrobials, bulk drugs, and certain drug categories. These changes aim to improve drug quality, safety, and supply chain accountability, which are crucial for public health and combating antimicrobial resistance.

The Health Ministry's proposed amendments enhance central regulation of antimicrobials, introduce separate licensing for bulk drug wholesale, and provide regulatory flexibility for specific drug categories.

Why is this in the news?

On 19 November 2026, the Union Ministry of Health and Family Welfare issued draft notifications proposing amendments to drug and clinical trial rules to improve regulation of antimicrobials, bulk drugs, and other drug categories.

What changes are proposed for antimicrobial regulation?

  • Inclusion under New Drugs. The Ministry proposes to include 'antimicrobials' in the definition of 'New Drug' under Rule 2(1)(w)(v) of the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019, to bring them under central regulatory oversight.
  • Reason for amendment. This aims to address concerns about manufacturing licenses being granted without adequate scrutiny like bioavailability and stability studies, which can affect antimicrobial quality and contribute to antimicrobial resistance (AMR).
  • Regulatory impact. Manufacturers would need permission from the Central Licensing Authority before applying for a manufacturing license from the State Licensing Authority, strengthening central oversight to ensure safety and efficacy.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How will bulk drug wholesale licensing change?

  • Separate licensing framework. Amendments to the Drugs Rules, 1945 propose a distinct application form (Form 19-BC) and license form (Form 20-BC) specifically for wholesale sale of bulk drugs, separate from formulations.
  • Purpose of change. This creates focused regulatory oversight and greater accountability in the pharmaceutical supply chain, including drugs under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act.
  • Supervision and compliance. Wholesale sale of bulk drugs will require personal supervision by a competent person, and existing licensees will have up to 12 months after amendment publication to comply.

[2] Press Information Bureau

What regulatory flexibility is proposed for certain drug categories?

  • Categories covered. The amendment seeks to provide regulatory flexibility under the revised Schedule M for medicinal gases, empty gelatin capsules, and disinfectant fluids.
  • Rationale. The Drugs Technical Advisory Board noted that some revised Schedule M requirements are not relevant to these categories' manufacturing processes and recommended continuing pre-revision exemptions.
  • Regulatory approach. Licensing authorities can make product-specific decisions based on scientific and practical considerations without diluting quality, safety, or oversight standards.

[3] Press Information Bureau

Why do these amendments matter for public health and governance?

  • Addressing antimicrobial resistance. Stronger regulation of antimicrobials is critical to prevent substandard products that contribute to AMR, a major global health threat.
  • Improving supply chain accountability. Separate licensing for bulk drugs enhances control over drug distribution, including narcotics, reducing risks of misuse and ensuring quality.
  • Balancing regulation and practicality. Providing regulatory flexibility for specific drug categories ensures oversight is relevant and feasible, supporting industry compliance without compromising safety.

Back to basics: Drug Regulatory Framework in India

India regulates drugs through laws like the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and Rules, which set standards for manufacturing, sale, and clinical trials. The Central Licensing Authority oversees approvals for new drugs, while State Licensing Authorities regulate manufacturing licenses. Amendments to these rules can strengthen oversight, ensure drug quality and safety, and address public health challenges like antimicrobial resistance.
