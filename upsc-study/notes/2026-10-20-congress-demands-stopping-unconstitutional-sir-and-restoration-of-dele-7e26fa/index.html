Congress Demands Halt to Unconstitutional SIR and Restoration of Deleted Voters

The Congress party has demanded an immediate stop to the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, calling it unconstitutional due to widespread wrongful deletions of voters. This issue raises important questions about electoral rights and constitutional safeguards relevant for UPSC exams.

The Congress alleges that the SIR process has wrongfully deleted over 9 crore voters, demanding their restoration to protect electoral rights.

Why is this in the news?

On October 19, 2026, Congress general secretary Jairam Ramesh publicly criticized the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls for wrongful voter deletions and demanded its immediate suspension and voter restoration.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the controversy surrounding the Special Intensive Revision (SIR)?

  • Wrongful voter deletions. The SIR process has resulted in widespread wrongful deletions of voters, particularly in the Absent, Shifted, Dead, and Duplicate (ASDD) categories, with at least 100 voters wrongly deleted in just two polling booths in Delhi’s Okhla constituency.
  • Scale of deletions. Over 9 crore voters have been deleted across the country in the ASDD categories alone, raising concerns about genuine voters being denied their right to vote.
  • Failure of corrective measures. Many wrongfully deleted voters remain unaware of the special camps and door-to-door visits promised by the Election Commission of India (ECI), and have not been contacted by Booth Level Officers (BLOs).
  • Political response. Congress has termed the SIR illegal and unconstitutional, demanding its immediate suspension and restoration of all legitimately deleted voters.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for electoral rights and constitutional safeguards?

  • Right to vote at risk. Wrongful deletions in the electoral roll threaten the fundamental right of citizens to vote, undermining democratic participation.
  • Constitutional concerns. The Congress alleges that the SIR process violates constitutional provisions by disenfranchising legitimate voters without due process.
  • Role of Election Commission. The ECI’s responsibility includes ensuring accurate electoral rolls and protecting voter rights, but the current situation indicates lapses in implementation and oversight.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the challenges in addressing the SIR-related issues?

  • Disagreement within Election Commission. Reports indicate that election commissioners have objected multiple times to decisions related to the SIR process, reflecting internal disagreements.
  • Need for corrective action. The Congress demands that the ECI itself, not civil society, should take responsibility for clearing the mess created by the SIR.

Back to basics: Electoral Rolls and Voter Rights

Electoral rolls are official lists of eligible voters maintained by the Election Commission to ensure free and fair elections. Accurate rolls are essential to uphold the right to vote, a fundamental democratic right. Revisions like the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) aim to update these rolls by removing ineligible voters but must be conducted transparently and constitutionally to avoid wrongful disenfranchisement.

What happens next?

  • Suspend SIR process. Congress demands an immediate halt to the SIR process to prevent further wrongful deletions.
  • Restore deleted voters. All voters illegitimately removed from the rolls must be restored to protect their voting rights.
  • Election Commission accountability. The ECI should take direct responsibility for correcting errors and ensuring voter awareness about special camps and door-to-door verification.
