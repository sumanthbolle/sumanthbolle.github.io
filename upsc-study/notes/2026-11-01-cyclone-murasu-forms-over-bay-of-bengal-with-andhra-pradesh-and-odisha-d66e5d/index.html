Cyclone Murasu Forms Over Bay of Bengal, Andhra Pradesh and Odisha on Alert

Cyclone Murasu has developed over the Bay of Bengal, prompting high alert in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha due to expected adverse weather. This situation highlights the vulnerability of coastal communities, especially fisherfolk, and the importance of disaster management and relief measures.

Cyclone Murasu's formation over the Bay of Bengal has led to warnings and fishing bans in Andhra Pradesh, causing significant financial distress to the coastal fisherfolk.

Why is this in the news?

Cyclone Murasu formed over the Bay of Bengal, leading to high alert in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha, with concerns over the impact on coastal fisherfolk and maritime activities as of October 31, 2026.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current situation with Cyclone Murasu?

  • Cyclone formation. Cyclone Murasu has formed over the Bay of Bengal, prompting weather warnings and high alert status in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha.
  • Impact on fishing. Frequent weather warnings and fishing bans have paralyzed maritime trade and disrupted supply chains, severely affecting the coastal fishing community.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does Cyclone Murasu matter for coastal fisherfolk?

  • Financial crisis. Repeated fishing bans since June have caused cumulative losses exceeding ₹36 crore for coastal Andhra Pradesh's fisherfolk.
  • Livelihood disruption. Cyclone Arnab, a previous storm, caused a direct trade loss of nearly ₹20 crore due to a six-day fishing ban, with over 60% of the fishing fleet remaining anchored during adverse weather.
  • Wider economic effects. Low prices for commercial fish varieties have further hurt vendors, auctioneers, ice suppliers, and transport operators, compounding the economic distress.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What relief measures are being sought for affected fisherfolk?

  • Demand for compensation. Fisherfolk organisations have petitioned for 100% financial compensation for damaged gear and craft affecting 3.32 lakh active marine fishermen statewide.
  • Subsistence allowance. They also demand a daily subsistence allowance of ₹600 per fisherman for seven days of forced idleness due to fishing bans.
  • Relief fund provisions. The petitioners argue compensation should be aligned with the expanded Chief Minister’s Relief Fund provisions under G.O. Ms. No. 1 (2025) rather than the more restrictive State Disaster Response Fund limits.

Back to basics: Cyclones and Disaster Management

Cyclones are intense low-pressure systems that form over warm ocean waters, bringing strong winds and heavy rains, often causing damage to coastal areas. Disaster management involves preparedness, early warning systems, relief, and rehabilitation efforts to reduce the impact of such natural calamities on vulnerable communities like fisherfolk.
