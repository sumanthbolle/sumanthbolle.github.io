India implications

  • Higher value realisation can support incomes and rural livelihoods if small producers gain access to aggregation, finance, infrastructure and markets.
  • Value-added fish products can strengthen food and nutritional security, employment, exports and entrepreneurship among women’s groups, youth, cooperatives and small enterprises.
  • Mobile marketing and digital tools may shorten producer-consumer linkages, but their benefits depend on connectivity, digital literacy, food safety and reliable logistics.
  • Sustainable expansion requires balancing productivity and export orientation with aquatic ecosystem health, disease control, responsible aquaculture and fisher welfare.

Way forward

  • Build shared cold-chain, testing, processing and logistics infrastructure accessible to small producers.
  • Strengthen FFPOs, cooperatives and women-led enterprises for aggregation, bargaining power, branding and market access.
  • Expand affordable institutional finance, working capital, insurance, skills and digital literacy for fishers and fish farmers.
  • Use interoperable traceability, quality standards and food-safety systems without imposing disproportionate compliance costs on microenterprises.
