RBI Cancels Gandhinagar Leasing Registration and Extends Abhyudaya Bank Board Supersession

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) cancelled the Certificate of Registration of Gandhinagar Leasing and Finance Ltd. on October 29, 2026, stopping it from operating as a Non-Banking Financial Institution. Simultaneously, RBI extended the supersession of the Board of Directors of Abhyudaya Co-operative Bank Ltd., Mumbai, for another year from November 24, 2026, due to ongoing governance concerns.

RBI exercised its regulatory powers to cancel Gandhinagar Leasing's registration and extended Abhyudaya Bank's board supersession to address governance issues and protect public interest.

Why is this in the news?

On November 9, 2026, the RBI announced the cancellation of Gandhinagar Leasing’s registration and extended the supersession of Abhyudaya Bank’s board, highlighting its regulatory role in banking governance.

What actions did the RBI take regarding Gandhinagar Leasing and Abhyudaya Bank?

  • Cancellation of Gandhinagar Leasing. RBI cancelled the Certificate of Registration of Gandhinagar Leasing and Finance Ltd. on October 29, 2026, under Section 45-IA(6) of the RBI Act, 1934, prohibiting it from transacting as a Non-Banking Financial Institution.
  • Extension of Abhyudaya Bank board supersession. RBI extended the supersession of the Board of Directors of Abhyudaya Co-operative Bank Ltd. for one more year from November 24, 2026, citing material concerns over poor governance standards.
  • Administrator and advisors continued. Shri Satya Prakash Pathak continues as Administrator of Abhyudaya Bank, assisted by the Committee of Advisors comprising Shri Venkatesh Hegde, Shri Devendra Kumar, and Shri Suhas Gokhale.

Why does RBI cancel registrations and supersede bank boards?

  • Regulatory authority. RBI exercises its powers under the Reserve Bank of India Act and Banking Regulation Act to regulate NBFCs and banks to ensure financial stability and protect depositors.
  • Addressing governance issues. Supersession of a bank’s board occurs when RBI identifies material concerns such as poor governance standards that could threaten the bank’s sound functioning.
  • Public interest safeguard. Such regulatory actions are taken in the public interest to maintain trust in the financial system and prevent risks to depositors and the economy.

  • Legal provisions invoked. Sections 45-IA(6) of the RBI Act and Section 36AAA read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act are key legal provisions governing these actions.

Back to basics: RBI's Regulatory Powers over Banks and NBFCs

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is the central banking authority responsible for regulating banks and Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) in India. It issues Certificates of Registration to NBFCs allowing them to operate. RBI can cancel these certificates if the NBFC fails to comply with regulations. For banks, RBI can supersede the Board of Directors if governance or operational issues threaten the bank's stability. These powers help maintain financial stability and protect depositors.
