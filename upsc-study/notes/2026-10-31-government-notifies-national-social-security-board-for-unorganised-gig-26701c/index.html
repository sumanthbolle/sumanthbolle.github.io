Government Notifies National Social Security Board for Unorganised, Gig & Platform Workers

The Ministry of Labour & Employment has officially constituted the National Social Security Board for Unorganised Workers and Gig & Platform Workers under the Labour Codes. This board aims to design and monitor welfare schemes for millions of informal sector workers, marking a shift towards integrating gig and platform workers into formal social security frameworks.

The National Social Security Board was notified to bridge the social security gap for unorganised, gig, and platform workers under the Labour Codes.

Why is this in the news?

On October 30, 2026, the Ministry of Labour & Employment notified the constitution of the National Social Security Board for Unorganised Workers and Gig & Platform Workers under the Labour Codes.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the National Social Security Board for Unorganised, Gig & Platform Workers?

  • Board constitution. The Board is established under the Code on Social Security and includes representatives from Central and State Governments, aggregator companies, employers, civil society, workers' unions, and gig & platform associations.
  • Leadership and meetings. It is presided over by the Union Minister for Labour & Employment and meets at least thrice a year following prescribed rules of procedure.
  • Functions. The Board recommends welfare schemes, advises on administration of the Code, monitors social welfare schemes, reviews record-keeping and expenditure, and performs other government-assigned functions.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does the National Social Security Board matter for workers and governance?

  • Addressing informal workforce needs. It marks a historic shift by formally integrating digital gig and platform workers into India's social security framework alongside traditional unorganised workers.
  • Social security gap. Despite claims of increased social security coverage from 19% in 2015 to 64.3% in 2025, over 92% of India's workforce in unorganised and gig sectors remain largely uncovered.
  • Federal concerns. Labour being a Concurrent List subject requires cooperation between Union and States, but the Board's highly centralised structure with limited State and opposition trade union representation may hinder optimal outcomes.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What challenges and criticisms exist regarding the Board?

  • Centralised control. The Union Government controls both Boards, chaired by the Union Minister for Labour, with members nominated primarily by the Union Government.
  • Limited representation. The Boards lack significant representation from Central Trade Unions of the Opposition and State Governments ruled by the Opposition.
  • Unaddressed past recommendations. Issues raised by the National Commission for Enterprises in the Unorganised Sector nearly two decades ago remain unresolved, and recent reports have flagged anti-federal and anti-worker characteristics needing amendment.

Back to basics: Social Security for Informal Workers

Social security refers to protection provided by society to individuals to ensure access to healthcare and income security during old age, unemployment, sickness, or other vulnerabilities. Informal sector workers, including unorganised, gig, and platform workers, often lack formal employment benefits, making targeted welfare schemes necessary to bridge this gap.

What happens next?

  • Inclusive representation. The Union Government should involve trade unions and State Governments, especially those in opposition, to create more inclusive and effective social security mechanisms.
  • Address federal concerns. Amendments are needed to address anti-federal and anti-worker aspects of the Social Security Code to improve cooperation between Union and States.
