TGCSB alerts NBFCs and fintech firms on AI-driven cyberattacks

The Telangana Cyber Security Bureau (TGCSB) warned NBFCs, fintech platforms, and banks about increasing AI-assisted cyberattacks targeting payment and loan disbursement systems. This alert is crucial for UPSC aspirants to understand cybersecurity threats in the financial sector and the regulatory responses to protect digital payment infrastructure.

TGCSB has issued a warning to financial institutions about AI-enabled cyberattacks exploiting payment systems, urging enhanced preventive cybersecurity measures.

Why is this in the news?

On November 12, 2026, TGCSB issued an advisory to NBFCs, fintech firms, and banks about AI-assisted cyberattacks on payment systems and loan disbursement platforms.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the AI-assisted cyber threats to payment systems?

  • Targeted institutions. Smaller NBFCs with weaker cybersecurity systems are primarily targeted by fraudsters using AI-assisted cyberattacks.
  • Attack methodology. Attackers study internal processes for 10 to 12 days to identify vulnerabilities like whitelisted IDs and payment API credentials, then exploit these to make fraudulent requests appear genuine.
  • Fraud techniques. Automated scripts initiate payout requests outside normal batch windows, split payments into smaller amounts, and rapidly transfer money through mule accounts to evade detection.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What preventive measures has TGCSB recommended?

  • Proactive security focus. Financial institutions should prevent fraud at the source by detecting suspicious activity in real time and disrupting fund movement through mule accounts.
  • Technical safeguards. Institutions are advised to conduct regular Vulnerability Assessment and Penetration Testing (VAPT), secure payment APIs, deploy real-time fraud detection, and implement transaction velocity checks and limits.
  • Response protocols. In case of an attack, institutions should switch to manual operations, freeze accounts, clear pending API calls, and resume automation only after patching vulnerabilities.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How should institutions report and respond to cyber incidents?

  • Immediate reporting. Suspected fraud must be reported immediately via the 1930 helpline, the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal, CERT-In within six hours, and the RBI through the DAKSH portal.
  • Coordination. Institutions should alert beneficiary banks and the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) to coordinate responses.
  • Record keeping. Complete records of all high-risk payouts should be maintained for investigation and audit.

Back to basics: Cybersecurity in Financial Systems

Cybersecurity involves protecting digital financial infrastructure from unauthorized access and fraud. Payment systems use APIs and automated processes to facilitate transactions, which require robust security to prevent exploitation. Vulnerability Assessment and Penetration Testing (VAPT) are methods to identify and fix security weaknesses. Real-time fraud detection systems monitor transactions to detect suspicious patterns and prevent financial losses.
