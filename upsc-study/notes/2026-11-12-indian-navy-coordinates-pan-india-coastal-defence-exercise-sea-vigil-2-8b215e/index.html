Indian Navy Leads Sea Vigil-26 and Conducts Exercise with Oman Navy

The Indian Navy is coordinating the fifth edition of the Pan-India Coastal Defence Exercise 'Sea Vigil-26' from 11 to 19 November 2026, covering the entire Indian coastline and Exclusive Economic Zone. Simultaneously, it conducted the 15th bilateral maritime exercise Naseem Al Bahr with the Royal Navy of Oman to enhance interoperability and maritime cooperation.

Sea Vigil-26 tests India's coastal defence readiness nationwide, while the bilateral exercise with Oman strengthens regional maritime partnerships.

Why is this in the news?

The Ministry of Defence announced on 11 November 2026 that the Indian Navy is coordinating Sea Vigil-26 and has conducted the 15th edition of the bilateral maritime exercise Naseem Al Bahr with Oman.

What is Sea Vigil-26 and how is it conducted?

  • Exercise overview. Sea Vigil-26 is the fifth edition of the Pan-India Coastal Defence Exercise held from 11 to 19 November 2026, coordinated by the Indian Navy as the lead agency.
  • Phases of exercise. The exercise is conducted in two phases: the Coastal Defence and Security Readiness Evaluation (CDSRE) from 11 to 17 November, followed by the Sea Phase on 18 and 19 November.
  • Participants and scope. It spans the entire Indian coastline and Exclusive Economic Zone, involving all Coastal States and Union Territories, Indian Navy, Coast Guard, Coastal Security Police, Customs, and other maritime agencies.
  • Purpose and significance. Conceptualised in 2018 after the 26/11 Mumbai attacks, Sea Vigil validates maritime security measures and assesses national preparedness for maritime and coastal defence.
  • Coordination with ministries. The exercise is facilitated by multiple ministries including Defence, Home Affairs, Ports, Shipping and Waterways, Petroleum and Natural Gas, and Fisheries.
  • Link to TROPEX. Sea Vigil serves as a build-up to the Indian Navy's biennial Theatre Level Readiness Operational Exercise (TROPEX), together addressing maritime security from coast to high seas.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the bilateral maritime exercise with Oman?

  • Exercise details. The Indian Navy and Royal Navy of Oman conducted the 15th edition of their bilateral maritime exercise Naseem Al Bahr (Sea Breeze) to enhance interoperability and professional cooperation.
  • Harbour phase activities. Subject Matter Expert Exchanges covered Explosive Ordnance Disposal, Mine Countermeasure Operations, and Hydrography, facilitating knowledge and operational experience sharing.
  • Sea phase activities. Joint operations included Visit, Board, Search and Seizure drills and cross-deck operations between Indian Naval Ship Surat and Royal Navy of Oman Vessel Al-Mu’azzar.
  • Strategic importance. The exercise reflects close maritime partnership and supports India's vision of MAHASAGAR for mutual security and growth across regions.

Back to basics: Maritime Security Exercises

Maritime security exercises are coordinated drills involving naval and maritime agencies to test readiness, interoperability, and response to threats in coastal and offshore waters. They help improve coordination among various agencies and build capacity to protect national maritime interests.

Mains question (15 marks)

Examine the role of Indian Navy’s exercises like Sea Vigil-26 and bilateral drills in strengthening India’s maritime security and regional cooperation.

Examine: Explain how these naval exercises enhance coastal defence and foster international maritime partnerships.

Intro: India conducts naval exercises to enhance maritime security and cooperation.

  • Sea Vigil-26 tests national coastal defence readiness involving multiple agencies across the entire coastline.
  • The exercise validates security measures post-26/11 attacks and prepares for larger operational readiness through TROPEX.
  • Bilateral exercises like Naseem Al Bahr with Oman improve interoperability and professional cooperation in specialized maritime domains.
  • Such exercises support India's strategic vision of regional security and growth under initiatives like MAHASAGAR.

Conclusion: Naval exercises are vital for securing India's maritime interests and strengthening regional partnerships.
