Static anchor

Rewilding is the carefully managed restoration of a species in its natural habitat, including release or augmentation of individuals and monitoring of whether they survive, reproduce and contribute to a self-sustaining wild population.

Background

  • The Great Indian Bustard is a critically endangered grassland bird for which India has pursued conservation breeding and habitat-based recovery.
  • The programme is now moving from building a conservation-breeding population towards rewilding and augmentation in natural habitat.
  • The jumpstart approach places captive-laid eggs in wild nests; accepted eggs are incubated by wild females, allowing chicks to develop in a natural setting.

India implications

  • India can strengthen species recovery by combining ex-situ conservation, assisted reproduction or incubation, and carefully monitored in-situ release.
  • The approach requires coordination among the Union Environment Ministry, state forest departments, scientific institutions and local habitat managers.
  • Rewilding outcomes should be assessed through post-release survival, dispersal, breeding success and threats at release landscapes.
  • Conservation planning should avoid treating release numbers as the sole indicator of recovery; habitat security and ecological functionality remain decisive.

Way forward

  • Adopt phased, scientifically supervised releases rather than treating release as the endpoint.
  • Monitor survival, movement, behaviour, breeding and causes of mortality after release.
  • Protect and restore grassland habitat around release landscapes and address human-induced threats.
  • Coordinate the Union government, state forest departments, scientific institutions and local stakeholders.
