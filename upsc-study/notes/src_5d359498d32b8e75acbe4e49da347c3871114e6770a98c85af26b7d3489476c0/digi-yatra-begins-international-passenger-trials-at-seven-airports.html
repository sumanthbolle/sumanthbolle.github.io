India implications

  • India is testing how digital identity infrastructure can be extended from domestic airport processing to selected international departures.
  • The phased approach can help authorities identify operational, interoperability and passenger-experience issues before wider adoption.
  • Implementation should preserve access for passengers unable or unwilling to use the digital pathway and maintain clear separation between facilitation and statutory immigration functions.
  • The source does not establish specific privacy, cybersecurity or exclusion outcomes; these should therefore be treated as policy questions for evaluation, not official findings.

Way forward

  • Evaluate the trial through measurable indicators such as processing reliability, accessibility, user consent, grievance resolution and continuity during technical failure.
  • Retain non-digital alternatives and ensure that digital facilitation never becomes a barrier to lawful travel.
  • Apply purpose limitation, data minimisation, secure authentication, audit trails and clear responsibility for errors or misuse.
  • Test interoperability among airport systems, airlines and relevant statutory processes before wider expansion.
