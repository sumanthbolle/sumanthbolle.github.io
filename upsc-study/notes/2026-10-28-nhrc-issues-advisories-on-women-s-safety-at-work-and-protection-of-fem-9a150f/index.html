NHRC Advisories on Women’s Safety at Work and Rights of Female Inmates

The National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) issued two advisories in October 2026 focusing on women’s safety at workplaces and public spaces, and the protection of human rights of female inmates and their children. These advisories highlight persistent challenges in women’s safety and call for gender-responsive actions by multiple government bodies.

NHRC’s advisories emphasize gender-sensitive measures to improve women’s safety at work and safeguard rights of female prisoners and their children.

Why is this in the news?

In October 2026, NHRC issued advisories to various Union Ministries, state authorities, and the Railway Board on women’s safety at work and public spaces, and on protecting female inmates’ human rights.

What are the key features of the NHRC advisory on women’s safety at work and public spaces?

  • Recipients of advisory. The advisory was sent to Secretaries of ten Union Ministries, the Chairperson of the Railway Board, and Chief Secretaries/Administrators of all States and Union Territories.
  • Context and concerns. Despite stringent laws, women’s safety at workplaces and public spaces remains a concern, with 4.4 lakh cases of crimes against women reported in 2026 and women’s labour force participation at 34.6%.
  • Focus areas. The advisory highlights eight key areas including awareness on safety and sexual harassment, infrastructure, bystander intervention, police role, survivor support, cybercrime, long-term reforms, and other measures.
  • Implementation and monitoring. NHRC urged authorities to implement recommendations and submit Action Taken Reports within two months.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What does the NHRC advisory on protection of female inmates and their children entail?

  • Targeted authorities. The advisory was issued to nine Union Ministries, Chief Secretaries/Administrators, and Directors General of Police of all States and Union Territories.
  • Focus on vulnerable group. It emphasizes a sensitive, gender-responsive approach to the rights and needs of female prisoners and their children, who are considered more vulnerable.
  • Key areas covered. Ten broad areas include pre-admission and arrest procedures, admission records, child documentation, healthcare, living conditions, hygiene, family contact, legal aid, gender-responsive facilities, rehabilitation, and foreign national inmates.
  • Call for humane treatment. The advisory calls for humane prison conditions, maternal and child healthcare, access to justice, preservation of family ties, protection of children’s interests, and social reintegration opportunities.
  • Follow-up. Authorities are required to report progress within two months to NHRC.

Why do these NHRC advisories matter for civil services preparation?

  • Human rights protection. They illustrate the role of NHRC as a statutory body in safeguarding fundamental rights, especially of vulnerable groups like women workers and female inmates.
  • Gender justice and social issues. The advisories highlight ongoing challenges in women’s safety and rights, relevant to social justice topics in GS1 and GS2.
  • Inter-ministerial coordination. They show how multiple ministries and state authorities must collaborate for effective policy implementation.
  • Monitoring and accountability. The requirement of Action Taken Reports reflects governance mechanisms for transparency and accountability.

Back to basics: Role of NHRC in human rights protection

The National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) is a statutory body responsible for protecting and promoting human rights in India. It issues advisories and monitors implementation of laws and policies to safeguard rights, especially of vulnerable groups. NHRC coordinates with various ministries and state authorities to ensure compliance and accountability through reports and follow-ups.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the NHRC advisories issued in October 2026: 1. The advisory on women’s safety at work was sent to the Chairperson of the Railway Board and Secretaries of ten Union Ministries. 2. The advisory on protection of female inmates was sent only to the Ministry of Home Affairs and state police authorities. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the advisory on female inmates was sent to nine Union Ministries, Chief Secretaries/Administrators, and Directors General of Police of all States and UTs, not only the Ministry of Home Affairs and police.
