RBI Releases Data on Foreign Liabilities of Indian Entities and NBFC Credit Deployment

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) published the provisional results of the 2025-26 Census on Foreign Liabilities and Assets of Indian direct investment entities and the sectoral deployment of credit by Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) as of September 2026. These data provide insights into India's external investment positions and credit flow patterns, relevant for understanding economic growth, financial stability, and policy formulation.

RBI's 2025-26 census details foreign direct investment equity and debt in Indian entities, while NBFC credit data show sectoral growth trends important for economic analysis.

Why is this in the news?

On November 6, 2026, the RBI released the provisional results of the 2025-26 Census on Foreign Liabilities and Assets and data on sectoral credit deployment by NBFCs as of September 2026.

What does the RBI Census on Foreign Liabilities and Assets reveal?

  • Scope of census. The census covers cross-border liabilities and assets of Indian entities including companies, LLPs, AIFs, and partnership firms with inward or outward direct investment.
  • Entities reporting FDI and ODI. A total of 43,123 entities reported foreign direct investment (FDI) and/or overseas direct investment (ODI) as of end-March 2026, with about 97% being unlisted entities holding the major share of FDI equity capital.
  • Equity and debt composition. Non-financial companies accounted for over 90% of FDI equity at face value, and unlisted companies held 96.6% of FDI debt as of March 2026.
  • Inward vs outward investment ratio. The ratio of inward FDI to ODI moderated to 4.2 times by end-March 2026 from 5.5 times a year earlier.
  • Top countries for FDI and ODI. The United States, Mauritius, Singapore, and the United Kingdom were the top sources of inward FDI, while Singapore, the UAE, and the United States were the main destinations for ODI.
  • Sectoral distribution. Manufacturing accounted for the highest share (50.1%) of total outstanding FDI equity capital by market value, followed by services sector with 45%.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What are the trends in NBFC credit deployment?

  • Overall credit growth. NBFC credit grew by 15.4% year-on-year in September 2026, up from 13.3% a year ago.
  • Agriculture credit growth. Credit to agriculture and allied activities recorded a strong growth of 17.8% compared to 5.2% a year earlier.
  • Industry sector credit. Credit growth in the industry sector declined to 7.4% year-on-year, with subdued growth in infrastructure.
  • Services sector credit. Credit growth in services moderated to 17.3% from 23.0% a year ago, with commercial real estate, trade, and transport operators seeing higher credit growth.
  • Retail loans growth. Retail loans grew at 21.9% year-on-year, with accelerated growth in housing loans, loans against gold jewellery, and vehicle loans.

Back to basics: Foreign Direct Investment and NBFC Credit

Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) involves investment from foreign entities into Indian companies or entities, either through equity or debt, impacting India's external financial position. Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) provide credit to various sectors of the economy and their credit deployment patterns influence economic growth and financial stability.
