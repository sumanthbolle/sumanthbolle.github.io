NHAI Standardises Multi-Lane Tolling; Launch of Semicon 2.0 Portal and ECMS Approvals

The National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) has issued guidelines to standardise multi-lane free flow tolling infrastructure on four-lane and above highways to enable barrier-free toll collection. Simultaneously, the Union Minister launched the Semicon 2.0 web portal and announced new approvals under the Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme, marking progress in India's semiconductor and electronics manufacturing ecosystem.

NHAI’s tolling standardisation and Semicon 2.0 initiatives aim to modernise infrastructure and boost domestic electronics manufacturing, supporting economic growth and self-reliance.

Why is this in the news?

On 6 November 2026, NHAI issued guidelines for multi-lane free flow tolling and the Union Minister launched the Semicon 2.0 portal with new electronics manufacturing approvals.

What are the NHAI guidelines for multi-lane tolling?

  • Barrier-free tolling. NHAI has standardised design and infrastructure guidelines to enable Multi-Lane Free Flow (MLFF) tolling on four-lane and above National Highways, allowing vehicles to pass without stopping at toll booths.
  • Design standardisation. The guidelines prescribe uniform layouts, lane configurations, gantry placements, lighting, signage, and road safety features to ensure seamless and safe toll collection.
  • Implementation scope. These guidelines apply to all future and ongoing National Highway projects, with DPR-stage projects incorporating the design and ongoing projects adopting changes contractually.
  • Benefits. The initiative aims to eliminate traffic queues, reduce fuel consumption at toll plazas, and improve safety and traffic flow efficiency.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is Semicon 2.0 and its significance?

  • Programme overview. Semicon 2.0 is the next phase of India's semiconductor mission with a fiscal outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore, focusing on six strategic pillars including chip design, machines and materials, fabs, ATMP/OSAT industry, R&D, and talent development.
  • Web portal launch. The Semicon 2.0 web application portal was launched for applications under Pillars 2, 3, and 4, enabling participation in equipment, materials, and manufacturing expansion.
  • Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS). 12 new applicants were approved under ECMS with a projected investment of ₹7,503 crore, expected production of ₹39,045 crore, and generation of 7,127 direct jobs across seven states.
  • Focus on domestic value chain. The scheme supports manufacturing of components like multi-layer PCBs, relays, heat sinks, specialty ceramics, and supply chain products such as copper foil and cathode active material, expanding domestic electronics manufacturing.

[2] Press Information Bureau

Why do these developments matter for India’s economy and industrial policy?

  • Infrastructure modernisation. NHAI’s tolling standardisation enhances highway efficiency and safety, reducing delays and fuel wastage, which supports economic productivity.
  • Electronics manufacturing growth. Semicon 2.0 and ECMS approvals deepen India’s semiconductor ecosystem, reduce import dependence, and build advanced manufacturing capabilities aligned with Atmanirbhar Bharat.
  • Employment and regional spread. The electronics manufacturing projects are spread across multiple states and sectors, generating significant direct employment and promoting regional industrial development.
  • Strategic self-reliance. These initiatives aim to position India as a globally competitive hub for electronics and semiconductor manufacturing through quality, R&D, and global competitiveness.

Back to basics: Multi-Lane Free Flow Tolling and Semiconductor Ecosystem

Multi-Lane Free Flow (MLFF) tolling is a technology-enabled system that allows vehicles to pass toll plazas without stopping, using electronic toll collection methods to reduce congestion and improve traffic flow. The semiconductor ecosystem involves the design, manufacturing, and supply chain of semiconductor chips and components, which are critical for electronics manufacturing and technological self-reliance.

What happens next?

  • Wider adoption of MLFF. NHAI field offices are directed to incorporate MLFF design requirements in all upcoming and ongoing projects to ensure nationwide uniformity and efficiency.
  • Focus on quality and R&D. Semicon 2.0 emphasizes rigorous project selection based on quality, technological depth, and research to build a globally competitive semiconductor ecosystem.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements: 1. NHAI’s new guidelines mandate barrier-free Multi-Lane Free Flow tolling for all National Highways regardless of lane number. 2. Semicon 2.0 focuses on six strategic pillars including chip design, fabs, and talent development. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (b). Statement 1 is incorrect because the NHAI guidelines apply only to four-lane and above National Highways, not all National Highways.
