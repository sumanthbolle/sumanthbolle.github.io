CPCB Proposes Annual Fly Ash Audits for Thermal Power Plants

The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) has submitted a proposal to the National Green Tribunal (NGT) mandating annual audits of fly ash utilisation and disposal by thermal power plants. This move aims to enforce compliance with environmental norms and reduce pollution from unutilised fly ash.

The CPCB requires thermal power plants to undergo annual fly ash audits to ensure compliance with utilisation norms and environmental standards.

Why is this in the news?

The CPCB submitted the proposal before the NGT following a petition by the Tamil Nadu Fly Ash Brick and Blocks Manufacturers Association seeking clarity on CPCB's audit role.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the CPCB propose regarding fly ash audits?

  • Annual audits mandated. The CPCB has proposed that thermal power plants must undergo annual audits to verify compliance with fly ash utilisation and disposal norms.
  • Audit reports submission. Audit reports must be submitted to the CPCB and respective State Pollution Control Boards by November 30 each year.
  • Authorised auditors. The CPCB has authorised 124 auditors to conduct these compliance audits.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why are these fly ash audits important?

  • Ensuring utilisation targets. The Union Environment Ministry's 2021 Ash Utilisation Notification mandates thermal power plants to achieve 100% fly ash utilisation over the compliance cycle, with a minimum annual target of 80%.
  • Environmental compensation. Plants failing to meet utilisation targets must pay an environmental compensation of ₹1,000 per tonne of unutilised ash.
  • Pollution control. Regular audits help enforce environmentally sound management of fly ash, reducing pollution risks from improper disposal.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does this affect thermal power plants?

  • Compliance responsibility. Coal and lignite-based thermal power plants are responsible for ensuring proper utilisation and disposal of the fly ash they generate.
  • Use of fly ash. Permitted uses include manufacturing bricks, blocks, cement, and other approved applications.
  • Enforcement actions. The CPCB has issued directions under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, to non-compliant plants to meet requirements within stipulated periods.

Back to basics: Fly Ash and Environmental Regulation

Fly ash is a byproduct of coal combustion in thermal power plants and contains harmful substances that can pollute air and water if not managed properly. Environmental regulations mandate its utilisation in construction materials and other approved uses to minimise waste and pollution. Compliance is monitored through audits and penalties to ensure sustainable environmental management.
