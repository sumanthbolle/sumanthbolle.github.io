Renewal Prospects of the 1996 Ganga Water Treaty Between India and Bangladesh

The 30-year-old Ganga Water Treaty between India and Bangladesh is set to lapse on December 12, 2026, with ongoing discussions about its renewal. Understanding the treaty’s provisions, challenges, and current negotiation stances is crucial for grasping India’s water diplomacy and regional cooperation with Bangladesh.

The 1996 Ganga Water Treaty established a framework for sharing dry-season Ganga waters between India and Bangladesh, and its upcoming renewal highlights evolving bilateral water management challenges.

Why is this in the news?

With the Ganga Water Treaty expiring on December 12, 2026, India and Bangladesh held a joint committee meeting in Dhaka in November 2026 to discuss water sharing and treaty renewal prospects.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

What is the history and purpose of the Ganga Water Treaty?

  • Origins of dispute. Tensions over Ganga water sharing began in the 1950s with India’s construction of the Farakka barrage to divert water to the Hooghly river, causing concerns in Bangladesh over reduced flow and desertification.
  • Treaty formation. After several ad hoc agreements failed, India and Bangladesh signed the Ganga Water Treaty in December 1996 to create a 30-year framework for sharing dry-season water flows and to establish a joint committee for monitoring.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

What are the key provisions of the 1996 treaty?

  • Flow-based sharing. The treaty sets a three-tiered water allocation system based on 10-day average flows at Farakka: equal sharing if flow is ≤70,000 cusecs; Bangladesh gets 35,000 cusecs if flow is 70,000–75,000 cusecs; and India gets 40,000 cusecs if flow is ≥75,000 cusecs.
  • Guaranteed releases. Between March 11 and May 10, both countries receive guaranteed 35,000 cusecs in alternate 10-day periods, though the treaty lacks a minimum guaranteed flow clause.
  • Emergency clause. If flow falls below 50,000 cusecs, both governments must consult immediately to adjust sharing based on equity and no harm principles.
  • Dispute resolution. A Joint Committee with equal representatives manages implementation and disputes, with unresolved issues referred to the Joint Rivers Commission overseeing 54 shared rivers.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

What are the current challenges and negotiation positions?

  • Criticism of treaty adequacy. The treaty is criticised for not addressing future population growth and climate change impacts on water availability.
  • India’s demands. India reportedly seeks an additional 30,000 to 35,000 cusecs during the March 11-May 10 period and a shorter renewal duration of 10 to 15 years.
  • Bangladesh’s demands. Bangladesh requests a guaranteed 40,000 cusecs release between February and May, reinstatement of a minimum guarantee clause from a 1977 agreement, longer renewal periods, and enhanced flood data sharing.
  • Points of contention. India opposes Bangladesh’s fixed volumetric minimum guarantee demand due to the asymmetric hydrological risk it imposes on upstream India, especially amid climate variability.
  • Political and regional context. Tensions over political issues like former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina’s extradition and Bihar’s opposition to the treaty renewal add complexity to negotiations.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

Why does the treaty renewal matter for India-Bangladesh relations?

  • Bilateral water diplomacy. The treaty renewal is a test of cooperative water management and regional diplomacy between India and Bangladesh.
  • Institutional strengthening. Experts suggest the renewal offers an opportunity to build resilient transboundary water institutions addressing sharing, data, dispute resolution, and risk management.
  • Strategic regional vision. Renewal could reimagine regional cooperation amid shared vulnerabilities and a fragmented global economy, requiring reinvestment in political capital and bilateral mechanisms.

Back to basics: Transboundary River Water Sharing Treaties

Transboundary river water sharing treaties are agreements between countries sharing rivers to allocate water equitably, manage disputes, and ensure sustainable use. They often include mechanisms for data sharing, joint monitoring, and emergency consultations to address variability in water flow. Such treaties are crucial for maintaining peaceful relations and cooperation between riparian states.

What happens next?

  • Strengthen institutions. The renewal process should focus on building robust transboundary institutions for water sharing, data collection, monitoring, and dispute resolution to address new challenges.
  • Reinvest political capital. Both countries need to rebuild the political will and trust that enabled the 1996 treaty to manage evolving water sharing issues effectively.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the 1996 Ganga Water Treaty between India and Bangladesh: 1. The treaty sets a three-tiered water sharing system based on flow thresholds measured at Farakka. 2. The treaty guarantees a minimum water release to Bangladesh regardless of actual flow levels. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the treaty does not guarantee a minimum water release regardless of flow; it only has guaranteed releases in alternate periods and an emergency consultation clause.
