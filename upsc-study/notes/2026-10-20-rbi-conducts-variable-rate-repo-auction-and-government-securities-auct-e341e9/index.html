RBI Conducts 7-Day Variable Rate Repo Auction and State Government Securities Auctions

On October 19, 2026, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) conducted a 7-day Variable Rate Repo (VRR) auction and multiple State Government Securities (SGS) auctions. Understanding these operations is crucial for UPSC aspirants as they reflect key monetary policy tools and government borrowing mechanisms impacting liquidity and fiscal management.

The RBI's VRR auction and government securities auctions are tools to manage liquidity and government borrowing costs, essential for monetary and fiscal policy understanding.

Why is this in the news?

The RBI conducted a 7-day Variable Rate Repo auction and State Government Securities auctions on October 19, 2026, as part of its liquidity and debt management operations.

What is the Variable Rate Repo (VRR) auction and its significance?

  • VRR auction details. The RBI conducted a 7-day VRR auction on October 19, 2026, with a notified amount of ₹1,00,000 crore and a window timing from 09:30 AM to 10:00 AM, with reversal on October 26, 2026.
  • Purpose of VRR auction. The VRR auction is a liquidity adjustment facility tool used by the RBI to manage short-term liquidity in the banking system by borrowing excess funds from banks at a variable rate.
  • Auction results. The auction received bids worth ₹1,50,735 crore and allotted ₹1,00,014 crore at a cut-off rate of 5.51%, with a weighted average rate of 5.52%.

[1] Reserve Bank of India[2] Reserve Bank of India

What are State Government Securities (SGS) auctions and their outcomes?

  • Auction overview. On October 19, 2026, the RBI conducted auctions for various State Government Securities with a total notified amount of ₹24,400 crore across multiple states and tenors.
  • Competitive and non-competitive bids. The auctions involved competitive bids where bidders specify yield and non-competitive bids where bidders accept the cut-off yield; partial allotments were made based on bids received.
  • Yield and allotment. Cut-off yields ranged approximately between 7.53% and 8.23% depending on the state and security, with partial allotments reflecting demand and market conditions.

[3] Reserve Bank of India

Why do these auctions matter for monetary policy and fiscal management?

  • Liquidity management. The VRR auction helps the RBI absorb excess liquidity from the banking system, influencing short-term interest rates and inflation control.
  • Government borrowing. State Government Securities auctions enable states to raise funds for their fiscal needs, impacting overall government debt and market interest rates.
  • Market signals. The cut-off yields and auction participation provide signals about market expectations on interest rates, inflation, and fiscal health.

Back to basics: Monetary Policy Instruments

Monetary policy involves tools used by central banks to regulate money supply and interest rates to achieve economic objectives like controlling inflation and supporting growth. The Variable Rate Repo auction is a liquidity adjustment facility where the central bank borrows funds from banks at variable rates to manage short-term liquidity. Government securities auctions are a method for governments to raise funds by issuing debt instruments to investors, influencing fiscal deficit and interest rates.
