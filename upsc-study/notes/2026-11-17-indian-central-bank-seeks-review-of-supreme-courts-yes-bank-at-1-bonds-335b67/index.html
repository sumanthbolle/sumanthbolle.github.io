RBI Seeks Review of Supreme Court Order on Yes Bank AT-1 Bonds

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) filed a review petition in the Supreme Court against the judgment that set aside the write-off of ₹8,415 crore of Yes Bank’s additional tier-1 (AT-1) bonds. This development is important for understanding the judiciary's role in financial regulation and the legal treatment of bank restructuring instruments.

The RBI challenges the Supreme Court's decision that invalidated the write-off of Yes Bank’s AT-1 bonds during its insolvency resolution.

Why is this in the news?

On November 13, 2026, the RBI filed a review petition in the Supreme Court against its earlier judgment that set aside the write-off of ₹8,415 crore of Yes Bank’s AT-1 bonds.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Supreme Court order on Yes Bank AT-1 bonds?

  • Judgment details. The Supreme Court upheld a Bombay High Court ruling that set aside the write-off of ₹8,415 crore of Yes Bank’s additional tier-1 bonds, declaring the write-off impermissible.
  • Background of Yes Bank. Yes Bank was declared insolvent six years ago, and as part of its restructuring, new investors were brought in and the AT-1 bonds were written off.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why did the RBI seek a review of the Supreme Court judgment?

  • RBI’s action. The RBI filed a petition in the Supreme Court seeking a review of the judgment that invalidated the write-off of Yes Bank’s AT-1 bonds, challenging the court’s decision.
  • Lack of disclosed grounds. Yes Bank informed exchanges about filing the review petition but did not disclose the grounds for seeking relief.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the implications of this legal dispute?

  • Financial regulation impact. The case affects how additional tier-1 bonds, a key instrument in bank capital structure, are treated during insolvency and restructuring.
  • Judiciary’s role. It highlights the judiciary’s intervention in financial regulation and the balance between regulatory decisions and legal oversight.
  • Bondholders’ concerns. Axis Trustee Services filed a contempt petition alleging Yes Bank’s failure to provide a concrete roadmap for restoring the bonds and implementing the court’s judgment.

Back to basics: Judicial Review

Judicial review is the power of courts to examine the actions of the executive and legislature to ensure they comply with the law and constitution. It acts as a check on regulatory and administrative decisions, including those affecting financial institutions. This case exemplifies judicial review in the context of banking regulation and insolvency resolution.
