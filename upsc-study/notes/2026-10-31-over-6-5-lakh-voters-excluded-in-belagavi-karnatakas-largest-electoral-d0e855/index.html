Over 6.5 Lakh Voters Excluded in Karnataka’s Largest Electoral District Belagavi

The Election Commission of India’s Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls in Karnataka led to the exclusion of 6,58,862 voters, about 15.8% of Belagavi district’s electorate. This raises concerns about electoral integrity and voter disenfranchisement ahead of elections.

The SIR process in Belagavi district excluded over 6.5 lakh voters, highlighting challenges in electoral roll management and potential impacts on democratic participation.

Why is this in the news?

On October 30, 2026, The Hindu reported that over 6.5 lakh voters were excluded from Belagavi’s electoral rolls following the Election Commission of India’s Special Intensive Revision in Karnataka.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What happened during the Special Intensive Revision in Belagavi?

  • Voter exclusion scale. Belagavi district’s electoral rolls shrank from 41,53,775 voters in January 2025 to 34,94,913 after the SIR, excluding 6,58,862 voters or 15.8% of the electorate.
  • Comparison with other regions. The deletion percentage in Belagavi is higher than Karnataka’s average of 12% but lower than Bengaluru’s 43% deletion rate.
  • Muslim voter impact. Among the excluded voters, approximately 1,03,839 were Muslims, with significant deletions in constituencies like Belgaum Uttar and Kudachi.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this voter exclusion matter for elections and democracy?

  • Effect on electoral outcomes. In several constituencies, the number of deleted voters far exceeds the winning margins of elected representatives, potentially affecting election results.
  • Allegations of misuse. Complaints were made about violations of Form 7 application rules, including document fudging and intimidation, suggesting possible manipulation of the voter deletion process.
  • Voter disenfranchisement. Large-scale deletions, especially of minority voters, raise concerns about disenfranchisement and the fairness of the electoral process.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How were deletions categorized and contested?

  • Categories of deletions. Deletions included Absent, Shifted, Dead, Duplicate and Others (ASDDO), pre-SIR deletions, and those based on third-party objections via Form 7 applications.
  • Role of Form 7 objections. Many deletions resulted from third-party objections filed under Form 7, some of which were later withdrawn, admitting misuse of the process.
  • Political and legal responses. Affected leaders and parties lodged complaints with the Election Commission and law enforcement, highlighting irregularities and seeking redress.

Back to basics: Electoral Roll Revision

Electoral rolls are periodically revised to remove names of ineligible voters such as those who have died, moved away, or are duplicates. The Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is a process conducted by the Election Commission to update and clean voter lists to ensure accuracy. However, this process must balance removing ineligible names with protecting eligible voters from wrongful exclusion, as voter rolls are fundamental to free and fair elections.
