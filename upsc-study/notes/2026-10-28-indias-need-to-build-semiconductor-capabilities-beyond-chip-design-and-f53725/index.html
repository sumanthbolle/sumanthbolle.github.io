India’s Need to Build Semiconductor Capabilities Beyond Chip Design

India must reduce foreign dependence across the semiconductor value chain by developing manufacturing, materials, R&D, and specialised skills. This is critical for strengthening supply-chain resilience and advancing technological sovereignty, as highlighted at the Semiconductor Summit 2026.

India needs to build comprehensive semiconductor capabilities beyond chip design to reduce foreign dependence and enhance technological and economic security.

Why is this in the news?

At the Semiconductor Summit 2026 in Chennai on October 27, 2026, the Semiconductor Laboratory chief emphasized India’s need to build capabilities beyond chip design to cut foreign dependence across the semiconductor value chain.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why must India build semiconductor capabilities beyond chip design?

  • Foreign dependence. India currently depends on foreign technologies for critical semiconductor inputs like electronic design automation (EDA) tools, which are mostly imported from the U.S.
  • Supply chain resilience. Developing domestic manufacturing, materials, research, and specialised skills is essential to strengthen India’s semiconductor supply chain and reduce vulnerabilities.
  • Technological sovereignty. Building end-to-end capabilities will help India emerge as a technological power and reduce strategic dependencies in a sector central to economic competitiveness and national security.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What steps has India taken to develop semiconductor capabilities?

  • India Semiconductor Mission. Under India Semiconductor Mission 1.0, ₹76,000 crore was committed with 12 projects approved and five operational, focusing on chip design and manufacturing.
  • Expanded focus in Semicon 2.0. The second phase includes R&D, skill development, manufacturing, materials, and machinery to bridge the gap between research and commercial production.
  • Education and skill development. The Semiconductor Laboratory integrated its process design kit with open-source tools to widen access to semiconductor education, especially in tier-two and tier-three cities.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

How is international cooperation shaping India’s semiconductor ambitions?

  • India-Germany cooperation. India and Germany signed a joint declaration to encourage investment and strengthen links among companies, research institutions, and government agencies in chip design, power semiconductors, manufacturing technologies, and skills development.
  • Mutual benefits and trust. Cooperation is based on mutual benefit, reciprocity, and trust while protecting intellectual property and sensitive technologies.
  • Complementary strengths. German expertise in power semiconductors and precision engineering complements India’s engineering talent and expanding electronics market.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What challenges must India overcome to build semiconductor capabilities?

  • Technology and resources. India faces challenges in technology development, resource allocation, and capital investment to build a competitive semiconductor ecosystem.
  • Talent gap. India needs about three lakh semiconductor professionals by 2030 across fabrication, packaging, testing, and equipment fields, beyond chip design.
  • Market acceptance and time. Bridging the gap between research and commercial production requires time and acceptance by the market, supported by industry participation.

Back to basics: Semiconductor Value Chain

The semiconductor value chain includes multiple stages such as research and development, chip design, manufacturing (fabrication), testing, packaging, and materials supply. Each stage requires specialised skills and technology. Dependence on foreign suppliers for any stage can create vulnerabilities in supply and national security. Building domestic capabilities across the entire chain reduces such risks and supports technological sovereignty.

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because India still depends on foreign EDA tools, mainly from the U.S., as highlighted by the Semiconductor Laboratory chief.
