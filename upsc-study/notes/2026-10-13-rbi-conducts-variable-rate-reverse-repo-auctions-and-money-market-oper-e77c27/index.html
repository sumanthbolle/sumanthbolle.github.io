RBI's Variable Rate Reverse Repo Auctions and Money Market Operations Explained

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) conducted Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions and other money market operations from October 9 to 11, 2026, managing liquidity and influencing interest rates. Understanding these operations is crucial for UPSC aspirants as they reflect RBI's monetary policy tools and their impact on the economy.

RBI uses VRRR auctions and money market operations to absorb liquidity and guide short-term interest rates in line with its monetary policy.

Why is this in the news?

RBI conducted VRRR auctions and money market operations on October 9, 10, and 11, 2026, to manage liquidity and influence interest rates.

What are Variable Rate Reverse Repo auctions and their purpose?

  • Definition of VRRR. Variable Rate Reverse Repo auctions are operations where RBI absorbs excess liquidity from banks by borrowing funds at variable rates determined through auction.
  • Purpose of VRRR. These auctions help RBI manage surplus liquidity in the banking system, thereby influencing short-term interest rates and maintaining monetary stability.

How did RBI conduct money market operations from October 9 to 11, 2026?

  • Liquidity absorption. RBI absorbed liquidity through VRRR auctions and Standing Deposit Facility (SDF), with net liquidity absorption ranging from ₹1,38,979 crore to ₹1,40,490 crore daily.
  • Rates and volumes. The weighted average rates for overnight segments hovered around 5.30%, while VRRR cut-off rates were at 5.49% during these days.
  • Other operations. RBI also conducted repo operations, marginal standing facility (MSF), and standing liquidity facility (SLF) to manage liquidity and short-term rates.

Back to basics: Monetary Policy Tools

Monetary policy tools are instruments used by central banks like RBI to control money supply and interest rates in the economy. These include repo and reverse repo operations, where the central bank lends or borrows money from banks, influencing liquidity and inflation. Variable Rate Reverse Repo auctions allow RBI to absorb excess liquidity by borrowing funds at variable interest rates determined through auctions.
