India implications

  • Urban local bodies need to treat sanitation as a public-health and occupational-safety responsibility rather than only a municipal service.
  • Worker dignity requires prevention of hazardous exposure, access to protective equipment and training, and institutional accountability for safe sanitation practices.
  • The issue connects inclusive growth with the protection of vulnerable workers, especially where occupational hazards can produce health and income insecurity.

Way forward

  • Combine PPE distribution with mechanised sewer and septic-tank cleaning and trained response systems.
  • Ensure regular training, equipment maintenance, correct usage and workplace monitoring by accountable urban institutions.
  • Use worker profiling and grievance mechanisms to identify gaps in coverage and safety compliance.
  • Link occupational safety with health protection, social-security access and viable livelihood support.
