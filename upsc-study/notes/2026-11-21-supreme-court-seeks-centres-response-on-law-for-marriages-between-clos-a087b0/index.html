Supreme Court Seeks Centre’s Response on Law for Consanguineous Marriages

The Supreme Court of India has asked the Union Government to consider legislation or awareness measures regarding marriages between close blood relatives. This development is important for understanding the separation of powers and social legal reforms in India.

The Supreme Court has urged the Centre to consider a law or awareness campaign on consanguineous marriages, emphasizing legislative domain and sensitization.

Why is this in the news?

On November 21, 2026, the Supreme Court asked the Centre for a response on bringing legislation or awareness measures on marriages between close blood relatives.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court say about consanguineous marriages?

  • Legislative domain. The Supreme Court stated that regulating consanguineous marriages falls exclusively within the legislature's domain and the court cannot regulate such marriages.
  • Court’s limited role. The court can intervene only on sensitization and awareness, not on direct regulation or prohibition.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is the Supreme Court urging the Centre to act?

  • Petitioner's concern. The petitioner highlighted the need to create awareness about the inter-generational consequences of consanguineous marriages.
  • Court's notice. The court issued notice to impress upon the Union of India to consider suitable legislation or wide publicity through channels including educational curriculum.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does this matter for constitutional law and social justice?

  • Separation of powers. The case exemplifies the judiciary respecting the legislature's exclusive role in lawmaking on social issues.
  • Social reform and awareness. It highlights the judiciary’s role in promoting sensitization on social issues without direct regulation.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Back to basics: Separation of Powers

The separation of powers divides government responsibilities into distinct branches to limit any one branch from exercising the core functions of another. The legislature makes laws, the executive implements them, and the judiciary interprets them. This principle ensures checks and balances in governance and prevents concentration of power.
