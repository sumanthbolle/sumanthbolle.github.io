Maiden BIMSTEC Maritime Security Exercise Concludes at Andaman and Nicobar Command

The first BIMSTEC Maritime Security Exercise was successfully held at the Andaman and Nicobar Command from October 30 to November 3, 2026, involving naval ships from BIMSTEC member states. This exercise is important for UPSC aspirants to understand India's efforts in regional maritime security cooperation and its strategic role in the Bay of Bengal region.

The maiden BIMSTEC maritime exercise enhanced regional naval cooperation and underscored the Andaman and Nicobar Command's role in promoting security in the Bay of Bengal.

Why is this in the news?

The maiden BIMSTEC Maritime Security Exercise culminated on 3 November 2026 at the Andaman and Nicobar Command, marking a key event in regional maritime collaboration.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What was the BIMSTEC Maritime Security Exercise?

  • Exercise details. The exercise was conducted from 30 October to 3 November 2026 at Sri Vijaya Puram under the Andaman and Nicobar Command, India's only Operational Quad Service Command.
  • Participants. Naval ships from BIMSTEC member states participated, including Indian Naval Ships INS Taragiri, INS Karmuk, INS Saryu; Myanmar Navy Ship UMS King Aung Zeya; and Sri Lanka Navy Ship SLNS Gajabahu.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this exercise matter for regional security?

  • Maritime collaboration. The exercise advanced regional maritime collaboration and collective security among BIMSTEC nations, fostering interoperability and professional interactions.
  • Shared maritime challenges. It demonstrated the collective resolve of BIMSTEC states to address common maritime challenges and safeguard shared maritime interests in the Bay of Bengal.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What role does the Andaman and Nicobar Command play?

  • Host and coordinator. The Andaman and Nicobar Command hosted the exercise, reaffirming its role in promoting regional maritime cooperation and contributing to peace, security, and stability in the Bay of Bengal region.
  • Operational significance. As India's only Operational Quad Service Command, it coordinates joint operations among the Army, Navy, Air Force, and Coast Guard in this strategic maritime area.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How was the exercise conducted?

  • Harbour phase. The harbour phase at Sri Vijaya Puram included high-level engagements, professional interactions, and cultural exchanges to deepen institutional ties.
  • Sea phase. The sea phase involved coordinated maritime deployments and collaborative operational activities off Sri Vijaya Puram from 2 to 3 November 2026.

Back to basics: Maritime Security Cooperation

Maritime security cooperation involves joint efforts by countries to ensure safe and secure seas, prevent threats like piracy, and protect shared maritime interests. Regional exercises enhance interoperability among navies, build trust, and promote stability in strategically important maritime zones.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the maiden BIMSTEC Maritime Security Exercise: 1. It was conducted under the aegis of the Andaman and Nicobar Command, India's only Operational Quad Service Command. 2. The exercise included naval ships from India, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, and Bangladesh.

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because Bangladesh's naval ship did not participate in the exercise; only India, Myanmar, and Sri Lanka sent naval ships.

Mains question (15 marks)

Examine the significance of regional maritime security cooperation through exercises like the BIMSTEC Maritime Security Exercise for India's strategic diplomacy.

Examine: Explain how regional maritime exercises contribute to India's strategic interests and diplomatic relations.

Intro: Regional maritime security exercises are vital tools for enhancing cooperation and stability in India's neighbourhood.

  • They improve interoperability among BIMSTEC navies, enabling coordinated responses to maritime threats.
  • Such exercises strengthen diplomatic ties and trust among member states, supporting India's strategic outreach.
  • The Andaman and Nicobar Command's role highlights India's commitment to securing the Bay of Bengal and the Indo-Pacific region.

Conclusion: Maritime security cooperation through BIMSTEC exercises advances India's regional security and diplomatic objectives.
