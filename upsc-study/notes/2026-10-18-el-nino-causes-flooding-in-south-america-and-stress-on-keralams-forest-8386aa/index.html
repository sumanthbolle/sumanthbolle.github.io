El Niño Causes Flooding in South America and Ecological Stress in Kerala

El Niño, a climate phenomenon involving warmer Pacific waters, has caused severe flooding in parts of South America including Argentina, Paraguay, and Uruguay. Understanding El Niño's global weather impacts is important for UPSC aspirants studying disaster management and environmental conservation.

El Niño disrupts weather patterns causing floods in South America and ecological stress in regions like Kerala, highlighting climate change challenges.

Why is this in the news?

El Niño rains caused flooding in South America in October 2026, displacing thousands and affecting rivers and wildlife.

What is El Niño and how does it cause flooding?

  • Definition. El Niño is a natural climate phenomenon where warmer-than-usual waters in the Pacific Ocean disrupt global weather patterns.
  • Flooding mechanism. In South America, El Niño causes heavy rains that swell major rivers like the Parana, Uruguay, and Iguazu, leading to floods.
  • Recent impact. In October 2026, flooding forced thousands to evacuate in Argentina, Paraguay, and Uruguay, with rivers reaching record levels.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does El Niño matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Disaster management relevance. El Niño-induced floods exemplify climate-related disasters requiring effective preparedness and response strategies.
  • Environmental conservation. The phenomenon stresses ecosystems, such as Kerala’s forests and wildlife, linking to biodiversity and climate change topics.
  • Geographical understanding. Knowledge of El Niño’s regional impacts aids in understanding monsoon variability and global climatic interconnections.

Back to basics: El Niño Phenomenon

El Niño is a periodic warming of the central and eastern Pacific Ocean that alters atmospheric circulation and weather worldwide. It affects rainfall patterns, often causing droughts in some regions and floods in others. Understanding El Niño helps explain variations in monsoon and global climate anomalies.
