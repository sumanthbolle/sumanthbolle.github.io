Bombay HC Allows Section 75 Charge for Transgender Woman Complainant

The Bombay High Court ruled that Section 75 of the Bharatiya Nyay Sanhita (BNS) applies when the complainant is a transgender person identifying as a woman. This clarifies legal protections for transgender individuals under criminal law, relevant for gender rights and law questions.

The Bombay High Court held that criminal charges under Section 75 BNS can be applied when the complainant is a transgender woman, affirming legal recognition of gender identity in criminal law.

Why is this in the news?

On November 3, 2026, the Bombay High Court refused to discharge an accused under Section 75 of the BNS, ruling it applies when the complainant is a transgender woman who identifies as female after hormone therapy.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Bombay High Court decide regarding Section 75 and transgender complainants?

  • Section 75 applicability. The court ruled that Section 75 of the Bharatiya Nyay Sanhita applies to cases where the complainant is a transgender person identifying as a woman, especially after hormone therapy.
  • Rejection of discharge plea. The court refused to discharge the accused Vishal Ingle, holding that allegations of outraging the modesty of a woman can be sustained even if the victim is a transgender woman.
  • Recognition of gender identity. The complainant's identification as a woman and hormone treatment were key factors in applying Section 75, affirming legal recognition of gender identity.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How did the court interpret the Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019 in this case?

  • Certificate of identity not mandatory. The court held that absence of a certificate of identity at the time of the offence does not bar invoking protections under the 2019 Act.
  • Definition of transgender identity. Section 2(k) of the Act allows a person to identify as transgender even without a certificate, and Section 5 provides a process to obtain one.
  • Certificate received post-offence. The complainant received the certificate of identity on February 12, 2026, after the assault dates in October 2025.

Back to basics: Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019

This Act provides legal recognition and protection for transgender persons in India. It defines transgender identity, prescribes rights, and sets procedures for obtaining a certificate of identity. The Act aims to prevent discrimination and protect dignity, including in criminal law contexts.
