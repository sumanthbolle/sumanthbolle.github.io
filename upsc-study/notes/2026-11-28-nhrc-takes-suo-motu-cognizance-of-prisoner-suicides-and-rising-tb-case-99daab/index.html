NHRC Takes Suo Motu Cognizance of Prisoner Suicides and Rising TB Cases

The National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) has taken suo motu cognizance of two prisoner suicides in Lucknow jails and the rising tuberculosis cases in Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh. This highlights NHRC's role in addressing human rights violations related to custodial deaths and public health issues.

NHRC's suo motu actions on prisoner suicides and TB cases underline its mandate to protect human rights in custodial and health contexts.

Why is this in the news?

On 27 November 2026, NHRC took suo motu cognizance of media reports about two prisoner suicides in Lucknow and the continuous rise in TB cases in Bhopal, issuing notices for detailed reports.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What actions did NHRC take regarding prisoner suicides in Lucknow?

  • Suo motu cognizance. NHRC took suo motu cognizance of two prisoner suicides within seven days at District Jail and Model Jail in Lucknow on 19 and 25 November 2026.
  • Notices issued. The Commission issued notices to the Director General of Prisons, Uttar Pradesh, and the District Magistrate, Lucknow, seeking a detailed report within two weeks.
  • Violation concerns. NHRC observed that if the reports are true, these suicides raise serious human rights violation issues.
  • Delay in reporting. NHRC noted the custodial deaths were not reported to it within 24 hours as required and sought an explanation within 24 hours for this lapse.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What concerns did NHRC raise about rising TB cases in Bhopal?

  • Increase in TB cases. Registered TB patients in Bhopal increased by about 19% from 10,773 in 2022 to 12,850 in 2024 despite the ongoing Tuberculosis Free India campaign.
  • Financial assistance gap. Only 1.43 lakh out of 1.77 lakh registered TB patients in 2024 received a single instalment of Rs. 1,000 per month under a government nutrition scheme.
  • Human rights issues. NHRC observed that the reported situation, if true, indicates serious human rights violations related to health and welfare.
  • Notice to state government. NHRC issued a notice to the Chief Secretary of Madhya Pradesh, calling for a detailed report within two weeks.
  • Statewide TB impact. About 1.72 lakh TB cases and 4,733 deaths were reported across Madhya Pradesh in 2025.

[2] Press Information Bureau

Why does NHRC's intervention matter for governance and human rights?

  • Role of NHRC. NHRC acts as a statutory body to protect human rights, including in custodial settings and public health.
  • Suo motu powers. NHRC can initiate action on its own based on media reports without waiting for formal complaints.
  • Accountability mechanism. By issuing notices and seeking reports, NHRC holds state authorities accountable for human rights compliance.
  • Human rights and health. Addressing TB cases underlines the link between health governance and human rights protection.

Back to basics: National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)

The NHRC is a statutory body established to protect and promote human rights in India. It has the power to take suo motu cognizance of human rights violations based on media reports or other sources. NHRC can issue notices to government authorities, seek reports, and recommend actions to ensure accountability and safeguard rights, including in custodial and health-related matters.
