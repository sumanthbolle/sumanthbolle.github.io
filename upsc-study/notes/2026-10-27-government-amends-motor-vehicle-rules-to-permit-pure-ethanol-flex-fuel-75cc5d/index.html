Government Amends Motor Vehicle Rules to Permit E85/E100 Flex-Fuel Vehicles

The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways amended the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989, to allow vehicles designed for ethanol blends up to 85% (E85) and pure ethanol (E100). This policy supports the adoption of flex-fuel vehicles, which can run on petrol, ethanol blends, or pure ethanol, impacting energy security and environmental goals.

The government now permits flex-fuel vehicles certified for E85 and E100 fuels, enabling use of high-ethanol blends and pure ethanol in compatible vehicles.

Why is this in the news?

On October 26, 2026, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways notified amendments to the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989, to facilitate flex-fuel vehicles using E85 and E100 ethanol fuels.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What changes did the government make to motor vehicle rules?

  • Amendment details. The Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989, were amended to include emission certification provisions explicitly covering E100 fuel alongside E85, allowing vehicles designed for these fuels to meet applicable emission and safety standards.
  • Flex-fuel vehicle definition. Flex-fuel vehicles are designed to operate on a range of ethanol and petrol blends using a single fuel tank, with engine controls adjusting automatically to the fuel composition.
  • Fuel specifications. E85 contains up to 85% ethanol by volume, while E100 is nearly pure ethanol; both are now recognized for use in certified vehicles.
  • No mandatory switch. Existing vehicle owners are not required to switch to E85 or modify their vehicles; compatibility depends on manufacturer certification and fuel compatibility stickers.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does permitting E85/E100 flex-fuel vehicles matter for India?

  • Energy security. Greater use of domestically produced ethanol can reduce dependence on imported crude oil by substituting petrol with ethanol blends.
  • Environmental benefits. Ethanol use in suitable vehicles can reduce certain exhaust pollutants and lower greenhouse gas emissions over the fuel's lifecycle, depending on feedstock and production methods.
  • Technology adoption. Flex-fuel technology, already used in countries like Brazil and the United States, offers users flexibility to choose fuels within certified ranges, promoting cleaner fuel options.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What should motorists know about using E85/E100 fuels?

  • Vehicle compatibility. A vehicle's compatibility with lower ethanol blends like E10 or E20 does not imply suitability for E85 or E100; only vehicles certified as flex-fuel can use these fuels.
  • Manufacturer guidance. Motorists should check the fuel compatibility sticker on their vehicle and follow the owner's manual instructions when choosing fuel at stations.
  • No retrofitting required. The notification does not require current vehicle owners to modify their vehicles or switch fuels at present or in the future.

Back to basics: Flex-Fuel Vehicles and Ethanol Blending

Flex-fuel vehicles are designed to run on varying proportions of ethanol and petrol blends using a single fuel system, with engine controls adjusting automatically to fuel composition. Ethanol blending involves mixing ethanol, an alcohol-based renewable fuel, with petrol to reduce fossil fuel use and emissions. Ethanol blends are denoted by 'E' followed by the percentage of ethanol, such as E10 (10% ethanol) or E85 (85% ethanol). These vehicles require certification to ensure emission and safety standards are met for the specific fuel blends they use.

Common mistake. Compatibility with E10 or E20 ethanol blends does not imply that a vehicle can use E85 or E100 fuels without certification.

Quick revision

What is E85 fuel?
E85 is a petrol and ethanol blend containing up to 85% ethanol by volume.
Does the amendment require existing vehicles to switch to E85 or E100 fuels?
No, existing vehicle owners are not required to switch or modify their vehicles.
Can vehicles compatible with E10 or E20 use E85 or E100 fuels?
No, compatibility with E10 or E20 does not establish suitability for E85 or E100.
