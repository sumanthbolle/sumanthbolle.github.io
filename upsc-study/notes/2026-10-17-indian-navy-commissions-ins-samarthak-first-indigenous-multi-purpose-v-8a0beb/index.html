Indian Navy Commissions INS Samarthak, First Indigenous Multi-Purpose Vessel

INS Samarthak, with over 75% indigenous content, was commissioned by the Vice-President on October 16, 2026, marking a milestone in India's defence manufacturing and naval self-reliance. This vessel enhances the Navy's capability to test indigenous weapons and sensors and reflects the growing role of private shipyards in defence production.

INS Samarthak is the Indian Navy’s first indigenous multi-purpose vessel built by a private shipyard, supporting trials of next-generation naval technologies and strengthening maritime security.

Why is this in the news?

The Indian Navy commissioned INS Samarthak, its first indigenous multi-purpose vessel, on October 16, 2026, at Karwar, Karnataka, highlighting India's progress in defence self-reliance and private sector shipbuilding.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau[3] The Hindu · Secondary

What is INS Samarthak and why is it significant?

  • First indigenous MPV. INS Samarthak is the Indian Navy’s first indigenous multi-purpose vessel with over 75% indigenous content, designed and built by Larsen & Toubro Shipyard, marking the first major warship constructed by an Indian private shipyard.
  • Versatile operational platform. The vessel supports trials and evaluation of next-generation weapons, sensors, and unmanned systems, and is capable of maritime surveillance, offshore patrolling, reconnaissance, and humanitarian assistance.
  • Technological and industrial milestone. INS Samarthak embodies modular construction and pre-outfitting techniques that optimize build time and reflects India’s growing industrial and technological capabilities in defence manufacturing.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau[3] The Hindu · Secondary

How does INS Samarthak contribute to India’s defence and maritime strategy?

  • Enhancing indigenous testing. The vessel provides a dedicated platform for testing and training on indigenously developed naval sensors and weapons, reducing dependence on foreign facilities.
  • Supporting maritime security. INS Samarthak strengthens the Navy’s ability to adapt to emerging technological developments and respond to traditional and non-traditional maritime threats.
  • Boosting self-reliance and economy. The project contributes to employment generation, capability development, and economic plough back, reinforcing the vision of Aatmanirbhar Bharat in defence.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau[3] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the strategic importance of Karwar and the role of private industry?

  • Karwar’s strategic location. Karwar’s deep natural harbour and proximity to the Arabian Sea make it a vital part of India’s maritime security architecture, supported by infrastructure development under Project Seabird.
  • Private shipbuilding role. Larsen & Toubro’s construction of INS Samarthak marks a significant step in the maturity of India’s private shipbuilding industry and its collaboration with the Navy.
  • Collaborative defence ecosystem. The commissioning highlights partnerships among the Navy, defence PSUs, private shipyards, DRDO, academia, MSMEs, and start-ups to strengthen indigenous defence manufacturing.

Back to basics: Indigenisation in Defence Manufacturing

Indigenisation refers to the process of developing and producing defence equipment domestically to reduce dependence on foreign suppliers. It involves collaboration between government, public sector units, private industry, and research organizations to build capabilities in design, production, and testing. This enhances strategic autonomy, supports economic growth, and strengthens national security by ensuring availability and control over critical defence technologies.
