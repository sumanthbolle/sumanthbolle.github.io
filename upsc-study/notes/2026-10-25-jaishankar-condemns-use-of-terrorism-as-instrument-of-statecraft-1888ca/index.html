Jaishankar Condemns Terrorism as an Instrument of Statecraft

External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar stated on October 25, 2026, that using terrorism as a tool by states is unacceptable and unjustifiable. This statement is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights India's stance on terrorism in the context of internal security and international diplomacy.

Jaishankar emphasized that terrorism as state policy is unacceptable and calls for global unity against it.

Why is this in the news?

On October 25, 2026, External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar condemned the use of terrorism as an instrument of statecraft during an event marking the 75th anniversary of Chinmaya Mission in New Delhi.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did Jaishankar say about terrorism as statecraft?

  • Unacceptable practice. Jaishankar said that using terrorism as an instrument of statecraft is simply unacceptable and can never be justified or rationalised.
  • Continued due to tolerance. He noted terrorism persists because not all states show zero tolerance, and some prioritize political calculations over countering terrorism.
  • Global call for unity. He urged the world to develop sharper awareness of terrorism as a common threat and to come together with stronger resolve to counter it in all forms.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this statement matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Internal security relevance. The statement relates to internal security by addressing terrorism, a major threat to peace and stability.
  • Foreign policy context. It reflects India's diplomatic position on terrorism amid ongoing global conflicts like those in Ukraine and West Asia.
  • Exam linkage. Understanding India's stance on terrorism helps in answering questions on internal security, international relations, and counter-terrorism policies.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Back to basics: Terrorism as a security threat

Terrorism involves unlawful acts of violence to create fear and achieve political aims. When states use terrorism as a tool, it complicates international relations and security. Counter-terrorism requires cooperation among nations to uphold peace and stability.
