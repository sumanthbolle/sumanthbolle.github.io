India's Horticultural Production Rises 4.36% in 2025-26 Crop Year

India's total horticultural production is projected to reach 3,869.15 lakh tonnes in 2025-26, a 4.36% increase over the previous year despite a slight reduction in cultivated area. This growth reflects shifts towards high-value crops and has implications for nutritional security and farmer incomes.

India's horticultural production increased by 4.36% in 2025-26, driven by higher yields in fruits, vegetables, flowers, and plantation crops despite reduced cultivation area.

Why is this in the news?

The Union Agriculture Minister announced on November 17, 2026, that India's horticultural production is expected to increase by 4.36% in 2025-26 compared to 2024-25.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the key statistics of horticultural production growth?

  • Total production increase. Horticultural production is expected to reach 3,869.15 lakh tonnes in 2025-26, up by 161.77 lakh tonnes or 4.36% from 3,707.38 lakh tonnes in 2024-25.
  • Area under cultivation. The total area under horticultural crops decreased from 301.36 lakh hectares in 2024-25 to approximately 297.21 lakh hectares in 2025-26.
  • Fruit production growth. Fruit production is projected to rise by 4.60%, from 1,176.49 lakh tonnes to 1,230.66 lakh tonnes, with better yields in banana, mango, papaya, apple, pomegranate, and jackfruit.
  • Vegetable production growth. Vegetable production is expected to increase by 4.35%, from 2,177.97 lakh tonnes to 2,272.68 lakh tonnes, driven by higher output of potato, tomato, brinjal, and other vegetables.
  • Floriculture and plantation crops. Flower production is projected to grow by 14.45%, and plantation crops production by 7.56%, including arecanut, coconut, cashew, betel leaves, and cocoa.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this horticultural growth matter for India’s economy and food security?

  • Nutritional security. The increase in horticultural production strengthens the country’s nutritional security by providing more fruits, vegetables, and other nutritious crops.
  • Farmer incomes. Higher production of commercial and high-value crops opens new income avenues for farmers, contributing to rural economic growth.
  • Shift in cropping patterns. Farmers are moving beyond traditional crops to cultivate fruits, vegetables, spices, flowers, and medicinal plants, reflecting diversification and commercialisation.
  • Policy impact. The growth is attributed to the impact of the Union government’s farmer-friendly policies that support horticulture development.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How is production increasing despite reduced cultivation area?

  • Improved productivity. Production increases across fruits, vegetables, flowers, plantation crops, and aromatic and medicinal plants despite a decline in total area, indicating better yields and farming practices.
  • Focus on high-value crops. Farmers’ growing interest in commercial and high-value crops like banana, mango, potato, tomato, and flowers contributes to higher output per hectare.

Back to basics: Horticulture and Agricultural Production

Horticulture involves the cultivation of fruits, vegetables, flowers, spices, and plantation crops. It is a key sector for nutritional security and farmer incomes in India. Production depends on area under cultivation and yield per hectare, which can improve through better farming techniques and crop diversification.
