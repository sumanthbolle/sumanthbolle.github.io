PLI Scheme Disburses ₹4,385.28 Crore Incentives in FY 2026–27 for Auto Sector

The Ministry of Heavy Industries reported that the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme for Automobile and Auto Component Industry disbursed ₹4,385.28 crore in incentives during FY 2026–27, supporting domestic manufacturing and advanced automotive technologies. UPSC aspirants should note this as a key example of India's industrial policy promoting manufacturing growth and employment.

The PLI Scheme for Automobile and Auto Component Industry disbursed ₹4,385.28 crore incentives in FY 2026–27 to boost domestic manufacturing and advanced automotive technology production.

Why is this in the news?

On 25 November 2026, the Ministry of Heavy Industries announced the disbursement of ₹4,385.28 crore incentives under the PLI Scheme for Automobile and Auto Component Industry for FY 2026–27.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the PLI Scheme for Automobile and Auto Component Industry?

  • Scheme objective. The scheme aims to enhance domestic manufacturing capabilities, promote advanced automotive technologies, and position India as a global hub for automobile manufacturing.
  • Budget and scope. It has a total approved budgetary outlay of ₹25,938 crore to promote investments, strengthen domestic supply chains, and encourage manufacturing of Advanced Automotive Technology (AAT) products.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What progress has the scheme made in FY 2026–27?

  • Incentive disbursement. In FY 2026–27, the scheme disbursed ₹4,385.28 crore in incentives as of 25 November 2026, with cumulative disbursement since inception reaching ₹6,771.64 crore.
  • Investment and sales. Approved applicants reported cumulative investments of ₹52,806 crore by 30 September 2026, exceeding the targeted ₹42,500 crore, and incremental sales of ₹82,993 crore over the base year FY 2019–20.
  • Employment generation. The scheme generated 77,755 employment opportunities, reflecting its role in strengthening India's automotive manufacturing ecosystem.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How does the scheme promote domestic value addition?

  • Domestic Value Addition requirement. The scheme mandates a minimum Domestic Value Addition (DVA) of 50% for eligible products to qualify for incentives.
  • DVA certification. As of 25 November 2026, DVA certificates were issued to 9 Champion OEMs covering 139 products/variants and 10 Component Champions covering 53 products/variants, indicating growing domestic manufacturing capabilities.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this matter for India's industrial policy?

  • Alignment with Aatmanirbhar Bharat. The scheme supports the Government of India's vision of Aatmanirbhar Bharat by reinforcing the country's commitment to becoming a leading global destination for automobile and auto component manufacturing.
  • Policy support. The Ministry of Heavy Industries continues to back the growth of a robust, resilient, and technologically advanced automotive industry through sustained policy initiatives and collaboration.

Back to basics: Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme

The PLI Scheme is a government initiative designed to boost domestic manufacturing by providing financial incentives linked to incremental production. It encourages companies to invest in production capacity and technology upgrades, aiming to enhance competitiveness and reduce import dependence. The scheme is part of broader industrial policies to promote self-reliance and export growth.
