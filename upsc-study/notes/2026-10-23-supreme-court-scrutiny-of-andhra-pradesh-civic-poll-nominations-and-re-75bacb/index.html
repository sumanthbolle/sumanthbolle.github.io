Supreme Court Examines Andhra Pradesh Civic Poll Nominations

The Andhra Pradesh State Election Commission has started the nomination process for MPTC and ZPTC elections with polling scheduled for November 3, 2026. The Supreme Court's scrutiny of these nominations highlights the judiciary's role in electoral governance and federalism.

The Supreme Court's review of Andhra Pradesh civic poll nominations illustrates judicial oversight in local electoral processes and its impact on federal governance.

Why is this in the news?

The Supreme Court is scrutinizing nominations for the Andhra Pradesh MPTC and ZPTC elections, which began filing on October 21, 2026, with polling on November 3, 2026.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current status of Andhra Pradesh civic polls?

  • Nomination process started. Nominations for Mandal Parishad Territorial Constituency (MPTC) and Zilla Parishad Territorial Constituency (ZPTC) elections began on October 21, 2026, and will close on October 23.
  • Polling schedule. Polling is scheduled for November 3, 2026, with re-polling if needed on November 4 and counting on November 5.
  • Voter and polling details. In Visakhapatnam, 1,62,407 voters across 203 polling stations will participate, managed by 1,462 staff and equipped with 480 ballot boxes.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the nomination requirements and procedures?

  • Mandatory documents. Candidates must submit Form-4 with complete details, including age certificate, Aadhaar card, Voter ID, educational certificates, caste and residential certificates, and four passport-sized photos.
  • Financial and legal clearances. A declaration of assets and liabilities, non-defaulter certificates from banks, no-dues certificates for taxes and utilities, and a police No-Objection Certificate confirming no pending criminal cases are required.
  • Proposers and access. Two proposers who are registered voters in the constituency must sign and provide Aadhaar copies; only the candidate and proposers can access the Returning Officer’s hall during submission.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does the State Election Commission regulate deposits and symbols?

  • Security deposits. Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Backward Classes candidates pay ₹1,500 for MPTC and ₹3,000 for ZPTC seats; General category candidates pay ₹3,000 for MPTC and ₹6,000 for ZPTC.
  • Election symbols. The State Election Commission allots free, common symbols to three parties contesting the MPTC and ZPTC elections.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the timeline for scrutiny and finalisation of candidates?

  • Scrutiny and appeals. Nomination papers will be scrutinised on October 24; candidates with rejected nominations may appeal on October 25 at the Revenue Divisional Officer office, with decisions by October 26 evening.
  • Withdrawal and final list. The deadline for withdrawal of candidature is October 27, after which the final list of candidates will be published.

Back to basics: Role of State Election Commissions

State Election Commissions are constitutional bodies responsible for conducting elections to local bodies such as Panchayats and Municipalities. They ensure free and fair elections by issuing notifications, setting guidelines, scrutinising nominations, and declaring results. Their functioning is crucial for grassroots democracy and electoral governance.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the Andhra Pradesh MPTC and ZPTC elections: 1. Candidates must submit a police No-Objection Certificate confirming no pending criminal cases. 2. The State Election Commission requires a security deposit of ₹6,000 for Scheduled Caste candidates contesting ZPTC seats. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the ₹6,000 security deposit for ZPTC seats applies to General category candidates, not Scheduled Caste candidates.
