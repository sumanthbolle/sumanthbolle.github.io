Supreme Court's Role in Due Process for Death Penalty Cases

The Supreme Court upheld the death penalty in 1980 but established judicial guidelines to ensure careful discretion in sentencing. These safeguards remain relevant for understanding judicial processes and human rights in capital punishment.

The Supreme Court shaped due process in death penalty cases by mandating judicial discretion and specific conditions for imposing capital punishment.

Why is this in the news?

On the Day Against the Death Penalty, the Supreme Court's 1980 guidelines on judicial discretion in capital punishment were revisited to assess their impact over four decades.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court decide about the death penalty in 1980?

  • Upholding death penalty. In Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980), the Supreme Court upheld the constitutionality of the death penalty but imposed conditions for its application.
  • Judicial discretion guidelines. The Court mandated that death sentences be imposed only when life imprisonment is unquestionably foreclosed, requiring courts to weigh both crime and accused's circumstances.
  • Legislative alignment. The Court followed the legislative scheme under Section 354(3) of the CrPC (now Section 393(3) of BNSS, 2023), which makes life imprisonment the norm and death penalty exceptional.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What procedural safeguards did the Court prescribe later?

  • Practical guidelines in recent rulings. In Manoj v. State of Madhya Pradesh, the Court prescribed practical guidelines including mental health evaluations and custody conduct reports of the accused.
  • Ensuring fair trial process. These safeguards aim to ensure that the accused's mental state and treatment in custody are considered before imposing the death penalty.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Judicial process and human rights. Understanding these rulings helps aspirants grasp how the judiciary balances capital punishment with constitutional safeguards and human rights.
  • Relevant for criminal justice topics. The Supreme Court’s approach exemplifies judicial discretion and due process, key themes in criminal law and constitutional governance.

Back to basics: Judicial discretion and due process

Judicial discretion allows courts to decide appropriate sentences based on case facts within legal limits. Due process ensures fair legal procedures before depriving a person of life or liberty, protecting constitutional rights.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the Supreme Court's rulings on the death penalty: 1. The Court upheld the death penalty in 1980 but required courts to impose it only when life imprisonment is unquestionably foreclosed. 2. The Court abolished the death penalty in 1980 and replaced it with life imprisonment as the only option.

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the Court did not abolish the death penalty but upheld it with conditions.
