Rupee Appreciation and RBI's Forex Interventions Explained

The Indian rupee appreciated by 31 paise to 94.86 against the US dollar on October 15, 2026, aided by RBI's structural forex interventions and liquidity tightening. Understanding this helps UPSC aspirants grasp how exchange rate fluctuations impact inflation, monetary policy, and external sector management.

The RBI's forex interventions and monetary policy influence the rupee's exchange rate, affecting inflation and economic stability.

Why is this in the news?

On October 15, 2026, the rupee appreciated against the US dollar due to RBI's forex interventions amid rising inflation pressures.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What caused the rupee to appreciate recently?

  • RBI interventions. The rupee's 31 paise appreciation to 94.86 was supported by the Reserve Bank of India's structural forex interventions and liquidity-tightening measures.
  • Market factors. The unwinding of long dollar positions and reduced direct dollar demand from oil companies also contributed to the rupee's gain.
  • External pressures. Despite appreciation, ongoing external macroeconomic pressures are expected to limit further gains.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this rupee movement matter for the economy?

  • Inflation impact. India's WPI inflation accelerated to 11.39% and CPI inflation stood at 5.54%, creating dual inflation pressures that complicate RBI's monetary policy decisions.
  • Monetary policy stance. High inflation makes it difficult for the RBI to adopt a dovish stance; market expectations of rate hikes have increased.
  • Exchange rate and interest rates. A tighter monetary policy could support the rupee through favorable interest-rate differentials and carry flows.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does RBI manage forex market pressures?

  • Special dollar window. The RBI's special dollar window for oil companies helps ease immediate pressure on the spot forex market.
  • Liquidity tightening. Structural forex interventions combined with liquidity-tightening measures stabilize the rupee and manage exchange rate volatility.

Back to basics: Exchange Rate and Monetary Policy

The exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another, influenced by supply and demand in the forex market. The Reserve Bank of India manages the rupee's value through interventions and monetary policy tools like liquidity adjustments and interest rate changes. Inflation affects monetary policy decisions because high inflation may prompt rate hikes to stabilize prices and support currency value.
