India’s Agriculture Grows Over 4% Annually for First Post-Independence Decade

Prime Minister Modi announced that Indian agriculture achieved an average growth rate exceeding 4% annually over the past decade, the first time since Independence. He highlighted government support in subsidy absorption and efforts to modernize farming, which are key for rural poverty reduction and agricultural development.

India’s agricultural sector grew over 4% annually for the first decade since Independence, supported by government subsidies and modernization efforts.

Why is this in the news?

On November 12, 2026, PM Modi spoke at the Kisan Tak Bharat Agriculture Conference about the historic agricultural growth rate and government measures supporting farmers.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the significance of the 4% agricultural growth rate?

  • Historic growth milestone. For the first time since Independence, Indian agriculture has maintained an average growth rate exceeding 4% annually over a decade.
  • Poverty reduction link. This growth coincided with a large number of small farmers and agricultural labourers escaping poverty, as noted in a World Bank report cited by the Prime Minister.
  • Agriculture’s role in development. Agriculture is described as the foundation of social life and a key stream of strength for building a developed India.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How has the government supported agricultural growth and farmers?

  • Fertiliser subsidy. The Centre spent ₹16 lakh crore on fertiliser support over 12 years, providing DAP fertiliser at ₹1,300 per 50-kg bag despite international prices around ₹5,000.
  • Diesel price subsidy. Despite a ₹50 per litre international diesel price hike, the government raised prices by only ₹7 per litre, absorbing ₹43 per litre and bearing a monthly loss of about ₹14,000 crore.
  • Modernisation initiatives. Efforts include increasing soil testing labs to over 8,400, expanding Krishi Vigyan Kendras to over 7,500, providing 12 lakh solar pumps under PM-KUSUM, and promoting Farmer Producer Organisations.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the key outcomes and future directions for Indian agriculture?

  • Production achievements. India is the world’s top producer of milk, pulses, jute, rice, wheat, cotton, fruits, vegetables, fish, and millets, with milk production up 70%, egg production 90%, and fish production more than doubling in a decade.
  • Crop diversification. The Prime Minister emphasized moving beyond wheat, rice, and sugarcane to multiple income sources through crop diversification.
  • Natural farming promotion. The government supports natural farming techniques using cow manure, encouraging farmers to reduce chemical use to protect soil and environment.

Back to basics: Agricultural Growth and Subsidy Support

Agricultural growth rate measures the increase in agricultural output over time, reflecting sector health and rural economy strength. Subsidies like fertiliser support and fuel price absorption reduce input costs for farmers, helping stabilize production and incomes. Crop diversification and natural farming are strategies to improve sustainability and farmer resilience.
