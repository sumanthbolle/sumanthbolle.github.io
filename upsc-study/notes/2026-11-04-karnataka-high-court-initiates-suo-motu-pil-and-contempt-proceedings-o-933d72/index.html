Karnataka High Court Initiates Suo Motu PIL on Violence Against Advocates

The Karnataka High Court started a suo motu Public Interest Litigation (PIL) addressing recent violence against advocates, including murders and assaults. This development highlights the judiciary's proactive role in protecting legal professionals and ensuring the rule of law.

The Karnataka High Court initiated suo motu PIL and contempt proceedings to address violence against advocates and police misconduct.

Why is this in the news?

On November 3, 2026, the Karnataka High Court initiated suo motu PIL following multiple violent incidents against advocates in the state.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered the Karnataka High Court's suo motu PIL?

  • Violence incidents. The PIL was initiated after murders of advocates Sriram V. and Manjunath, and an attempted murder of advocate Venkataramanappa Sannakki in October 2026.
  • Representations received. The Court acted on representations from the Advocates’ Association, Bengaluru, and the Karnataka State Bar Council highlighting increasing violence and threats against lawyers.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What issues did the High Court identify regarding police conduct?

  • Police as 'parallel courts'. The Court noted grievances that police stations were settling civil disputes like property and contractual matters without statutory authority, violating Supreme Court judgments.
  • Disrespect to advocates. Complaints included disrespectful treatment of advocates at police stations, such as not providing even a chair.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What directions did the High Court give to the State government?

  • Notice to government. The Court ordered the State government to respond with measures to strengthen statutory protection for advocates.
  • Details on advocate murders. The government was asked to provide case-wise details including FIRs, arrests, evidence collected, charge sheet status, and reasons for any delays.

Back to basics: Suo Motu Public Interest Litigation

Suo motu PIL is a legal action initiated by a court on its own motion without a formal petition from an external party. It allows courts to address issues of public interest proactively, especially when fundamental rights or rule of law are at risk.
