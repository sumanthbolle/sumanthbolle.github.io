Centre sanctions new roads and bridges along Arunachal-China border

The Union Government has approved new infrastructure projects including roads and bridges along the Line of Actual Control (LAC) in Arunachal Pradesh amid ongoing tensions with China. These developments are significant for India's border management and security strategy against China.

The Centre sanctioned 26 new roads and multiple pedestrian and log bridges along the Arunachal Pradesh-China border to strengthen border infrastructure.

Why is this in the news?

On November 13, 2026, The Hindu reported that the Centre sanctioned new roads and bridges along the Arunachal Pradesh-China border following continuing face-offs with Chinese troops since July 2026.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What new infrastructure projects were sanctioned along the Arunachal-China border?

  • Road construction. The Cabinet Committee on Security cleared the third phase of the Indo-China Border Roads (ICBR-III) project, sanctioning 26 new roads along the LAC in Arunachal Pradesh.
  • Bridge construction. The Indo-Tibetan Border Police sanctioned the construction of 15 pedestrian suspension bridges and 18 log bridges in the Upper Subansiri and Kurung Kumey districts bordering China.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why were these infrastructure projects approved now?

  • Post-Galwan infrastructure push. This is the second major infrastructure push after the 2020 Galwan clashes, with earlier phases of ICBR initiated in 2005 and expanded in 2020 to improve connectivity along the border.
  • Ongoing border tensions. The projects follow continuing face-offs with Chinese troops in Arunachal Pradesh since July 2026, highlighting the need for better border infrastructure for patrols and security.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How do these projects impact India-China relations and border management?

  • Strategic significance. Improved roads and bridges enhance mobility for the Army and ITBP, enabling better surveillance and rapid deployment along the sensitive Arunachal border.
  • Diplomatic context. The infrastructure clearance came shortly after the 18th BRICS Summit in September 2026, where both India and China emphasized peace and tranquility in border areas.
  • Border talks. Despite three rounds of high-level border talks since July 2026, tensions persist, underscoring the importance of infrastructure for border preparedness.

Back to basics: Border Infrastructure and Security

Border infrastructure includes roads, bridges, and facilities that enable effective patrolling, logistics, and rapid deployment of security forces along international boundaries. In sensitive border areas, such infrastructure is crucial for maintaining territorial integrity and responding to incursions or conflicts.
