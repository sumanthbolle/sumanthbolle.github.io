RBI Money Market Operations and Liquidity Management in October 2026

The Reserve Bank of India conducted money market operations on October 14 and 15, 2026, involving repo, reverse repo, and standing deposit facilities to manage liquidity. Understanding these operations is crucial for UPSC aspirants as they reflect monetary policy implementation and influence economic stability.

RBI's money market operations in mid-October 2026 show active liquidity absorption through reverse repo and standing deposit facilities, impacting short-term interest rates and cash reserves.

Why is this in the news?

RBI released detailed data on money market operations for October 14 and 15, 2026, showing liquidity absorption measures and auction results.

What were the key RBI money market operations on October 14 and 15, 2026?

  • Liquidity absorption. On October 14, RBI absorbed ₹1,29,265 crore net liquidity through repo, reverse repo, MSF, SLF, and standing deposit facility operations; on October 15, net liquidity absorption was ₹1,88,553 crore.
  • Use of standing deposit facility. The standing deposit facility (SDF) was extensively used, with ₹1,05,429 crore on October 14 and ₹1,79,621 crore on October 15, reflecting RBI's tool to absorb excess liquidity without collateral.
  • Overnight variable rate reverse repo auction. On October 15, RBI conducted an overnight VRRR auction with a notified amount of ₹50,000 crore, receiving offers worth ₹9,562 crore, all accepted at a cut-off rate of 5.49%.

How do these operations affect the money market and economy?

  • Impact on short-term rates. Weighted average rates in the overnight segment ranged from 5.55% on October 14 to 5.70% on October 15, indicating RBI's influence in keeping rates within the policy corridor.
  • Cash reserve position. Scheduled commercial banks' cash balances with RBI were slightly below the average daily cash reserve requirement, showing tight liquidity conditions.
  • Monetary policy signalling. Through repo and reverse repo operations, RBI signals its stance on liquidity and inflation control, crucial for economic growth and stability.

What should UPSC aspirants remember about RBI's liquidity management tools?

  • Repo and reverse repo. Repo operations inject liquidity by lending to banks against collateral; reverse repo absorbs liquidity by borrowing from banks.
  • Standing Deposit Facility. SDF allows RBI to absorb liquidity without collateral, complementing reverse repo in liquidity management.
  • Marginal Standing Facility and Standing Liquidity Facility. MSF provides overnight funds to banks at a higher rate, while SLF includes refinance facilities extended by RBI.

Back to basics: Liquidity Adjustment Facility and Money Market Operations

The Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) is a monetary policy tool used by RBI to manage liquidity in the banking system through repo and reverse repo operations. Repo operations provide short-term funds to banks against collateral, injecting liquidity, while reverse repo absorbs excess liquidity by borrowing from banks. The Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) is another tool to absorb liquidity without collateral. These operations influence short-term interest rates and help RBI maintain price stability and economic growth.
