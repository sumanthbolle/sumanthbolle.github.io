Government Constitutes High Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat

The Government of India has set up a High Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat to review and recommend reforms in the banking sector. This committee aims to align banking practices with the goals of Viksit Bharat 2047, focusing on competitiveness, financial inclusion, and resilience.

The High Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat will review India's banking sector and suggest reforms to support the vision of Viksit Bharat 2047.

Why is this in the news?

The Government of India constituted the High Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat on October 30, 2026, as announced in the Budget 2026-27 by the Finance Minister.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the High Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat?

  • Committee formation. The Government of India formed the committee to review the banking sector and recommend reforms to make it globally competitive, financially inclusive, and resilient for Viksit Bharat 2047.
  • Leadership and members. Shri Suresh N. Patel, former Central Vigilance Commissioner, chairs the committee, with members including Shri Shirish Chandra Murmu (Deputy Governor, RBI), Smt. Padmaja Chunduru (Former MD & CEO, Indian Bank), and Shri N S Vishwanathan (Former Deputy Governor, RBI).
  • Tenure and secretariat. The committee has a tenure of six months, with the Joint Secretary (Banking), Department of Financial Services, serving as the Member Secretary.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the committee’s key objectives and terms of reference?

  • Assess banking practices. Evaluate current banking practices and prudential norms, identify global best practices, and adapt them for the Indian banking system to create globally competitive banks.
  • Review regulatory frameworks. Examine existing regulatory and legal frameworks for banks, including Regional Rural Banks and Cooperative Banks, to suggest reforms that improve efficiency and ease of banking.
  • Capital and credit growth. Assess long-term capital needs for banks, Development Financial Institutions, and Non-Banking Financial Companies, recommending funding sources and mechanisms to support credit growth.
  • Boost private investment. Analyze differences in cost of capital for businesses and suggest policy options to encourage private investment for sustainable economic growth.
  • AI and digital innovation. Develop a roadmap for AI and digital innovation in banking, including safeguards against cyber risks.
  • Talent and capacity building. Propose strategies for talent acquisition and capacity building to prepare a future-ready banking workforce.
  • Review priority schemes. Evaluate the Priority Sector Lending framework and Lead Bank Scheme for their relevance, effectiveness, and coordination in promoting inclusive and data-driven credit delivery.
  • Additional recommendations. Make recommendations on any other matters referred by the Government of India.

Back to basics: Banking Sector Reforms

Banking sector reforms involve changes in policies, regulations, and practices to improve the efficiency, stability, and inclusiveness of banks. These reforms aim to align the banking system with economic goals, enhance competitiveness, and protect against risks such as cyber threats. Committees and expert groups are often constituted to review the sector and recommend necessary changes.
