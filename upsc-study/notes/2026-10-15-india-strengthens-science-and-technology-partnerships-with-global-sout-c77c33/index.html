India Launches MEA-CSIR Tech Programme to Boost Global South Partnerships

India initiated the MEA–CSIR Tech Familiarization Programme on 14 October 2026 to strengthen science and technology ties with 29 countries from the Global South. This programme offers UPSC aspirants insights into India's science diplomacy and international collaboration efforts.

The MEA–CSIR Tech Familiarization Programme exemplifies India's strategy to enhance scientific cooperation and technology sharing with Global South nations.

Why is this in the news?

The Ministry of External Affairs and CSIR launched the inaugural MEA–CSIR Tech Familiarization Programme on 14 October 2026 to showcase India's scientific capabilities to diplomats from Global South countries.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the MEA–CSIR Tech Familiarization Programme?

  • Programme launch. The programme was launched on 14 October 2026 by CSIR in collaboration with the Ministry of External Affairs' New, Emerging and Strategic Technologies Division to strengthen science and technology partnerships with Global South countries.
  • Participants. It brought together 33 Ambassadors and senior embassy officials from 29 countries, providing them direct exposure to India's indigenous technologies and research infrastructure.
  • Objectives. The initiative aims to build stronger scientific linkages, identify collaborative research opportunities, facilitate technology transfer, and promote capacity building addressing shared developmental challenges.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Which institutions and technologies were showcased?

  • CSIR laboratories visited. Delegates visited two premier CSIR labs in New Delhi: the Central Road Research Institute (CSIR-CRRI) and the Institute of Genomics and Integrative Biology (CSIR-IGIB).
  • Technologies at CSIR-CRRI. The delegation saw advanced facilities like a Driving Simulator, Cyclic Load Test Facility, Steel Slag Laboratory, bio-binder technology models, and a live demonstration of Ecofix, a steel slag-based instant pothole repair mix.
  • Technologies at CSIR-IGIB. They were introduced to genome sequencing, biosafety labs, zebrafish research, biobanking, and Good Manufacturing Practice facilities, highlighting India's capabilities in genomics, biomedical research, and precision healthcare.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this programme matter for India’s foreign policy and science diplomacy?

  • Strengthening Global South ties. The programme fosters international partnerships by showcasing India's scientific strengths and promoting collaboration with developing countries sharing similar developmental challenges.
  • Platform for cooperation. By facilitating direct interaction between foreign diplomats and Indian scientists, it identifies practical cooperation areas and accelerates adoption of indigenous innovations.
  • Long-term engagement. It opens avenues for sustained scientific engagement, technology partnerships, knowledge exchange, and capacity building, reinforcing India's commitment to inclusive and sustainable development.

Back to basics: Science Diplomacy

Science diplomacy refers to the use of scientific collaborations among countries to address common problems and build constructive international partnerships. It involves sharing knowledge, technology, and research to promote peace, development, and mutual benefits. India uses science diplomacy to strengthen ties with developing countries by showcasing its indigenous innovations and research capabilities.
