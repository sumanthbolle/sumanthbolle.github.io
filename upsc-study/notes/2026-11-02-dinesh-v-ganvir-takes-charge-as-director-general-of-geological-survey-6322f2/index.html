Dinesh V. Ganvir Assumes Charge as Director General of Geological Survey of India

Shri Dinesh V. Ganvir took charge as the 55th Director General of the Geological Survey of India (GSI) on November 1, 2026. His leadership focuses on enhancing mineral security, scientific excellence, and technological advancement, which are critical for India's resource management and development.

Dinesh V. Ganvir became the 55th Director General of GSI, emphasizing mineral exploration and scientific innovation to support India's mineral security and Atmanirbhar Bharat vision.

Why is this in the news?

On November 1, 2026, Shri Dinesh V. Ganvir assumed charge as the Director General of the Geological Survey of India, marking a leadership change with renewed focus on mineral security and technological advancement.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Who is Dinesh V. Ganvir and what is his background?

  • Extensive experience. Shri Dinesh V. Ganvir has over 32 years of experience in geological investigations, mineral exploration, engineering geology, and institutional leadership within GSI.
  • Career progression. He joined GSI on July 1, 1994, after completing his Master's in Geology from RTMNU University, Nagpur, and served in various scientific, technical, and administrative roles across multiple regions.
  • Previous roles. Before becoming Director General, he was Additional Director General and Head of Department, Central Region, Nagpur, and also held the additional charge of National Mission Head-II.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are his contributions and priorities as Director General?

  • Contributions to mineral exploration. He led a five-fold increase in investigations for critical and strategic minerals and facilitated the handover of 359 Geological Reports and 377 Geological Memorandums, supporting mineral auctions.
  • Focus on mineral security. Ganvir aims to intensify exploration for critical and strategic minerals using integrated geoscientific investigations and advanced mineral prospectivity mapping to reduce import dependence.
  • Technological advancement. He emphasizes harnessing emerging technologies, advanced data analytics, strengthening laboratory infrastructure, and upgrading analytical capabilities to improve exploration precision and efficiency.
  • Broader scientific roles. Beyond mineral exploration, GSI under his leadership will enhance research in landslide early warning, glacial and cryospheric studies, infrastructure projects, and hazard assessment.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this leadership change matter for India’s development and governance?

  • Supports Atmanirbhar Bharat. The intensified focus on critical mineral exploration aligns with India's goal of mineral security and self-reliance under the Atmanirbhar Bharat vision.
  • Informs policymaking and infrastructure. GSI’s scientific inputs on resource availability, hazard assessment, and infrastructure planning are vital for informed government decisions and sustainable development.
  • Institutional strengthening. Ganvir stresses empowering scientific personnel, fostering innovation, recognizing merit, and improving coordination among GSI units to enhance organizational effectiveness.

Back to basics: Geological Survey of India (GSI)

The Geological Survey of India is a government agency under the Ministry of Mines responsible for conducting geological surveys and mineral exploration across India. It provides scientific data and reports that support mineral resource management, infrastructure development, disaster preparedness, and environmental studies. GSI's work is crucial for identifying mineral reserves, assessing geological hazards, and aiding sustainable development planning.
