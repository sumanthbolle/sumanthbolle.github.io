NITI Aayog and TEXMiN Collaborate to Strengthen India's Critical Minerals Ecosystem

NITI Aayog and TEXMiN signed a Joint Statement of Intent to enhance India's capabilities in critical and strategic minerals, focusing on research, innovation, and sustainable value chains. This collaboration supports the National Critical Mineral Mission and India's vision of technological self-reliance and Viksit Bharat 2047.

The partnership between NITI Aayog and TEXMiN aims to build indigenous technology and resilient supply chains for critical minerals vital to India's strategic and industrial growth.

Why is this in the news?

On 3 November 2026, NITI Aayog and TEXMiN signed a Joint Statement of Intent to strengthen collaboration in India's critical minerals sector, aligning with the National Critical Mineral Mission and the vision of Viksit Bharat 2047.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the collaboration between NITI Aayog and TEXMiN about?

  • Joint Statement of Intent. NITI Aayog and TEXMiN signed a Joint Statement of Intent to advance indigenous technological capabilities, promote research and innovation, and develop resilient and sustainable critical mineral value chains in India.
  • Key areas of cooperation. The collaboration focuses on collaborative research and technology development, technical and strategic studies, policy and institutional support, and capacity building and knowledge exchange.
  • Alignment with national missions. This initiative aligns with the National Critical Mineral Mission and supports India's goals of technological self-reliance and Viksit Bharat 2047.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why are critical minerals important for India?

  • Strategic enablers. Critical minerals are essential for national security, technological self-reliance, and achieving India's vision of Viksit Bharat 2047.
  • Integrated value chain approach. The focus is on moving beyond a mine-centric approach to an integrated value chain that includes exploration, processing, refining, advanced materials, and recycling.
  • Secondary resource potential. There is significant potential in recovering critical minerals from secondary sources such as coal fly ash, red mud, mine tailings, and electronic waste.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How will the collaboration impact India's critical minerals ecosystem?

  • Strengthening indigenous capabilities. The partnership aims to build indigenous technological capabilities through research, innovation, and industry collaboration.
  • Facilitating technology translation. It promotes translating research into commercially viable technologies and sustainable resource utilization.
  • Enhancing linkages. The initiative fosters stronger connections among government, academia, research institutions, and industry to build resilient critical mineral supply chains.

Back to basics: Critical Minerals and Value Chains

Critical minerals are essential raw materials required for advanced technologies and national security. Their value chain includes exploration, extraction, processing, refining, manufacturing of advanced materials, and recycling. Developing a resilient and sustainable value chain reduces dependency on imports and supports technological self-reliance.
