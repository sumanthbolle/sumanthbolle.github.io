Major Cross-Border Drug Network Busted with Multiple Arrests and Seizures

The Central Bureau of Narcotics (CBN) Madhya Pradesh Unit seized 2,774.650 kg of illicit poppy straw and arrested four persons in coordinated operations. The Enforcement Directorate (ED) arrested four more accused, including a Myanmar national, in connection with an international cross-border narco-hawala network involving ₹11.44 crore.

Coordinated enforcement by CBN and ED dismantled a major cross-border drug trafficking and narco-hawala network involving large seizures and multiple arrests.

Why is this in the news?

On 31 October 2026, the CBN Madhya Pradesh Unit announced major seizures of poppy straw and arrests, while the ED arrested four accused in a cross-border narco-hawala network involving Myanmar.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What were the major drug seizures and arrests by the CBN Madhya Pradesh Unit?

  • Three major operations. The CBN Madhya Pradesh Unit conducted three significant operations in late October 2026, seizing a total of 2,774.650 kg of illicit poppy straw and five vehicles.
  • Largest seizure details. On 27 October, a Rajasthan-registered container truck was intercepted on the Mumbai–Delhi Expressway near Sitamau carrying 2,308 kg of poppy straw concealed under chana husk sacks; one person was arrested.
  • Second operation details. On the night of 28-29 October, a Mahindra pickup truck and a Mahindra Bolero car were intercepted in Mandsaur district, recovering 416.560 kg of poppy straw; one person was arrested and another fled.
  • Third operation details. On 28 October, a Hyundai Grand i10 car and a TVS motorcycle were stopped on the Fatehgarh–Nayakheda–Pipliya Mandi route, recovering 50.090 kg of poppy straw; two persons were arrested.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What did the Enforcement Directorate uncover in the cross-border narco-hawala network?

  • Arrests and network scope. The ED arrested four accused, including a Myanmar national, linked to an international narco-hawala network involved in drug trafficking and money laundering.
  • Key persons and roles. Chintuang, a Myanmar-based supplier, was linked to a 49.1 kg methamphetamine seizure in August 2025; Lalrammuana was the main handler; Jabrul Haque the receiver at the Damchhera-Kanhmun border; Abu Saleh operated accounts receiving ₹11.44 crore.
  • Hawala operations and transactions. The network converted Indian currency into Myanmarese Kyat at daily rates of about 42 to 48 Kyat per Rupee, with individual transactions up to ₹54.47 lakh a day and cumulative transactions exceeding 43 crore Kyat.
  • Use of drug money. Drug money was converted into ordinary goods like flour, edible oil, fuel, groceries, medicines, footwear, and cloth, which were sent to Myanmar, masking the illicit funds.
  • Asset seizures. Cash books, registers, handwritten accounts, seven signed ₹10 notes used as payment tokens, an immovable property purchased for ₹60 lakh, and 991 bank accounts linked to the network were seized or frozen.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for internal security and India’s neighbourhood relations?

  • Cross-border drug trafficking threat. The bust exposes the scale and sophistication of drug trafficking networks operating between India and Myanmar, threatening internal security and public health.
  • Narco-hawala and money laundering. The network’s use of hawala channels and conversion of drug money into goods complicates detection and enforcement, highlighting challenges in financial crime control.
  • Multi-agency enforcement importance. Coordinated actions by CBN, ED, and Narcotics Control Bureau demonstrate the need for integrated efforts to dismantle such transnational criminal networks.

Back to basics: Narco-hawala and cross-border drug trafficking

Narco-hawala is an informal money transfer system used to launder drug money by converting it into local currency or goods, often bypassing formal banking channels. Cross-border drug trafficking involves illegal cultivation, production, and movement of narcotic substances across national borders, posing challenges to law enforcement and national security. Agencies like the Central Bureau of Narcotics and Enforcement Directorate coordinate to detect, seize contraband, and arrest offenders under laws such as the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act.
