NITI Aayog Releases Report on Impact of Production Linked Incentive Schemes

NITI Aayog, with IIM Ahmedabad, released a report assessing the impact of Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes across 14 sectors. The report evaluates investment, production, exports, and employment effects, highlighting sectors on track and areas needing focus.

The PLI schemes have stimulated investment and production in key sectors, advancing India's manufacturing competitiveness and export potential.

Why is this in the news?

On 12 November 2026, NITI Aayog released a detailed report on the impact assessment of PLI schemes, prepared with IIM Ahmedabad as knowledge partner.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes and their objectives?

  • Flagship policy intervention. PLI schemes are government initiatives providing production-linked incentives to select high-performing firms to boost manufacturing competitiveness and integrate Indian firms into global value chains.
  • Self-sustaining design. The schemes encourage adoption of advanced technologies, capacity creation, and sustained growth in output and exports without ongoing subsidies.
  • Multi-sector implementation. Implemented by various Ministries and Departments, PLI schemes cover 14 notified sectors aiming to scale up domestic manufacturing, investment, production, and exports.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What does the NITI Aayog report reveal about the impact of PLI schemes?

  • Sector-wise assessment. The report assesses investment, additional production, exports, and employment generation across all 14 sectors using firm-level data and industry inputs.
  • Categorization of sector outcomes. Sectors are grouped into On-Track, Partial Impact, Insignificant Evidence of Impact, and Data Unavailable to provide a comparative overview.
  • Sectors with strong outcomes. Pharmaceuticals, Mobile Manufacturing under Large Scale Electronics Manufacturing, Food Processing, Telecom, White Goods, Automobiles, and Drones show notable progress in investment and capacity creation.
  • Emerging technology sectors. Sectors like ACC Batteries, Specialty Steel, and Technical Textiles are gradually developing capacity and ecosystems, expected to contribute to long-term industrial growth.
  • Renewable energy manufacturing. High Efficiency Solar PV Modules sector has expanded manufacturing capacity significantly, strengthening India's renewable energy manufacturing ecosystem.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does the PLI scheme matter for India's industrial growth and policy?

  • Catalyst for industrial deepening. PLI schemes stimulate investment, enhance manufacturing capacity, and strengthen industrial competitiveness, advancing the vision of Viksit Bharat 2047.
  • Employment and export expansion. The initiative acts as a catalyst for employment generation and export growth by building domestic capabilities.
  • Need for MSME participation. The report highlights the importance of deeper MSME participation and harmonized data systems to strengthen sectoral outcomes.
  • Long-term effectiveness factors. Sustained growth depends on deepening domestic value addition and strengthening supply chains.

Back to basics: Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme

PLI schemes provide financial incentives to companies based on their incremental production to encourage domestic manufacturing. They aim to make Indian industries globally competitive by promoting investment, technology adoption, and export growth. The schemes cover multiple sectors and are designed to be self-sustaining without continuous subsidies.

What happens next?

  • Enhance MSME involvement. The report stresses the need for deeper participation of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises to broaden the scheme's impact.
  • Improve data systems. Harmonized and robust data collection and sharing mechanisms are necessary to better track and support sectoral progress.
  • Focus on ecosystem development. Sustained development of industrial ecosystems is crucial for long-term manufacturing growth and competitiveness.
Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes: 1. PLI schemes provide incentives to firms based on their incremental production to boost manufacturing competitiveness. 2. The NITI Aayog report categorizes sector outcomes into On-Track, Partial Impact, Insignificant Evidence of Impact, and Data Unavailable. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). A trap could be confusing the basis of incentives; PLI schemes link incentives to production increments, not just investment or exports.
