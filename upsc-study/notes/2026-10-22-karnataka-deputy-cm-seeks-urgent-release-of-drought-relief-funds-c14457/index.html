Karnataka Deputy CM urges urgent release of ₹20,585.73 crore drought relief

Karnataka's Deputy Chief Minister G. Parameshwara has written to the Prime Minister, Union Home Minister, and Agriculture Minister seeking quick approval and release of ₹20,585.73 crore in drought relief under the National Disaster Response Fund. This is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights disaster management, cooperative federalism, and welfare schemes for vulnerable farmers amid severe drought conditions.

Karnataka seeks urgent central assistance of ₹20,585.73 crore for drought relief to support affected farmers and mitigate economic losses.

Why is this in the news?

On October 22, 2026, Karnataka's Deputy Chief Minister wrote to central leaders seeking urgent release of drought relief funds amid severe rainfall deficit and crop damage.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the drought situation in Karnataka?

  • Rainfall deficit. Karnataka recorded a 35% rainfall deficit during the 2026 southwest monsoon, receiving just 551 mm against the normal 852 mm, marking the second-lowest seasonal rainfall since 1956.
  • Extent of drought. The state government declared drought in 226 taluks with crop damage reported across 57.93 lakh hectares, including 53.92 lakh hectares of agriculture crops and 4.01 lakh hectares of horticulture crops.
  • Economic loss. The estimated economic loss due to drought, based on cultivation costs, stands at ₹62,481.88 crore.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What drought relief funds has Karnataka sought from the Centre?

  • Total assistance requested. Karnataka has submitted a cumulative drought memorandum seeking ₹20,585.73 crore in Central assistance under the National Disaster Response Fund.
  • Breakdown of funds. The request includes ₹6,128.70 crore for crop loss input subsidy, ₹12,146.99 crore for gratuitous relief to 61.91 lakh small and marginal farmers, ₹1,727 crore for drinking water supply, and ₹583.04 crore for animal husbandry and livestock protection.
  • Additional assistance. The state also sought ₹427.20 crore under PM POSHAN for nutritious meals during summer vacations in drought-affected taluks and ₹70.96 crore under the Supplementary Nutrition Programme for vulnerable beneficiaries.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is the release of drought relief funds urgent and what challenges are highlighted?

  • Need for immediate relief. The Deputy CM emphasized the urgent need for financial relief to small and marginal farmers to mitigate the severe impact of drought.
  • Concerns over outdated data. Use of outdated Agricultural Census 2015–16 data for input subsidy assessment could cause a shortfall of about ₹1,669.50 crore; Karnataka urged the Centre to consider its updated FRUITS database for fairer assessment.
  • Call for cooperative federalism. The Deputy CM appealed for coordinated action between the Union and State governments, transcending political differences, to expedite approval and release of funds.

Back to basics: Disaster Management and National Disaster Response Fund

Disaster management involves preparedness, mitigation, and relief measures to reduce the impact of natural calamities like droughts. The National Disaster Response Fund (NDRF) is a central fund used to provide immediate relief to states affected by disasters, including droughts, ensuring timely financial assistance for affected populations and sectors.
