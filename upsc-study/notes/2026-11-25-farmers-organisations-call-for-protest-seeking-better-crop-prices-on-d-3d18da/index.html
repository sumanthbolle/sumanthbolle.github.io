Farmers’ Organisations Protest for Better Crop Prices on December 2

Farmers’ groups in Haryana have called for a protest on December 2 demanding remunerative prices for bajra and sugarcane crops. This issue is important for understanding agricultural marketing, MSP policy, and farmer welfare in India.

Farmers in Haryana are protesting for procurement at announced MSP and higher sugarcane prices due to inadequate returns and rising input costs.

Why is this in the news?

Farmers’ organisations announced a protest on December 2, 2026, demanding better crop prices amid dissatisfaction with current MSP procurement and sugarcane pricing in Haryana.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the farmers demanding in the December 2 protest?

  • Bajra price demand. Bajra growers demand procurement at the announced MSP of ₹2,900 per quintal, as government procurement has been limited to just 420 quintals despite high production.
  • Sugarcane price demand. Sugarcane farmers reject the ₹15 per quintal hike by the Haryana government, demanding ₹600 per quintal in line with the Swaminathan Commission’s recommendations.
  • Compensation and procurement. Farmers allege they have not received compensation under the Bhavantar Bharpai Yojana and demand reopening of the e-Kharid portal until December 15 for procurement at MSP.
  • Support for commission agents. Farmers also seek a 2.5% commission for commission agents involved in crop sales.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why are farmers protesting for better crop prices?

  • Inadequate returns. Farmers face inadequate prices for bajra and sugarcane, with bajra sold below MSP and sugarcane price hikes considered insufficient given rising input costs.
  • Rising input costs. Prices of diesel, fertilisers, labour, and electricity have increased, but crop prices have not kept pace, squeezing farmers’ incomes.
  • Limited government procurement. Despite Haryana producing 1.3 million metric tonnes of bajra in 2025-26, government procurement at MSP has been negligible, forcing farmers to sell at lower prices.
  • Political criticism. Farmers have criticised the Haryana Chief Minister for ignoring their interests while seeking votes in Punjab.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How are farmers organising their protest and demands?

  • Joint front and panchayats. A joint front of farmer organisations has called for a demonstration in Kurukshetra, and a panchayat organised by the Samyukt Kisan Morcha rejected the sugarcane price hike.
  • Memorandum submission. Protesters submitted a memorandum to the Haryana Chief Minister demanding higher sugarcane prices and warned of sugar mill shutdowns if demands are unmet.
  • Agitation intensification. Farmer organisations led by Bharatiya Kisan Union (Haryana) plan to intensify agitation due to the gap between rising input costs and stagnant crop prices.

Back to basics: Minimum Support Price (MSP)

MSP is a government-declared price at which it procures crops from farmers to ensure them a minimum profit and protect against price fluctuations. It aims to provide farmers with remunerative prices and reduce distress sales. Procurement at MSP is a key policy tool in agricultural marketing and farmer welfare.

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because farmers rejected the ₹15 per quintal hike, demanding ₹600 per quintal instead.

Mains question (15 marks)

Discuss the significance of Minimum Support Price (MSP) and government procurement in protecting farmers’ incomes, with reference to recent protests in Haryana.

Discuss: Explain the role of MSP and procurement in farmer welfare and analyse the challenges highlighted by the Haryana protests.

Intro: MSP and government procurement are key tools to ensure remunerative prices for farmers and prevent distress sales.

  • MSP provides a price floor to protect farmers from market fluctuations and supports income stability.
  • Government procurement at MSP ensures market access and price assurance, especially for staple crops like bajra.
  • The Haryana protests reveal challenges such as limited procurement despite high production and inadequate compensation schemes.
  • Rising input costs without corresponding price increases squeeze farmers’ incomes, leading to demands for higher MSP and better procurement.

Conclusion: Effective MSP and procurement policies are essential for farmer welfare but require proper implementation and responsiveness to input cost changes.
