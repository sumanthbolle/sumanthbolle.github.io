WMO Warns Current El Nino Could Be Unprecedented in Strength

The World Meteorological Organization (WMO) has stated that the ongoing El Nino event in the tropical Pacific may reach sea surface temperatures 3.8 degrees Celsius above average, a level never recorded in the past four decades. This event is important for UPSC aspirants as it affects global and regional climate patterns, impacting agriculture, water resources, and disaster management.

The WMO has warned that the current El Nino could be the strongest on record, with sea surface temperatures rising 3.8°C above average in the tropical Pacific.

Why is this in the news?

On October 21, 2026, the WMO released a seasonal climate outlook warning that the current El Nino event could be unprecedented in strength, with sea surface temperatures rising significantly above average.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current El Nino event and how strong is it?

  • Unprecedented strength. The WMO stated that sea surface temperatures in the tropical Pacific may rise to 3.8 degrees Celsius above average during November 2026 to January 2027, which is far above the 0.5 degrees Celsius anomaly that defines El Nino.
  • Definition of El Nino. El Nino is a phase of the El Nino Southern Oscillation (ENSO) characterized by warmer sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean, disrupting normal temperature and rainfall patterns globally.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this El Nino event matter for climate and society?

  • Global climate impacts. A very strong El Nino can cause severe droughts in some regions, heavy rainfall and flooding in others, and increased heat across large parts of the world, affecting agriculture, water supply, and disaster preparedness.
  • Long-lasting effects. The WMO Secretary-General noted that while El Nino will peak towards the end of 2026, its impacts will continue to be felt long after that period.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How is El Nino monitored and classified?

  • Temperature anomaly threshold. The WMO declares an El Nino event when the sea surface temperature anomaly in the tropical Pacific reaches 0.5 degrees Celsius above average.
  • ENSO phases. ENSO has three phases: El Nino (warming), La Nina (cooling), and a neutral phase, each influencing global weather patterns differently.

Back to basics: El Nino Southern Oscillation (ENSO)

ENSO is a climate phenomenon involving periodic fluctuations in sea surface temperatures and atmospheric pressure in the tropical Pacific Ocean. It has three phases: El Nino (warmer sea surface temperatures), La Nina (cooler sea surface temperatures), and a neutral phase. These phases influence global weather patterns, including rainfall and temperature variations, affecting agriculture, water resources, and disaster risks worldwide.
