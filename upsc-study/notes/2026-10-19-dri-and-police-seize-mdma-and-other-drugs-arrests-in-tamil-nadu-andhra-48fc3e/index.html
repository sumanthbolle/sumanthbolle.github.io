DRI and Police Seize MDMA and Crystal Meth in Southern India Drug Busts

The Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI) seized 137 kg of crystal methamphetamine in Tamil Nadu, disrupting a coastal drug trafficking network. Police in Vijayawada arrested a woman and seized about 100 grams of MDMA during a railway search, highlighting ongoing drug smuggling challenges in southern India.

DRI and police operations in Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh seized large quantities of banned drugs, exposing organized trafficking networks and enforcing the NDPS Act.

Why is this in the news?

In October 2026, DRI seized 137 kg of crystal meth in Tamil Nadu and police arrested a woman with 100 grams of MDMA in Vijayawada, exposing drug smuggling networks in southern India.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What happened in the Tamil Nadu drug seizure?

  • DRI operation details. The Directorate of Revenue Intelligence seized 137 kg of crystal methamphetamine from a coastal village in Nagapattinam district, Tamil Nadu, during 16–17 October 2026.
  • Network disruption. The seizure dismantled a coastal drug trafficking network attempting to smuggle the narcotic to Sri Lanka via mid-sea transfer.
  • Legal action. Four persons were arrested under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985, and remanded to judicial custody.
  • Additional seizures. Rs. 10 lakh in cash and vehicles used for transporting the drugs were also seized.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What occurred in the Vijayawada MDMA case?

  • Arrest and seizure. Police arrested a woman named Y. Naga Sowmya and seized about 100 grams of MDMA during searches on the Bengaluru-Howrah Express at Vijayawada railway station.
  • Investigation status. Authorities are investigating the extent of her involvement in drug peddling and the drug racket.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why do these drug seizures matter for internal security?

  • Disrupting trafficking routes. The Tamil Nadu seizure prevented the use of India's coastline as a corridor for illicit narcotics trafficking to Sri Lanka.
  • Enforcement of NDPS Act. These operations demonstrate enforcement of the NDPS Act, 1985, aimed at controlling banned substances.
  • Highlighting drug smuggling challenges. The arrests and seizures reveal ongoing challenges in combating drug smuggling via coastal and railway routes in southern India.

Back to basics: Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985

The NDPS Act, 1985 is an Indian law that prohibits the production, manufacture, possession, sale, purchase, transport, warehousing, use, consumption, import inter-State, export inter-State, import into India, export from India or transshipment of narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances. It aims to control and regulate operations related to these substances to prevent drug abuse and trafficking.
