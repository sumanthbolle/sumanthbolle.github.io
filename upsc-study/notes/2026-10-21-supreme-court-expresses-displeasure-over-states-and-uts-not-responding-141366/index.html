Supreme Court Criticizes States for Non-Compliance on Menstrual Hygiene Judgment

The Supreme Court expressed displeasure over States and Union Territories not responding to the Centre on implementing its judgment that declared menstrual hygiene a fundamental right under Article 21. This highlights judicial oversight on public health and governance, relevant for understanding the judiciary's role in social welfare.

The Supreme Court has emphasized that menstrual hygiene is a fundamental right under Article 21 and criticized States and UTs for not reporting on its implementation.

Why is this in the news?

On October 13, 2026, the Supreme Court expressed displeasure over States and UTs not responding to the Centre about implementing its January 30 judgment on menstrual hygiene as a fundamental right under Article 21.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court rule about menstrual hygiene?

  • Fundamental right under Article 21. The Supreme Court declared on January 30, 2026, that menstrual hygiene and menstrual health are facets of the right to life under Article 21 of the Constitution.
  • Judgment directives for schools. The Court directed States and UTs to ensure all schools have functional, gender-segregated toilets with water connectivity and provide free sanitary napkins to girl students.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why did the Supreme Court express displeasure over States and UTs?

  • Lack of response to Centre. Despite the Centre's efforts, many States and UTs have not responded with information on steps taken to implement the Court's judgment.
  • Concern over implementation. The Court questioned how the fundamental right to menstrual health can be effective if States and UTs do not act on the judgment, warning of contempt notices for non-compliance.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What steps has the Centre taken and what is expected next?

  • Centre's coordination role. The Centre has taken the issue seriously and urged States and UTs to report on implementation, including construction of gender-segregated toilets.
  • Next hearing and compliance deadline. The Court gave a final opportunity to States and UTs to comply and scheduled the next hearing for December 14, 2026.

Back to basics: Right to Life under Article 21

Article 21 of the Indian Constitution guarantees the protection of life and personal liberty. The Supreme Court has interpreted this right expansively to include various aspects essential to human dignity, such as health and sanitation. Declaring menstrual hygiene as part of Article 21 means it is a fundamental right that the State must protect and promote.

What happens next?

  • Enforcement of Judgment. The Supreme Court has directed the Centre to obtain detailed reports from States and UTs on implementation and warned of contempt proceedings for non-compliance, emphasizing judicial enforcement of social welfare rights.

Answer and trap

Answer: (c). A trap would be to confuse the Court's role with the Centre's role; the judgment and directions were issued by the Supreme Court, not the Centre.
