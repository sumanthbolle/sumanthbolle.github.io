RBI Announces ₹33,000 Crore Government Securities Auction on November 27, 2026

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) announced an underwriting auction for the sale of Government Securities worth ₹33,000 crore on November 27, 2026. This auction is significant for understanding government borrowing, debt management, and monetary policy operations.

RBI's auction of ₹33,000 crore in Government Securities on November 27, 2026, reflects ongoing government debt management and monetary policy implementation.

Why is this in the news?

On November 26, 2026, the RBI announced the underwriting auction for Government Securities worth ₹33,000 crore to be held on November 27, 2026.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What is the Government Securities auction announced by RBI?

  • Auction details. The Government of India will sell Government Securities worth ₹33,000 crore through an underwriting auction conducted by RBI on November 27, 2026.
  • Securities involved. The auction includes four securities: 6.20% GS 2029 (₹9,000 crore), 6.57% GS 2033 (₹12,000 crore), 7.63% GS 2056 (₹9,000 crore), and 7.50% GOI SGrB 2056 (₹3,000 crore).
  • Auction method and timing. The auction will be conducted using a multiple price-based method, with bids submitted electronically via RBI's e-Kuber system between 9:00 AM and 9:30 AM on the auction day.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Why does this Government Securities auction matter for the exam?

  • Government borrowing. The auction reflects the government's method of raising funds to meet its expenditure through debt instruments.
  • Debt management. Understanding such auctions helps explain how the government manages its debt maturity profile and interest costs.
  • Monetary policy operations. RBI's role in conducting these auctions links to its function in regulating liquidity and implementing monetary policy.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

How are Primary Dealers involved in the auction?

  • Underwriting commitments. Primary Dealers (PDs) have minimum underwriting commitments and minimum bidding commitments under the Additional Competitive Underwriting auction for each security.
  • Commission and settlement. The underwriting commission is credited to PDs' current accounts with RBI on the issue day of the securities.

Back to basics: Government Securities and Auctions

Government Securities (G-Secs) are debt instruments issued by the government to borrow money from the public. Auctions are the process by which these securities are sold to investors, often including Primary Dealers who underwrite the issue. The Reserve Bank of India conducts these auctions to manage government borrowing and influence liquidity in the economy.
