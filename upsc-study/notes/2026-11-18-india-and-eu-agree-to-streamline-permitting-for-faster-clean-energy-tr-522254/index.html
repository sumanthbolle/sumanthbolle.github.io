India and EU Collaborate to Accelerate Wind Energy Transition

India is expanding its wind energy capacity with a target of 156 GW by 2036, supported by international cooperation including with the EU. This cooperation aims to streamline permitting and enhance technology transfer for a faster clean energy transition, relevant for UPSC topics on energy policy and international relations.

India's wind energy expansion and EU cooperation exemplify efforts to achieve sustainable energy targets through technology and regulatory collaboration.

Why is this in the news?

On 18 November 2026, the Press Information Bureau detailed India's wind energy growth and its cooperation with the EU and other countries to accelerate clean energy transition.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the current status of India's wind energy sector?

  • Installed capacity. As of 30 September 2026, India’s total installed renewable energy capacity was 299.25 GW, with wind power accounting for 59.20 GW, about 19.8% of the total renewable capacity.
  • Growth trajectory. Installed wind capacity grew nearly 2.8 times from 21 GW in March 2014 to 59.20 GW in September 2026, reflecting its increasing role in India’s energy mix.
  • Wind potential. India’s gross wind power potential is estimated at 695.5 GW at 120 metres and 1,163.9 GW at 150 metres above ground, concentrated mainly in eight states including Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, and Karnataka.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How is India promoting wind energy development?

  • Government initiatives. Key interventions include ₹6,853 crore Viability Gap Funding for offshore projects, Generation Based Incentive schemes, Contracts for Difference pilot projects, and a task force addressing regulatory and transmission challenges.
  • Technological and manufacturing growth. Wind turbine manufacturing capacity increased from 10 GW in 2014 to about 24 GW by March 2026, with 70-80% indigenisation of components and strong domestic supply chains.
  • Grid integration. Promotion of hybrid and Round-the-Clock Renewable Energy projects and AI-based forecasting tools are being implemented to improve grid efficiency and reliability.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What role does international cooperation play in India’s wind energy transition?

  • Partnerships with EU and others. India is strengthening cooperation with the EU, UK, Denmark, Belgium, and Germany focusing on offshore wind deployment, advanced technology transfer, grid integration, and policy design.
  • Specific collaborations. The India–UK Offshore Wind Taskforce launched in February 2026 targets market design and supply chains; India-Belgium cooperation includes offshore wind research; and India-Denmark agreements cover power system modelling and expert training.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Energy policy relevance. India’s wind energy expansion is a key example of sustainable development and renewable energy policy, important for GS Paper 3 topics on environment and economy.
  • International relations. The cooperation with the EU and other countries illustrates India’s engagement in global clean energy partnerships, relevant for GS Paper 2 on India’s foreign policy and global groupings.

Back to basics: Renewable Energy and Wind Power

Renewable energy comes from natural sources that replenish quickly, such as wind, solar, and hydro. Wind power generates electricity by converting the kinetic energy of wind using turbines. It is a clean alternative to fossil fuels, helping reduce carbon emissions and enhance energy security. Governments often support wind energy through policies, incentives, and international cooperation to meet climate goals.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about India's wind energy sector: 1. India’s installed wind power capacity was 59.20 GW as of September 2026. 2. The India–UK Offshore Wind Taskforce was launched in 2019 to promote offshore wind development. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the India–UK Offshore Wind Taskforce was launched in February 2026, not 2019.
