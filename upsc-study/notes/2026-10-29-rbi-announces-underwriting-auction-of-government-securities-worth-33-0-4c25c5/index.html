RBI Announces Underwriting Auction of Government Securities Worth ₹33,000 Crore

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) announced an underwriting auction for the sale of Government Securities worth ₹33,000 crore on October 30, 2026. This auction is significant for understanding government borrowing operations and monetary policy instruments relevant to the economy.

RBI conducts an underwriting auction to sell ₹33,000 crore of government securities, reflecting government borrowing and monetary policy mechanisms.

Why is this in the news?

On October 29, 2026, RBI announced an underwriting auction for Government Securities worth ₹33,000 crore to be held on October 30, 2026.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What is the underwriting auction of Government Securities?

  • Definition. An underwriting auction is a process where Primary Dealers (PDs) commit to buying a minimum amount of government securities, ensuring the government raises the required funds.
  • Auction details. The auction for ₹33,000 crore government securities will be conducted using a multiple price-based method on October 30, 2026.
  • Role of Primary Dealers. Each Primary Dealer has a Minimum Underwriting Commitment (MUC) and a minimum bidding commitment under Additional Competitive Underwriting (ACU) auction, ensuring participation and liquidity.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Which Government Securities are involved and their commitments?

  • Securities and notified amounts. The auction includes four securities: 6.20% GS 2029 (₹9,000 crore), 6.57% GS 2033 (₹12,000 crore), 7.63% GS 2056 (₹9,000 crore), and 7.50% GOI SGrB 2056 (₹3,000 crore).
  • Minimum Underwriting Commitment. For example, each PD must underwrite ₹215 crore for 6.20% GS 2029 and ₹286 crore for 6.57% GS 2033, ensuring minimum participation.
  • Bidding process. PDs submit bids electronically via RBI’s e-Kuber system between 9:00 AM and 9:30 AM on auction day.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Why does this underwriting auction matter for the economy and exams?

  • Government borrowing. The auction reflects how the government raises funds to meet its fiscal needs, a key aspect of resource mobilisation.
  • Monetary policy instrument. Government securities auctions influence liquidity and interest rates, impacting monetary policy implementation.
  • Exam relevance. Understanding such auctions helps answer questions on fiscal policy, government budgeting, and the role of RBI in managing public debt.

Back to basics: Government Securities and Underwriting Auction

Government securities are debt instruments issued by the government to borrow money from the public. An underwriting auction involves Primary Dealers committing to buy a minimum amount of these securities, ensuring the government can raise the required funds. The Reserve Bank of India facilitates this process, which affects liquidity and interest rates in the economy.
