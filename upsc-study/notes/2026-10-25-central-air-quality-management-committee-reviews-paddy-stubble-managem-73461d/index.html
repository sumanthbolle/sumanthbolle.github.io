CAQM Reviews Paddy Stubble Management Measures in Punjab Districts

The Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) conducted high-level reviews of paddy stubble management in Punjab's districts including Ropar, Hoshiyarpur, Amritsar, and Tarn Taran in October 2026. These reviews focused on preventing stubble burning by assessing machinery availability, utilization of paddy straw in industries, and strengthening field-level monitoring.

CAQM's review of paddy stubble management in Punjab emphasizes machinery facilitation, industrial utilization, and strict monitoring to reduce stubble burning incidents.

Why is this in the news?

In October 2026, CAQM led by its Chairperson reviewed paddy stubble management efforts in Punjab districts to address increasing stubble burning and air pollution.

What measures did CAQM review for paddy stubble management?

  • Field visits and machinery assessment. CAQM's team visited thermal power plants, biomass plants, Krishi Vigyan Kendra, and farm fields to assess in-situ and ex-situ paddy straw management measures and machinery availability for farmers.
  • Industrial utilization of paddy straw. The Commission reviewed mechanisms for using paddy straw as fuel in thermal power plants, brick kilns, and industries, including inspection of industrial boilers using paddy stubble pellets.
  • Monitoring and coordination. CAQM emphasized proactive field-level monitoring, timely resolution of operational gaps, and strengthening coordination among farmers, Custom Hiring Centres, and machinery providers.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

Why is CAQM focusing on paddy stubble management in Punjab?

  • Rising stubble burning incidents. The Commission expressed concern over increased fire incidents in districts like Tarn Taran, directing intensified efforts to arrest and eliminate stubble burning.
  • Air pollution control. Paddy stubble burning significantly contributes to air pollution, and CAQM's measures aim to reduce this source through effective residue management and resource utilization.

[2] Press Information Bureau

How is CAQM ensuring effective implementation of stubble management?

  • Assigning sector-wise nodal officers. The Commission directed appointment of nodal officers responsible for ensuring actual utilization of paddy stubble by sectors and regular compliance monitoring.
  • Facilitating farmers with machinery. CAQM stressed maximizing facilitation to farmers by ensuring timely availability and deployment of Crop Residue Management (CRM) machinery during peak harvesting.
  • Accountability and strict action. The Commission warned that any laxity in taking effective measures against stubble burning would invite strict action and accountability.

Back to basics: Paddy Stubble Burning and Air Pollution

Paddy stubble burning is the practice of setting fire to the leftover stalks after harvesting rice crops. This releases large amounts of smoke and particulate matter, contributing to severe air pollution and health hazards. Managing paddy stubble through in-situ methods (like machinery that chops and incorporates residue into soil) and ex-situ methods (like using residue as fuel in industries) helps reduce pollution and promotes resource utilization.
