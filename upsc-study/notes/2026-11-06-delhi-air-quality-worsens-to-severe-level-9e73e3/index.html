Delhi Air Quality Deteriorates to Severe Pollution Level

Delhi's air quality worsened to a 'severe' level on November 6, 2026, affecting many parts of the city and nearby regions. This is important for UPSC aspirants to understand urban pollution dynamics, health impacts, and government monitoring systems.

Delhi experienced severe air pollution due to factors like meteorology, stubble burning, and local emissions, posing health risks and triggering government alerts.

Why is this in the news?

On November 6, 2026, official data showed Delhi's air quality worsening to a severe level, with many city areas and neighboring towns affected.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current air quality status in Delhi?

  • Severe pollution in many areas. On November 6, 2026, parts of Delhi including IIT Delhi, ITO, Dwarka, Rohini, Punjabi Bagh, Alipur, Bawana, and Jahangirpuri recorded 'severe' level air pollution.
  • Overall air quality category. The overall air quality of Delhi was in the 'very poor' category with a 24-hour average Air Quality Index (AQI) of 391 at 9 a.m. on November 6, 2026.
  • Neighboring cities affected. Nearby cities like Noida, Gurugram, and Faridabad also experienced 'very poor' air quality on the same day.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does Delhi’s air quality worsen in winter?

  • Meteorological factors. Lower wind speeds and a drop in temperature during winter reduce dispersion of pollutants, causing air quality to deteriorate.
  • Stubble burning and firecrackers. Pollution from stubble burning in October-November and firecracker bursting during festivals adds to existing pollution from vehicles and dust.
  • Seasonal spike pattern. Every winter, these factors combine to cause a spike in air pollution levels in Delhi-NCR and the Indo-Gangetic Plains.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the health and policy implications?

  • Health impact of severe pollution. 'Severe' level air pollution affects healthy people and seriously impacts those with existing diseases, according to the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB).
  • Government monitoring and warnings. The Central government’s 'Air Quality Early Warning System for Delhi' predicts the air quality to remain in the 'severe' category for three days.
  • Regulatory response. GRAP-3 curbs (Graded Response Action Plan) were imposed in Delhi-NCR to control pollution as air quality worsened.

Back to basics: Air Quality Index (AQI) and Pollution Levels

The Air Quality Index (AQI) is a numerical scale used to communicate how polluted the air currently is or how polluted it is forecast to become. It ranges from 'good' to 'severe' levels, with higher values indicating worse air quality. 'Severe' pollution means the air is harmful even to healthy individuals and poses serious health risks to vulnerable groups. Factors like weather conditions, emissions from vehicles, industries, and seasonal activities like crop burning affect AQI.
