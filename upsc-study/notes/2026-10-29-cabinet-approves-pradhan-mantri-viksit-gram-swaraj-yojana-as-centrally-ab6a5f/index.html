Cabinet approves Pradhan Mantri Viksit Gram Swaraj Yojana as Centrally Sponsored Scheme

The Union Cabinet approved the Pradhan Mantri Viksit Gram Swaraj Yojana (PM VGSY) as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme for 2026-27 to 2030-31 to strengthen rural local governance. This scheme aims to empower Gram Panchayats by integrating planning, digital tools, capacity building, and financial accountability to improve rural development outcomes.

PM Viksit Gram Swaraj Yojana is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme to strengthen Gram Panchayats as centers of integrated rural development planning and governance from 2026-27 to 2030-31.

Why is this in the news?

On 28 October 2026, the Union Cabinet chaired by the Prime Minister approved the Pradhan Mantri Viksit Gram Swaraj Yojana as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme for 2026-27 to 2030-31.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the Pradhan Mantri Viksit Gram Swaraj Yojana?

  • Scheme overview. PM VGSY is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme approved for implementation from 2026-27 to 2030-31 with a total outlay of ₹7,395.55 crore, shared between the Centre (₹4,869.48 crore) and States (₹2,526.07 crore).
  • Coverage and beneficiaries. The scheme covers all States and Union Territories, including traditional rural local government institutions in areas without Panchayats, benefiting around 32 lakh elected representatives, Panchayat functionaries, and stakeholders across over 2.55 lakh Panchayats and Traditional Local Bodies.
  • Purpose. It aims to strengthen Gram Panchayats as centers of integrated and participatory development planning, making them empowered, accountable, and self-reliant institutions of local self-government.

How does the scheme strengthen rural governance?

  • Gram Panchayats as development hubs. The scheme places Gram Panchayats at the center of local development planning by coordinating village priorities, government interventions, and available resources to align national development goals with rural community needs.
  • Integrated planning. It brings together planning processes of Central Ministries and State Governments on a common digital platform to prepare Gram Panchayat Development Plans (GPDPs) that identify gaps, align interventions, and optimize resource use.
  • Digital governance. Building on eGramSwaraj and e-Panchayat systems, the scheme consolidates information on government schemes, beneficiaries, community assets, and resources on a single digital platform to improve planning, coordination, and monitoring.
  • Capacity building and awareness. The scheme supports capacity building for around 32 lakh governance stakeholders annually and uses digital and outreach platforms, including PANCHAM, to reach at least 1 crore citizens per awareness episode, fostering informed community discussions.
  • Financial accountability. It strengthens Panchayats’ ability to utilize Finance Commission grants and other funds effectively, mobilize Own Source Revenue, and ensures online accounting, auditing, and public disclosure to promote transparency and financial self-reliance.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What are the implementation and institutional arrangements?

  • Implementation framework. The scheme will be implemented through State Governments and Union Territory Administrations, which will submit Annual Action Plans for appraisal and approval by a designated Central Empowered Committee.
  • Use of existing systems. It leverages existing institutional, capacity-building, digital, and communication systems at the grassroots level without creating permanent posts but may engage contractual personnel as needed for technical support.
  • Alignment with Finance Commission. The scheme period aligns with the Sixteenth Finance Commission's period, which recommends around ₹4.35 lakh crore in grants for Rural Local Bodies, enabling Panchayats to use these resources more effectively.

Back to basics: Gram Panchayat and Rural Local Governance

Gram Panchayats are the basic units of local self-government in rural India responsible for planning and development at the village level. They function under the Panchayati Raj system, which decentralizes governance to empower local communities. Effective rural governance requires integration of planning, resource management, and community participation to address local needs and implement government schemes.
