Static anchor

Coldwater aquaculture is the managed breeding, rearing and harvesting of aquatic organisms in low-temperature water environments; in this source, it refers principally to rainbow-trout farming in Himalayan streams and related infrastructure.

Background

  • The Department of Fisheries is using fisheries clusters under the Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana to support location-specific fisheries infrastructure and livelihoods.
  • Munsyari in Pithoragarh has suitable coldwater conditions, including glacial streams and water temperatures conducive to rainbow-trout culture.
  • The source identifies cooperation among local trout farmers, Uttarakhand institutions, ICAR-Central Institute of Coldwater Fisheries Research, and the Indo-Tibetan Border Police as part of the development ecosystem.

India implications

  • Livelihood diversification: Trout farming can supplement rural incomes in high-altitude and border areas.
  • Nutrition and markets: Greater production and cold-chain infrastructure can improve availability of high-value fish and support domestic as well as export markets.
  • Border-area development: Institutional demand and cooperative activity can strengthen economic resilience in remote villages.
  • Ecological governance: Expansion should be aligned with conservation of river ecosystems, native species and sustainable water use.

Way forward

  • Adopt carrying-capacity-based planning for streams, raceways and hatcheries.
  • Strengthen disease surveillance, quality seed, farmer training and scientific extension.
  • Build cooperative-owned cold chains, grading, processing and transparent market linkages.
  • Prioritise small and marginal farmers and ensure fair benefit sharing within fisheries clusters.
