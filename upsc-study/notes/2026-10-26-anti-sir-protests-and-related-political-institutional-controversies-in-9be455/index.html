Anti-SIR Protests and State Assembly Resolutions in Tamil Nadu and Kerala

CPI (M) leaders have urged Tamil Nadu to pass a resolution against the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, following Kerala's unanimous resolution demanding its scrapping. This issue raises important questions about electoral governance, federalism, and institutional autonomy relevant for UPSC exams.

The demand to scrap SIR highlights tensions between state legislatures and the Union government over electoral roll management and democratic rights.

Why is this in the news?

On October 25, 2026, CPI (M) politburo member G. Ramakrishnan called for Tamil Nadu to pass a resolution in its State Assembly to scrap the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, following Kerala's similar unanimous resolution.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the controversy around the Special Intensive Revision (SIR)?

  • SIR and voter deletion. The BJP-led Union government is accused of using SIR to delete around 13.5 crore voters from electoral rolls, raising concerns about disenfranchisement.
  • Opposition unity. Except for the BJP, the entire opposition has united in demanding the removal of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over the SIR issue.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How have states responded to the SIR issue?

  • Kerala's resolution. Kerala's Legislative Assembly, including ruling Congress and opposition Left Front parties, unanimously passed a resolution demanding the scrapping of SIR.
  • Tamil Nadu's demand. CPI (M) leader G. Ramakrishnan urged Tamil Nadu's Chief Minister to introduce and adopt a similar resolution against SIR in the State Assembly.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the SIR controversy matter for federalism and governance?

  • State autonomy in electoral matters. The demand for state Assembly resolutions against SIR reflects tensions over state roles in electoral roll management versus Union government actions.
  • Institutional autonomy. Calls for removal of the Chief Election Commissioner highlight concerns about the independence of constitutional bodies overseeing elections.

Back to basics: State Assembly Resolutions and Electoral Rolls

State Legislative Assemblies can pass resolutions expressing their stance on policies or actions, which can influence governance and political discourse. Electoral rolls are official lists of eligible voters maintained to ensure free and fair elections, and their revision is a sensitive process requiring transparency and fairness.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements regarding the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls: 1. The BJP-led Union government is accused of deleting around 13.5 crore voters through SIR. 2. Kerala and Tamil Nadu have both passed unanimous resolutions scrapping SIR. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Kerala has passed a unanimous resolution against SIR, but Tamil Nadu has only been urged to pass such a resolution; it has not passed one yet.
