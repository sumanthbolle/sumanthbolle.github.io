Newsprint Industry Seeks PM Modi's Intervention Over Coal Shortages

The Indian Newsprint Manufacturers' Association (INMA) has urged Prime Minister Modi to address coal supply disruptions that are affecting newsprint production. This issue highlights challenges in coal allocation between power and non-power sectors, relevant for understanding India's energy and industrial policies.

Coal supply disruptions and allocation imbalances are threatening newsprint production, prompting industry appeals for government intervention.

Why is this in the news?

On October 20, 2026, INMA sought Prime Minister Modi's direct intervention to resolve coal shortages disrupting newsprint production.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the coal supply issue faced by the newsprint industry?

  • Coal supply disruptions. Despite valid long-term fuel supply agreements with Coal India Limited subsidiaries, newsprint manufacturers are not receiving coal rakes as per agreed schedules.
  • Consequences for production. Irregular and inadequate coal dispatches have forced mills to buy coal at high open market prices and rely on expensive grid electricity, raising operational costs and risking production cuts.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does coal allocation ratio matter for the newsprint industry?

  • Current allocation imbalance. More than 95% of coal supplies are reportedly directed to power plants, leaving non-regulated sectors like newsprint manufacturers struggling to secure adequate coal despite contractual commitments.
  • Demand for 75:25 ratio. INMA has urged enforcement of the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA)-recommended coal allocation ratio of 75% to power and 25% to non-power sectors to ensure predictable coal supply for industries.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the broader implications of coal shortages for the newsprint sector?

  • Supply chain risks. Persistent coal shortages threaten domestic manufacturing capacity and could disrupt the availability of newsprint, impacting newspapers and print media across India.
  • Financial and logistical challenges. Delayed and bunched coal deliveries create working capital pressures, storage and transportation challenges, further straining manufacturers.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What solutions has the newsprint industry proposed?

  • Direct government intervention. INMA has requested Prime Minister Modi's personal intervention and direct instructions to Coal India and its subsidiaries to resume timely, regular, and evenly scheduled coal dispatches as per existing fuel supply agreements.
  • Review of coal dispatch patterns. The industry seeks a review and implementation of the recommended coal allocation ratio to balance supplies between power and non-power sectors.

Back to basics: Coal Allocation and Fuel Supply Agreements

Coal allocation refers to the distribution of coal resources between different sectors such as power generation and industrial use. Fuel Supply Agreements (FSAs) are contracts between coal producers and consumers that specify quantities and schedules for coal delivery. Proper allocation and timely dispatches are essential to ensure uninterrupted industrial production and energy security.

  2. 2 only

Answer and trap

Quick revision

Why is the newsprint industry facing coal shortages?
Because coal dispatches under existing agreements are irregular and insufficient, forcing reliance on costly open market coal and electricity.
What coal allocation ratio does the newsprint industry demand?
They demand a 75:25 allocation ratio between power and non-power sectors as recommended by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs.
What risks do coal shortages pose to the newsprint industry?
They risk production cuts, supply disruptions to newspapers, and financial and logistical challenges for manufacturers.
