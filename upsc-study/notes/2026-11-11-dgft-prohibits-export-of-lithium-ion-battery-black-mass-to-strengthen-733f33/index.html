DGFT bans export of Lithium-Ion Battery Black Mass to boost domestic recycling

The Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) has prohibited the export of Lithium-Ion Battery Black Mass to ensure availability of critical minerals within India and strengthen domestic recycling capacity. This policy supports the government’s ₹1,500 crore Incentive Scheme for Critical Mineral Recycling and aims to reduce import dependence on critical minerals.

DGFT’s export ban on Lithium-Ion Battery Black Mass aims to enhance domestic recovery of critical minerals and support sustainable battery recycling.

Why is this in the news?

On 10 November 2026, DGFT amended export policy to prohibit export of Lithium-Ion Battery Black Mass to strengthen domestic recycling and critical mineral recovery.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is Lithium-Ion Battery Black Mass and why is it important?

  • Definition. Black Mass is the powdery material obtained from shredding end-of-life lithium-ion batteries and battery manufacturing waste.
  • Composition. It contains recoverable quantities of critical minerals such as lithium, cobalt, nickel, and manganese essential for electric mobility and clean energy technologies.
  • Significance. These critical minerals are vital inputs for advanced batteries, energy storage, electronics, and renewable energy sectors.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why did DGFT prohibit export of Black Mass?

  • Export policy change. DGFT changed the export policy of Lithium-Ion Battery Black Mass from ‘Free’ to ‘Prohibited’ effective immediately to retain this resource within India.
  • Feedstock availability. Exports were diverting valuable secondary resources abroad, causing domestic recyclers to sometimes import material to meet demand.
  • Domestic recycling boost. The ban ensures Black Mass is recycled and refined domestically, improving capacity utilization and encouraging investment and employment in the sector.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How does this policy impact India’s critical mineral supply and environment?

  • Import dependence reduction. Increasing domestic recovery of lithium, cobalt, nickel, and other minerals reduces import dependence and foreign exchange outgo.
  • Supply chain strengthening. It strengthens supply chains for battery manufacturing, electric vehicles, energy storage, and clean energy technologies aligned with Aatmanirbhar Bharat.
  • Environmental benefits. The measure promotes circular economy and urban mining, reducing reliance on primary mining and supporting environmentally sound recycling of hazardous material.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How does this policy align with government initiatives?

  • National Critical Mineral Mission. The export prohibition complements the ₹1,500 crore Incentive Scheme approved in September 2025 to develop recycling capacity for critical minerals from battery waste and e-waste.
  • Global practices. The step aligns with measures by major economies to retain critical mineral-bearing battery waste for domestic processing.

Back to basics: Critical Minerals and Recycling

Critical minerals like lithium, cobalt, nickel, and manganese are essential for modern technologies such as electric vehicles and renewable energy. Recycling battery waste recovers these minerals sustainably, reducing the need for primary mining and import dependence. Policies that retain recyclable materials domestically help develop local industries and secure supply chains.
