Water Sharing Challenges Amid Monsoon Variability in Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh

Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh face water supply challenges due to depleted reservoirs amid monsoon deficits. The longstanding Telugu Ganga Project's water-sharing commitments are under strain, highlighting inter-state cooperation and conflict issues important for UPSC exams.

The Telugu Ganga Project's water-sharing agreement between Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh is strained due to low reservoir levels amid monsoon deficits, affecting Chennai's drinking water supply.

Why is this in the news?

Tamil Nadu Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay met Andhra Pradesh Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu on October 13, 2026, to discuss Krishna river water supply for Chennai amid depleted reservoir storage and monsoon deficits.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Telugu Ganga Project and its current challenges?

  • Project purpose. The Telugu Ganga Project, agreed in 1983, aims to supply 15 TMC of Krishna river water annually to Tamil Nadu for Chennai's drinking water and irrigation in Andhra Pradesh's drought-prone areas.
  • Current reservoir status. Low storage in key reservoirs like Srisailam, Somasila, and Kandaleru due to inadequate flood inflows has complicated full water delivery.
  • Partial water release. Andhra Pradesh is considering a partial release of up to 5 TMC from available reserves as a compromise to meet Tamil Nadu's needs.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this water-sharing issue matter for inter-state relations and governance?

  • Impact of state bifurcation. The bifurcation of Andhra Pradesh has complicated water-sharing arrangements, with Telangana also claiming responsibility for its share of Krishna water.
  • Balancing competing demands. Andhra Pradesh must balance Chennai's drinking water needs against its own irrigation and drinking water requirements and those of other riparian states.
  • Cooperative federalism challenge. The negotiations reflect the complexities of cooperative federalism in managing shared natural resources amid changing political and hydrological realities.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does monsoon variability affect water management in southern India?

  • Monsoon deficits. Inadequate monsoon rainfall leads to low reservoir levels, reducing water availability for drinking and irrigation.
  • Need for river linking. The proposed Godavari-Krishna-Cauvery river-linking project is seen as a potential long-term solution to water shortages across southern India.
  • Immediate challenges. Short-term solutions require practical compromises on water releases without worsening inter-state disputes.

Back to basics: Inter-state Water Sharing and Monsoon Impact

Inter-state water sharing involves agreements between states to allocate river water, often complicated by changing political boundaries and hydrological conditions. Monsoon rainfall variability directly affects reservoir levels, influencing water availability for drinking and irrigation. Managing these resources requires balancing competing demands and cooperative federalism principles.
