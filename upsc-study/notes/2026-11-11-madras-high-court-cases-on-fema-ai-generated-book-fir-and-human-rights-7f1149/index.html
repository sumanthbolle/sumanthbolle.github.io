Madras High Court on FEMA Property Seizure Case of DMK MP

The Madras High Court admitted appeals by DMK MP Jagathrakshakan and family challenging the ED's seizure of properties worth ₹45.34 crore under FEMA violations related to a Singapore company. This case illustrates judicial oversight on economic offences and FEMA enforcement, relevant for understanding legal processes in financial regulation.

The Madras High Court is hearing appeals against property seizures by the ED for alleged FEMA violations involving foreign shareholding and transfer.

Why is this in the news?

On November 10, 2026, the Madras High Court admitted civil appeals filed by DMK MP Jagathrakshakan and family members against the ED's property seizure order under FEMA.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the background of the FEMA case involving DMK MP Jagathrakshakan?

  • Company incorporation. Silver Park International Pte. Limited was incorporated in Singapore on June 15, 2017, with Jagathrakshakan and his family holding shares.
  • Shareholding details. Jagathrakshakan held 70 lakh shares, his son 20 lakh, and another individual 10 lakh shares, each with a nominal value of 1 Singapore dollar.
  • ED inquiry. The ED initiated inquiry after the MP disclosed directorship in the company, finding no RBI records on the company and alleging contravention of FEMA for not obtaining RBI permission for share subscription and transfers.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What legal proceedings have taken place so far?

  • Seizure order. In September 2020, the ED seized immovable properties worth ₹89.17 crore for alleged FEMA contraventions amounting to ₹161.12 crore.
  • Set aside by competent authority. The Commissioner of Customs, acting as competent authority under FEMA, set aside the seizure order entirely in February 2021.
  • Appellate tribunal ruling. The FEMA appellate tribunal partly confirmed the seizure order but limited it to properties worth ₹45.34 crore, rejecting the ED's argument that acquisition and transfer of shares are separate contraventions.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this case matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • FEMA enforcement. The case highlights how FEMA regulates foreign exchange transactions, including shareholding and transfer, and the role of RBI permission.
  • Judicial review. It exemplifies judicial oversight on enforcement agencies like the ED and the appellate process under FEMA law.
  • Economic offences and law. Understanding this case aids comprehension of legal procedures in economic offences and property seizure under financial laws.

Back to basics: Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA)

FEMA regulates foreign exchange transactions in India to facilitate external trade and payments and promote orderly development of the foreign exchange market. It requires prior approval from the Reserve Bank of India for certain foreign investments and transactions. Violations can lead to penalties including property seizure by enforcement agencies.
