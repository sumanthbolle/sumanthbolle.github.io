Preventive Detention and Constitutional Rights in India

Preventive detention allows authorities to detain individuals without trial under specific legal conditions. Understanding its constitutional safeguards is essential for grasping the balance between state security and individual freedoms.

Preventive detention permits lawful detention without trial under constitutional safeguards to balance state security and individual rights.

Why is this in the news?

The renewed attention to preventive detention followed detentions during the October 10 protests, highlighting constitutional protections and limits on state power as explained in a 2026 article by The Hindu.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is preventive detention?

  • Definition. Preventive detention is the legal practice of detaining a person without trial to prevent them from committing a potential crime or threat to public order.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What constitutional protections exist against preventive detention?

  • Article 22 safeguards. Article 22 of the Indian Constitution provides specific protections to individuals against arbitrary preventive detention, including the right to be informed of grounds of detention and the right to legal representation.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

When can authorities legally detain without trial?

  • Legal conditions. Authorities can detain individuals preventively under laws like Section 163 of the BNSS when there is a reasonable suspicion of threat to public order or security, subject to constitutional safeguards.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does preventive detention matter for civil liberties?

  • Balancing security and freedom. Preventive detention raises issues about the limits of state power and the protection of individual rights, especially concerning the right to peaceful protest and mobility.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Back to basics: Preventive Detention

Preventive detention is a legal measure allowing the state to detain a person without trial to prevent potential harm or threat to public order. It differs from punitive detention, which follows a trial and conviction. The Constitution provides safeguards to prevent misuse, ensuring that detention is not arbitrary and that detainees have rights such as being informed of charges and access to legal counsel.
