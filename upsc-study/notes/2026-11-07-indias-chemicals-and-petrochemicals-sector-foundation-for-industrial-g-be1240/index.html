India’s Chemicals and Petrochemicals Sector: Foundation for Industrial Growth

India’s chemicals and petrochemicals industry is expanding its manufacturing base, investment potential, and international engagement with dedicated parks, research funding, and quality standards. This sector is a key pillar of manufacturing and industrial development, making it important for UPSC aspirants studying economic growth and industrial policy.

India’s chemicals and petrochemicals sector is strategically important for industrial growth, with expanding production, investment, and global market integration.

Why is this in the news?

On 7 November 2026, the Press Information Bureau released a detailed backgrounder on India’s chemicals and petrochemicals sector highlighting its growth, infrastructure initiatives, research, and quality standards.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the current status and significance of India’s chemicals and petrochemicals sector?

  • Sector size and ranking. India is the sixth-largest chemical producer globally and fourth-largest in Asia, with the sector valued at around USD 220 billion, projected to reach USD 300 billion by 2030 and USD 1 trillion by 2040.
  • Industrial importance. The sector supports diverse industries such as pharmaceuticals, textiles, construction, automobiles, electronics, and consumer goods, making it a pillar of manufacturing and agriculture.
  • Production and enterprises. There are about 13,000 large-scale units and 4.45 lakh MSMEs in the sector, with production increasing from 45,638 thousand metric tonnes in 2015-16 to 58,617 thousand metric tonnes in 2024-25.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How is India strengthening manufacturing capacity and industrial infrastructure?

  • Investment and FDI. India allows 100% FDI through the automatic route for most chemical sectors, with FDI inflows reaching ₹1,04,895 crore during 2014–2026, more than double the ₹45,240 crore during 2004–2014.
  • Petroleum, Chemicals and Petrochemicals Investment Regions. Three operational PCPIRs in Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, and Odisha cover about 250 sq. km each, providing common infrastructure and attracting ₹3.4 lakh crore investment, generating 3.7 lakh jobs and over 2,200 chemical units.
  • BHAVYA Rasayan chemical parks. The BHAVYA Rasayan Scheme approved in July 2026 plans three dedicated chemical parks with grants up to ₹1,000 crore per park, totalling ₹3,030 crore over five years, to provide plug-and-play infrastructure and promote sustainable industrial development.
  • Plastic Parks scheme. Ten Plastic Parks have been approved to consolidate plastic processing industries, with government grants up to 50% of project cost capped at ₹40 crore, promoting investment, exports, and environmentally sustainable growth.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What initiatives support research, innovation, and skill development in the sector?

  • Chemical Promotion and Development Scheme. CPDS supports knowledge creation, research promotion, and farmer training programmes on safe pesticide use, benefiting over 1 lakh farmers through more than 250 programmes.
  • Research, Development and Innovation Scheme. Launched in November 2025 with a corpus of ₹1 lakh crore, the RDI Scheme finances transformative projects, including advanced chemicals and petrochemicals from October 2026, to boost technology adoption and competitiveness.
  • CIPET’s role. The Central Institute of Petrochemicals Engineering & Technology operates 51 centres, including plastic waste management, and has trained nearly 6.72 lakh professionals and completed 8.53 lakh technology support assignments.
  • Centres of Excellence. Eighteen centres have filed 85 patents, published over 600 research papers, and developed 105 new technologies, advancing indigenous capabilities and industrial innovation.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How are quality, safety, and responsible chemical use ensured?

  • Quality Control Orders. As of August 2026, 37 QCOs are in force mandating specified chemical standards for domestic manufacturers and importers, enforced by the Bureau of Indian Standards.
  • Workplace safety training. A nationwide training programme under Viksit Bharat@2047 covers 2,393 Major Accident Hazardous units over five years, training 2,441 personnel from 1,376 units as of August 2026 to improve hazardous chemical management.

Back to basics: Industrial Clusters and Investment Regions

Industrial clusters are geographic concentrations of interconnected companies and institutions in a particular field, which benefit from shared infrastructure and services. Investment regions like PCPIRs provide integrated infrastructure such as roads, ports, and utilities to attract investment and promote industrial growth. These clusters improve resource utilisation, reduce costs, and enhance competitiveness.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about India’s chemicals and petrochemicals sector: 1. The BHAVYA Rasayan Scheme provides grants up to ₹1,000 crore per dedicated chemical park with a total outlay of ₹3,030 crore. 2. The Central Institute of Petrochemicals Engineering & Technology (CIPET) has established over 100 centres across India to support skill development. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because CIPET operates 51 centres, not over 100.
