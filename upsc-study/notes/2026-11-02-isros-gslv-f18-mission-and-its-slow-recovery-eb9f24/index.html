ISRO’s GSLV-F18 Mission and Challenges in Recovery

ISRO successfully launched the GSLV-F18 carrying the NVS-03 satellite to strengthen India's NavIC navigation system. However, recent launch failures and resource constraints have slowed ISRO's mission pace, highlighting the need for increased support and reforms.

The GSLV-F18 mission marks progress in India's indigenous satellite navigation but exposes challenges in ISRO's resource capacity and programme management.

Why is this in the news?

On 30 October 2026, ISRO launched the GSLV-F18 mission successfully, placing the NVS-03 satellite into Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit to enhance NavIC, after a period of constrained launches due to previous failures.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What was the GSLV-F18 mission and its objectives?

  • Mission success. ISRO launched the GSLV-F18 on 30 October 2026, placing the NVS-03 satellite into a precise Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit from Sriharikota.
  • Satellite purpose. NVS-03 is the third of five next-generation NavIC satellites designed to improve India's regional satellite navigation system's coverage, accuracy, and reliability.
  • Navigation signals. The satellite carries navigation payloads in L1, L5, and S frequency bands, enhancing NavIC's compatibility and coverage over India and 1,500 km beyond.
  • Indigenous technology. NVS-03 includes an indigenously developed atomic clock for precise timing, essential for accurate positioning.
  • Launch vehicle details. GSLV-F18 is a three-stage vehicle with an indigenous cryogenic upper stage, marking the 20th flight of the GSLV series.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does the GSLV-F18 mission matter for India's space capabilities?

  • Strengthening NavIC. The mission supports India's independent Positioning, Navigation, and Timing capabilities, reducing reliance on foreign systems.
  • Strategic and civilian use. NavIC provides open positioning services for civilians and encrypted services for strategic users.
  • Technological self-reliance. Use of indigenous cryogenic technology and atomic clocks enhances India's space sovereignty and technological capacity.
  • Applications. NavIC supports transportation, maritime, aviation, disaster management, and precision agriculture.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What challenges has ISRO faced recently in its space missions?

  • Launch failures. ISRO experienced two PSLV launch failures in the last 18 months, affecting mission frequency and reliability.
  • Slow mission pace. GSLV-F18 was only the second successful launch in 2026, indicating a slow recovery for the national space programme.
  • Resource constraints. ISRO faces shortages in human and material resources, impacting its ability to meet ambitious plans like human spaceflight and reusable launch vehicles.
  • Procurement bottlenecks. Difficulties in obtaining specialised components and vendor readiness have delayed satellite projects like NavIC.
  • Need for reforms. Experts recommend increased budget allocation, dedicated NavIC operators, and a national space law to address private participation and liability.

Back to basics: Satellite Navigation Systems

Satellite navigation systems use a constellation of satellites that transmit signals with precise timing and location data. Receivers on Earth use these signals to determine their own position, speed, and time. Systems like GPS and NavIC support applications in navigation, tracking, and timing across various sectors.

What happens next?

  • Increase budget and resources. The government should raise the Department of Space budget to around ₹17,000 crore to enhance manufacturing, satellite replacement, and recruitment.
  • Dedicated NavIC operator. A separate operator for NavIC is needed to allow ISRO to focus on core space missions.
  • National space law. India requires a legal framework addressing private sector participation and liability in space activities.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about ISRO's GSLV-F18 mission and NavIC system: 1. The GSLV-F18 mission successfully placed the NVS-03 satellite into Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit, enhancing NavIC's navigation capabilities. 2. The NVS-03 satellite carries only imported atomic clocks for timing accuracy. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because NVS-03 carries an indigenously developed atomic clock alongside procured ones, not only imported clocks.
