RBI Auctions Treasury Bills and State Securities; India’s Exports Grow 16.66%

The Reserve Bank of India conducted auctions for treasury bills and state government securities in November 2026. Meanwhile, India’s cumulative exports for April-October 2026-27 rose by 16.66% compared to the previous year, reflecting strong trade growth.

RBI’s treasury and state securities auctions support government borrowing while India’s exports grew 16.66% in April-October 2026-27, indicating robust trade performance.

Why is this in the news?

On November 13, 2026, RBI announced auctions for treasury bills and state government securities, alongside Ministry of Commerce reporting India’s export growth for April-October 2026-27.

What treasury bills did RBI auction and how?

  • Auction details. RBI announced auctions for 91-day, 182-day, and 364-day treasury bills with a notified amount totaling ₹23,000 crore, scheduled for November 18, 2026.
  • Participation and method. State governments, eligible provident funds, foreign central banks, and individuals can participate, with retail investors limited to 5% of notified amount; auctions use a price-based multiple price method.
  • Bidding process. Competitive bids are accepted from 10:30 am to 11:30 am and non-competitive bids from 10:30 am to 11:00 am on the auction day via RBI’s E-Kuber system.

[2] Reserve Bank of India

What state government securities were auctioned by RBI?

  • Securities offered. State governments offered securities worth ₹23,300 crore across various tenors, including re-issues and new yield-based auctions from states like Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Punjab, and West Bengal.
  • Auction schedule and process. The auction was held on November 17, 2026, through RBI’s E-Kuber system with competitive bids accepted between 10:30 am and 11:30 am and non-competitive bids between 10:30 am and 11:00 am.
  • Investor participation. Up to 10% of each stock is allotted to eligible individuals and institutions under a non-competitive bidding scheme, with a maximum of 1% per single bid.
  • Interest and regulations. New stocks pay interest semi-annually, governed by the Government Securities Act, 2006, and qualify for Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) investments by banks.

[3] Reserve Bank of India

How did India’s exports perform in April-October 2026-27?

  • Overall export growth. India’s cumulative exports of merchandise and services during April-October 2026-27 were estimated at US$ 568.84 billion, a 16.66% increase over US$ 487.59 billion in the same period of 2025-26.
  • Merchandise exports. Merchandise exports grew by 21.96% to US$ 309.06 billion, driven by sectors like petroleum products, electronic goods, gems and jewellery, chemicals, and engineering goods.
  • Services exports. Services exports rose by 10.93% to an estimated US$ 259.78 billion, contributing to a services trade surplus of US$ 124.80 billion.
  • Trade balance and imports. Total imports during April-October 2026-27 increased by 16.00% to US$ 662.19 billion, resulting in a trade deficit of US$ 93.35 billion.

Back to basics: Government Securities and Export Trade

Government securities are debt instruments issued by the government to raise funds; treasury bills are short-term securities while state government securities have longer maturities. Auctions determine the yield or price at which these securities are sold. Export trade data reflects a country’s economic health by showing growth or decline in goods and services sold abroad, impacting foreign exchange earnings and trade balance.

Answer and trap
