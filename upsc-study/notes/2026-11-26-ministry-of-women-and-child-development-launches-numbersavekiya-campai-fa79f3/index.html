Ministry of Women and Child Development launches #NumberSaveKiya campaign and inter-ministerial MoU for women safety

On 25 November 2026, the Ministry of Women and Child Development launched the #NumberSaveKiya campaign urging women to save emergency helpline numbers for immediate help. It also signed an inter-ministerial MoU to strengthen services for women’s safety through the Violence-Free Village Initiative.

The #NumberSaveKiya campaign and inter-ministerial MoU aim to enhance women's safety by promoting awareness and converging support services at the grassroots level.

Why is this in the news?

The Ministry of Women and Child Development launched the #NumberSaveKiya campaign and signed an inter-ministerial MoU on 25 November 2026 to mark the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the #NumberSaveKiya campaign?

  • Campaign objective. The campaign urges every woman to save the Women Helpline 181 and Emergency Response Support System 112 on their phones to enable immediate access to help in distress.
  • Helpline details. Helpline 181 is a 24x7, toll-free service connecting women facing violence to police, One Stop Centres, medical care, and legal aid.
  • Awareness strategy. The campaign is promoted through the Ministry’s digital platforms and grassroots networks including Anganwadi workers, One Stop Centres, and Mission Shakti hubs.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the inter-ministerial MoU and its significance?

  • MoU signatories. The Ministry of Women and Child Development, Department of Justice, NALSA, and Department of Rural Development signed the MoU.
  • Violence-Free Village Initiative. The MoU launches the Violence-Free Village Initiative to integrate legal aid, women’s support services, and community institutions at the village level.
  • Framework purpose. It aims to bring safety, justice, rehabilitation, and livelihood support together, encouraging States, SHGs, Anganwadi networks, and communities to sustain grassroots action.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this matter for women’s safety and empowerment?

  • Timely access to help. Saving emergency numbers ensures women can quickly reach support services even under extreme stress when remembering numbers may be difficult.
  • Government convergence. The initiative promotes convergence of multiple ministries and agencies to provide comprehensive support for women facing violence.
  • Community involvement. The campaign and MoU emphasize grassroots participation through SHGs, Anganwadi workers, and local leaders to challenge patriarchal mindsets and promote dignity and safety.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How was the campaign launched and supported?

  • Event details. The launch took place at Nayi Chetna 5.0 on 25 November 2026, coinciding with the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women and the start of 16 Days of Activism against Gender-Based Violence.
  • Key participants. Union Minister Annpurna Devi and Union Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan addressed the event, highlighting women’s empowerment and the role of rural women in the economy.
  • Supporting activities. The event included panel discussions on ministerial convergence, launch of new Gender Resource Centres, release of video resources, and the Nayi Chetna 4.0 Assessment Report.

Back to basics: Women Helpline and Support Services

Women Helpline 181 is a toll-free service available 24x7 that connects women facing violence to police, medical, legal, and counseling support. One Stop Centres provide integrated services to women affected by violence. These helplines and centres form a critical part of the government’s framework to ensure timely assistance and protection for women in distress.
