BIMSTEC Adopts Delhi Declaration to Strengthen Regional Security Cooperation

The first BIMSTEC Home Ministers’ Meeting was held in New Delhi on 13 October 2026, where member states adopted the Delhi Declaration to combat terrorism and related transnational crimes. This development is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights India's role in regional security cooperation and multilateral efforts to tackle terrorism, drug trafficking, and cybercrime in the Bay of Bengal region.

BIMSTEC’s Delhi Declaration reaffirms member states’ commitment to counter terrorism, radicalization, and transnational crime through enhanced regional cooperation.

Why is this in the news?

India hosted the first-ever BIMSTEC Home Ministers’ Meeting in New Delhi on 13 October 2026, where the Delhi Declaration was adopted to enhance cooperation against terrorism and transnational crimes.

What was decided at the first BIMSTEC Home Ministers’ Meeting?

  • Meeting details. The inaugural BIMSTEC Home Ministers’ Meeting was held in New Delhi on 13 October 2026 under the chairmanship of India's Union Home Minister Amit Shah, with delegations from all seven member states participating.
  • Delhi Declaration. Member states adopted the Delhi Declaration, which reaffirmed their commitment to regional cooperation for a secure and prosperous BIMSTEC region and condemned terrorism and violent extremism in all forms.
  • Key agreements. The meeting approved the BIMSTEC Convention against Trafficking in Persons, a Plan of Action on Drug Control, an MoU on Capacity Building to combat money laundering and terror financing, and a Plan of Action on Illicit Financial Flows.
  • Focus areas. Five key areas of cooperation highlighted were drug control, counter-terrorism and legal cooperation, repatriation of fugitives, cyber-security and cybercrime investigation, and institutional cooperation and capacity building.

Why does the Delhi Declaration matter for regional security?

  • Comprehensive counter-terrorism. The declaration calls for preventing, combating, and eliminating terrorism and violent extremism in accordance with the UN Charter and international law, including implementation of UN Security Council sanctions.
  • Addressing transnational crimes. It recognizes challenges like drug trafficking, human trafficking, cybercrime, money laundering, and misuse of cyberspace for terrorist activities as shared regional security threats.
  • Emphasis on cooperation. The declaration stresses timely intelligence sharing, mutual legal assistance, extradition, and capacity building among member states to effectively counter these threats.
  • Human trafficking and cyber slavery. It highlights the exploitation of trafficking victims in online fraud and scams and calls for a victim-centered approach and common protocols for rescue and reintegration.

How does this meeting reflect India’s role and policy?

  • India’s leadership. India initiated and hosted the first BIMSTEC Home Ministers’ Meeting, reinforcing its commitment to regional peace, security, and cooperation under the spirit of ‘Vishwamitra’.
  • Zero tolerance policy. India maintains a policy of zero tolerance towards terrorism and advocates a ‘whole-of-region’ approach to tackle drug trafficking and terror financing.
  • Focus on technology and finance. India highlighted the evolving tactics of criminal networks using encrypted communication, social media, drones, cryptocurrency, and the dark web, calling for real-time intelligence sharing.
  • Disaster management cooperation. The meeting also emphasized cooperation in disaster management, including establishing a BIMSTEC Disaster Management Centre in Jamnagar, Gujarat.

Back to basics: Regional Security Cooperation

Regional security cooperation involves countries working together to address common security threats such as terrorism, organized crime, and trafficking. It includes sharing intelligence, legal assistance, joint operations, and capacity building to enhance collective security. Such cooperation is essential in regions with shared borders and transnational challenges to ensure peace and stability.

Quick revision

What is the Delhi Declaration adopted at the BIMSTEC Home Ministers’ Meeting?
It is a commitment by BIMSTEC member states to strengthen cooperation against terrorism, violent extremism, drug trafficking, human trafficking, and cybercrime.
Which five key areas of cooperation were highlighted by Union Home Minister Amit Shah at the meeting?
Drug control, counter-terrorism and legal cooperation, repatriation of fugitive criminals, cyber-security and cybercrime investigations, and institutional cooperation and capacity building.
Why is regional cooperation important in combating drug trafficking according to the meeting?
Because drug trafficking finances organized crime and terrorism, and its complex nature requires a whole-of-region approach beyond individual country efforts.
