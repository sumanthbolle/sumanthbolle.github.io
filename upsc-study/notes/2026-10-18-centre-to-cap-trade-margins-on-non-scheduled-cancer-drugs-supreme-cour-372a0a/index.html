Centre Caps Trade Margins on Non-Scheduled Cancer Drugs at 30% of MRP

The Union government has capped trade margins on all non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs at 30% of their maximum retail price to reduce patients' out-of-pocket expenses. This move follows evidence of excessive trade mark-ups and aims to make cancer treatment more affordable, a key issue for UPSC aspirants studying health policy and judiciary roles in welfare.

The government capped trade margins on non-scheduled cancer drugs at 30% of MRP to cut high mark-ups and reduce treatment costs.

Why is this in the news?

On October 17, 2026, the Centre announced capping trade margins on non-scheduled cancer drugs to 30% of MRP to reduce high mark-ups and patient costs.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the new government measure on cancer drug pricing?

  • Trade margin cap. The government capped trade margins on all non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs at 30% of the maximum retail price (MRP) to reduce excessive mark-ups.
  • Coverage of drugs. This cap applies to branded and generic drugs, domestically manufactured and imported medicines, and both patented and non-patented products.
  • Role of expert committee. An expert committee under the Directorate General of Health Services will finalize the list of medicines covered under this measure.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why was this intervention necessary?

  • High existing mark-ups. The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority found average trade mark-ups on non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs around 170%, reaching up to 700% in some cases.
  • Financial burden on patients. About 75% of cancer treatment expenditure is borne out of pocket by patients, making affordability a serious concern.
  • Market size. The anti-cancer medicines market includes around 225 drugs and 500 formulations, with an annual turnover of approximately ₹12,500 crore.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What impact is expected from the margin cap?

  • Price reduction. The intervention is expected to reduce prices by up to 70% of MRP, significantly lowering costs for patients.
  • Annual savings. The government estimates cumulative annual savings of around ₹2,500 crore for patients due to this measure.
  • Production maintenance. Manufacturers will be required to maintain existing production levels to ensure drug availability despite margin caps.
  • Focus on trade margins. The measure regulates trade margins across the distribution chain rather than manufacturers’ selling prices or revenues.

Back to basics: Drug Pricing and Trade Margins

Trade margin refers to the difference between the maximum retail price of a drug and the price at which it is sold by manufacturers or distributors. Regulating trade margins helps control the final price paid by consumers without directly fixing manufacturers' prices. This is important in healthcare to ensure essential medicines remain affordable while maintaining supply.
