India implications

  • Inter-state connectivity can support labour, educational and professional mobility across Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Maharashtra.
  • Rail planning should connect major urban nodes with intermediate towns so that benefits are not concentrated only at terminal cities.
  • Assessment should include affordability, accessibility for persons with disabilities, punctuality, safety, environmental performance and integration with local transport.
  • Improved rail connectivity can contribute to more integrated national infrastructure, but the official record does not establish measurable economic or social outcomes.

Way forward

  • Publish clear service indicators covering punctuality, cancellations, safety, accessibility, capacity and passenger demand.
  • Keep fares and booking access compatible with the needs of students, workers and other ordinary travellers.
  • Integrate terminal and intermediate stations with buses, metro systems, taxis and safe pedestrian access.
  • Evaluate regional inclusion and environmental performance periodically instead of treating connectivity expansion as an end in itself.
