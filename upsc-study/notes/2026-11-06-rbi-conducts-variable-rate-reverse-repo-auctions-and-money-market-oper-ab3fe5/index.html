RBI Conducts Variable Rate Reverse Repo Auctions to Manage Liquidity

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) conducted variable rate reverse repo (VRRR) auctions on November 4 and 5, 2026, absorbing liquidity from the banking system. Understanding these operations is important for UPSC aspirants as they illustrate key monetary policy tools used to regulate money market liquidity and influence interest rates.

RBI uses variable rate reverse repo auctions to absorb excess liquidity and maintain monetary stability.

Why is this in the news?

The RBI conducted VRRR auctions on November 4 and 5, 2026, as part of its liquidity management operations.

What are variable rate reverse repo auctions and why does RBI conduct them?

  • Definition of VRRR. Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions are monetary policy operations where RBI absorbs excess liquidity from banks by borrowing funds at variable interest rates.
  • Purpose of VRRR. RBI conducts VRRR auctions to manage surplus liquidity in the banking system, thereby helping to maintain short-term interest rates within the policy corridor.

[3] Reserve Bank of India

What were the key details of the recent VRRR auctions?

  • Auction on November 5, 2026. The notified amount was ₹75,000 crore; total offers received were ₹7,767 crore, all of which were accepted at a cut-off and weighted average rate of 5.49%.
  • Liquidity absorption on November 4 and 5. On November 4, RBI absorbed ₹1,78,010 crore and on November 5, ₹1,81,410 crore through liquidity adjustment facility and reverse repo operations.

How do these operations impact the money market and economy?

  • Effect on money market rates. By absorbing excess liquidity, VRRR auctions help keep overnight money market rates close to the policy repo rate, stabilizing short-term interest rates.
  • Role in monetary policy. These operations are tools for RBI to implement monetary policy stance, influencing inflation and economic growth by controlling liquidity.

Back to basics: Monetary Policy Tools

Monetary policy tools are instruments used by central banks like RBI to regulate money supply and interest rates in the economy. Reverse repo operations involve the central bank borrowing money from commercial banks, thus absorbing liquidity. Variable rate auctions allow the interest rate to be determined by market demand, helping RBI manage short-term liquidity efficiently.
