Manufacturing PMI Eases in October with Rising Overseas Demand

India's manufacturing sector growth slowed slightly in October 2026 as per the HSBC India Manufacturing PMI, but export orders hit a six-month high. Understanding this trend helps UPSC aspirants analyze industrial growth and external demand's role in the economy.

India's manufacturing PMI slipped to 54.7 in October 2026, indicating slower growth but stronger export demand supporting output expansion.

Why is this in the news?

The HSBC India Manufacturing Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) for October 2026 showed a slight decline in manufacturing activity but a notable rise in new export orders to a six-month high.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What happened to India's manufacturing PMI in October 2026?

  • PMI decline. The HSBC India Manufacturing PMI fell to 54.7 in October 2026 from 55.1 in September, indicating a slight slowdown in manufacturing activity while remaining in expansion territory.
  • Expansion indication. A PMI reading above 50 signals expansion, so despite the dip, the manufacturing sector continued to grow, just at a slower pace.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the rise in overseas demand matter?

  • Export orders growth. New export orders increased to their highest level in six months, driven by demand from countries including Bangladesh, Canada, Indonesia, Nepal, Oman, Saudi Arabia, Sri Lanka, the UK, the US, and the UAE.
  • Support to output. Strong international demand supported a robust rise in manufacturing output despite the overall slowdown in domestic activity.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What other factors influenced manufacturing in October 2026?

  • Cost pressures eased. Input cost inflation retreated to its lowest level since February, even though prices for some materials and energy rose.
  • Precautionary production. Businesses increased input inventories to the greatest extent in eight months to mitigate potential shortages amid geopolitical uncertainties.

Back to basics: Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI)

PMI is an economic indicator derived from monthly surveys of private sector companies, reflecting business conditions such as output, new orders, and employment. A PMI above 50 indicates expansion in the sector, while below 50 signals contraction. It is widely used to gauge the health of manufacturing and services sectors.
