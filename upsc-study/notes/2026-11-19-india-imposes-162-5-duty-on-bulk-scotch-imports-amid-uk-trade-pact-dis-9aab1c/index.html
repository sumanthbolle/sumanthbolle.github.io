India Imposes 162.5% Duty on Bulk Scotch Imports Amid UK Trade Dispute

India raised the effective import duty on bulk Scotch whisky to 162.5% due to a customs classification dispute following the India-UK trade agreement. This development is important for understanding bilateral trade tensions and tariff enforcement in international economic relations.

India increased duties on bulk Scotch imports to 162.5% amid a customs dispute despite the India-UK trade pact reducing tariffs to 75%.

Why is this in the news?

On November 18, 2026, India imposed a 162.5% duty on bulk Scotch imports amid a customs classification dispute linked to the India-UK trade agreement implemented in July 2026.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered the increase in duty on bulk Scotch imports?

  • Trade agreement background. The India-UK trade agreement implemented in July 2026 halved tariffs on Scotch whisky to 75%, which was seen as a win for companies importing Scotch in bulk.
  • Customs classification dispute. Indian investigators argued that bulk Scotch concentrates were misclassified under customs as raw materials, leading to reclassification under a new category attracting a 150% tariff plus consumption tax, totaling 162.5%.
  • Impact on imports. More than 80 containers of bulk Scotch from Pernod Ricard, Diageo, and Bacardi remain stuck at Indian ports due to this dispute.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for India-UK trade relations and the liquor industry?

  • Economic stakes. India is the largest market for Scotch by volume, with about 80% of Scotch imports arriving in bulk to produce Indian-Made Foreign Liquor (IMFL).
  • Industry concerns. Companies like Pernod Ricard and Diageo argue both bulk and bottled Scotch should be taxed at 75% under the trade agreement, fearing disruption and higher costs.
  • Diplomatic efforts. The British government is working with India to resolve the dispute, but the issue has already affected share prices and business continuity.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How are Indian customs and taxation affecting the dispute?

  • New customs category. Since May 2026, bulk Scotch concentrates are classified under 'compound alcoholic preparations' attracting a 150% tariff.
  • Additional taxes. Besides the tariff, a consumption tax is applied, raising the total duty to 162.5%.
  • Enforcement and challenges. Importers must pay 75% of the goods' value upfront with bank guarantees for the rest, creating working capital stress especially for smaller companies.

Back to basics: Trade agreements and customs classification

Trade agreements set tariff rates for imports between countries to facilitate commerce. Customs classification determines how imported goods are categorized for taxation. Misclassification can lead to disputes and higher tariffs, affecting trade flows and business operations.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the India-UK trade dispute on Scotch imports: 1. The India-UK trade agreement reduced tariffs on Scotch imports to 75%. 2. India imposed a 162.5% effective duty on bulk Scotch imports due to customs reclassification. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). Statement 1 is correct about the tariff reduction under the trade agreement; statement 2 is correct about the increased duty due to customs classification, so both statements are true.
