National Orientation Programme for Tribal Healers on Public Health in Hyderabad

The Ministry of Tribal Affairs and ICMR-NIHR organised a two-day National Orientation Programme for Tribal Healers on Public Health at Kanha Shanti Vanam, Hyderabad, on 1–2 November 2026. This programme aims to strengthen tribal healers' knowledge on public health issues and link traditional healing with formal healthcare systems, relevant for tribal welfare policy.

The programme orients tribal healers on public health to improve community health and integrate traditional and formal medical practices.

Why is this in the news?

The Ministry of Tribal Affairs, in collaboration with ICMR-NIHR, organised the first National Orientation Programme for Tribal Healers on Public Health on 1–2 November 2026 at Kanha Shanti Vanam, Hyderabad.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What was the National Orientation Programme for Tribal Healers?

  • Programme details. The two-day programme held on 1–2 November 2026 at Kanha Shanti Vanam, Hyderabad, was organised by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs and ICMR-NIHR under the Janjatiya Health Institutional Transformation initiative.
  • Participants. Around 550 tribal healers from nine states and UTs, nominated by State Tribal Welfare departments, attended the programme.
  • Inauguration. The programme was inaugurated by Shri Manish Thakur, Additional Secretary, Ministry of Tribal Affairs, and Dr. Sanghamitra Pati, Director of ICMR-NIHR.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What topics and training modules were covered?

  • Health issues addressed. Training covered communicable and vector-borne diseases, skin diseases, non-communicable diseases, metabolic and blood disorders, liver diseases, and mental health.
  • Referral and care. Sessions focused on early identification of danger signs and appropriate referral for snakebites, scorpion stings, animal bites, injuries, eye and ear disorders, oral health, and newborn and infant care.
  • Training methods. The programme included integrated practice, review sessions, post-training assessment, and participant feedback to reinforce learning.
  • Language and experts. State-wise sessions were conducted in regional languages by experts from AIIMS, ICMR, MS Swaminathan Foundation, Amrita Institute, IIHMR Bengaluru, and Piramal Swasthya.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this programme matter for tribal health and policy?

  • Role of tribal healers. Tribal healers play an important role in their communities, and orienting them on public health issues enhances community-level health support.
  • Integration with formal healthcare. The initiative encourages linkages between traditional healing practices and the formal healthcare system for timely medical care.
  • Capacity building. This structured capacity-building module represents a milestone effort to integrate tribal healthcare with formal medical systems.
  • Policy relevance. The programme aligns with the Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas Pakhwada, reflecting government commitment to tribal welfare through inclusive health interventions.

Back to basics: Role of Tribal Healers in Public Health

Tribal healers are traditional practitioners who provide healthcare within tribal communities using indigenous knowledge. They are often the first point of contact for health issues in remote areas. Integrating their practices with formal healthcare can improve early disease detection, referral, and overall health outcomes in tribal populations.
