RBI Announces Redemption Prices for Premature Redemption of Sovereign Gold Bonds

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has declared the redemption prices for premature redemption of Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) 2020-21 Series VIII and Series II, effective November 18 and 19, 2026 respectively. Understanding these prices and the scheme’s redemption rules is important for UPSC aspirants preparing for questions on government financial instruments and public investment schemes.

RBI sets the premature redemption prices of Sovereign Gold Bonds based on the average gold price of the preceding three business days, allowing investors to redeem after five years from issue.

Why is this in the news?

On November 17 and 18, 2026, RBI announced the redemption prices for premature redemption of Sovereign Gold Bonds issued in 2020-21 Series VIII and Series II, respectively.

What is the Sovereign Gold Bond Scheme and its redemption rules?

  • Scheme basics. The Sovereign Gold Bond Scheme allows investors to buy gold bonds issued by the government as an alternative to physical gold.
  • Premature redemption eligibility. Premature redemption of these bonds is permitted only after the fifth year from the date of issue, on the date interest is payable.
  • Redemption price calculation. The redemption price is calculated as the simple average of the closing price of gold of 999 purity for the three business days preceding the redemption date, as published by the India Bullion and Jewellers Association Ltd (IBJA).

What are the recent redemption prices announced by RBI?

  • Series VIII redemption price. For SGB 2020-21 Series VIII due on November 18, 2026, the redemption price is ₹13,499 per unit, based on gold prices from November 13, 16, and 17, 2026.
  • Series II redemption price. For SGB 2020-21 Series II due on November 19, 2026, the redemption price is ₹13,345 per unit, based on gold prices from November 16, 17, and 18, 2026.

Back to basics: Sovereign Gold Bond Scheme

The Sovereign Gold Bond Scheme is a government initiative that allows investors to buy bonds denominated in grams of gold instead of physical gold. These bonds pay interest and can be redeemed at maturity or prematurely after a specified period. The scheme reduces the risks and costs associated with holding physical gold and helps the government mobilize resources.
