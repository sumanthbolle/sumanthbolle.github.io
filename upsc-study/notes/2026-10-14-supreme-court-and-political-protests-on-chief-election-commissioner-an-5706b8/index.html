Supreme Court and Political Protests on Chief Election Commissioner and Special Summary Revision

A plea filed by Rahul Gandhi and other opposition leaders challenges alleged irregularities in the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls and questions the conduct of the Chief Election Commissioner. This issue highlights constitutional and legal debates on the Election Commission's independence and electoral integrity, relevant for both Prelims and Mains.

The Supreme Court is examining a petition alleging irregularities in electoral roll revisions and the conduct of the Chief Election Commissioner, raising questions on constitutional safeguards for the Election Commission.

Why is this in the news?

On October 13, 2026, the Supreme Court registry flagged defects in a petition filed by Rahul Gandhi and other opposition leaders challenging the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls and the conduct of the Chief Election Commissioner, delaying its hearing.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the petition about?

  • Petition details. The petition alleges large-scale voter deletions and irregularities during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls and seeks an independent audit headed by a retired apex court judge.
  • Key demands. It requests suspension of the ongoing SIR, transparency in deletion processes, and ensuring no elector is removed without notice and a hearing opportunity.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Who are the petitioners and respondents?

  • Petitioners. Rahul Gandhi, Mallikarjun Kharge, and leaders from parties like TMC, Samajwadi Party, RJD, NCP, Shiv Sena-UBT, CPI, CPI(M), JMM, and IUML are co-petitioners.
  • Respondents. The Election Commission of India, Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, and Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi are named respondents.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What constitutional and legal issues does the petition raise?

  • Article 324 powers questioned. The petition questions whether decisions related to the SIR comply with Article 324 of the Constitution, which vests superintendence, direction, and control of elections in the Election Commission.
  • Legal compliance. It seeks examination of compliance with the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners Act, 2023, and the Representation of the People Act, 1950.
  • Transparency and accountability. The petition demands disclosure of files, agendas, minutes, objections, and access logs related to SIR decisions to ensure lawful authority and accountability.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What procedural issues delayed the Supreme Court hearing?

  • Registry defects. The Supreme Court registry flagged 14 defects in the petition, including missing clear copies of ID cards of co-petitioners and illegible or missing pages.
  • Judicial propriety. The Chief Justice declined a meeting to submit a memorandum on sub judice issues, citing separation of powers and judicial ethics.
  • Hearing assurance. The Chief Justice assured early hearing once defects are cured and instructed the petitioners to rectify them.

Back to basics: Election Commission of India and Special Intensive Revision

The Election Commission of India (ECI) is a constitutional body responsible for conducting free and fair elections, with powers under Article 324 of the Constitution. The Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is a process to update electoral rolls by adding eligible voters and deleting ineligible ones, ensuring electoral accuracy and integrity. The Chief Election Commissioner heads the ECI, and the Commission must follow legal procedures and provide transparency in electoral roll revisions.
