Cabinet Approves Continuation of Fast Track Special Courts Scheme

The Union Cabinet approved the continuation of the Centrally Sponsored Scheme for Fast Track Special Courts (FTSCs) for rape and POCSO cases for five more years from 2026-27 to 2030-31. This scheme aims to expedite trials, reduce case backlogs, and provide victim-friendly court environments, which is crucial for justice delivery in sexual offence cases.

The Cabinet extended the Fast Track Special Courts scheme to ensure speedy justice for rape and child sexual offence victims by expanding courts and strengthening judicial support.

Why is this in the news?

What is the Fast Track Special Courts Scheme?

  • Scheme purpose. The scheme provides dedicated courts for speedy trial and disposal of rape and offences under the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012.
  • Operational details. It includes operationalisation of 850 Fast Track Special Courts with dedicated Judicial Officers, Special Public Prosecutors, and six support staff per court.
  • Funding. The scheme has a total outlay of Rs.3,788.04 crore, with the central share of Rs.2,353.15 crore funded from the Nirbhaya Fund and the rest from States/UTs.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

Why was the scheme extended and what are its impacts?

  • Extension rationale. The scheme, launched in 2019 and extended multiple times, was continued to strengthen judicial capacity, reduce pendency, and ensure timely justice for victims.
  • Major impacts. It aims to provide speedy justice, reduce pending cases, create child-friendly court spaces, and reduce inequalities in access to justice for vulnerable groups.
  • Victim-centric approach. The scheme encourages Vulnerable Witness Deposition Centres to facilitate victim testimonies without causing further distress.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

How is the scheme implemented and monitored?

  • Implementation. The Department of Justice coordinates with State Governments and UTs to operationalise courts based on case pendency data.
  • Staffing and support. Each FTSC has a Judicial Officer, Special Public Prosecutor, and six support staff to expedite case disposal.
  • Monitoring. Performance is tracked via an FTSC Dashboard, progress reports, expenditure statements, and consultations with High Courts and States.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What is the background and legal basis of the scheme?

  • Launch and legal basis. The scheme was launched on 2 October 2019 following the Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2018, and Supreme Court directions for exclusive courts in districts with over 100 pending POCSO cases.
  • Supreme Court directive. The Supreme Court in July 2019 mandated establishment of exclusive special courts for timely disposal of POCSO cases.
  • Scheme evolution. Initially for one year, the scheme has been extended several times to maintain dedicated fast track courts for sexual offence cases.

Back to basics: Fast Track Courts

Fast Track Courts are special courts set up to expedite the trial and disposal of cases that require urgent attention, such as sexual offences. They aim to reduce pendency and deliver timely justice by dedicating judicial and prosecutorial resources exclusively to these cases. The scheme is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme, meaning funding is shared between the central government and states/UTs.
