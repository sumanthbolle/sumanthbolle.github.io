India and Japan Hold 4th 2+2 Ministerial Dialogue to Review Defence Ties

India's Defence Minister Rajnath Singh and External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar co-chaired the 4th India-Japan 2+2 Ministerial Dialogue with their Japanese counterparts in Tokyo on November 18-19, 2026. This dialogue reviewed bilateral strategic and defence cooperation, regional developments, and reinforced commitment to a rules-based Indo-Pacific, important for understanding India's evolving foreign and security policy.

The 4th India-Japan 2+2 Ministerial Dialogue strengthened defence cooperation and strategic ties, reaffirming shared commitment to a free and inclusive Indo-Pacific.

What is the 2+2 Ministerial Dialogue between India and Japan?

  • Definition and participants. The 2+2 Ministerial Dialogue is a strategic forum where India’s Defence and External Affairs Ministers meet their Japanese counterparts to discuss bilateral defence and foreign policy issues.
  • Purpose of the dialogue. It reviews the full spectrum of bilateral strategic and defence cooperation, exchanges views on regional and global developments, and reaffirms commitment to a free, open, inclusive, and rules-based Indo-Pacific.
  • Frequency and history. The 4th edition was held in November 2026, following earlier rounds including one in New Delhi in August 2024.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What were the key agenda items discussed in the 4th dialogue?

  • Defence equipment and technology collaboration. Both sides deliberated on advancing cooperation in defence equipment and technology development.
  • Maritime security and joint exercises. They discussed enhancing maritime security and increasing the scale and complexity of joint military exercises across all three services.
  • Defence industrial partnerships. The dialogue aimed to deepen industrial partnerships to boost operational and industrial synergy between the two countries.
  • Regional and global developments. The ministers exchanged views on regional security dynamics and reaffirmed their shared strategic interests in the Indo-Pacific.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the India-Japan 2+2 dialogue matter for India’s foreign and security policy?

  • Strengthening strategic partnership. India and Japan share a Special Strategic and Global Partnership rooted in democratic values, mutual trust, and converging strategic interests.
  • Commitment to Indo-Pacific stability. The dialogue reaffirms both countries' commitment to a free, open, inclusive, and rules-based Indo-Pacific region.
  • Enhancing defence cooperation. It facilitates closer defence ties, technology collaboration, and joint exercises, which are key to regional security.
  • Linkage with Quad and regional frameworks. The dialogue complements India’s role in the Quad and regional humanitarian and disaster relief exercises, enhancing multilateral cooperation.

Back to basics: India’s Strategic Partnerships

Strategic partnerships are long-term relationships between countries based on shared interests, values, and goals, especially in defence and foreign policy. India pursues such partnerships to enhance its security, economic growth, and regional influence. The 2+2 dialogue format involves Defence and External Affairs Ministers meeting to coordinate and deepen cooperation on strategic and security issues.
