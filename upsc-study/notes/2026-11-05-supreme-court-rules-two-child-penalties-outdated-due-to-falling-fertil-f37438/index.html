Supreme Court Declares Two-Child Penalties Outdated Amid Falling Fertility Rate

The Supreme Court ruled on November 4, 2026, that laws penalising having more than two children are no longer relevant due to India's declining fertility rate. This judgment has implications for population policy and constitutional rights, making it important for UPSC aspirants studying social issues and governance.

The Supreme Court held that two-child penalty laws have lost relevance as India's fertility rate has fallen below replacement level, impacting population control policies and constitutional rights.

Why is this in the news?

On November 4, 2026, the Supreme Court ruled that laws penalising more than two children are outdated due to demographic changes, especially the falling fertility rate in India.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court rule about two-child penalty laws?

  • Outdated laws. The Supreme Court held that laws penalising having more than two children have lost their relevance because the fear of overpopulation has diminished due to demographic changes over the past decades.
  • Judgment basis. The Court noted India's Total Fertility Rate (TFR) fell from 3.02 to 1.9, below the replacement rate of 2.1, indicating a demographic transition towards an older population.
  • Violation of rights. The Court found that linking motherhood to political representation violates Article 21 of the Constitution, impinging on reproductive autonomy and democratic participation.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this ruling matter for population policy and governance?

  • Shift in population concerns. The judgment rejects the outdated view that population growth hinders development and instead calls for encouraging healthy population growth.
  • Demographic transition impact. With a rising share of elderly persons projected to reach nearly 15% by 2036, the population structure is changing, reducing the significance of population control laws.
  • Legal and policy implications. The Court ordered a revisit of the 2003 Javed versus State of Haryana judgment that upheld two-child penalties, indicating a need to update laws in line with current demographics.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What was the case that triggered this judgment?

  • Petition details. The judgment arose from a petition by Mangala Bhimrao Ingle, whose election as Sarpanch was set aside due to having more than two children, violating the Maharashtra Village Panchayats Act.
  • Court's interim relief. The Supreme Court allowed her to continue in office and stayed proceedings against her while calling for a larger Bench to reconsider the earlier precedent.

Back to basics: Total Fertility Rate and Replacement Rate

Total Fertility Rate (TFR) measures the average number of children a woman is expected to have during her lifetime. The replacement rate, usually 2.1, is the TFR at which a population exactly replaces itself from one generation to the next without migration. Falling below this rate signals a demographic shift towards an aging population and potential population decline.
