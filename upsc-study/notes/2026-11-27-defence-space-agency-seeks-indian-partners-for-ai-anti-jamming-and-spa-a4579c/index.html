Defence Space Agency Seeks Indian Partners for AI and Space Security Tech

The Defence Space Agency (DSA) invited Indian research institutions and startups to co-develop technologies such as AI-driven orbital analytics, anti-jamming systems, and space situational awareness sensors to strengthen space-based defence capabilities. This development is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights India's focus on indigenisation and technological preparedness in the contested military domain of space.

The Defence Space Agency is partnering with Indian entities to develop indigenous AI, anti-jamming, and space surveillance technologies to secure India's space assets.

Why is this in the news?

On November 26, 2026, the Defence Space Agency announced its intent to collaborate with Indian research and startup sectors to develop advanced space defence technologies at Tec-Verse 2026 in Chennai.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What technologies is the Defence Space Agency seeking to develop?

  • AI and orbital analytics. The DSA aims to develop AI-driven orbital analytics for autonomous target recognition, anomaly detection, and real-time sensor-to-shooter data fusion onboard spacecraft.
  • Anti-jamming and resilient satellites. Technologies such as anti-jamming payloads, resilient satellite transponders, and quantum-resistant cryptography are priorities to counter electronic warfare threats.
  • Space situational awareness. Indigenous radar and electro-optical sensor networks are needed to track space debris and detect adversarial proximity operations in orbit.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does space technology matter for India's defence?

  • Space as a contested military theatre. Space has evolved from a support domain to a contested military theatre critical for communication, navigation, and meteorology, making satellite protection vital for national security.
  • Dependence on space infrastructure. Modern military operations across land, sea, air, and cyber domains depend heavily on space-based infrastructure, so disruption can blind and disable terrestrial forces.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How is India addressing technological self-reliance in space defence?

  • Reducing foreign dependence. The DSA emphasizes developing indigenous components like specialised electronic materials, chipsets, power amplifiers, and thermal protection systems to strengthen strategic autonomy.
  • Collaborations with domestic entities. The agency is actively seeking partnerships with Indian research labs and startups to co-develop technologies rather than relying on traditional procurement methods.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the armed forces' broader technology needs related to space and communication?

  • Secure communication networks. The armed forces require secure, reliable communication networks with military data stored in private clouds to enhance cybersecurity.
  • Software-defined radios. There is a demand for software-defined radios with indigenous waveforms to connect land, sea, and air platforms wirelessly at the tactical edge.
  • AI applications for battlefield awareness. AI tools that provide real-time enemy position awareness, automatic target detection, and decision support for commanders are sought to improve operational effectiveness.

Back to basics: Indigenisation in Defence Technology

Indigenisation refers to developing and producing defence technologies within the country to reduce reliance on foreign suppliers. It enhances strategic autonomy and security by ensuring critical components and systems are domestically controlled. This approach is vital for sensitive sectors like space defence where foreign dependency can pose risks to national security.
