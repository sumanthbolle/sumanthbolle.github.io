Union Environment Minister Reviews Air Pollution Control at Anand Vihar Hotspot

On October 13, 2026, the Union Environment Minister and Delhi's Chief Minister reviewed air pollution mitigation efforts at Anand Vihar, a major pollution hotspot. This included inaugurating electric vehicle infrastructure and new air quality monitoring stations, highlighting advances in environmental governance and technology use in pollution control.

The Union Environment Minister and Delhi CM jointly reviewed and enhanced pollution control measures at Anand Vihar, including AI-enabled monitoring and e-mobility initiatives.

Why is this in the news?

The Union Environment Minister and Delhi CM reviewed pollution mitigation measures at Anand Vihar hotspot and inaugurated new air quality monitoring infrastructure on October 13, 2026.

What air pollution challenges does Anand Vihar face?

  • Pollution sources. Anand Vihar hotspot experiences air pollution due to traffic congestion, bus terminal traffic, road dust from unpaved roads and potholes, construction and demolition activities, garbage dumping, and open burning of waste.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What mitigation measures have been implemented at Anand Vihar?

  • Short-term actions. Internal roads were paved, encroachments removed, construction activities regulated with dust control and green netting, illegal construction and demolition waste dumping cleared, and traffic congestion reduced.
  • Long-term actions. Greening activities are being undertaken to improve environmental quality at the hotspot.
  • Agency coordination. Eleven agencies were assigned short-term and long-term action plans to address pollution at Anand Vihar.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How is technology used to monitor and control air pollution in Delhi?

  • AI-enabled ICCC. The Delhi Pollution Control Committee's AI-enabled Integrated Command and Control Centre (ICCC) integrates data from air quality, dust control, noise levels, industrial emissions, stubble burning, citizen grievances, and enforcement vehicles for real-time monitoring and coordinated enforcement.
  • Data integration. The ICCC is designed to integrate 15 external data systems, with 9 already connected and the rest being phased in.
  • Expanded monitoring network. Thirteen new Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Stations (CAAQMS) were inaugurated, expanding Delhi's network to 60 stations providing real-time data on PM2.5 and PM10 levels.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What steps have been taken to promote clean mobility in Delhi?

  • Electric buses. 150 new electric buses were flagged off at Anand Vihar ISBT to reduce vehicular emissions.
  • EV charging infrastructure. Public electric vehicle charging stations were inaugurated at five locations including ISBT Anand Vihar and several depots, strengthening Delhi's clean mobility infrastructure.

Back to basics: Air Quality Monitoring and Pollution Control

Air quality monitoring involves measuring pollutants like PM2.5 and PM10 to assess pollution levels. Pollution control includes measures such as reducing emissions from vehicles, controlling dust from construction, and managing waste burning. Integrated Command and Control Centres use technology and data integration to enable real-time monitoring and coordinated enforcement actions across agencies.
