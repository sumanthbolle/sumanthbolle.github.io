Amit Shah Affirms 33% Women Reservation Despite Congress Opposition

Union Home Minister Amit Shah stated on November 26, 2026, that women will receive 33% reservation in Parliament and State Assemblies, asserting that the Congress party cannot prevent this. This development is significant for UPSC aspirants as it highlights ongoing debates on gender reservation, constitutional amendments, and political dynamics in India.

Amit Shah declared that 33% reservation for women legislators will be implemented despite opposition from Congress, linking it to the Delimitation Bill and emphasizing BJP's commitment to women's political representation.

What is the current status of the 33% women reservation in India?

  • Home Minister's statement. Amit Shah declared that women will definitely get 33% reservation in Parliament and State Assemblies and that Congress and its leader Rahul Gandhi cannot stop this implementation.
  • Opposition to the bill. The Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026, which aimed to expedite women’s reservation by redistributing Lok Sabha seats based on the 2011 Census, was defeated by the Opposition in April 2026.
  • Link to Delimitation Bill. The government has linked the passage of the Women’s Reservation Act to the Delimitation Bill, which is scheduled for discussion in the reconvened Parliament session starting November 30, 2026.

Why does the 33% women reservation matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Gender justice and political representation. The reservation aims to increase women's political participation and leadership, addressing gender disparities in legislative bodies.
  • Constitutional amendment process. The issue involves constitutional amendments and legislative procedures, important topics in polity and governance.
  • Political dynamics and opposition. The opposition by Congress and the BJP's commitment reflect ongoing political contestations over social justice policies.

What other constitutional and social issues did Amit Shah highlight?

  • Uniform Civil Code (UCC). Amit Shah reiterated BJP's commitment to implementing the UCC in BJP-ruled states, emphasizing governance under a common civil law irrespective of religion.

Back to basics: Reservation Policy and Constitutional Amendments

Reservation in India is a policy to provide representation to underrepresented groups in legislative bodies and public employment. It often requires constitutional amendments to change the composition of Parliament or State Assemblies. The process involves introducing bills in Parliament, which must be passed by a majority and sometimes ratified by states. Women’s reservation seeks to address gender imbalance in political representation by reserving a certain percentage of seats for women legislators.
