TDB-DST Agreement with Manastu Space for Indigenous Green Propulsion Technology

The Technology Development Board (TDB) under the Department of Science & Technology (DST) signed an agreement with Manastu Space Technologies for ₹116 crore funding to develop indigenous green propulsion systems for satellites and launch vehicles. This initiative aims to advance the technology from TRL-6 to TRL-9, enhancing India's space technology capabilities and promoting sustainable space access.

The TDB-DST agreement supports Manastu Space in advancing indigenous green propulsion technology for scalable satellite and launch vehicle applications.

Why is this in the news?

On 26 October 2026, the Technology Development Board (TDB), Department of Science & Technology (DST), signed an agreement with Manastu Space Technologies for ₹116 crore financial support under the Research Development and Innovation (RDI) Fund to develop indigenous green propulsion technology.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the TDB-DST agreement with Manastu Space about?

  • Financial support. TDB-DST agreed to provide ₹116 crore through the RDI Fund to Manastu Space for developing indigenous green propulsion technology.
  • Project scope. The project aims to develop scalable, integrated green propulsion systems for satellites and launch vehicles with thrust ranging from 5 mN to 300 N.
  • Technology advancement. The programme plans to advance the propulsion technology from Technology Readiness Level (TRL)-6 to TRL-9, supporting further development, validation, and deployment.
  • Funding mechanism. The financial support will be provided through Optionally Convertible Debt (OCD).

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the features and significance of the indigenous green propulsion technology?

  • Technology details. Manastu Space is developing a hydrogen peroxide-based green propellant with additives, integrated with an engine and catalyst system.
  • Advantages. The system offers improved performance, cost efficiency, and reduces operational challenges compared to conventional propellants.
  • Scalability. The propulsion system has a scalable architecture to serve propulsion needs from small satellites to higher-thrust launch vehicles.
  • Technical components. The project includes development of propulsion engines, propellant feed systems, and an ultra-high-temperature ceramic catalyst for reliable operation.
  • Current status. The propulsion system has already been demonstrated in space, with plans for further engineering, integration, testing, and validation.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this agreement matter for India’s space technology and economy?

  • Strengthening indigenous capabilities. The initiative aims to reduce reliance on externally sourced propulsion technologies by strengthening domestic capabilities in a critical space value chain component.
  • Supporting sustainable space access. The green propulsion technology promotes safer and more sustainable satellite operations and space missions.
  • Alignment with Deep Technology sector. The project falls under the Deep Technology sector with Space Technology as the sub-sector, reflecting focus on advanced technology development.
  • Government support. TDB’s RDI Fund supports technology-intensive programmes requiring sustained investment to progress from advanced development to deployment.

Back to basics: Technology Readiness Level (TRL)

Technology Readiness Level (TRL) is a scale from 1 to 9 that measures the maturity of a technology, from basic research (TRL-1) to fully operational systems (TRL-9). Advancing a technology from TRL-6 to TRL-9 involves engineering development, integration, testing, and validation to prepare it for deployment in real-world applications.
