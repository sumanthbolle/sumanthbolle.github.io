Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment at 45th IITF 2026 Promotes Inclusive Economic Empowerment

The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment showcased handicrafts and traditional products by over 130 artisans from 19 states at the 45th India International Trade Fair (IITF) 2026, recording cumulative sales of ₹17.37 lakh by November 16. This participation highlights the Ministry’s efforts to strengthen market access and sustainable livelihoods for marginalized artisans under initiatives like the TULIP brand.

The Ministry’s participation at IITF 2026 advances inclusive socio-economic development by promoting traditional artisans and entrepreneurship from disadvantaged communities.

Why is this in the news?

The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment’s pavilion at the 45th IITF 2026 recorded increasing sales and showcased traditional artisans’ products, reinforcing its commitment to inclusive empowerment and sustainable livelihoods.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What was the Ministry’s role at IITF 2026?

  • Showcasing artisans. Over 130 artisans from 19 states participated through the Ministry’s three Apex Corporations, exhibiting handicrafts, handlooms, and traditional products reflecting India's rich cultural heritage.
  • Market platform. The Ministry’s pavilion at Hall No. 5, ITPO provided a platform for direct interaction between artisans and consumers, enabling artisans to understand market trends and generate income.
  • Sales growth. Cumulative sales increased from approximately ₹5.50 lakh on November 15 to ₹17.37 lakh by November 16, indicating growing consumer appreciation and expanding market opportunities.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

How does this participation support inclusive empowerment?

  • Targeted beneficiaries. The Ministry focuses on artisans and entrepreneurs from Scheduled Castes, Backward Classes, Safai Mitras, and De-Notified, Nomadic and Semi-Nomadic Tribes, promoting their economic participation.
  • TULIP initiative. The Traditional Artisans' Upliftment Livelihood Programme (TULIP) brand was promoted to enhance visibility, recognition, and market access for artisan-made products nationally and internationally.
  • Preserving heritage and innovation. TULIP connects traditional craftsmanship with contemporary consumer preferences, encouraging innovation, entrepreneurship, and economic self-reliance among artisans.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

Why do national exhibitions matter for social justice and economic development?

  • Market linkage. Exhibitions like IITF bridge the gap between traditional producers and mainstream markets, enabling artisans to convert skills into sustainable income.
  • Entrepreneurial growth. Such platforms help artisans explore emerging trends, establish business connections, and identify opportunities for long-term growth.
  • Role of Apex Corporations. The Ministry’s Apex Corporations facilitate market access, promote entrepreneurship, and strengthen socio-economic status of marginalized beneficiaries.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What are the Ministry’s broader efforts for inclusive development?

  • Samaveshi Niti Sankalp Summit. The Ministry is hosting a two-day Departmental Summit to strengthen Centre-State partnerships for inclusive socio-economic development, focusing on social justice and welfare programme implementation.
  • Policy consultations. Extensive consultations with over 13,500 responses from 450+ districts informed the Summit’s thematic areas, aiming to improve welfare delivery and social inclusion.
  • Digital initiatives. The Summit features a digital exhibition showcasing key programmes and technology-driven tools enhancing service delivery and institutional mechanisms.

Back to basics: Inclusive Socio-Economic Empowerment

Inclusive socio-economic empowerment aims to integrate marginalized and disadvantaged groups into mainstream economic activities by providing them access to resources, markets, and opportunities. It involves targeted welfare schemes, skill development, and market linkages to ensure sustainable livelihoods and reduce social disparities. The government often facilitates this through specialized corporations and initiatives that promote entrepreneurship among vulnerable communities.

What happens next?

  • Strengthen market access. The Ministry should continue expanding platforms like IITF and promote initiatives like TULIP to enhance national and international market reach for traditional artisans.
  • Enhance Centre-State coordination. The Departmental Summit aims to improve coordination between Centre and States for effective implementation of social justice programmes with clear timelines and shared responsibilities.
  • Leverage digital tools. Adoption of integrated digital platforms and mobile applications can improve service delivery and monitoring of welfare schemes for marginalized sections.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment’s participation at the 45th IITF 2026: 1. The Ministry showcased products from artisans belonging to Scheduled Castes, Backward Classes, Safai Mitras, and De-Notified Tribes through its three Apex Corporations. 2. The TULIP initiative aims to connect traditional craftsmanship with contemporary market demands and was launched during the 45th IITF 2026. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the TULIP initiative was launched earlier at the Shilp Samagam Mela at Dilli Haat, not during the IITF 2026.
