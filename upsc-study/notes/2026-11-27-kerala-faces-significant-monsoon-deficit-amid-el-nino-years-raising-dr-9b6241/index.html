Kerala's Monsoon Deficit and El Niño Impact: Historical Trends and Concerns

Kerala has experienced below normal monsoon rainfall in only 5 of the 15 El Niño years between 1951 and 2022, indicating that El Niño does not always cause drought in the state. Understanding this pattern is important for assessing drought risk and water management in Kerala.

Kerala's monsoon deficit during El Niño years is less frequent than the national average, with only 5 deficient monsoons in 15 El Niño years.

Why is this in the news?

Concerns about Kerala's monsoon deficit have risen due to forecasts of El Niño conditions in 2023, prompting analysis of historical rainfall patterns during El Niño years by the India Meteorological Department (IMD).

How has Kerala's monsoon rainfall been affected during El Niño years?

  • Historical rainfall data. Between 1951 and 2022, Kerala experienced deficient monsoon rainfall in only 5 out of 15 El Niño years, specifically in 1965, 1972, 1987, 2002, and 2015.
  • Comparison with national trend. India as a whole saw poor monsoon in 9 of those 15 El Niño years, indicating Kerala's monsoon is less frequently affected by El Niño than the national average.
  • Monsoon deficit frequency. From 1901 to 2021, Kerala had 14 deficient monsoon years, with the lowest rainfall recorded in 1918 at 1,104.3 mm compared to the current average of 2,018.7 mm.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does El Niño not always cause monsoon deficit in Kerala?

  • Timing of El Niño events. El Niño events typically develop by August-September, by which time Kerala may have already received over 60% of its annual rainfall.
  • Types of El Niño. Different strengths of El Niño (weak to very strong) influence its impact, and not all El Niño events lead to deficient rainfall in Kerala.
  • Influence of Indian Ocean Dipole. A positive Indian Ocean Dipole, with warmer western Indian Ocean waters, can counteract El Niño's effects, resulting in normal or above normal rainfall in Kerala.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the implications for drought and water management in Kerala?

  • Monsoon rainfall variability. Kerala's monsoon variability during El Niño years suggests drought risk is not uniform and depends on multiple climatic factors.
  • Drought preparedness. Historical data can guide water resource planning and drought mitigation strategies by identifying years with potential rainfall shortfall.
  • Forecasting challenges. Despite El Niño forecasts, Kerala may still receive normal rainfall, so reliance on multiple climatic indicators is essential for accurate predictions.

Back to basics: Monsoon and El Niño

The southwest monsoon is the main rainy season in Kerala, supplying most of its annual rainfall. El Niño is a climatic phenomenon characterized by warming of the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean, which can influence monsoon patterns in India. However, El Niño's effects vary by region and are influenced by other factors like the Indian Ocean Dipole, which affects sea surface temperatures in the Indian Ocean and can modulate rainfall in Kerala.
