DRI Busts Gold Smuggling via Indo-Myanmar Border; ED Probes Betel Nut Smuggling

The Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI) seized 6.28 kg of foreign-origin gold worth ₹9.18 crore concealed in hardware items at Trivandrum airport, busting a sophisticated smuggling network using unaccompanied baggage. Meanwhile, the Enforcement Directorate (ED) searched 16 locations linked to a well-organised racket smuggling foreign-origin betel nut through the Indo-Myanmar border, involving forged documents and extensive money laundering.

DRI and ED uncovered and disrupted sophisticated smuggling networks exploiting the Indo-Myanmar border to illegally bring gold and betel nut into India, highlighting challenges in border security and economic regulation.

Why is this in the news?

On October 15 and 16, 2026, the Directorate of Revenue Intelligence seized gold smuggled through unaccompanied baggage at Trivandrum, and the Enforcement Directorate conducted searches linked to betel nut smuggling via the Indo-Myanmar border.

What was the gold smuggling modus operandi busted by DRI?

  • Concealment method. The DRI found 6.28 kg of foreign-origin gold ingeniously concealed inside hardware items like floor springs, metal locks, a grease gun, and a door closer packed in a trolley.
  • Unaccompanied baggage channel. The gold was smuggled from Dubai to Trivandrum as unaccompanied baggage, with the trolley secured by a number lock whose combination was pre-decided among syndicate members.
  • Insider involvement. Warehouse personnel opened the trolley upon arrival, retrieved the concealed gold, and handed it to a mediator who delivered it to the receiver.
  • Arrests made. Three persons were arrested: a warehouse staff member, a customs broker mediator, and the receiver, all under the Customs Act, 1962.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How does the ED investigation expose betel nut smuggling through the Indo-Myanmar border?

  • Smuggling route. Dried areca nut consignments were brought overland to the Indo-Myanmar border opposite Champhai in Mizoram, ferried across the Tiau river at Zokhawthar in small lots, bypassing the Land Customs Station.
  • Racket structure. Agents on the Myanmar side sold consignments to front firms in India, which were shell companies registered in Mizoram and Assam but controlled by operators in Silchar, Assam.
  • Document forgery and transport. The smuggled betel nut was transported using forged GST invoices, e-way bills, and transport documents falsely describing it as Indian-origin produce.
  • Money laundering and asset seizure. Unaccounted cash of about ₹95 lakh was seized, fixed deposits worth nearly ₹85 lakh frozen, and bank credits exceeding ₹1,000 crore linked to the syndicate were identified.
  • Geographical significance. Champhai has no recorded areca nut production and Mizoram had no betel nut processing units, indicating the consignments were smuggled in rather than locally produced.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why do these smuggling cases matter for internal security and economy?

  • Border vulnerabilities. The Indo-Myanmar border is exploited for smuggling due to porous terrain and bypassing of official customs checkpoints.
  • Economic impact. Smuggling leads to loss of customs revenue and affects domestic industries like gutka and pan masala manufacturers who face unfair competition.
  • Organised crime and money laundering. These cases reveal sophisticated networks involving insiders, shell companies, forged documents, and extensive money laundering, challenging enforcement agencies.

Back to basics: Smuggling and Border Security

Smuggling involves illegal movement of goods across borders to evade customs duties and regulations. Porous borders and inadequate surveillance create vulnerabilities exploited by organized crime. Enforcement agencies like the Directorate of Revenue Intelligence and Enforcement Directorate investigate and act against smuggling and related money laundering to protect economic interests and internal security.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the recent DRI and ED operations: 1. The DRI seized gold concealed in hardware items shipped as unaccompanied baggage from Dubai to Trivandrum. 2. The ED investigation revealed betel nut smuggling through the Indo-Myanmar border involving forged documents and shell companies. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). A trap would be to confuse the location or method of smuggling; DRI's gold seizure was at Trivandrum airport via unaccompanied baggage from Dubai, while ED's betel nut smuggling involved the Indo-Myanmar border and shell companies.
