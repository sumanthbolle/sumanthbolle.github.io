Cyclone Arnab Causes Fatalities and Disruptions in Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, Telangana

Cyclone Arnab struck Andhra Pradesh on October 28, 2026, causing fatalities, extensive damage to crops, power infrastructure, and displacing thousands. The Union Home Minister assured full central assistance, with rescue and relief operations underway, highlighting disaster management and cooperative governance challenges.

Cyclone Arnab caused deaths, crop and infrastructure damage in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha, prompting central and state coordinated relief efforts.

Why is this in the news?

Cyclone Arnab made landfall on October 28, 2026, causing fatalities and damage in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha, triggering central government intervention and relief operations.

What were the impacts of Cyclone Arnab?

  • Fatalities and damage. Cyclone Arnab caused five deaths in Andhra Pradesh due to falling trees, electric poles, electrocution, and collapsing walls; Odisha experienced heavy rainfall affecting 17 districts with no major deaths reported yet.
  • Crop and infrastructure loss. Over 70,000 hectares of agricultural crops and more than 10,500 hectares of horticultural crops were damaged in Andhra Pradesh; power supply was disrupted for over 25 lakh non-farm and nearly one lakh farm connections, with thousands of electric poles and transformers damaged.
  • Displacement and evacuations. More than 30,000 people were evacuated in Odisha from low-lying and landslide-prone areas; relief camps were set up in Andhra Pradesh with provisions of food and financial aid to affected families.

[3] The Hindu · Secondary

How did the government respond to Cyclone Arnab?

  • Central government assistance. Union Home Minister Amit Shah assured full assistance to affected states and directed National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) and State Disaster Response Force (SDRF) teams to conduct rescue and relief operations on a war footing.
  • State government measures. Andhra Pradesh Chief Minister Chandrababu Naidu sought central aid, announced compensation of ₹5 lakh to families of the deceased, and financial support to affected farmers and households; Telangana extended support for restoration works.
  • Impact assessment and monitoring. Impact Monitoring and Coordination Teams (IMCTs) were to be deployed to assess damage and determine further assistance; meteorological departments monitored cyclone weakening and forecasted rainfall and flood risks.

[1] Press Information Bureau[3] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does Cyclone Arnab matter for UPSC preparation?

  • Disaster management relevance. The cyclone exemplifies inter-agency coordination in disaster response involving central and state governments, NDRF, SDRF, and meteorological departments, a key topic in disaster management.
  • Geographical and climatic understanding. The event highlights the vulnerability of coastal and adjoining inland districts to cyclones and heavy rainfall, important for physical geography and climate impact studies.
  • Governance and cooperative response. The role of cooperative governance in relief distribution and restoration of services reflects governance and administrative challenges in crisis situations.

Back to basics: Disaster Management

Disaster management involves preparedness, response, relief, and rehabilitation activities coordinated by central and state governments. It includes early warning systems, evacuation, rescue operations, and restoration of essential services to reduce the impact of natural calamities like cyclones and floods.
