Cyclone Arnab to Hit Andhra Pradesh Coast as Very Severe Cyclonic Storm

Cyclone Arnab intensified over the Bay of Bengal and is forecast to make landfall on the Andhra Pradesh coast by October 28, 2026, with winds reaching 115-125 kmph gusting to 140 kmph. UPSC aspirants should note the cyclone's progression, affected regions, and disaster preparedness measures as they relate to physical geography and disaster management.

Cyclone Arnab is expected to cross the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts as a very severe cyclonic storm on October 28, 2026, with significant wind speeds and impact.

Why is this in the news?

On October 25, 2026, the India Meteorological Department (IMD) issued a bulletin forecasting that Cyclone Arnab would intensify into a very severe cyclonic storm and make landfall on the Andhra Pradesh coast by October 28.

What is the current status and forecast of Cyclone Arnab?

  • Cyclone intensification. Cyclone Arnab intensified from a depression over the eastcentral and adjoining southeast Bay of Bengal into a very severe cyclonic storm by October 28, 2026.
  • Location and movement. As of October 25, the cyclone was centered about 870 km east-southeast of Vishakhapatnam, moving southwestwards and expected to move west-northwestwards towards Andhra Pradesh.
  • Expected landfall. The cyclone is forecast to cross the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam on the morning of October 28 with maximum sustained winds of 115-125 kmph gusting to 140 kmph.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What disaster management preparations are underway for Cyclone Arnab?

  • Evacuations planned. People in coastal districts of Andhra Pradesh including Bapatla, Krishna, West Godavari, and Visakhapatnam are to be evacuated to cyclone shelters by October 27.
  • Deployment of forces. The Andhra Pradesh government has deployed 12 battalions of the State Disaster Response Force and seven battalions of the National Disaster Response Force, with three more NDRF battalions requisitioned.
  • Special care measures. Special arrangements are in place for vulnerable groups such as pregnant women, senior citizens, children, and persons with disabilities.
  • Odisha's preparedness. Odisha has deployed ministers and IAS officers to supervise disaster management in five districts expected to face heavy rainfall, with evacuation and health measures underway.

Back to basics: Cyclones and Disaster Management in India

Cyclones are intense circular storms originating over warm ocean waters, characterized by low pressure and strong winds. The Bay of Bengal is prone to cyclones, especially during the post-monsoon season. India’s disaster management involves early warning by the India Meteorological Department, evacuation plans, deployment of specialized forces like the NDRF and SDRF, and special care for vulnerable populations to reduce loss of life and property during such events.
