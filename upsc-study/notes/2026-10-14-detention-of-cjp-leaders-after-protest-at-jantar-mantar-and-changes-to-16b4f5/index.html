Detention of CJP Leaders After Jantar Mantar Protest and Protest Site Changes

Fourteen leaders of the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) were detained by Delhi Police on October 13, 2026, after attempting to protest at Jantar Mantar, which was de-notified as an authorised protest site a day earlier. This incident highlights ongoing tensions over the regulation of protest sites and the right to demonstrate in India's capital, a key issue for civil liberties and governance.

Delhi Police detained CJP leaders after Jantar Mantar was de-notified as a protest site, raising questions on protest rights and state regulation.

Why is this in the news?

On October 13, 2026, Delhi Police detained CJP leaders following the de-notification of Jantar Mantar as an authorised protest site and designated alternative venues for demonstrations.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What happened during the CJP protest at Jantar Mantar?

  • Detention of leaders. Fourteen members of the Cockroach Janta Party, including leaders Saurav Das, Ashutosh Ranka, and Ajinkya Shinde, were detained by Delhi Police while trying to enter Jantar Mantar on October 13, 2026.
  • Charges filed. The New Delhi district police registered an FIR against the detained individuals for unlawful assembly and disobedience of a public servant's order.
  • Entry through eateries. Police stated the protesters entered the area through eateries and food stalls near Jantar Mantar, which are usually open to the public except on major protest days.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why was Jantar Mantar de-notified as a protest site?

  • De-notification decision. Delhi Police removed Jantar Mantar from the list of authorised protest sites and designated three alternative venues for demonstrations starting October 12, 2026.
  • Legal implication. With Jantar Mantar no longer authorised, protests there are considered illegal, leading to detentions and FIRs.
  • Criticism from activists. Activists argue that removing Jantar Mantar as a protest site distances demonstrations from the political establishment and makes it harder for citizens to press their demands.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What broader issues does this incident highlight?

  • Right to protest. The incident raises questions about the balance between public order and citizens' constitutional right to protest in public spaces.
  • State regulation of protests. The police action and site de-notification reflect state efforts to regulate where and how protests can occur in the national capital.
  • Political context. The protests were linked to demands for the removal of the Chief Election Commissioner and scrapping of the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls.

Back to basics: Right to Protest and Public Space Regulation

The right to protest is a fundamental democratic right allowing citizens to express dissent publicly. Governments regulate protests to balance public order and safety with civil liberties. Designated protest sites are official locations where demonstrations can be held legally, ensuring order while allowing public expression.
