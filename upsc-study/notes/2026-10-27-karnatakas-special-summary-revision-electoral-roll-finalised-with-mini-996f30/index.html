Karnataka Finalises Special Summary Revision Electoral Roll with Minimal Net Additions

Karnataka completed its Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of the electoral roll on October 27, 2026, resulting in a significant deletion of names but only a small net increase in voters. This update is important for understanding electoral roll management and reforms in Indian elections.

Karnataka's SIR exercise deleted over one crore names from its electoral roll, finalising at around 4.54 crore voters with minimal net additions.

Why is this in the news?

The final electoral roll after Karnataka's Special Intensive Revision was published on October 27, 2026, following a clean-up exercise that removed over one crore names.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What was the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) exercise in Karnataka?

  • Purpose of SIR. The SIR aimed to ensure no eligible voter was excluded and no ineligible voter remained on the electoral rolls.
  • Duration and process. The exercise lasted over three months, involving deletion of duplicate, dead, and shifted voters from the rolls.
  • Final publication. The final electoral roll was published on October 27, 2026, on the Chief Electoral Officer, Karnataka's website.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What changes occurred in the electoral roll after the SIR?

  • Initial voter count. Karnataka had 5.54 crore voters before the revision began.
  • Number of deletions. The exercise deleted 1.07 crore names, including 16.39 lakh dead voters, over 7 lakh duplicates, and 80.62 lakh shifted or untraceable voters.
  • Final voter count. The final roll has around 4.54 crore voters after deletions.
  • Minimal additions. Only 7,87,534 applications for inclusion were received, including new voters, which did not offset the deletions.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the SIR matter for electoral reforms and governance?

  • Improving roll accuracy. The SIR helps maintain accurate voter lists by removing ineligible names and duplicates.
  • Challenges in voter inclusion. Despite deletions, many eligible voters who shifted residences need to apply through Form 6 to be restored, but applications remain low.
  • Transparency issues. Details on categories like 'Others' deleted and counts of foreign citizens identified remain unclear.

Back to basics: Electoral Roll Revision

Electoral rolls are periodically revised to ensure accuracy by adding eligible voters and removing ineligible ones such as deceased persons, duplicates, or those who have shifted residence. Special Summary Revision is a focused exercise conducted to clean up the rolls before elections, improving the integrity of voter lists and preventing electoral fraud.

Answer and trap

Mains question (15 marks)

Examine the significance of Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls in strengthening democratic governance in India.

Examine: Explain how the SIR process improves electoral roll accuracy and discuss its challenges and implications for democracy.

Intro: Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is a focused exercise to update and clean electoral rolls before elections.

  • SIR removes ineligible voters such as deceased, duplicates, and shifted voters to improve roll accuracy.
  • It helps prevent electoral fraud and ensures only eligible voters participate in elections.
  • Challenges include low applications for re-inclusion by shifted voters and lack of transparency in some deletion categories.
  • Effective SIR strengthens democratic governance by enhancing voter list integrity but requires better awareness and transparency.

Conclusion: SIR is a vital electoral reform tool that, if implemented well, supports free and fair elections in India.
