Odisha Files Contempt Petition Against Andhra Pradesh Over Kotia Panchayat Election

The Odisha government filed a contempt petition in the Supreme Court challenging Andhra Pradesh's notification for elections in the disputed Kotia group of villages. This case highlights constitutional and federal issues related to state boundaries, elections, and judicial authority.

Odisha's contempt petition against Andhra Pradesh over Kotia elections raises key questions on federalism and judicial jurisdiction in inter-state disputes.

Why is this in the news?

Odisha filed a contempt petition in the Supreme Court on October 23, 2026, against Andhra Pradesh for issuing an election notification in the disputed Kotia villages.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Kotia territorial dispute about?

  • Disputed villages. The Kotia group consists of 21 villages in Odisha's Koraput district whose jurisdiction is disputed by Andhra Pradesh since 1955.
  • Historical control. Odisha exercised administrative control over these villages from 1936, but Andhra Pradesh claims revenue collection since 1947 and electoral participation in 1954.
  • Boundary demarcation controversy. Andhra Pradesh relies on a 1942 boundary demarcation by M. Gilby, which Odisha rejects as inconsistent with the 1936 Government of India order.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What legal and political steps have been taken so far?

  • Supreme Court suit and status quo. Odisha filed a suit in the Supreme Court in 1968, but it was dismissed in 2006 for lack of jurisdiction under Article 131, with a directive to maintain status quo.
  • Failed negotiations. Multiple talks between state leaders in 1968, 1978, and 2021 failed to resolve the dispute.
  • Recent contempt petitions. Odisha filed contempt petitions in 2021 and 2026 alleging Andhra Pradesh violated the status quo by holding elections in Kotia.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for federalism and elections?

  • Inter-state boundary conflict. The dispute exemplifies challenges in resolving state boundary issues that affect local governance and electoral jurisdiction.
  • Judicial limitations. The Supreme Court's dismissal citing Article 131 shows that such disputes require parliamentary or executive resolution, limiting judicial intervention.
  • Election administration. Notification of elections by Andhra Pradesh in disputed areas raises questions about authority and the maintenance of status quo in electoral processes.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How are the states trying to influence the Kotia population?

  • Odisha's approach. Odisha focuses on infrastructure development like schools, roads, bridges, and government institutions to win local support.
  • Andhra Pradesh's approach. Andhra Pradesh emphasizes admitting Kotia children to its schools and distributing pension benefits to residents.

Back to basics: Inter-state boundary disputes and Article 131

Inter-state boundary disputes arise when two states claim jurisdiction over the same territory. Article 131 of the Constitution grants the Supreme Court original jurisdiction in disputes between states, but with limitations. The Supreme Court can only adjudicate these disputes if they involve questions of law and not if barred by specific provisos. Often, such disputes require political or parliamentary resolution rather than judicial intervention. Maintaining status quo means no administrative or electoral changes should occur in the disputed area until resolution.

Common mistake. The Supreme Court dismissed Odisha's original suit not because the dispute was invalid, but because it lacked jurisdiction under Article 131, requiring parliamentary action for resolution.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the Kotia panchayat election dispute: 1. The Supreme Court dismissed Odisha's original suit in 2006 citing lack of jurisdiction under Article 131. 2. Andhra Pradesh has been collecting revenue from the Kotia villages since 1936. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because Andhra Pradesh claims revenue collection since 1947, not 1936; Odisha exercised control from 1936.

Intro: Inter-state boundary disputes in India pose challenges to federalism and governance.

  • Historical claims and administrative control often conflict, as seen in the Kotia villages dispute between Odisha and Andhra Pradesh.
  • Judicial limitations exist because the Supreme Court dismissed Odisha's suit citing lack of jurisdiction under Article 131, indicating the need for parliamentary or executive resolution.
  • Election notifications in disputed areas raise questions about authority and the maintenance of status quo, complicating local governance.
  • Both states attempt to influence local populations through development and welfare schemes, reflecting political stakes in boundary disputes.

Conclusion: Resolving inter-state boundary disputes requires coordinated judicial, legislative, and political efforts to uphold federal principles and local governance.
