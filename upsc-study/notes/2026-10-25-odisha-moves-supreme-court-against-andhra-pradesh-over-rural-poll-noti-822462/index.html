Odisha Challenges Andhra Pradesh Over Panchayat Polls in Kotia Area

Odisha has filed a contempt petition in the Supreme Court against Andhra Pradesh for issuing a notification to conduct rural local body elections in the disputed Kotia region, which Odisha claims lies within its territory. This dispute highlights issues of inter-state boundary conflicts and the role of the judiciary in enforcing status quo orders under India's federal structure.

Odisha moved the Supreme Court against Andhra Pradesh for violating a 2006 order by conducting panchayat elections in the disputed Kotia area, underscoring federalism and judicial oversight in inter-state disputes.

Why is this in the news?

On October 24, 2026, Odisha filed a contempt of court petition in the Supreme Court against Andhra Pradesh for conducting panchayat elections in the disputed Kotia region, violating a 2006 Supreme Court order to maintain status quo.

[3] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the dispute over the Kotia area?

  • Disputed villages. All 28 villages under Kotia Gram Panchayat are in Odisha territory, but Andhra Pradesh claims rights over 21 of these villages.
  • Historical legal challenge. Odisha challenged Andhra Pradesh's claim in the Supreme Court in 1968, which led to a 2006 order directing both states to maintain the status quo.

[3] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered Odisha's contempt petition in 2026?

  • Election notification. Andhra Pradesh issued a notification on October 10, 2026, to conduct Zilla Parishad and Panchayat Samiti elections in the disputed Kotia villages, violating the Supreme Court's 2006 order.
  • Legal response. Odisha filed a contempt petition through the Koraput district collector with approval from the Chief Minister, making Andhra Pradesh officials involved in election management parties to the case.

[3] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for Indian federalism and judiciary?

  • Inter-state boundary conflict. The dispute exemplifies challenges in resolving inter-state boundary claims and the importance of respecting judicial orders to maintain federal harmony.
  • Role of Supreme Court. The Supreme Court's 2006 status quo order and Odisha's contempt petition demonstrate judicial activism in enforcing compliance and preventing unilateral actions by states.

Back to basics: Inter-state boundary disputes and judicial intervention

Inter-state boundary disputes arise when two states claim overlapping territories. The Constitution provides mechanisms for resolving such disputes, including judicial adjudication by the Supreme Court. The Court can issue orders such as maintaining status quo to prevent escalation while the dispute is resolved. Contempt petitions enforce compliance with such orders, ensuring respect for judicial authority and federal balance.
