Air Chief Marshal Ashutosh Dixit Assumes Charge as 29th Chief of Air Staff, Unveils ‘VAYU’ Vision

On 1 November 2026, Air Chief Marshal Ashutosh Dixit took charge as the 29th Chief of the Indian Air Force and outlined his strategic vision ‘VAYU’ focusing on operational readiness, self-reliance, innovation, and jointness. This leadership change and vision are important for understanding the future trajectory of India's air defence capabilities and modernization efforts.

Air Chief Marshal Ashutosh Dixit became the 29th Chief of Air Staff on 1 November 2026, introducing the ‘VAYU’ vision to enhance IAF's readiness, indigenous capability, innovation, and joint operations.

Why is this in the news?

Air Chief Marshal Ashutosh Dixit assumed charge as the 29th Chief of Air Staff on 1 November 2026 and unveiled the ‘VAYU’ vision for the Indian Air Force.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Who is Air Chief Marshal Ashutosh Dixit and what is his background?

  • Profile and experience. Air Chief Marshal Ashutosh Dixit is an Experimental Test Pilot and Qualified Flying Instructor with over 3,500 flying hours, having held key operational and strategic roles including Chief of Integrated Defence Staff and Vice Chief of the Air Staff.
  • Ceremonial assumption of charge. He assumed office on 1 November 2026, paying homage at the National War Memorial and receiving a ceremonial Guard of Honour at Air Force Station New Delhi.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the ‘VAYU’ vision outlined by the new Chief of Air Staff?

  • V - Vigilance, Victory and Readiness. Focus on maintaining the highest operational preparedness across all formations and restoring the IAF's combat strength to sanctioned levels.
  • A - Aatmanirbharta (Self-reliance). Accelerating indigenous development of platforms, sensors, weapons, and technologies to ensure operational self-reliance and sustained combat capability.
  • Y - Yukti (Ingenuity and Innovation). Harnessing innovation in doctrine, technology, and operations including AI-enabled command and control, counter-drone capabilities, and emerging operational domains.
  • U - Unity of Effort. Strengthening jointness with the Army, Navy, and national agencies aligned with the national vision of Jointness, Aatmanirbharta, and Innovation.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the ‘VAYU’ vision matter for India’s defence strategy?

  • Adapting to evolving warfare. The vision addresses the changing character of warfare shaped by drones, long-range precision weapons, space, cyber, AI, and electromagnetic spectrum.
  • Focus on indigenous capabilities. Prioritising indigenous platforms like Tejas Mk1A and Mk2, Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA), and Indian sensors and weapons to reduce dependence on foreign technology.
  • Enhancing joint operations. Emphasising integrated planning and operations with other armed forces and agencies to maximise air power effectiveness.
  • Personnel welfare and morale. Commitment to the training, professional excellence, morale, safety, and welfare of Air Warriors, Agniveervayu, civilian employees, veterans, and their families.

Back to basics: Role of Chief of Air Staff and Air Force Modernization

The Chief of Air Staff is the professional head of the Indian Air Force responsible for operational readiness, strategic planning, and modernization. The Air Force continuously adapts to technological and geopolitical changes by upgrading platforms, enhancing indigenous capabilities, and fostering jointness with other services to maintain national security.
