Delhi SIR Public Audit Finds Over 100 Voters Wrongly Marked Absent or Shifted

A public audit in two polling booths of Delhi’s Okhla constituency found over 100 voters wrongly classified as absent, shifted, dead, or duplicate during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls. This issue highlights challenges in electoral roll accuracy and transparency, important for fair democratic processes.

The Delhi SIR public audit revealed significant errors in voter classification, affecting electoral roll integrity and voter rights.

Why is this in the news?

On October 18, 2026, a public hearing in Delhi’s Okhla constituency revealed over 100 voters wrongly marked absent, shifted, or dead in the draft electoral roll after the SIR enumeration phase.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Delhi SIR public audit find?

  • Wrong voter classification. The audit found at least 100 voters wrongly classified as absent, shifted, dead, or duplicate in two polling booths covering 258 households.
  • Affected voters’ complaints. Long-term residents, including a retired professor and others living at the same address for decades, were marked 'untraceable' or 'permanently shifted' despite submitting enumeration forms.
  • Scale of deletions. Okhla constituency had over 1.85 lakh voters in the draft roll, with nearly 1.6 lakh names in the absent, shifted, dead, or duplicate (ASDD) list, making it the second highest in deletions.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for electoral integrity?

  • Voter disenfranchisement risk. Wrongly marking voters as absent or shifted forces them to re-enroll using Form 6, risking loss of voting records and disenfranchisement.
  • Transparency and accountability gaps. The public hearing exposed lack of communication from Booth Level Officers (BLOs) and election officials, with many voters unaware of special camps for corrections.
  • Impact on democratic participation. Errors in electoral rolls undermine voter confidence and participation, affecting the legitimacy of elections.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the corrective measures and challenges?

  • Special camps and re-verification. The Delhi Chief Electoral Officer’s office has held weekend camps since September 20 to ensure no eligible voter is left out and directed re-verification of ASDD entries through door-to-door visits.
  • Need for fresh enrolment. Affected voters currently have to apply afresh using Form 6, as their names were deleted or marked untraceable in the draft roll.
  • Administrative gaps. Election officials and BLOs did not attend the public hearing, and many voters were unaware of correction processes, indicating gaps in outreach and accountability.

Back to basics: Electoral Roll Revision and Voter Registration

Electoral rolls are periodically revised to update voter information, remove ineligible names, and add new voters. The Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is a process to verify and update the rolls comprehensively. Voters can challenge errors through public hearings and apply for corrections or fresh enrolment using prescribed forms like Form 6. Accuracy and transparency in this process are essential for protecting voter rights and ensuring credible elections.

What happens next?

  • Improve voter outreach. Election authorities need to enhance communication with voters about correction camps and procedures to prevent disenfranchisement.
  • Strengthen accountability. Booth Level Officers and election officials should be more responsive and present during public audits and hearings to address voter grievances promptly.
