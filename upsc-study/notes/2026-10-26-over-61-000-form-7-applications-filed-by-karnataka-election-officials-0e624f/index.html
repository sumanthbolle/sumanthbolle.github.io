Over 61,000 Form-7 Applications Filed by Karnataka Election Officials Without Reasons

Karnataka election officials filed over 61,000 Form-7 applications to delete voter names without citing mandatory reasons, raising concerns about electoral roll transparency and procedure. This issue is important for understanding election governance and administrative accountability in India.

Karnataka election officials filed thousands of Form-7 applications without reasons, potentially undermining the transparency and fairness of electoral roll deletions.

Why is this in the news?

On October 26, 2026, The Hindu reported that over 61,000 Form-7 applications were filed by Karnataka election officials without stating reasons, violating the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are Form-7 applications and why were they filed without reasons?

  • Form-7 purpose. Form-7 applications are filed to object to and seek deletion of names from electoral rolls for reasons like shifted residence, duplicate entries, underage status, death, or non-citizenship.
  • Mandatory reason requirement. Under the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960, stating the reason for deletion in Form-7 is mandatory, and failure to do so can lead to rejection of the objection.
  • Auto-generation of forms. Election officials said the system auto-generated these forms with their digital signatures but left the reason blank, as voters could not be reached during hearings.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What issues does this raise about electoral roll management?

  • Transparency concerns. Without stated reasons, neither voters nor officials can verify why names were deleted, undermining transparency and accountability in electoral roll management.
  • Procedural irregularities. Names were deleted even after the official claims and objections period ended, which contradicts Rule 17 of the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960 that mandates rejection of late or improperly filed objections.
  • Conflict of interest. Election officials themselves filed the objections and decided on deletions, raising questions about impartiality in the process.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the scale and impact of these Form-7 applications in Karnataka?

  • Number of applications. At least 61,059 Form-7 applications were filed by election officials without reasons, with a sharp rise after the claims and objections period ended on September 23, 2026.
  • Constituency-wise pattern. In some constituencies like Bommanahalli, officials filed more Form-7 applications than voters or third parties, indicating a systemic pattern.
  • Effect on electoral rolls. Names cited in these applications were already deleted from the electoral roll, including those filed after the deadline, affecting the accuracy of the final roll published on October 27, 2026.

Back to basics: Electoral Roll Revision and Form-7 Objections

Electoral rolls are periodically revised to ensure accuracy by adding eligible voters and removing ineligible ones. Form-7 is the prescribed objection form used to seek deletion of names from the rolls for valid reasons such as death, relocation, or duplicate entries. The Registration of Electors Rules, 1960, mandates that objections must state reasons and be filed within a specified period to be valid. This process ensures transparency and fairness in maintaining electoral rolls.
