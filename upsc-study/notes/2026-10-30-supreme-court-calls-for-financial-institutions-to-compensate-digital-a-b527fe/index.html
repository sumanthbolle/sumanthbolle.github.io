Supreme Court directs compensation for digital arrest victims by banks

The Supreme Court on October 29, 2026, urged the Reserve Bank of India to mandate financial institutions to compensate victims of digital arrest scams caused by institutional negligence. This ruling highlights judicial efforts to curb cyber fraud and protect citizens’ digital rights, relevant for both Prelims and Mains.

The Supreme Court called for mandatory compensation by banks to victims of digital arrest scams due to lapses in due diligence.

Why is this in the news?

On October 29, 2026, the Supreme Court expressed frustration over digital arrest scams siphoning crores and called on RBI to enforce compensation by financial institutions for victims.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are digital arrest scams and why are they a concern?

  • Definition and impact. Digital arrest scams involve fraudsters impersonating law enforcement to extort money via mobile phone calls, causing losses over ₹800 crore annually.
  • Victim vulnerability. Even educated individuals fall prey due to psychological manipulation, with victims often unable to comprehend that no arrest can happen over a phone call.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court direct regarding financial institutions?

  • Compensation mandate. The Court called on RBI to mandate banks and financial institutions to fully indemnify victims if scams occur due to institutional lapses in due diligence.
  • Transaction monitoring. It emphasized systems to temporarily suspend suspicious transactions deviating from normal patterns and to alert police for verification.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How is the RBI responding to the Supreme Court’s concerns?

  • Proposed regulatory framework. RBI plans to notify the Know Your Customer (KYC) Amendment Directions, 2026, by November 30 and operationalize them by December 31 to curb cyber fraud.
  • Reduction in cases. Instances of digital arrests have reduced by 50% this year compared to the previous year, with over 11 lakh cases of money restoration.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What role do intermediaries like WhatsApp play in this issue?

  • Platform responsibility. WhatsApp has banned 9,400 suspicious accounts and is developing software to identify accounts impersonating law enforcement to prevent misuse.
  • Court intervention. The Supreme Court allowed WhatsApp to intervene and submit a response on measures taken against digital arrest scams.

Back to basics: Digital arrest scams and financial due diligence

Digital arrest scams are cyber frauds where criminals impersonate police or government officials via phone calls to extort money. Financial institutions have a due diligence responsibility to monitor and prevent suspicious transactions to protect customers. Regulatory frameworks like Know Your Customer (KYC) guidelines help banks verify identities and detect fraud, ensuring financial stability and consumer protection.

What happens next?

  • Operationalize RBI KYC Amendments. The RBI plans to notify and implement the Know Your Customer Amendment Directions by December 31, 2026, to strengthen cyber fraud controls.
  • Enhance inter-agency coordination. The Court directed states to provide information on FIRs and investigations related to digital arrest scams to improve prosecution and deterrence.
  • Engage intermediaries. Platforms like WhatsApp are developing tools to identify and block accounts impersonating law enforcement, reducing scam opportunities.
