Madras HC Advocates Challenge 1965 English Language Policy in Courts

The Madras High Court Advocates Association filed a PIL in 2026 challenging the 1965 Union Cabinet decision to continue English as the common language for the Supreme Court and all High Courts. This raises constitutional questions on language use in judiciary and regional language rights, relevant for language policy and federalism in UPSC exams.

The 1965 Cabinet decision mandating English as the common judicial language is challenged for violating constitutional rights and obstructing regional language use in courts.

Why is this in the news?

In October 2026, the Madras High Court Advocates Association challenged the 1965 Union Cabinet decision that English remain the common language for the Supreme Court and High Courts, citing constitutional violations.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What was the 1965 Union Cabinet decision on court language?

  • Decision details. The 1965 Union Cabinet approved a note recommending English continue as the common language for the Supreme Court and all High Courts until Hindi could replace it.
  • Basis of decision. The decision was based on a Chief Justices' conference resolution that regional languages should not be introduced in High Courts to maintain judicial unity and ease of judge elevation.
  • Cabinet note reasoning. The Ministry of Home Affairs noted that using regional languages could fragment the judicial system and hinder a unified all-India bar.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is the 1965 decision challenged now?

  • Delayed awareness. The Madras High Court Advocates Association only learned of the 1965 decision in 2025 through an RTI request.
  • Constitutional grounds. The association contends the ban on regional languages violates Articles 14 (equality), 19 (freedom of speech), and 21 (right to life) of the Constitution.
  • Regional language demand. The challenge supports the longstanding demand to make Tamil the official language of the Madras High Court.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does the 1965 decision affect regional language use in courts?

  • Presidential consent and consultation. The decision introduced a convention requiring consultation with the Chief Justice of India before the President consents to regional language use in High Courts under Article 348(2).
  • Inconsistent approvals. While Tamil Nadu's requests to use Tamil were rejected, Hindi use was permitted in Rajasthan (1950), Uttar Pradesh (1969), Madhya Pradesh (1971), and Bihar (1972).
  • Impact on litigants. Separate PILs argue litigants have the right to plead, argue, and receive judgments in a language they understand, highlighting access to justice concerns.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the legal and policy implications for language in judiciary?

  • Judicial unity vs linguistic diversity. The 1965 decision prioritizes a common language to maintain judicial unity but raises questions about accommodating linguistic diversity.
  • Constitutional interpretation. The challenge tests the balance between official language policy and fundamental rights under the Constitution.
  • Federalism and state demands. The case exemplifies tensions in federalism where states seek recognition of their regional languages in official judicial proceedings.

Back to basics: Official Language Policy in Judiciary

The Constitution allows the use of English or Hindi in the Supreme Court and High Courts, with provisions for regional languages in certain cases. Article 348(2) permits Governors to authorize regional languages in High Court proceedings with Presidential consent. Language policy in courts balances judicial efficiency, accessibility, and federal diversity.

It required mandatory consent from the Chief Justice of India before the President could allow regional languages in High Courts. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the 1965 decision introduced a convention to consult the CJI but did not legally mandate his consent for Presidential approval under Article 348(2).
