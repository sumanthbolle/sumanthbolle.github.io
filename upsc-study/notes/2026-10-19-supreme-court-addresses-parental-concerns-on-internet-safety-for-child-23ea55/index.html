Supreme Court on Internet Safety for Children and Digital Rights

The Supreme Court ruled on October 15, 2026, that children have a fundamental right to access the Internet while also requiring measures to protect them from harmful online content like Child Sexual Exploitative and Abuse Material (CSEAM). This judgment is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights the balance between digital rights and child protection under law.

The Supreme Court affirmed children's right to Internet access alongside directives to intermediaries for stricter detection and removal of harmful content.

Why is this in the news?

The Supreme Court delivered a judgment on October 15, 2026, addressing parental concerns on children's Internet safety and setting guidelines for intermediaries to combat online child exploitation.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court rule about children's Internet rights?

  • Fundamental right to Internet. The Court declared that children have a fundamental right to access and use the Internet, recognizing it as a marketplace of ideas, creative expression, and vital information.
  • Need for safety measures. It emphasized that children must be protected from exploitation and harmful content online, but restricting their Internet access is not the solution.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the judgment matter for child protection online?

  • Focus on perpetrators, not children. The Court held that the wrongdoers are those who create, circulate, or fail to remove CSEAM content, not the children exposed to it.
  • Harm from CSEAM circulation. The judgment noted that every instance of circulation, view, or download of CSEAM revictimizes children and normalizes abuse.
  • State of online platforms. It described the failure of social media platforms to effectively detect and remove abusive content as disquieting and called for better synergy in countermeasures.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What measures did the Court direct for intermediaries?

  • National Hash and URL Database. The Court ordered creation of a database for known CSEAM content to enable immediate removal by intermediaries.
  • Two-hour reporting rule. Unknown origin CSEAM content removed on complaints or due diligence must be reported to authorities within two hours.
  • Accessible reporting and trained review. Platforms must provide an easy-to-find CSEAM reporting category and have trained officers review complaints with acknowledgment within 24 hours.
  • Strict scrutiny of paid content. Subscription and paywalled content must be closely monitored to prevent CSEAM circulation, with violations attracting severe fines and forfeiture of revenue.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does this judgment compare with foreign laws on online child safety?

  • United States. US law requires providers to report CSEAM instances to a CyberTipline with heavy penalties for non-compliance.
  • United Kingdom. The UK’s Online Safety Act mandates risk assessment and swift removal of illegal content, including CSEAM.
  • European Union. The EU’s Digital Services Act imposes fines up to 6% of annual turnover for failure to remove illegal content like CSEAM.

Back to basics: Digital Rights and Child Protection

Digital rights include the right to access and use the Internet freely, which is essential for information and expression. Child protection online involves safeguarding children from harmful content and exploitation, requiring legal and technological measures to detect and remove abusive material while respecting their rights.

Intermediaries must report removal of unknown origin CSEAM content to authorities within two hours. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. 2 only
  2. 1 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 1 is incorrect because the Court affirmed children's fundamental right to Internet access; Statement 2 correctly states the two-hour reporting rule for intermediaries.

Mains question (15 marks)

Discuss the balance between children's digital rights and protection from online exploitation as reflected in the recent Supreme Court judgment.

Discuss: Explain how the judgment balances children's right to Internet access with the need to protect them from harmful online content.

Intro: The Supreme Court recognized children's fundamental right to Internet access alongside the need for protective measures.

  • Affirmation of children's right to access the Internet as essential for learning and expression.
  • Identification of perpetrators and intermediaries as responsible for harmful content, not children.
  • Directives for intermediaries to detect, remove, and report CSEAM content promptly.
  • Comparison with international laws highlighting global trends in online child protection.

Conclusion: The judgment exemplifies a legal approach that safeguards children's digital rights while enforcing accountability for online child safety.

Quick revision

What fundamental right did the Supreme Court affirm for children regarding the Internet?
The Court affirmed that children have a fundamental right to access and use the Internet.
Within what time must intermediaries report removal of unknown origin CSEAM content?
Intermediaries must report such removal within two hours.
Who did the Court identify as responsible for harmful child sexual exploitative material online?
The creators, circulators, sellers of the content, and intermediaries who fail to remove it.
