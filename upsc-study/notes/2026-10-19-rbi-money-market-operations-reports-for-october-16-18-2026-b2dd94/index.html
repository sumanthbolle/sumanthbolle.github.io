RBI Money Market Operations Report: October 16-18, 2026

The Reserve Bank of India released detailed data on money market operations for October 16 to 18, 2026, showing liquidity conditions and interest rates across various instruments. Understanding these operations is essential for grasping monetary policy implementation and its impact on economic stability.

RBI's money market operations data from mid-October 2026 reflect liquidity absorption and interest rate trends crucial for monetary policy and banking sector regulation.

Why is this in the news?

RBI published official money market operations data for October 16, 17, and 18, 2026, detailing liquidity injections, absorption, and interest rates.

What do RBI's money market operations data show for October 16-18, 2026?

  • Liquidity absorption. RBI absorbed liquidity each day, with net liquidity injections of -₹1,51,299 crore on October 16, -₹1,36,687 crore on October 17, and -₹1,34,268 crore on October 18, indicating liquidity tightening.
  • Interest rates range. Overnight segment weighted average rates ranged from 5.72% on October 16 to 5.61% on October 17, with call money rates around 5.28-5.39%, reflecting short-term borrowing costs.
  • Cash reserve position. Scheduled commercial banks' cash balances with RBI were ₹8,44,129.11 crore on October 16, ₹8,43,229.42 crore on October 17, and ₹8,43,207.56 crore on October 18, slightly above the average daily cash reserve requirement of ₹8,33,309 crore.
  • Standing Liquidity Facility usage. Banks availed ₹8,476.05 crore under the Standing Liquidity Facility during this period, providing short-term liquidity support.

How does RBI conduct liquidity adjustment through market operations?

  • Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF). RBI uses repo and reverse repo operations under LAF to manage short-term liquidity, with repo rates at 5.75% and reverse repo rates at 5.25% during this period.
  • Marginal Standing Facility (MSF). MSF operations provided ₹8,316 crore on October 16, ₹6,414 crore on October 17, and ₹10,410 crore on October 18 at 5.75%, allowing banks to borrow overnight funds at a higher rate.
  • Standing Deposit Facility (SDF). SDF absorbed large amounts daily, ₹1,58,474 crore on October 16, ₹1,40,986 crore on October 17, and ₹1,44,678 crore on October 18 at 5.25%, helping RBI mop up excess liquidity.

Why do these money market operations matter for monetary policy and economic stability?

  • Liquidity management. RBI's absorption of liquidity through reverse repo and SDF indicates efforts to control money supply and keep inflation in check.
  • Interest rate signaling. The weighted average rates in call money and repo markets influence short-term interest rates, affecting credit availability and cost.
  • Banking sector health. Cash reserve positions above the required minimum show banks maintain adequate liquidity buffers, supporting financial stability.

Back to basics: Monetary Policy and Liquidity Management

Monetary policy involves regulating money supply and interest rates to maintain price stability and support economic growth. The Reserve Bank of India uses tools like the Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF), Marginal Standing Facility (MSF), and Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) to inject or absorb liquidity from the banking system. Repo operations provide short-term funds to banks, while reverse repo and SDF absorb excess liquidity, influencing short-term interest rates and inflation.

Mains question (10 marks)

Explain how the Reserve Bank of India uses money market operations to manage liquidity and support economic stability.

Explain: Describe the mechanisms and instruments RBI uses in money market operations and their impact on liquidity and economic stability.

Intro: RBI manages liquidity and economic stability through various money market operations.

  • RBI uses Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) involving repo and reverse repo operations to inject or absorb short-term liquidity.
  • Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) allows banks to borrow overnight funds at a higher rate to meet urgent liquidity needs.
  • Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) helps RBI absorb excess liquidity from the banking system.
  • These operations influence short-term interest rates, money supply, and help maintain price stability.

Conclusion: Through these tools, RBI effectively regulates liquidity and supports financial and economic stability.
