Thousands of Parents Younger Than Children Found in Karnataka Electoral Rolls

An analysis of Karnataka's final electoral rolls revealed over 17,000 entries where parents are recorded as younger than or the same age as their children, highlighting persistent discrepancies despite revision efforts. This issue raises concerns about the accuracy of voter data, which is crucial for election integrity and governance.

Karnataka's electoral rolls contain thousands of entries with implausible parent-child age gaps, undermining the accuracy of voter records.

Why is this in the news?

On November 27, 2026, The Hindu reported that Karnataka's final electoral rolls include more than 17,000 entries where parents are younger than or the same age as their children, exposing flaws in the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) process.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What discrepancies were found in Karnataka’s electoral rolls?

  • Age anomalies. More than 17,000 entries show parents listed as younger than or the same age as their children, with thousands more having an age gap of less than 15 years.
  • Examples. A 70-year-old man is recorded as the son of a 59-year-old, and in another case, a 40-year-old man is listed as the father of another 40-year-old at the same address.
  • Geographical spread. These discrepancies are spread across Karnataka, with the highest numbers in Raichur (1,960 entries), Kalaburagi (over 1,700), and Yadgir.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why do these discrepancies matter for elections?

  • Impact on voter data accuracy. Such age inconsistencies indicate errors in voter details, which can affect the credibility and fairness of elections.
  • SIR’s role. The Special Intensive Revision (SIR) aimed to identify and correct logical discrepancies like implausible parent-child age gaps but thousands of such errors remain.
  • Legal and governance concerns. Accurate electoral rolls are essential for upholding voter rights and ensuring transparent election processes.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How was the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) supposed to address these issues?

  • Identification of discrepancies. SIR identified nine categories of logical discrepancies, including an age gap of less than 15 years between parent and child, as implausible.
  • Notices and hearings. Over 43 lakh people in Karnataka were served notices to attend hearings to verify and correct their details.
  • Persistence of errors. Despite these measures, thousands of entries with such discrepancies still appear in the final rolls, indicating incomplete correction.

Back to basics: Electoral Rolls and Voter Registration

Electoral rolls are official lists of eligible voters in a constituency, maintained to ensure free and fair elections. Voter registration requires accurate personal details, including age and relationship data, to prevent fraud. Periodic revisions like the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) are conducted to update and correct the rolls, removing duplicates and errors to uphold election integrity.
