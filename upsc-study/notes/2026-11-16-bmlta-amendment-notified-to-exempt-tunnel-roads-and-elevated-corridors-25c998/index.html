BMLTA Amendment Exempts Tunnel Roads and Elevated Corridors from Scrutiny

The Karnataka government notified an amendment to the Bengaluru Metropolitan Land Transport Authority (BMLTA) Act in 2026, exempting certain urban infrastructure projects like tunnel roads and elevated corridors from institutional scrutiny. This change affects governance and regulatory oversight of major transport projects in Bengaluru, a key topic for understanding urban infrastructure regulation.

The 2026 BMLTA Amendment retrospectively exempts tunnel roads and elevated corridors from scrutiny, shielding projects initiated between 2022 and 2026 under the Act.

Why is this in the news?

The Karnataka government notified the BMLTA Amendment Act, 2026, on November 15, 2026, which exempts tunnel roads and elevated corridors from institutional scrutiny under the BMLTA.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What does the BMLTA Amendment 2026 entail?

  • Retrospective validation. Section 19(5) of the BMLTA Amendment retrospectively validates decisions, plans, operations, and pending proceedings related to projects initiated between the 2022 adoption of the BMLTA Act and the 2026 notification of its rules.
  • Exemption of projects. Tunnel roads, 11 elevated corridors, Sancharayukta, and the Hebbal short tunnel projects are exempted from the BMLTA's institutional scrutiny.
  • Incorporation of Rule 24. The amendment incorporates the substance of Rule 24, the savings clause from the BMLTA Rules, into the Act itself, which had faced over 200 public objections.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this amendment matter for governance and public accountability?

  • Shielding controversial projects. The amendment protects several controversial infrastructure projects championed by the Chief Minister from scrutiny, raising concerns about transparency and accountability.
  • Public objections ignored. Despite numerous objections labeling Rule 24 as unconstitutional and unscientific, the government retained and incorporated it into the Act, limiting public participation.
  • Pending legal challenges. Multiple Public Interest Litigations challenging the savings clause are pending, and the amendment raises uncertainty about their outcomes.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How was the amendment enacted and received?

  • Hasty legislative process. The BMLTA (Amendment) Bill, 2026, was introduced and passed during the Monsoon Session in August without meaningful debate amid political protests.
  • Governor's assent and notification. The amendment received the Governor's assent on October 9, 2026, and was subsequently notified in the State Gazette.
  • Civil society opposition. Civic activists condemned the amendment for undermining regulatory oversight and enabling projects to evade scrutiny.

Back to basics: Regulatory Authority and Legislative Amendments

A regulatory authority like the BMLTA is established by legislation to oversee and coordinate urban transport projects. Amendments to such Acts can change the scope and powers of the authority, including exemptions from scrutiny. Retrospective amendments validate past actions and can affect ongoing legal challenges, impacting governance and accountability.
