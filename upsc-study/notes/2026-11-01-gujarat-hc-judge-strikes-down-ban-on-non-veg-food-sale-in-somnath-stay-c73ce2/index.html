Gujarat HC Judge Strikes Down Non-Veg Food Sale Ban in Somnath; Stay by Division Bench

A single judge of the Gujarat High Court struck down the ban on non-vegetarian food sale near Somnath temple, citing business rights. The Gujarat Government appealed, and a two-judge division bench stayed this order, continuing the ban for now.

The Gujarat High Court's division bench stayed the single judge's order that struck down the non-veg food sale ban near Somnath temple, upholding the state's notification for now.

Why is this in the news?

On October 30, 2026, a Gujarat High Court single judge struck down the ban on non-vegetarian food sale near Somnath temple, but a division bench stayed this order following the Gujarat Government's urgent appeal.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What was the court's ruling on the non-veg food sale ban in Somnath?

  • Single judge's decision. Justice Nisha Thakore struck down the Collector's notification banning the sale of non-vegetarian food within a 500-metre radius of Somnath temple and along six pilgrimage routes.
  • Division bench's stay. A two-judge bench led by Chief Justice Manash Ranjan Pathak stayed the single judge's order, directing that the ban will continue until further hearing.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why did the Gujarat Government seek to maintain the ban?

  • Spiritual significance. The Government argued that Somnath is not an ordinary commercial town but holds extraordinary spiritual importance for millions of Hindus.
  • Balancing business and sanctity. It submitted that the constitutional freedom to carry on business does not allow all commercial activities at every location, especially near religious sites.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What were the arguments of the petitioners against the ban?

  • Fundamental rights claim. Hotel and restaurant owners contended that the ban infringed their fundamental right to carry on lawful business and earn livelihood.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current status and next steps in the case?

  • Stay in effect. The division bench's stay means the Collector's notification banning non-vegetarian food sale remains in force.
  • Next hearing. The division bench will hear the matter next on December 26, 2026.

Back to basics: Judicial Review and Fundamental Rights

Judicial review is the power of courts to examine the constitutionality of legislative and executive actions. Fundamental rights include the right to carry on business, but this right can be reasonably restricted for public interest, such as preserving religious sanctity. Courts balance individual rights against societal interests when adjudicating such cases.
