Kerala CM Pinarayi Vijayan Accuses UDF of Undermining Constitutional Institutions

Kerala Opposition leader Pinarayi Vijayan accused the UDF government of weakening constitutional institutions, secularism, and development in Kerala, alleging that it advances the RSS agenda. This controversy highlights issues of constitutional governance, federalism, and political accountability relevant for UPSC exams.

Kerala CM Pinarayi Vijayan charged the UDF with undermining constitutional bodies and secularism, raising concerns about governance and democratic rights.

Why is this in the news?

On October 25, 2026, Kerala Opposition leader Pinarayi Vijayan publicly accused the UDF government of undermining constitutional institutions and secular fabric in the state.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the main accusations by Pinarayi Vijayan against the UDF government?

  • Undermining constitutional institutions. Vijayan accused the UDF government of weakening constitutional bodies such as the Kerala Public Service Commission and the Election Commission of India by supporting unconstitutional actions.
  • Advancing RSS agenda. He alleged that UDF policies facilitate the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh's influence in Kerala, including in higher education through appointments and interventions in university bodies.
  • Threat to secularism and welfare. Vijayan claimed the UDF government is eroding Kerala's secular fabric and discontinuing welfare schemes like monthly assistance for women and youth.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this controversy matter for constitutional governance?

  • Right to vote and electoral integrity. Vijayan highlighted alleged irregularities in the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls that excluded over 13 crore voters nationwide, threatening democratic rights.
  • Governor's constitutional role. He emphasized that the Governor must act according to the Constitution and Council of Ministers' advice, criticizing unilateral actions regarding the Public Service Commission.
  • Protection of statutory bodies. The Kerala PSC's credibility and sanctity are crucial as it has appointed over three lakh people in the last decade, and any undermining affects governance and public trust.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the political and social implications of these allegations?

  • Democratic protests and repression. Protests demanding the removal of the Chief Election Commissioner have gained momentum, with reports of police repression, including assaults on women protesters.
  • Historical context of democracy in Kerala. Vijayan recalled the 1959 dismissal of the elected Communist government as a grave attack on democracy, linking past and present threats to democratic institutions.
  • Impact on development initiatives. He alleged attempts to undermine the Kerala Infrastructure Investment Fund Board, which has implemented projects worth ₹40,000 crore and is linked to nearly ₹1 lakh crore in development.

Back to basics: Constitutional and statutory bodies

Constitutional bodies are institutions created by the Constitution to perform specific functions, such as the Election Commission and Public Service Commissions, ensuring democratic processes and governance. Statutory bodies are created by laws passed by the legislature to carry out specialized functions. Both types of bodies are essential for maintaining checks and balances, protecting rights, and implementing policies impartially.

Answer and trap
