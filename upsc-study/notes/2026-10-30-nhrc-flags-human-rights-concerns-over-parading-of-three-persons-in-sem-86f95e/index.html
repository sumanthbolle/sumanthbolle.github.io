NHRC Takes Suo Motu Cognizance of Police Parading Three Persons Semi-Nude in Kolkata

The National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) has taken suo motu cognizance of media reports that Kolkata police paraded three persons in a semi-nude condition with ropes tied around their waists. This raises serious human rights concerns and highlights issues of police accountability and dignity of arrested persons, important for understanding constitutional bodies and human rights protection.

NHRC has issued a notice to Kolkata Police Commissioner seeking a detailed report on the parading of three persons semi-nude, citing potential human rights violations.

Why is this in the news?

On October 29, 2026, NHRC took suo motu cognizance of media reports about the parading of three persons in a semi-nude condition by Kolkata police and issued a notice to the Police Commissioner for a detailed report within two weeks.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What happened in the Kolkata police parading incident?

  • Incident details. Three persons, including a girl's father, her maternal uncle, and his friend, were paraded semi-nude with ropes tied around their waists by Kolkata police in Behala area, purportedly for crime scene reconstruction.
  • Background events. A day before, an 18-year-old youth and his 19-year-old friend, reportedly seen with a minor girl, were beaten and humiliated by the girl's family, who also assaulted the youth's father.
  • Police action. The police made the three arrested persons walk shirtless and barefoot for over 100 metres on the same road where the youths were forced to parade the previous day.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for human rights and police accountability?

  • Human rights concerns. NHRC observed that if the media reports are true, the incident raises serious issues of violation of human rights, including dignity and protection against humiliation.
  • Legal perspective. The Calcutta High Court in June 2026 stated that while the State can arrest and prosecute, it cannot subject accused persons to humiliation or deliberate defamation.
  • Civil rights response. Civil rights organisations have criticized the police conduct as an attempt to normalise revenge and punish outside the judicial process.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What role does NHRC play in such cases?

  • Suo motu cognizance. NHRC took suo motu cognizance based on media reports, which means it acted on its own without a formal complaint to address potential human rights violations.
  • Notice to authorities. The Commission issued a notice to the Kolkata Police Commissioner, demanding a detailed report within two weeks to investigate the incident.
  • Human rights protection. NHRC functions as a statutory body to protect and promote human rights, ensuring accountability of law enforcement agencies.

Back to basics: National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)

The NHRC is a statutory body established to protect and promote human rights in India. It can take suo motu cognizance of violations, issue notices to authorities, and recommend actions to ensure accountability. It acts as a watchdog over law enforcement agencies to prevent abuse and uphold dignity and rights of individuals.
