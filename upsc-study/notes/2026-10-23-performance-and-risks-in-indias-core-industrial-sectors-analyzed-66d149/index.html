Analysis of India's Core Industrial Sectors Performance and Risks

India's core industrial sectors showed a growth uptick in September 2026, led by coal and electricity, but this rise may not be sustainable. Understanding these sectoral trends is important for grasping economic growth challenges and fiscal pressures in India.

India's core industrial sectors experienced a temporary growth surge in September 2026, driven mainly by coal and electricity, but underlying risks and fiscal constraints threaten sustainability.

Why is this in the news?

The Hindu published an analysis on October 23, 2026, highlighting recent performance data of India's core industrial sectors and the risks to sustained growth.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What drove the recent growth in core industrial sectors?

  • Growth spike in coal. Coal sector growth surged to 10.7% in September 2026, a sharp rise following a 1.1% contraction over April-September 2026, mainly due to power plants replenishing depleted coal stocks amid a heat wave.
  • Electricity sector expansion. Electricity production grew 11.4% in September, continuing a half-year trend of double-digit growth driven by increased demand linked to the heat wave.
  • Steel and iron ore growth. Steel sector growth accelerated in September, leading to double-digit growth in iron ore production, which supports mining and industrial output.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is the recent industrial growth considered unsustainable?

  • Temporary coal demand. The coal sector's September growth is likely a temporary blip influenced by a statistical low base and short-term demand from power plants, expected to decline as the El Niño-driven heat wave ends.
  • Fiscal pressures from oil imports. Despite contraction in crude oil and natural gas sectors since 2024, refinery output rose 10.3% in September, reflecting processing of expensive imported oil, which increased India's oil import value by 47% despite a 7% volume rise.
  • Impact of global oil prices. Global oil prices rose above $112 a barrel due to attacks on Saudi Arabia's energy infrastructure, increasing import costs and straining India's foreign exchange reserves.
  • Limited sectoral growth. Only cement and steel sectors show sustainable growth, primarily supported by government infrastructure spending, indicating dependency on fiscal stimulus.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the broader economic implications of these trends?

  • Industrial output volatility. The temporary nature of growth in key sectors suggests potential industrial slowdown risks, affecting overall economic growth momentum.
  • Shrinking fiscal space. Rising costs of oil imports and reliance on government spending for sectoral growth indicate shrinking fiscal room for the government to support the economy.
  • Energy and resource challenges. Dependence on imported oil at premium prices and fluctuating coal demand highlight challenges in India's energy transition and resource management.

Back to basics: Core Industrial Sectors and Index of Industrial Production

Core industrial sectors include key industries like coal, electricity, steel, cement, crude oil, natural gas, and refinery products that significantly influence the economy's industrial output. The Index of Industrial Production (IIP) measures the performance of these sectors and is an important indicator of economic growth and industrial health.

Consider the following statements about India's core industrial sectors in September 2026: 1. The coal sector grew due to temporary demand from power plants amid a heat wave. 2. The refinery output contracted due to falling crude oil imports.

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because refinery output actually rose by 10.3% due to processing more expensive imported oil, despite contraction in crude oil and natural gas sectors.
