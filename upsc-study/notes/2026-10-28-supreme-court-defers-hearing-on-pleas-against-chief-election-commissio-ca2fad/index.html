Supreme Court defers hearing on pleas against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar

The Supreme Court postponed hearing petitions challenging Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and decisions made during the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls. This case highlights the legal protections and controversies surrounding the independence of the Chief Election Commissioner, a key constitutional authority.

The Supreme Court deferred hearing on pleas against CEC Gyanesh Kumar, emphasizing the legal safeguards for his removal and the Election Commission’s autonomy during electoral roll revisions.

Why is this in the news?

On October 27, 2026, the Supreme Court deferred hearing on pleas against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar concerning decisions made during the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current status of the Supreme Court hearing on pleas against the CEC?

  • Hearing deferred. The Supreme Court deferred the hearing of pleas against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar by 10 days, allowing petitioners to respond to the Election Commission’s affidavit.
  • Petitioners’ response time. Petitioners, including Rahul Gandhi, were given 10 days to file responses to the joint affidavit filed by the Election Commission of India (ECI).

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the legal protections and controversies involving the Chief Election Commissioner?

  • Removal safeguards. The ECI affidavit stated that the Chief Election Commissioner can only be removed from office in the same manner and on grounds identical to those for removing a Supreme Court judge.
  • Independence defended. The ECI, CEC Gyanesh Kumar, and the two Election Commissioners jointly asserted that any attempt to restrain the functioning of the CEC would be contrary to law.
  • Controversy over electoral roll revision. The Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls involved removing over 99.32 lakh names in Karnataka to ensure a clean electorate, which has been challenged in court.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the key issues raised in the electoral roll revision and related legal scrutiny?

  • Form 6 controversy. The ECI introduced an additional declaration form alongside Form 6 for voters removed during the SIR to provide details of previous electoral roll entries, which the Supreme Court is examining.
  • Supreme Court’s stance. The Supreme Court clarified that it did not make changes to Form 6 and shifted scrutiny of the new Form 6 and voter mapping to the Election Commission.
  • Electoral roll impact. In Karnataka, the final electoral roll after SIR shows a reduction from 5,54,32,314 to 4,55,00,011 voters, reflecting the removal of nearly one crore names.

Back to basics: Chief Election Commissioner and Election Commission of India

The Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) heads the Election Commission of India, a constitutional body responsible for conducting free and fair elections. The CEC enjoys security of tenure and can only be removed in the same manner as a Supreme Court judge to ensure independence. The Election Commission oversees electoral rolls, voter registration, and election processes to maintain democratic integrity.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and the recent Supreme Court hearing: 1. The CEC can be removed only in the manner and on grounds identical to those for removing a Supreme Court judge. 2. The Supreme Court has permanently stayed all decisions taken by the Election Commission during the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the Supreme Court deferred the hearing and did not stay the decisions; it only gave time for petitioners to respond.
