Static anchor

A Free Trade Agreement is a reciprocal arrangement in which partner economies provide agreed preferential treatment to covered trade, commonly through reduced or eliminated customs duties, subject to negotiated conditions such as product coverage and rules of origin.

Background

  • Free Trade Agreements are reciprocal trade arrangements that reduce or remove selected barriers, especially customs duties, for goods and sometimes services between partner economies.

India implications

  • MSMEs and labour-intensive sectors may gain access to larger markets, supporting diversification, income and employment.
  • State-level outreach can connect Rajasthan’s gems, jewellery, textiles, handicrafts, furniture and minerals, and Uttarakhand exporters, with FTA opportunities.
  • Farmers and farmer-producer organisations may benefit where agricultural products receive tariff concessions, but must meet market, quality and supply requirements.
  • Greater FTA utilisation requires exporter awareness, affordable credit, testing, certification, logistics, market intelligence and compliance capacity.

Way forward

  • Publish simple, sector-specific FTA opportunity and compliance guides through DGFT, states, FIEO and export councils.
  • Provide targeted credit, testing, certification, logistics and warehousing support to smaller exporters and producer organisations.
  • Build district-level export capacity around local products while strengthening standards, traceability, packaging and digital market intelligence.
  • Track preference utilisation, new MSME exporters, product and market diversification, delivery performance and employment outcomes.
