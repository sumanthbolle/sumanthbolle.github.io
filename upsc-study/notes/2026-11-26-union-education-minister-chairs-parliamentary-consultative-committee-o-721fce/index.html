Union Education Minister Chairs Parliamentary Committee on Education Internationalisation; PM Chairs 55th PRAGATI Meeting

On 25 November 2026, the Union Education Minister chaired the Parliamentary Consultative Committee meeting focused on the internationalisation of Indian education, highlighting initiatives to expand India's educational footprint globally. On the same day, the Prime Minister chaired the 55th PRAGATI meeting reviewing major infrastructure projects and the Industrial Training Institute ecosystem to improve governance and skill development.

The government is advancing internationalisation of education and infrastructure development through parliamentary oversight and the PRAGATI platform to enhance global presence and skill-based employment.

What initiatives were discussed for internationalising Indian education?

  • Committee meeting details. The Union Education Minister chaired the first Parliamentary Consultative Committee meeting of 2026 for the Ministry of Education, attended by senior ministry officials and MPs.
  • Global footprint expansion. The Ministry aims to expand the global footprint of the Indian Knowledge System by setting up campuses of Indian Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) overseas and facilitating foreign universities to establish campuses in India.
  • Current foreign HEIs and collaborations. Around 21 foreign HEIs operate in India, alongside joint academic and research collaborations.
  • Four strategies for internationalisation. The Department of Higher Education promotes internationalisation through academic and research collaborations, attracting international students, allowing foreign HEIs to set up branch campuses in India, and enabling Indian HEIs to establish campuses abroad.
  • Key initiatives and regulations. Seventeen Letters of Intent have been issued to foreign universities from countries like the UK, USA, Australia, and Italy under UGC regulations; initiatives like Bharat Innovates, SPARC, GIAN, DASA, and the revamped Study in India portal support this effort.
  • School education internationalisation. 268 CBSE schools operate in 26 countries with a regional office in Dubai; curriculum alignment with global standards, multilingual education, exchange programs, and a standardised assessment framework support internationalisation.
  • Policy guidance. The National Education Policy 2020 and the vision of Viksit Bharat 2047 guide transformative shifts in Indian education towards internationalisation.

What were the key points from the 55th PRAGATI meeting chaired by the Prime Minister?

  • PRAGATI platform purpose. PRAGATI is an ICT-enabled, multi-modal platform that brings together Central and State Governments to address implementation bottlenecks and improve delivery of projects and welfare schemes.
  • Infrastructure projects reviewed. The Prime Minister reviewed five critical infrastructure projects in road, power, coal, and railway sectors worth over ₹78,000 crore.
  • Emphasis on timely execution. The PM stressed that delays in infrastructure projects cause cost escalation and postpone benefits, urging ministries and states to resolve issues with clear responsibility and realistic timelines.
  • Quality and rehabilitation focus. Timely execution must be accompanied by high construction quality; rehabilitation and resettlement in coal projects should be dignified and timely.
  • Innovative power projects. The PM suggested exploring decentralised solar and wind power projects closer to consumption centres to improve reliability and ease transmission pressure.
  • ITI ecosystem and PM-SETU. The PM reviewed the Industrial Training Institute ecosystem and the Pradhan Mantri Skilling and Employability Transformation through Upgraded ITIs (PM-SETU), calling for stronger PSU–ITI linkages and industry participation.
  • 3C approach for ITIs. The PM advocated the Classroom–Company–Career (3C) approach linking classroom learning, practical industry experience, and employment to enhance skill development and job opportunities.
  • Local employment and migration. Stronger links between ITIs and local employers can expand employment closer to home, reducing distress-driven migration and aiding regional economic development.

Back to basics: Parliamentary Consultative Committees and PRAGATI platform

Parliamentary Consultative Committees are forums where ministers and members of parliament discuss policies and programmes of ministries, enhancing parliamentary oversight and accountability. PRAGATI is a governance platform that integrates central and state governments to monitor and resolve implementation challenges in projects and schemes, promoting timely and quality execution.

The PRAGATI platform is used to review infrastructure projects and improve delivery of welfare schemes. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). Statement 1 is correct but must note it includes academic collaborations and attracting international students, not only campuses; Statement 2 correctly describes PRAGATI's role.

Mains question (15 marks)

Examine the role of Parliamentary Consultative Committees and platforms like PRAGATI in enhancing governance and accountability in India.

Examine: Explain how Parliamentary Consultative Committees and PRAGATI contribute to governance reforms and accountability with examples.

Intro: Parliamentary Consultative Committees and PRAGATI are mechanisms to improve governance and accountability in India.

  • Parliamentary Consultative Committees facilitate dialogue between ministers and MPs to review policies and programmes, ensuring parliamentary oversight.
  • The PRAGATI platform integrates central and state governments to monitor project implementation, address bottlenecks, and ensure timely delivery.
  • Examples include the Education Ministry's Consultative Committee meeting on internationalisation of education and the PM's review of infrastructure projects and ITI ecosystem under PRAGATI.
  • These mechanisms promote transparency, coordination, and responsiveness in governance.

Conclusion: Together, these platforms strengthen democratic accountability and improve policy implementation in India.
