Bombay High Court Questions ECI on Voter Deletions During Special Intensive Revision

The Bombay High Court has questioned the Election Commission of India (ECI) over deletion of voter names during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls. This raises important issues about voter rights, the validity of electoral rolls, and the legal procedures for updating voter information.

The Bombay High Court challenged the ECI's deletion of voter names during SIR, emphasizing the obligation to include all eligible citizens in the electoral roll.

Why is this in the news?

On October 7, 2026, the Bombay High Court questioned the Election Commission of India regarding the deletion of voter names during the Special Intensive Revision process.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered the Bombay High Court's intervention?

  • Petition by affected voters. The court was hearing a petition by the Salgaonkar family whose names were dropped from the draft electoral roll despite submitting Form 8 to change their address.
  • Concerns over deletion during SIR. The court questioned the ECI on deleting names during the Special Intensive Revision, noting that the current electoral roll remains valid until the draft is finalised.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the legal issues with voter deletions during SIR?

  • Obligation to include citizens. The Bombay High Court cited the Supreme Court's ruling that the ECI must ensure all citizens are included in the voter list.
  • Validity of electoral roll. The electoral roll currently in force cannot be ignored during SIR; it remains valid until the draft roll is finalised under the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960.
  • Form 8 vs enumeration form. The court held that if a voter changes residence, the ECI cannot compel them to submit an enumeration form declaring they still reside at the old address, which conflicts with facts.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How did the court view the ECI's procedural requirements?

  • ECI's stance on forms. The ECI argued that voters seeking address changes must submit an enumeration form along with Form 8, or else file Form 6.
  • Court's objection to enumeration form. The court observed that the enumeration form requires a declaration that the voter still resides at the old address, which cannot be true if the voter has moved.
  • Rejection of Form 8 applications. The court questioned why Form 8 applications for address changes were being automatically rejected if the enumeration form was not submitted.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for election integrity and voter rights?

  • Ensuring inclusion of eligible voters. The court emphasized that excluding citizens from the voter list defeats the purpose of the SIR process and undermines electoral integrity.
  • Preventing misuse of SIR. The court raised concerns about potential misuse of the SIR process for mass deletions without proper justification.
  • Respecting factual declarations. Voters cannot be forced to sign declarations that contradict their actual residence, protecting their rights and the accuracy of the electoral roll.

Back to basics: Electoral Roll Revision and Voter Registration

Electoral rolls are updated periodically to include new voters and remove ineligible ones. The Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is a process to revise electoral rolls more thoroughly. Voters can update their details using specific forms like Form 8 for address changes. The Election Commission of India oversees this process to ensure accurate and inclusive voter lists.

Quick revision

What is the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls?
SIR is a thorough process to revise electoral rolls, including adding new voters and updating existing entries.
Can the Election Commission compel a voter to submit an enumeration form if they have changed address?
No, the Bombay High Court held that the ECI cannot compel a voter to submit an enumeration form declaring residence at an old address if they have moved.
Until when does the current electoral roll remain valid during the revision process?
The current electoral roll remains valid until the draft roll is finalised under the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960.
