India Emerging as a Frontline Provider in AI-Guided, Gene-Driven Precision Medicine

India is advancing in AI-guided and gene-driven precision medicine, becoming a leading provider of advanced healthcare solutions. This development is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights India's growing capabilities in medical research, technology integration in healthcare, and preventive health priorities.

India is becoming a key player in AI-assisted and gene-driven precision medicine, combining technology with clinical practice to improve healthcare outcomes.

Why is this in the news?

Union Minister Dr. Jitendra Singh highlighted India's emergence as a frontline provider in AI-guided, gene-driven precision medicine during the 42nd Convocation of Sri Ramachandra Institute of Higher Education and Research (SRIHER), Chennai, on 22 October 2026.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is AI-guided, gene-driven precision medicine and why is it important?

  • Definition and scope. AI-guided, gene-driven precision medicine uses artificial intelligence and genomic information to tailor diagnosis, treatment, and personalized care to individual patients based on their genetic profiles, lifestyles, and environmental factors.
  • Importance for healthcare. This approach opens new possibilities for early intervention, prevention, and long-term disease management, especially for lifestyle diseases increasingly affecting younger populations.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How is India advancing in this field?

  • Indigenous medical research. India has developed indigenous vaccines including a DNA vaccine against COVID-19, and is working on vaccines for dengue and malaria, reflecting growing healthcare capabilities.
  • Gene therapy and drug development. Indian research institutions have made advances in gene therapy for haemophilia and developed drugs like nafithromycin for bacterial pneumonia, demonstrating innovation in complex medical conditions.
  • Nuclear medicine and research collaboration. Progress in nuclear medicine research for diseases such as acute lymphoblastic leukaemia and fatty liver disease shows expanding scientific capacity, with calls for greater collaboration between research institutions, hospitals, and industry.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What challenges and priorities does this advancement highlight?

  • Integration with clinical practice. Medical professionals must optimally integrate enabling technologies with sound clinical judgment to ensure effective patient care.
  • Preventive healthcare focus. Rising lifestyle diseases among young people make prevention, early intervention, and long-term management critical to protect India's human potential.
  • National priority and vision. Preventing long-term disorders in youth is essential to achieving the vision of Viksit Bharat 2047, requiring sustained efforts in research, innovation, and responsible clinical practice.

Back to basics: Precision Medicine

Precision medicine is a medical approach that customizes healthcare decisions and treatments to individual patients based on their genetic, environmental, and lifestyle factors. It contrasts with a one-size-fits-all approach by using detailed patient data to improve diagnosis, treatment efficacy, and prevention strategies.

What happens next?

  • Expand research collaboration. Encouraging collaboration between government research institutions, universities, hospitals, and industry can accelerate innovation and development of affordable healthcare solutions.
  • Strengthen clinical integration. Medical professionals need to combine emerging scientific knowledge with clinical experience to translate technologies into meaningful patient care improvements.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about India's role in AI-guided, gene-driven precision medicine: 1. India has developed indigenous vaccines including a DNA vaccine against COVID-19 and is advancing gene therapy. 2. The integration of AI and genomics in medicine eliminates the need for clinical judgment in patient care. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because clinical judgment remains essential even with AI and genomics integration.
