Supreme Court clarifies police custody scope under BNSS

The Supreme Court ruled that Section 187(2) of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) expands the period during which police custody can be sought, allowing custody in parts up to 15 days within the first 40 or 60 days of investigation. This clarification affects criminal procedure and safeguards related to police custody and legal representation.

The Supreme Court held that BNSS allows police custody in parts up to 15 days within the initial investigation period, and the accused's right to an advocate does not require continuous presence during interrogation.

Why is this in the news?

The Supreme Court clarified the scope of police custody under BNSS in its October 2026 judgment in The State of Andhra Pradesh vs Suda Suresh Veera Venkata Naga Raju.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What changes did the Supreme Court clarify about police custody under BNSS?

  • Expanded custody window. Section 187(2) of BNSS allows police custody in parts, not exceeding 15 days in total, during the first 40 or 60 days of investigation, rather than only the first 15 days of remand.
  • No absolute custody limit. A magistrate cannot impose a non-extendable outer limit on police custody since fresh facts during investigation may justify extending custody within the BNSS framework.
  • Default bail provision. If investigation is not completed within 60 or 90 days depending on the offence, the accused may be released on default bail as per Section 187(3) of BNSS.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the rules on legal representation during police interrogation?

  • Right to meet advocate. Section 38 of BNSS grants the accused the right to meet an advocate of choice during interrogation but does not guarantee continuous physical presence throughout.
  • Advocate's presence limited. The Court held that an advocate may be present within the interrogation site but cannot intervene in the investigation process.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does BNSS differ from previous law on police custody?

  • Previous CrPC limit. Under Section 167 of the CrPC, police custody could not exceed 15 days in total, confined to the initial remand period.
  • BNSS flexibility. BNSS allows police custody in parts during an extended investigation window of 40 or 60 days, accommodating fresh leads or discoveries.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the provisions on detention duration under BNSS?

  • Initial detention limit. Section 58 of BNSS states that a person arrested without warrant cannot be detained beyond 24 hours without magistrate's authorisation.
  • Judicial detention beyond 15 days. Section 187(3) allows magistrates to authorise detention beyond 15 days up to 90 days for serious offences and 60 days for others.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Court say about videography during interrogation?

  • Recording requirements. The Court held that audio-visual recording of interrogation sessions and discovery proceedings satisfies documentation requirements, not continuous videography of transit.

Back to basics: Police Custody and Judicial Custody

Police custody refers to the detention of an accused by police for investigation, while judicial custody means detention under court authority. Law limits police custody duration to protect accused rights and prevent abuse. Courts supervise custody and can authorise detention beyond initial limits if justified by investigation needs.
