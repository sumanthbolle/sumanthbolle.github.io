Telangana High Court Upholds 30% Reservation for D.Ed. Candidates

The Telangana High Court dismissed writ petitions challenging the State government's 2009 decision to reserve 30% of Secondary Grade Teacher posts for Diploma in Education (D.Ed.) holders. This ruling clarifies the scope of state authority in modifying reservation policies during recruitment processes, a key issue in social justice and education governance.

The Telangana High Court ruled that the state can modify reservation policies during recruitment, upholding 30% reservation for D.Ed. candidates in teacher selection.

Why is this in the news?

On October 17, 2026, the Telangana High Court upheld the legality of 30% reservation for D.Ed. candidates in Secondary Grade Teacher recruitment, dismissing petitions against the policy.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What was the Telangana High Court's ruling on the 30% reservation?

  • Reservation upheld. The Telangana High Court upheld the State government's 2009 order reserving 30% of Secondary Grade Teacher posts for candidates with a Diploma in Education (D.Ed.).
  • Timing of reservation change. The court observed that the reservation modification was made at the threshold of the selection process and not after it had begun, allowing the state to refine its recruitment design.
  • Legal challenge dismissed. Writ petitions challenging the reservation as illegal and arbitrary were dismissed, affirming the government's prerogative to modify reservation policies before the selection process concludes.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this ruling matter for reservation policy and recruitment?

  • State's prerogative. The ruling confirms that the State cannot be denied its ordinary prerogative to modify recruitment rules before the selection process is finalized.
  • Limits on reservation changes. The court emphasized that changes should be made transparently and must not deprive applicants of promised rights by stealth or afterthought.
  • Impact on social justice. By upholding reservation for D.Ed. candidates, the judgment supports affirmative action policies aimed at empowering qualified teacher candidates from reserved categories.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What was the background and context of this reservation case?

  • Initial notification. On December 6, 2008, the undivided Andhra Pradesh government issued a notification to fill 52,655 teacher posts through District Selection Committees.
  • Reservation order. About a month later, the government issued an order reserving 30% of posts exclusively for D.Ed. qualification holders.
  • Legal challenge timeline. Candidates who failed to secure posts challenged the reservation after the written test in May 2009, but their pleas were dismissed by the AP Administrative Tribunal and later by the High Court.

Back to basics: Reservation Policy in India

Reservation is an affirmative action policy in India that allocates a certain percentage of government jobs and educational seats to socially and educationally backward classes. It aims to promote social equality and empowerment. The state has the authority to modify reservation policies during recruitment processes, but such changes must be transparent and not violate applicants' rights.
