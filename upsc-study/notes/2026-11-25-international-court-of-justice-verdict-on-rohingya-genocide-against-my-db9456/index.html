ICJ Verdict Finds Myanmar Guilty of Rohingya Genocide

On November 19, 2026, the International Court of Justice (ICJ) ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya community. This verdict is significant for UPSC aspirants as it highlights international law on genocide, challenges in enforcement, and regional human rights concerns involving India’s neighbourhood.

The ICJ held Myanmar responsible for genocide against the Rohingya, ordering safe return and compensation, but enforcement remains uncertain due to geopolitical factors.

Why is this in the news?

The ICJ delivered its verdict on Myanmar’s genocide against the Rohingya on November 19, 2026, marking the first time the court has held a state responsible for genocide.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the ICJ verdict on Myanmar state?

  • First state genocide ruling. The ICJ found Myanmar guilty of genocide against the Rohingya, a first for the court to hold a state responsible for this crime.
  • Evidence and intent. The court inferred genocidal intent from facts such as killings, rapes, and destruction of villages during Myanmar’s 2017 military operations, despite lack of direct evidence of a plan.
  • Historical context. The court noted decades of discrimination including citizenship denial in 1982 and repeated mass displacements in 1978 and 1991-92.
  • Orders issued. Myanmar was ordered to allow safe, voluntary, and dignified return of Rohingya and to compensate victims.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is enforcement of the ICJ verdict challenging?

  • Lack of enforcement power. The ICJ itself cannot enforce the verdict; enforcement depends on the UN Security Council.
  • Geopolitical vetoes. China and Russia, Myanmar’s backers, hold veto power in the Security Council and are unlikely to support enforcement.
  • Regional political dynamics. Countries including India have engaged with Myanmar’s military regime despite the verdict, complicating pressure for compliance.
  • On-ground situation. Rakhine State remains unstable with ongoing conflict and atrocities by other armed groups, hindering safe return.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What does the verdict mean for India and regional human rights?

  • Human rights implications. The ruling highlights the plight of over 1.2 million Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh and the need for their safe repatriation.
  • India’s neighbourhood policy. India’s engagement with Myanmar’s military regime despite the verdict reflects complex regional security and diplomatic considerations.
  • Democracy and peace as solutions. The editorial suggests only a democratic, federal, plural, and peaceful Myanmar can ensure Rohingya safety and return.

Back to basics: International Court of Justice and Genocide

The International Court of Justice (ICJ) is the principal judicial organ of the United Nations that settles disputes between states according to international law. Genocide is defined as acts intended to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnic, racial, or religious group. The ICJ can rule on cases of genocide but relies on the UN Security Council for enforcement, which can be hindered by veto powers of permanent members.

Common mistake. The ICJ verdict itself does not guarantee enforcement; it depends on political will and Security Council action.

The ICJ found Myanmar guilty of genocide and ordered compensation and safe return of Rohingya refugees. 2.   1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the UN Security Council has not enforced the verdict due to vetoes by China and Russia.
