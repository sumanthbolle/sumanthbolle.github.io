India–New Zealand Free Trade Agreement Enters into Force on Vijay Dashmi

The India–New Zealand Free Trade Agreement (FTA) came into effect on 20 October 2026, coinciding with the Vijay Dashmi festival. This agreement provides 100% duty-free access for Indian exports to New Zealand and aims to boost trade, investment, and cooperation in agriculture and services.

The India–New Zealand FTA, effective from 20 October 2026, offers duty-free market access and cooperation to enhance bilateral trade and investment.

Why is this in the news?

On 19 October 2026, the Ministry of Commerce & Industry announced that the India–New Zealand FTA will come into force on 20 October 2026, coinciding with Vijay Dashmi.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the key features of the India–New Zealand FTA?

  • Duty-free market access. India gains 100% duty-free access to the New Zealand market, benefiting MSMEs, artisans, farmers, and women entrepreneurs.
  • Agricultural cooperation. The agreement includes an Agricultural Productivity Partnership with action plans for apples, kiwifruit, and honey, establishing Centres of Excellence to improve orchard management and productivity.
  • Services and visa provisions. New Zealand offers market access in around 118 service sectors and provides 5,000 Temporary Employment Entry visas and 1,000 Working Holiday visas annually for Indian youth and professionals.
  • Investment commitment. New Zealand has committed to facilitate USD 20 billion investment into India, supporting agriculture, manufacturing, infrastructure, and startups.
  • Safeguards for sensitive sectors. Sensitive products like dairy, sugar, edible oils, and key agricultural commodities remain excluded from concessions to protect farmers and rural communities.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does the India–New Zealand FTA matter for India’s economy and trade?

  • Boosts exports and MSME growth. The FTA opens new export opportunities for Indian textiles, apparel, leather, gems and jewellery, engineering goods, pharmaceuticals, and processed foods.
  • Strengthens supply chains. Duty-free access for wooden logs, coking coal, and metal waste will enhance manufacturing and MSME supply chains.
  • Promotes innovation and entrepreneurship. The agreement supports the Make in India vision by encouraging investment and fostering innovation, entrepreneurship, and employment generation.
  • Supports farmer welfare. Linking tariff quotas to Agricultural Productivity Partnership action plans ensures market access benefits are tied to farmer welfare and productivity improvements.
  • Enhances regulatory cooperation. Streamlined regulatory processes will benefit pharmaceutical and medical device industries, facilitating easier market access.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How does the FTA align with India’s broader strategic and development goals?

  • Supports Viksit Bharat 2047 vision. The agreement aligns with the vision of a developed India by 2047, focusing on competitiveness, innovation, productive capacity, and inclusive prosperity.
  • Deepens bilateral relations. It strengthens the India–New Zealand Strategic Partnership aiming to double bilateral trade to NZ$7 billion by 2030.
  • Empowers diverse groups. The FTA is people-centric, empowering farmers, MSMEs, women-led enterprises, artisans, youth, and professionals.
  • Promotes traditional medicine. For the first time, New Zealand provides dedicated access to Health and Traditional Medicine Services, including Ayurveda, Yoga, and other AYUSH systems.

Back to basics: Free Trade Agreement (FTA)

A Free Trade Agreement is a pact between two or more countries to reduce or eliminate tariffs, import quotas, and preferences on most (if not all) goods and services traded between them. FTAs aim to increase trade and investment by creating a more predictable and transparent trading environment. They often include provisions on services, investment, intellectual property, and cooperation in various sectors to deepen economic ties.
