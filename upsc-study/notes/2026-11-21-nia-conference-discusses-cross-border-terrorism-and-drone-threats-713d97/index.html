NIA Conference Focuses on Cross-Border Terrorism and Drone Threats

The National Investigation Agency (NIA) held its sixth Anti-Terror Conference in November 2026, addressing challenges like cross-border terrorism and the use of drones for smuggling arms and explosives. This event is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights current internal security threats and the government's coordinated counter-terrorism efforts.

The NIA's 2026 conference emphasized combating cross-border terrorism and drone-enabled smuggling, reflecting evolving internal security challenges.

Why is this in the news?

The NIA's sixth Anti-Terror Conference concluded on November 19, 2026, focusing on cross-border terrorism, drone threats, and strengthening prosecution and coordination among agencies.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What were the main issues discussed at the NIA conference?

  • Cross-border terrorism focus. The conference concentrated on dismantling terror networks, including cross-border handlers, financial channels, and local facilitators.
  • Drone threats. A key concern was the use of cheap and easily available drones for smuggling arms, narcotics, cash, and explosives across borders.
  • Technology and radicalisation. Discussions included online radicalisation, lone-wolf threats, hawala networks, trade-based money laundering, and terrorist use of artificial intelligence.
  • International cooperation. The conference emphasized extradition, deportation, evidence-sharing, and strengthening global cooperation in counter-terror investigations.
  • Prosecution and legal strategies. Sessions focused on new criminal laws, digital evidence admissibility, trial strategies, witness protection, and use of e-platforms for prosecution and forensics.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Who participated and what initiatives were launched?

  • Participants. 40 Directors General and heads of State police and Central agencies, along with 230 participants from Anti-Terror Investigation units, attended the conference.
  • Government leadership. Union Home Minister Amit Shah inaugurated the event and reiterated the Centre's zero tolerance policy towards terrorism.
  • New verification portal. The Home Minister launched 'Pushti', a citizen-facing portal to verify the authenticity of NIA notices and prevent fake digital arrest scams.
  • Strengthening investigation units. The Home Secretary urged States to strengthen Anti-Terror Investigation units, expand forensic and cyber capabilities, and integrate financial investigations from the start.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the significance of the NIA's recent performance?

  • Case registration and convictions. In 2026, the NIA registered 82 cases, with courts deciding 29 cases and securing convictions in 27, achieving a 93% conviction rate.
  • Sentences awarded. Among the convicted, 10 received death sentences and 14 life terms.
  • Special courts. To expedite trials, 25 exclusive NIA courts have been established, with 14 more expected soon.
  • International legal expertise. The NIA strengthened its Foreign Investigation Unit with international legal experts to enhance extradition and overseas investigation capabilities.

Back to basics: Internal Security and Counter-Terrorism

Internal security involves protecting a country from threats like terrorism, insurgency, and communal violence. Counter-terrorism includes investigation, prosecution, intelligence sharing, and coordination among agencies to prevent and respond to terror threats. Agencies like the NIA play a key role in investigating terror cases and coordinating with state and international bodies to dismantle terror networks.
