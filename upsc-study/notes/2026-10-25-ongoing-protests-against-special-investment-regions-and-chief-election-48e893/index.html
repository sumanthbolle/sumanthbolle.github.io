Protests Against Special Intensive Revision and Chief Election Commissioner

Around 500 protesters including students, farmers, and civil society groups marched in Delhi on October 23, 2026, demanding rollback of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls and resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. This ongoing protest highlights governance challenges and public dissent related to electoral reforms and constitutional accountability.

The protests against SIR and the Chief Election Commissioner reflect public concerns over electoral roll revisions and democratic rights.

Why is this in the news?

On October 23, 2026, protests erupted in Delhi and across India against the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls and the Chief Election Commissioner, demanding rollback of SIR and the CEC's resignation.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered the protests against SIR and the CEC?

  • Demand for rollback. Protesters demand the rollback of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, alleging it disenfranchises voters by rejecting over 76,000 inclusion applications and reducing net additions.
  • Call for resignation. The protesters seek the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, accusing the SIR exercise of undermining constitutional safeguards.
  • Wide participation. The protests involve around 500 participants from students, youth, women, farmers, and civil society groups under the banner 'People Against SIR', supported by leaders like Yogendra Yadav and Annie Raja.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How did the protests unfold and what was the response?

  • March and police action. Protesters marched towards the Election Commission of India headquarters but were stopped by barricades and security personnel, leading to about 20 detentions and one FIR under Section 163 of Bharatiya Nagrik Suraksha Sanhita.
  • Prohibitory orders. Authorities imposed prohibitory orders restricting protests, including denotification of Jantar Mantar as a protest site, which protesters opposed.
  • Support from farmers. Farmers’ groups under Samyukt Kisan Morcha held demonstrations at district and tehsil levels, submitting memoranda demanding scrapping of SIR and prosecution of the CEC.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why do these protests matter for governance and democracy?

  • Electoral integrity concerns. The SIR exercise is seen by protesters as a disenfranchisement tool that threatens the right to vote and undermines electoral integrity.
  • Accountability of constitutional bodies. The protests question the impartiality and accountability of the Chief Election Commissioner, a key constitutional authority overseeing elections.
  • Right to protest. Restrictions on protest sites and police actions have raised issues about citizens’ democratic right to dissent and assemble peacefully.

Back to basics: Election Commission and Electoral Rolls

The Election Commission of India is a constitutional body responsible for administering elections and maintaining electoral rolls. Electoral rolls list eligible voters and are periodically revised to include new voters and remove ineligible names. Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is an exercise aimed at updating these rolls comprehensively, but it can become controversial if perceived to exclude legitimate voters.
