India implications

  • Strengthening film education can contribute to India’s cultural industries, creative employment and soft-power ecosystem.
  • Curricula should balance craft training with Indian cinematic traditions, regional-language expression, inclusion and contemporary production technologies.
  • Greater links among film schools, festivals, studios and public institutions could improve transition from training to professional work.
  • Affirmative measures and affordable access are needed so that women and learners from under-represented regions can enter professional filmmaking.

Way forward

  • Design multidisciplinary, practice-based curricula combining craft fundamentals, collaborative production, safety and professional project management.
  • Provide affordable access to equipment, mentors, studio spaces and festival exposure, with specific measures to widen women’s participation and regional representation.
  • Create transparent partnerships among film schools, public cultural institutions, festivals and industry while protecting curricular and artistic autonomy.
  • Evaluate institutions through learner capability, experimentation, inclusion and professional transition rather than credentials alone.
