Piyush Goyal Criticizes EU Carbon Tax on Steel at Berlin Meetings

Union Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal met German officials and industry leaders in Berlin on 23 October 2026, focusing on strengthening India-Germany economic ties. He also criticized the European Union's carbon tax on steel, warning it harms competitiveness and raises costs, which is important for understanding India's trade diplomacy and environmental policy challenges.

Piyush Goyal warned that the EU's carbon tax on steel will increase costs and reduce competitiveness, affecting global trade relations with India.

Why is this in the news?

Piyush Goyal's statements on the EU carbon tax on steel during his official visit to Berlin on 23 October 2026 highlight India's concerns over trade barriers and environmental regulations.

What were the key points of Piyush Goyal's Berlin visit?

  • Meetings with German leaders. Piyush Goyal held discussions with senior German government officials, CEOs of major companies like Siemens Healthineers and Volkswagen, and research institutions to boost trade, investment, and technology cooperation.
  • Focus on economic cooperation. The engagements aimed to strengthen India-Germany ties in advanced manufacturing, research, emerging technologies, and industrial innovation.
  • Interaction with Indian diaspora. Goyal also met Indian researchers and students in Germany to promote industry-academia partnerships.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why did Piyush Goyal criticize the EU carbon tax on steel?

  • EU carbon tax called 'disaster'. Goyal said the EU is 'inviting disaster' by imposing a Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) on steel, which raises costs and reduces competitiveness.
  • Impact on EU economy. He warned that the tax and other regulations increase the cost of infrastructure and living in Europe, hurting industries like autos and making them uncompetitive globally.
  • Reciprocal trade concerns. Goyal cautioned that such regulations could provoke reciprocal barriers from other countries, further harming trade.
  • India's position on emissions. He noted India contributes only 3-3.5% of global carbon emissions despite having 17% of the world's population and is advancing clean energy and greener steel production.
  • Criticism of developed nations. Goyal criticized developed countries for not fulfilling climate finance and technology transfer commitments and for ignoring the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

How does this matter for India's trade and environmental policy?

  • Trade diplomacy challenge. India faces non-tariff barriers like the EU's carbon tax and anti-deforestation regulations, which affect exports of steel, aluminium, and cement.
  • Balancing growth and climate goals. India is working to increase competitiveness while pursuing clean energy and sustainable industrial practices.
  • Need for fair global rules. India advocates for equitable climate policies recognizing different countries' responsibilities and development stages.

Back to basics: Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)

CBAM is a trade policy tool that imposes a tax on imported goods based on their carbon emissions to prevent 'carbon leakage' and encourage cleaner production. It aims to level the playing field between domestic producers subject to carbon regulations and foreign producers from countries with laxer rules. However, it can act as a non-tariff barrier affecting international trade relations.
