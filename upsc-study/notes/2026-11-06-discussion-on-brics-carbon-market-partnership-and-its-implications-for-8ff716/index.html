BRICS Carbon Market Partnership: Progress and Challenges

At the 18th BRICS Summit in New Delhi, members reaffirmed their commitment to the BRICS Carbon Markets Partnership, focusing on capacity-building and experience-sharing without setting trading targets or timelines. This partnership's development is crucial for understanding international climate cooperation and India's role in global carbon markets.

The BRICS Carbon Markets Partnership aims to coordinate voluntary carbon markets through a common rulebook rather than a unified trading platform.

Why is this in the news?

The 18th BRICS Summit in New Delhi on September 12, 2026, reaffirmed the implementation of the BRICS Carbon Markets Partnership Memorandum of Understanding, emphasizing capacity-building and experience-sharing among members.

What is the BRICS Carbon Markets Partnership?

  • Partnership focus. The partnership centers on capacity-building and experience-sharing among BRICS members rather than establishing a common trading platform or setting emission targets.
  • Memorandum status. The Memorandum of Understanding was acknowledged at the 2025 Rio summit and reiterated at the 2026 New Delhi summit.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How do BRICS countries manage carbon markets currently?

  • Voluntary markets. Eight BRICS members operate voluntary carbon markets, with Brazil, China, India, and Indonesia hosting over a third of global carbon credit projects.
  • Compliance systems. China has expanded its emissions trading system to include steel, cement, and aluminium sectors; India has set emission-intensity targets for 490 entities across nine sectors and introduced an offset mechanism; Brazil has established a cap-and-trade system under its 2024 law.
  • Lack of linkage. Despite these developments, none of the BRICS carbon markets are linked to each other.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the BRICS carbon market partnership matter for climate action?

  • Need for coordination. The partnership could transform fragmented carbon markets into a coordinated trading framework, enhancing the effectiveness of climate mitigation efforts.
  • Common rulebook. Operationalizing the partnership requires establishing a common framework for measurement, reporting, and verification rather than focusing solely on a trading platform.

Back to basics: Carbon Markets and Emissions Trading

Carbon markets are systems where carbon emission allowances or credits can be bought and sold, incentivizing reductions in greenhouse gas emissions. Emissions trading systems (ETS) set a cap on total emissions and allow entities to trade emission permits, promoting cost-effective mitigation. Voluntary markets operate without mandatory caps, enabling projects to generate carbon credits for sale. Measurement, reporting, and verification frameworks ensure transparency and accountability in emissions data.
