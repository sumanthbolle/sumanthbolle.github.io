Supreme Court to Hear Contempt Pleas in Thirupparankundram Deepathoon Lamp Row

The Supreme Court of India is examining whether Tamil Nadu government authorities and the Arulmigu Subramaniaswamy temple administration wilfully disobeyed its November 20, 2026 order to light the ceremonial lamp on the Deepathoon at Thirupparankundram hill as a one-time arrangement. This case highlights the judiciary's role in enforcing its orders in religious disputes and the challenges in balancing tradition, state authority, and constitutional law.

The Supreme Court is addressing alleged contempt by Tamil Nadu authorities for not complying with its order to light the Karthigai Deepam lamp at Thirupparankundram’s Deepathoon.

Why is this in the news?

On November 25, 2026, the Supreme Court took up the issue of non-compliance by Tamil Nadu authorities and temple administration with its order to light the lamp at Deepathoon, amid petitions seeking contempt proceedings.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Thirupparankundram Deepathoon lamp controversy?

  • Supreme Court order. On November 20, 2026, the Supreme Court allowed a limited one-time lighting of the Karthigai Deepam lamp on the Deepathoon, a stone pillar atop Thirupparankundram hill.
  • Non-compliance allegations. Despite the order, the lamp was not lit at the Deepathoon on November 24, 2026, with the temple board choosing to light it at the traditional Deepa Mandapam instead.
  • State and temple roles. The Madurai District Collector reported making all arrangements for compliance, but the temple Devasthanam did not authorise a lamp-lighting team, leading to the non-compliance.
  • Public and security response. Devotees were barred from ascending the hill for two consecutive days, and heavy police deployment was enforced to maintain order.

What are the key developments and responses?

  • Tamil Nadu government apology. The state government apologised to the Supreme Court for the non-lighting of the lamp and blamed the temple trust for non-compliance.
  • Political reactions. The BJP accused the Tamil Nadu government and the INDIA alliance of contempt and anti-Hindu bias, while the Tamil Nadu Congress accused BJP and RSS of politicising the issue.
  • Legal actions. Contempt petitions were sought in the Madras High Court, which passed a 'John Doe' order restraining violations of the Supreme Court’s directive.
  • Temple administration stance. The temple trustees stated they followed age-old customs and agama rules, lighting the lamp only at the traditional spot.

Back to basics: Contempt of Court and Judicial Orders

Contempt of court refers to actions that disrespect or disobey the authority, justice, and dignity of the court. Courts can initiate contempt proceedings to enforce compliance with their orders and maintain the rule of law. In India, the Supreme Court can take suo motu cognizance or act on petitions alleging contempt. This mechanism is crucial for upholding judicial authority, especially in cases involving state agencies and sensitive issues like religious practices.
