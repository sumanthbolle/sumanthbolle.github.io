Cyclone Arnab Intensifies, Disrupts Andhra Pradesh and Telangana with Heavy Rainfall

Cyclone Arnab is forecast to intensify into a Very Severe Cyclonic Storm and make landfall on the Andhra Pradesh coast on 28 October 2026, causing heavy rainfall and flight disruptions. The Ministry of Civil Aviation reviewed operational readiness and coordinated with multiple agencies to ensure passenger safety during the cyclone.

Cyclone Arnab's intensification and impact on Andhra Pradesh and Telangana highlight the importance of disaster preparedness and inter-agency coordination in managing natural calamities and civil aviation safety.

Why is this in the news?

On 27 October 2026, the Ministry of Civil Aviation reviewed the situation and operational readiness in response to Cyclone Arnab, which was expected to intensify and make landfall on the Andhra Pradesh coast on 28 October.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the forecast and impact of Cyclone Arnab?

  • Cyclone intensification. Cyclone Arnab is forecast to intensify into a Very Severe Cyclonic Storm with wind speeds of 125–135 kmph and gusts up to 150 kmph, expected to cross the Andhra Pradesh coast on 28 October 2026.
  • Affected regions. The cyclone is impacting coastal Andhra Pradesh and southern Odisha, with heavy rainfall affecting northern districts of Andhra Pradesh and causing one reported death.
  • Flight disruptions. Operations at Visakhapatnam airport were suspended since the afternoon of 27 October, and flights at Vijayawada and Rajahmundry were suspended after 7 pm on 27 October; Delhi–Bhogapuram–Delhi service was cancelled on 28 October.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

How is the Civil Aviation Ministry managing the cyclone's impact?

  • Review meeting. The Ministry of Civil Aviation held a review meeting on 27 October 2026 to assess the situation and ensure aviation safety in light of the cyclone's intensification.
  • Inter-agency coordination. Coordination was emphasized among stakeholders including the Airports Authority of India (AAI), Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA), Bureau of Civil Aviation Security (BCAS), India Meteorological Department (IMD), airlines, airport operators, National Disaster Response Force (NDRF), and disaster management authorities of Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, and West Bengal.
  • Passenger advisories. Passengers were advised to check flight status before travel, avoid travelling unless flights are confirmed, and expect delays due to waterlogging and road closures in affected areas.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the airline responses and operational measures?

  • Flight cancellations. Air India cancelled 6 flights on Delhi–Visakhapatnam–Delhi and Delhi–Vijayawada–Delhi routes; Air India Express cancelled 13 flights mostly involving Visakhapatnam; IndiGo suspended all flights at Visakhapatnam and after 7 pm at Vijayawada and Rajahmundry; Akasa Air cancelled Delhi–Bhogapuram flights on 28 October.
  • Operational guidelines. Airlines were advised to adhere to operational guidelines regarding adverse weather, inform passengers about delays or cancellations, and comply with norms for re-booking and refunds.
  • Support for vulnerable passengers. Airlines and airport operators were instructed to extend support to senior citizens, persons with reduced mobility, parents with infants, and medical cases.

Back to basics: Cyclones and Disaster Management

A cyclone is a large-scale air mass that rotates around a strong center of low atmospheric pressure, often causing heavy rainfall and strong winds. Disaster management involves preparedness, coordination among agencies, and timely response to minimize damage and ensure public safety during such natural calamities.
