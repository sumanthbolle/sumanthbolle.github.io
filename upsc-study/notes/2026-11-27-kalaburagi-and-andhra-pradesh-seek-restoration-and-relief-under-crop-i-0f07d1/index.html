Kalaburagi Seeks ₹375 Crore Crop Insurance Relief for Deficit Rainfall Losses

Kalaburagi district administration has requested over ₹375 crore in interim compensation under the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana for crop losses caused by deficient rainfall in the 2026-27 kharif season. This case highlights the functioning and importance of crop insurance schemes in supporting farmers facing climatic risks.

Kalaburagi’s demand for interim crop insurance relief exemplifies how PMFBY aids farmers financially after weather-induced crop failures.

Why is this in the news?

On November 26, 2026, Kalaburagi district administration submitted a proposal seeking over ₹375 crore in interim crop insurance relief under PMFBY due to severe rainfall deficit impacting crops.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered Kalaburagi's request for crop insurance relief?

  • Rainfall deficit impact. Kalaburagi experienced severe rainfall deficit during the 2026-27 kharif season, adversely affecting crop growth and yields.
  • Affected crops. Major crops affected include green gram, black gram, soybean, tur, cotton, and others.
  • Drought declaration. The State Government declared all 11 taluks of Kalaburagi district as drought-affected.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does the crop insurance relief mechanism work here?

  • Scheme involved. The relief is sought under the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), which provides financial assistance to farmers facing crop losses.
  • Compensation proposal. Kalaburagi administration proposed over ₹375 crore in interim compensation to farmers, against their premium contribution of ₹67.82 crore.
  • Insurance company role. IndusInd General Insurance Company has agreed to the proposal, facilitating the compensation process.
  • Relief multiplier. The proposed relief amount is nearly six times the premium paid by farmers, offering substantial financial support.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Back to basics: Crop Insurance and PMFBY

Crop insurance schemes like the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana provide financial protection to farmers against crop losses due to natural calamities such as drought, floods, and pests. Farmers pay a premium, and in case of crop failure, they receive compensation to mitigate income loss and sustain agricultural activities. This mechanism reduces the risk burden on farmers and supports agricultural stability.
