A self-reliant defence ecosystem is an integrated arrangement in which domestic research, design, development, manufacturing, users, institutions and industry collectively build defence capability, while remaining able to engage partners through technology exchange and industrial cooperation.

India implications

  • Greater indigenous capability can reduce vulnerability arising from dependence on external suppliers and support strategic autonomy.
  • Defence exports and foreign-language communication can widen awareness of Indian defence solutions among partner nations.
  • Co-development and co-production can deepen industrial and technological partnerships, but require credible domestic manufacturing capacity and trusted safeguards.
  • The approach links defence preparedness with industrial capability, innovation and long-term diplomatic relationships.

Way forward

  • Strengthen the connection among DRDO, Armed Forces, academia, start-ups and established industry through user-led research, testing and iterative feedback.
  • Prioritise capability areas where domestic design and manufacturing can generate durable strategic value, while using international cooperation selectively.
  • Promote transparent frameworks for co-development, co-production, intellectual-property protection, technology security and responsible defence exports.
  • Evaluate partnerships by capability outcomes, lifecycle support, interoperability, resilience and long-term industrial learning rather than by transactions alone.
