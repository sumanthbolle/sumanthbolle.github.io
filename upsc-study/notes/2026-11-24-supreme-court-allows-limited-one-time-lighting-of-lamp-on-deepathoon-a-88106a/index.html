Supreme Court Allows One-Time Lighting of Lamp on Thirupparankundram Hill

The Supreme Court permitted a limited, one-time lighting of the lamp on the Deepathoon at Thirupparankundram Hill on Karthigai Deepam day, while staying the Madras High Court's earlier order. This case highlights judicial balancing of religious customs and statutory law, relevant for understanding the judiciary's role in religious disputes.

The Supreme Court allowed a restricted one-time lighting of the lamp on the Deepathoon, clarifying it does not establish any vested religious right.

Why is this in the news?

On November 20, 2026, the Supreme Court allowed a one-time lighting of the lamp on the Deepathoon at Thirupparankundram Hill, prompting a review plea by Tamil Nadu's HR&CE department challenging this interim order.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court order regarding the Deepathoon lamp?

  • One-time permission. The Supreme Court allowed the lighting of the lamp on the Deepathoon as a restricted and one-off measure on Karthigai Deepam day, emphasizing this would not create any precedent or vested right.
  • Restrictions imposed. The Court directed that the temple Devasthanam would exclusively decide the team to light the lamp and imposed several safeguards to limit the impact of this order.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why did Tamil Nadu file a review plea against the Supreme Court order?

  • Challenge to one-time order. The Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious & Charitable Endowments department filed a review petition arguing the one-time permission altered a long-standing traditional practice without judicial foundation.
  • Legal grounds. The State contended the order was contrary to the Tamil Nadu Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Act, which mandates administration according to binding customs, and claimed the Court's order was an error apparent on the record.
  • Traditional practice. The State argued that without the Court's order, the lamp would have been lit at the traditional place near the Uchi Pillaiyar Temple, preserving the long-unbroken usage.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the significance of this case for judicial review and religious disputes?

  • Judicial balancing. The case illustrates the Supreme Court's role in balancing religious customs and statutory law, allowing limited relief without establishing permanent rights.
  • Review petition standards. The review jurisdiction is rare and requires showing that the original decision was based on factual misconception or ignored binding precedents.
  • Impact on religious rights. The Court clarified that the interim order does not confer enforceable religious rights, highlighting judicial caution in altering religious practices.

Back to basics: Judicial Review and Religious Rights

Judicial review allows courts to examine the legality and constitutionality of decisions, including those affecting religious practices. Courts often balance the protection of religious customs with statutory laws and public order, ensuring that no permanent rights are granted without proper legal basis. Review petitions are a legal mechanism to challenge Supreme Court orders but require strong grounds such as factual errors or legal oversights.

What happens next?

  • Pending review decision. The Supreme Court is expected to examine the Tamil Nadu government's review petition to determine if the interim order should be modified or upheld.
