India’s Ramsar Sites Reach 103 with Sukhna Wetland and Sauj Jheel

India has added Sukhna Wetland in Chandigarh and Sauj Jheel in Uttar Pradesh to its list of Ramsar Sites, raising the total to 103. This reflects the government's ongoing efforts to conserve wetlands for biodiversity, water security, and livelihoods, relevant for environment and biodiversity topics in UPSC.

India’s Ramsar site count has reached 103 with the addition of Sukhna Wetland and Sauj Jheel, underscoring wetland conservation efforts.

Why is this in the news?

On October 15, 2026, the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change announced the designation of Sukhna Wetland and Sauj Jheel as Ramsar Sites, increasing India's tally to 103.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the new Ramsar Sites added in India?

  • New sites added. Sukhna Wetland in Chandigarh and Sauj Jheel in Uttar Pradesh were designated as Ramsar Sites, bringing India's total to 103.
  • Significance of sites. These wetlands contribute to biodiversity conservation, water and climate security, and support local livelihoods.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does wetland conservation matter for India?

  • Biodiversity protection. Wetlands are critical habitats for diverse flora and fauna, helping preserve ecological balance.
  • Water and climate security. Wetlands regulate water cycles and help mitigate climate impacts like floods and droughts.
  • Livelihood support. Many communities depend on wetlands for fishing, agriculture, and other economic activities.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How does this relate to India’s international commitments?

  • Ramsar Convention. India’s designation of wetlands as Ramsar Sites fulfills its obligations under the Ramsar Convention on Wetlands to conserve important wetland ecosystems.
  • Government focus. The additions reflect the government’s emphasis on environmental conservation aligned with global climate and biodiversity goals.

Back to basics: Ramsar Convention and Wetland Conservation

The Ramsar Convention is an international treaty for the conservation and sustainable use of wetlands. Wetlands are ecosystems that are inundated by water, either permanently or seasonally, and support unique biodiversity. Designation as a Ramsar Site recognizes a wetland's international importance and commits the country to protect it. Wetlands help in water purification, flood control, and climate regulation, making their conservation vital for environmental sustainability.
