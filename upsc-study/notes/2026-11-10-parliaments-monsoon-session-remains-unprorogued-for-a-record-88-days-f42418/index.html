Parliament’s Monsoon Session Unprorogued for Record 88 Days

The 2026 Monsoon session of Parliament has remained unprorogued for 88 days, surpassing the previous record of 87 days set in 2008. This unusual parliamentary procedure has implications for legislative processes and governance.

Parliament’s Monsoon session in 2026 remained unprorogued for 88 days, allowing flexibility for extended sittings and legislative action.

Why is this in the news?

As of November 9, 2026, neither the Lok Sabha nor the Rajya Sabha had been formally prorogued after the Monsoon session adjourned indefinitely on August 13, 2026.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What does it mean that Parliament remained unprorogued for 88 days?

  • Unprorogued session. Neither the Lok Sabha nor the Rajya Sabha was formally prorogued after the Monsoon session adjourned sine die on August 13, 2026, keeping the session technically open for 88 days.
  • Record duration. This 88-day unprorogued period broke the previous record of 87 days set in 2008.
  • Constitutional provision. The Constitution does not specify a time limit within which a House must be prorogued after adjournment sine die, so extended unprorogued periods are constitutionally permissible.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for parliamentary procedure and governance?

  • Flexibility for sittings. Keeping the session unprorogued allows the government to convene extended or special sittings within the same session with just two days' notice, rather than calling a fresh session which requires 14 days' notice.
  • Legislative strategy. This procedural choice is linked to the possibility of quickly convening Parliament to consider the Women's Reservation Bill, which proposes increasing Lok Sabha seats from 543 to a maximum of 850 through delimitation.
  • Past precedent. In 2008, a similar unprorogued period allowed the Lok Sabha to meet to discuss a no-confidence motion and later reconvene for further sittings.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the political and constitutional debates around this practice?

  • Speculation and criticism. The government's decision not to prorogue has led to speculation about manipulating the parliamentary majority to pass sensitive legislation.
  • Opposition view. The Congress party described the situation as 'abnormal' and suggested the prorogation delay is used to manage a 'super-tainted' two-thirds majority.
  • No impropriety by itself. However, the mere fact of extended unprorogued status does not amount to constitutional or procedural impropriety.

Back to basics: Prorogation of Parliament

Prorogation is the formal ending of a parliamentary session by the President, on the advice of the government, after which no business can be transacted until the next session begins. Adjournment sine die means the House is adjourned indefinitely without setting a date for resumption. A session remains unprorogued if it is adjourned sine die but not formally ended, allowing Parliament to reconvene without a new session being summoned.
