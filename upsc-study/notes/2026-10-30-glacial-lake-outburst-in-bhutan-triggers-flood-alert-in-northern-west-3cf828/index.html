Glacial Lake Outburst in Bhutan Triggers Flood Alert in West Bengal and Assam

A glacial lake outburst flood (GLOF) from Bhutan's Sintaphu lake caused a flood alert in northern West Bengal and Assam, prompting evacuation of over 4,000 people. This event highlights the risks of Himalayan glacial lakes and the importance of disaster preparedness and climate change adaptation in the region.

A glacial lake outburst flood in Bhutan led to flood warnings and evacuations along the Sankosh river in West Bengal and Assam, demonstrating the need for effective disaster management in Himalayan areas.

Why is this in the news?

On October 29-30, 2026, a glacial lake outburst flood from Bhutan's Sintaphu lake caused flood alerts and evacuations in northern West Bengal and Assam.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What caused the flood alert in West Bengal and Assam?

  • Glacial lake outburst flood. A breach in the Shintaphu glacial lake in Bhutan released nearly 7.4 million cubic metres of water into the Mo river, which flows into the Sankosh river along the West Bengal-Assam border.
  • Downstream impact. The surge raised fears of sudden water level rise in the Sankosh river, prompting flood alerts and evacuation orders in catchment areas of the river and its tributaries.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How did authorities respond to the flood threat?

  • Evacuation measures. Over 4,000 people from vulnerable areas in Darjeeling hills and Dooars region of West Bengal were moved to safer zones following alerts.
  • Monitoring and communication. State and district officials, along with disaster response forces, maintained constant monitoring and communication with Bhutan authorities to track water levels and manage the situation.
  • Public warnings. Loudspeaker announcements and video messages urged residents near riverbanks to evacuate immediately to avoid potential flooding.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this event matter for UPSC preparation?

  • Understanding GLOF. Glacial lake outburst floods are a key example of climate change-induced natural disasters in the Himalayan region, relevant for disaster management and environmental studies.
  • Disaster management practices. The event illustrates the importance of early warning systems, inter-state and international coordination, and evacuation protocols in managing floods.
  • Climate change adaptation. It highlights the increasing risks posed by melting glaciers and the need for policies addressing climate resilience in vulnerable regions.

Back to basics: Glacial Lake Outburst Flood (GLOF)

A GLOF occurs when a glacial lake, formed by melting glaciers, suddenly releases large volumes of water due to dam failure or breach. These floods can cause rapid downstream flooding, threatening lives and infrastructure. GLOFs are increasingly frequent due to climate change accelerating glacier melt, especially in mountainous regions like the Himalayas.

Intro: Glacial lake outburst floods are sudden releases of water from glacial lakes, posing serious risks in the Himalayas.

  • Causes include glacier melting and lake dam breaches due to climate change.
  • Impacts involve flooding, displacement, and damage to infrastructure downstream.
  • Disaster management requires early warning systems, evacuation plans, and inter-state coordination.
  • Climate adaptation involves monitoring glacial lakes, infrastructure resilience, and international cooperation.

Conclusion: Effective management of GLOFs is critical for safeguarding vulnerable Himalayan communities amid climate change.
