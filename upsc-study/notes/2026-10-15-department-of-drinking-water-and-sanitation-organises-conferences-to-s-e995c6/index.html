Strengthening Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 and Water Governance through Convergence and Community Ownership

The Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation (DDWS) organised virtual conferences and district-level dialogues in October 2026 to enhance Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) 2.0's focus on sustainable rural drinking water services. These events emphasised scientific planning, inter-departmental convergence, and Gram Panchayat-led governance to ensure long-term water source sustainability and reliable service delivery.

JJM 2.0 advances rural water security by integrating scientific tools, community participation, and convergence with other programmes under Gram Panchayat leadership.

Why is this in the news?

In October 2026, DDWS held thematic virtual conferences and the 13th District Collectors’ Peyjal Samvad to promote convergence of JJM 2.0 with other water security programmes and strengthen Gram Panchayat-led rural water governance.

What are the key objectives of Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 and its convergence framework?

  • Focus shift in JJM 2.0. JJM 2.0 prioritises long-term functionality, reliability, and sustainability of rural drinking water infrastructure rather than just creation of assets.
  • Convergence with VB-G RAM G. The programme aligns JJM 2.0 with VB-G RAM G to undertake water conservation, groundwater recharge, source sustainability, and maintenance interventions that enhance drinking water security.
  • Scientific planning tools. States are urged to use the Decision Support System (DSS) and Yuktdhara SOPs for identifying vulnerable water sources and prioritising interventions based on scientific assessments.
  • Community and institutional roles. Gram Panchayats, Village Water and Sanitation Committees, and local institutions play a central role in planning, implementation, and monitoring to ensure sustainability and accountability.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How is community ownership and accountability being strengthened under JJM 2.0?

  • District Collectors’ Peyjal Samvad. This platform enables district administrations to share innovative practices, address challenges, and promote community ownership of water assets.
  • Capacity building. Training of Gram Panchayats, VWSCs, Panchayat Secretaries, and Sujalam Shakti Groups supports operation and maintenance of water infrastructure.
  • Use of technology. Digital tools like SCADA systems, Jal Saarthi app, GIS mapping, and Jal Arpan module facilitate monitoring, grievance redressal, and formal handover of water schemes.
  • Community participation. Involvement of women volunteers, Self-Help Groups, Jal Sakhis, and local institutions enhances water quality surveillance, user-charge collection, and social audits.

[3] Press Information Bureau

What are some state-level initiatives and best practices showcased?

  • Odisha. Implemented large-scale water conservation, groundwater recharge, greywater management, and climate-resilient infrastructure through convergence.
  • Madhya Pradesh. Operationalised O&M rules and improved coordination between Rural Development and Water Supply departments for maintenance.
  • Rajasthan. Established preventive, corrective, and emergency maintenance frameworks involving Gram Panchayats and Paani Samitis.
  • District examples. Districts like Kanpur Nagar, West Tripura, Thanjavur, Baragada, and Palamu shared innovative practices including water budgeting, IoT monitoring, community engagement, and convergence with MGNREGA.

How is inter-departmental coordination and governance ensured?

  • Institutional platforms. Coordination occurs through District Water and Sanitation Missions, State Water and Sanitation Missions, and State-level Apex Committees.
  • Data sharing and monitoring. States are directed to establish mechanisms for technical scrutiny, approval, and joint monitoring of convergence works, supported by seamless data sharing.
  • Integration with Gram Panchayat plans. JJM Village Action Plans and Source Sustainability Plans are integrated with Viksit Gram Panchayat Plans and incorporated into Gram Panchayat Development Plans.
  • Evidence-based interventions. Water security interventions are based on scientific assessments approved by Gram Sabhas to ensure community ownership and accountability.

Back to basics: Decentralised Water Governance

Decentralised water governance involves local institutions like Gram Panchayats managing water resources and services at the community level. It ensures that water planning, implementation, and monitoring reflect local needs and knowledge, promoting accountability and sustainability. Scientific tools and inter-departmental coordination support these local bodies in effective water management.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 and related water governance initiatives: 1. Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 focuses on long-term sustainability and integrates with VB-G RAM G for water source conservation. 2. The District Collectors’ Peyjal Samvad is a platform for sharing best practices and strengthening community ownership under JJM 2.0. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). A trap would be confusing the focus of JJM 2.0 as only infrastructure creation, or misunderstanding the role of the Peyjal Samvad as a technical committee rather than a peer learning platform.
