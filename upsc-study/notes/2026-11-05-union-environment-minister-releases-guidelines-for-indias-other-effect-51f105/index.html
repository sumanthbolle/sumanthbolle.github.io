Union Environment Minister Releases Guidelines for India’s OECMs 2026

The Union Environment Minister released the Guidelines for Designation of India’s Other Effective Area-Based Conservation Measures (I-OECMs) 2026 at CBD COP-17 in Armenia. These guidelines establish a framework to recognize areas outside legally protected zones that contribute to long-term biodiversity conservation, supporting India's international biodiversity commitments.

India’s I-OECMs guidelines provide a framework to recognize and conserve biodiversity-rich areas outside protected zones, aiding the country’s global biodiversity targets.

Why is this in the news?

On 3 November 2026, the Union Environment Minister released the Guidelines for India’s Other Effective Area-Based Conservation Measures (I-OECMs) 2026 at the 17th Conference of the Parties to the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD COP-17) in Yerevan, Armenia.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are India’s Other Effective Area-Based Conservation Measures (I-OECMs)?

  • Definition. I-OECMs are areas outside legally protected zones that deliver positive and sustained long-term outcomes for in-situ biodiversity conservation through effective management.
  • Scope of ecosystems. The guidelines cover terrestrial, inland water, coastal, and marine ecosystems including biodiversity parks, sacred groves, community-managed irrigation tanks, seagrass and seaweed beds, and fisheries seasonal ban areas.
  • Recognition criteria. Four mandatory criteria include: the area must not be legally protected, must be governed under government or government-recognised local institutions, have defined boundaries without overlap with protected areas, and recognition must not alter existing governance or activities.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How are I-OECMs designated and managed?

  • Application process. Applications can be submitted to the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) by government departments, local self-governments, and Biodiversity Management Committees.
  • Validation and approval. NBA conducts preliminary examination, forwards applications to State Biodiversity Boards or Union Territory Biodiversity Councils for validation, and makes final decisions with MoEFCC coordination.
  • Monitoring framework. Each recognized site must have measurable indicators assessed at least every five years, with records maintained by State Biodiversity Boards or UT Biodiversity Councils.
  • Publication and capacity building. MoEFCC and NBA will liaise with UNEP-WCMC to publish recognized sites on the World Database on Protected and Conserved Areas, and provide capacity-building and non-monetary recognition for good practices.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why do I-OECMs matter for India’s biodiversity commitments?

  • Support to global targets. I-OECMs support India’s implementation of Target 3 (30x30) of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework and National Biodiversity Target 3 under the updated National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan (2024–2030).
  • Conservation beyond protected areas. They enable conservation of biodiversity through effective management outside legally protected areas, expanding the conservation estate.
  • Complementary government project. A GEF-8 project aims to implement the guidelines in six demonstration sites, adding 3% of India’s land and marine area to conservation estates beyond the existing 27% under protection.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the key features of the GEF-8 Project supporting I-OECMs?

  • Project duration and financing. The project runs for 72 months from 15 May 2026 to 15 May 2032, with total financing of USD 60.79 million including a GEF Trust Fund grant of USD 6.65 million.
  • Project goals. It aims to establish 50,000 hectares of new terrestrial conservation areas, restore at least 5,000 hectares of degraded land, and directly benefit at least 50,000 people, half of them women.
  • Demonstration sites. Sites include the Gori Valley (Uttarakhand), Kumbhalgarh–Mount Abu–Jaisalmer (Rajasthan), Satkosia–Kalahandi (Odisha), Srisailam–Cumbum (Andhra Pradesh and Telangana), Sahyadri–Konkan (Maharashtra and Goa), and Lakshadweep Islands Marinescape.
  • Project components. The project focuses on enabling frameworks, financing and capacity building, increased coverage and management effectiveness, knowledge management, and monitoring and evaluation.

Back to basics: Biodiversity Conservation and Protected Areas

Biodiversity conservation involves protecting species, habitats, and ecosystems to maintain ecological balance. Protected areas are legally designated regions like national parks and wildlife sanctuaries that conserve biodiversity. Other Effective Area-Based Conservation Measures (OECMs) are sites outside these protected areas that also contribute to biodiversity conservation through effective management and governance.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about India’s Other Effective Area-Based Conservation Measures (I-OECMs): 1. I-OECMs are legally protected areas managed only by the Central Government. 2. The GEF-8 project supporting I-OECMs aims to add 3% of India’s land and marine area to the conservation estate beyond the existing 27% under protection. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (b). Statement 1 is incorrect because I-OECMs are areas outside legally protected areas and can be managed by Central or State Governments or government-recognised local institutions, not only by the Central Government.
