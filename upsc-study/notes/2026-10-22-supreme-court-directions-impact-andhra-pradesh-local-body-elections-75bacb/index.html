Supreme Court Directions Shape Andhra Pradesh Local Body Elections

The Andhra Pradesh State Election Commission is conducting Mandal Parishad Territorial Constituency (MPTC) and Zilla Parishad Territorial Constituency (ZPTC) elections with polling on November 3, 2026. The Supreme Court's involvement in election scheduling and nomination scrutiny highlights the judiciary's role in electoral governance and federalism.

The Supreme Court's directions influence the conduct and scheduling of Andhra Pradesh's local body elections, underscoring judicial oversight in electoral processes.

Why is this in the news?

The Andhra Pradesh State Election Commission began the nomination process for MPTC and ZPTC elections on October 21, 2026, with polling scheduled for November 3, 2026, following Supreme Court directions on election scheduling and nomination scrutiny.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the key features of the Andhra Pradesh local body elections?

  • Election schedule. Nominations for MPTC and ZPTC elections opened on October 21, 2026, and closed on October 23, with polling set for November 3, 2026, and re-polling if needed on November 4.
  • Polling and counting. Polling will occur between 7 a.m. and 5 p.m., with counting of votes and result declaration on November 5, 2026.
  • Voter and staff details. In Visakhapatnam, 1,62,407 voters across 203 polling stations will participate, supported by 1,462 staff members and 480 ballot boxes.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the nomination requirements and procedures?

  • Candidate eligibility. Candidates must be at least 21 years old and submit age certificates, Aadhaar and Voter ID cards, educational certificates, caste and residential certificates, and passport-sized photographs.
  • Mandatory declarations. Nomination papers require a full declaration of assets and liabilities, non-defaulter certificates from banks, no-dues certificates for local taxes and utilities, and a police No-Objection Certificate confirming no pending criminal cases.
  • Proposers and submission. Two proposers who are registered voters in the constituency must sign and provide Aadhaar photocopies; only the candidate and proposers can access the Returning Officer’s hall during submission.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does the Supreme Court impact the election process?

  • Election scheduling. The Supreme Court deferred the final list for municipal polls to October 25 on a plea by a political party, directing the State Election Commission to decide on nominations accordingly.
  • Judicial oversight. The Court’s directions ensure adherence to proper procedures in nomination scrutiny and election scheduling, reinforcing the judiciary’s role in maintaining free and fair local elections.

Back to basics: Role of State Election Commissions

State Election Commissions are constitutional bodies responsible for conducting elections to local bodies such as municipalities and panchayats. They ensure elections are free, fair, and conducted according to legal provisions. Their functions include issuing notifications, overseeing nomination processes, polling, counting, and declaring results.
