Proposal to Use Voter ID as Welfare Document by Union Minister Rajiv Ranjan Singh

Union Minister Rajiv Ranjan Singh proposed using voter ID cards with biometric authentication as the basis for identifying beneficiaries and processing payments for welfare schemes. This proposal raises important questions on governance, transparency, and citizen rights relevant for UPSC exams.

Union Minister Rajiv Ranjan Singh suggested voter ID cards could replace Aadhaar for welfare scheme identification to reduce fraud.

Why is this in the news?

On November 3, 2026, Union Minister Rajiv Ranjan Singh proposed using voter ID cards with biometric authentication for welfare beneficiary identification and payment processing.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the proposal by Rajiv Ranjan Singh regarding voter ID?

  • Proposal details. Rajiv Ranjan Singh proposed that voter ID cards, combined with biometric authentication, be used as the primary document for identifying beneficiaries and processing payments under welfare schemes.
  • Reasoning behind proposal. He argued that Aadhaar cards can be easily obtained fraudulently, implying that voter ID cards could provide a more reliable mechanism to prevent fraudulent beneficiaries.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this proposal matter for governance and welfare?

  • Linking welfare to voter ID. Using voter ID as a welfare document links electoral identity with social welfare benefits, which raises issues about disenfranchisement and exclusion.
  • Concerns raised. Opposition parties have linked this proposal to the recent Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, where 13.5 crore electors were reportedly removed, fearing that disenfranchisement could lead to exclusion from welfare programmes.
  • Historical context. The ration card was the first mass-scale identity document linking welfare entitlements to stable residence and household enumeration, establishing a formal identity for Indian families.

Back to basics: Identity documents and welfare schemes

Identity documents like ration cards and Aadhaar are used to identify beneficiaries for welfare schemes to ensure targeted delivery of benefits. Linking welfare entitlements to stable residence and household enumeration helps prevent fraud and exclusion. Biometric authentication adds a layer of security to verify identity.
