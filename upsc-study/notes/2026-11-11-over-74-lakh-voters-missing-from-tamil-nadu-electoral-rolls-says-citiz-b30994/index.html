74 Lakh Voters Missing from Tamil Nadu Electoral Rolls: Citizens’ Collective

The Independent Young Citizens Collective (IYCC) alleges that 74.07 lakh voters have been removed from Tamil Nadu’s electoral rolls during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR). This raises concerns about electoral transparency and democratic participation, important for understanding election integrity in India.

The deletion of over 74 lakh voters from Tamil Nadu’s electoral rolls during the SIR has sparked demands for inquiry and electoral reforms.

Why is this in the news?

On November 10, 2026, the IYCC revealed that 74.07 lakh voters were removed from Tamil Nadu’s electoral rolls during the Special Intensive Revision, prompting calls for investigation and protests.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the issue with Tamil Nadu’s electoral rolls?

  • Massive voter deletions. The IYCC alleges that 74.07 lakh voters, about 11.5% of Tamil Nadu’s electorate, were removed from the electoral rolls during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR).
  • Concerns about democratic impact. These deletions have raised serious questions about the fairness of the electoral process and the potential disenfranchisement of eligible voters.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for electoral democracy?

  • Challenge to voter inclusion. The SIR exercise is criticized for focusing more on deleting voters than ensuring that all eligible citizens are included in the rolls.
  • Burden on voters. Voters were required to provide details from the 2002 electoral rolls, which many may not remember, creating unreasonable hurdles for inclusion.
  • Calls for accountability. The IYCC demands an inquiry into the deletions, annulment of elections held with these rolls, and reforms in the Election Commission’s appointment and immunity provisions.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What responses and actions have been proposed?

  • Rally and protests. The IYCC announced a rally on November 26 on Anna Salai as part of a nationwide day of action against the SIR.
  • Demands for electoral roll restoration. They call for scrapping the SIR and restoring all eligible voters removed during the exercise.
  • Election Commission reforms. Suggestions include repealing the 2023 law granting immunity to Election Commissioners and changing the appointment process to reduce executive dominance.

Back to basics: Electoral Rolls and Voter Registration

Electoral rolls are official lists of eligible voters maintained by the Election Commission to ensure free and fair elections. Voter registration involves verifying and including eligible citizens in these rolls. Periodic revisions update the rolls by adding new voters and removing ineligible or duplicate names to maintain accuracy and integrity in the electoral process.

What happens next?

  • Inquiry and transparency. An independent inquiry into the alleged mass deletions is necessary to restore trust and ensure electoral integrity.
  • Election Commission reforms. Reforming the appointment process and legal immunity of Election Commissioners can enhance accountability and public confidence.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls in Tamil Nadu: 1. The SIR led to the removal of over 74 lakh voters, about 11.5% of the electorate. 2. The Election Commission’s appointment process is currently free from executive influence. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the executive currently holds a 2:1 majority in the selection committee for appointing Election Commissioners, indicating executive influence.
