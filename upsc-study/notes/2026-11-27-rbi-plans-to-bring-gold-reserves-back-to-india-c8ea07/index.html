RBI Repatriates Gold Reserves to India Amid Global Trends

The Reserve Bank of India has increased the domestic storage of its gold reserves, holding 84% of its total gold in India by September 2026. This shift reflects a global trend among central banks to store gold domestically for greater control amid geopolitical uncertainties, which is important for understanding India's monetary policy and financial security.

The RBI has brought back a majority of its gold reserves to India to enhance control over its assets amid global geopolitical risks.

Why is this in the news?

The RBI's half-yearly report for 2026-27 revealed that it increased the share of gold held in India to 84%, reflecting a global trend of central banks preferring domestic gold storage amid geopolitical tensions.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

Why is RBI bringing gold reserves back to India?

  • Geopolitical risks. Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022 and the freezing of Russia's dollar reserves, central banks including RBI shifted towards holding more non-dollar assets like gold domestically to avoid foreign control risks.
  • Greater control over assets. Storing gold within India gives RBI more direct control over its reserves, reducing vulnerability to foreign political actions.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

How much gold does RBI hold domestically and how has this changed?

  • Increase in domestic gold storage. RBI's gold held in India rose from 680.05 metric tonnes in March 2026 to 740.13 metric tonnes by September 2026, making up 84% of its total 880.52 metric tonnes of gold reserves.
  • Trend over recent years. Four years ago, only 39% of RBI's gold was stored in India, showing a significant repatriation trend.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

What is the global context of gold reserve storage?

  • Popular foreign vaults. The Bank of England remains the most popular vault for central banks, with 57% storing some gold there.
  • Shift to domestic storage. Globally, 9% of central banks increased domestic gold storage and 10% diversified overseas storage locations in the past year, indicating a move towards reducing foreign dependency.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

Is RBI increasing its gold holdings?

  • No significant new purchases. Unlike some central banks, RBI has not aggressively bought gold recently, acquiring less than 1 metric tonne since March 2025.
  • Gold's share in forex reserves. Gold's share in RBI's forex reserves rose from 6% before the Covid pandemic to a peak of 19.02% in January 2026, then declined to 14.38% by September 2026 due to price changes, not quantity.

Back to basics: Foreign Exchange Reserves and Gold Reserves

Foreign exchange reserves are assets held by a central bank in foreign currencies, used to back liabilities and influence monetary policy. Gold reserves are a key component of these reserves, providing a non-currency asset that can stabilize a country's financial standing. Central banks store gold either domestically or in foreign vaults, balancing security, liquidity, and geopolitical risks.
