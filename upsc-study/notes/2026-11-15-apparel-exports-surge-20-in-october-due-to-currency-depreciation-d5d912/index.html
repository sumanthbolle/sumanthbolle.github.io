Apparel Exports Rise 20% in October Due to Rupee Depreciation

India's apparel exports grew by 20.33% in dollar terms in October 2026, mainly because of the rupee's depreciation. This increase signals a recovery in the sector and is important for understanding trade competitiveness and currency impact on exports.

Rupee depreciation boosted India's apparel exports by over 20% in October 2026 despite subdued shipment volume growth.

Why is this in the news?

On November 14, 2026, The Hindu reported that India's apparel exports surged 20.33% year-on-year in October 2026, driven largely by currency depreciation.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What caused the surge in apparel exports in October 2026?

  • Currency depreciation effect. The depreciation of the Indian rupee increased the dollar value of apparel exports, contributing to a 20.33% year-on-year growth in October 2026.
  • Volume growth subdued. Despite the rise in export value, the actual volume of garments shipped did not increase significantly, indicating the growth was mainly due to currency effects.
  • Strong international demand. Exporters reported strong orders across major markets like the U.S., UK, and UAE, supporting a positive outlook for the sector.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for UPSC preparation?

  • Trade competitiveness. The case illustrates how currency depreciation can enhance export competitiveness by making goods cheaper for foreign buyers in dollar terms.
  • Economic recovery indicator. The growth in apparel exports signals a gradual recovery in a key manufacturing sector, relevant for understanding India's economic trends.
  • Policy implications. Understanding export trends helps analyze the impact of exchange rates on trade policy and employment in export-oriented industries.

What are the key facts to remember for exams?

  • Export growth rate. Apparel exports grew 20.33% year-on-year in dollar terms in October 2026.
  • Export value. Garment exports were valued at $1.2 billion in October 2026 and $8.5 billion during April-October 2026.

Back to basics: Currency Depreciation and Export Competitiveness

Currency depreciation means the domestic currency loses value relative to foreign currencies. This makes exports cheaper and more competitive in international markets because foreign buyers can buy more goods for the same amount of their currency. However, volume growth depends on actual demand and production capacity, not just currency effects.
