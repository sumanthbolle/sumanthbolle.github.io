COP31 Ends Without Climate Pact Amid Fossil Fuel Disputes

COP31 concluded in Antalya, Türkiye, without a final climate agreement due to disagreements over fossil fuel language. The UN Secretary-General warned that despite the lack of consensus, climate science shows no ambiguity and urgent action is needed.

COP31 failed to reach a climate pact mainly because of fossil fuel disagreements, highlighting the urgent need for accelerated climate action.

Why is this in the news?

COP31 ended on 22 November 2026 without a climate pact after negotiators failed to overcome divisions over fossil fuels, prompting warnings from the UN Secretary-General about the urgency of climate action.

[1] United Nations News

Why did COP31 end without a climate pact?

  • Fossil fuel disagreement. Negotiators could not reach consensus due to divisions over fossil fuel language, with some countries pushing for stronger commitments to move away from coal, oil, and gas, while others sought to protect fossil fuel-dependent economies.
  • Failed compromise drafts. Multiple compromise drafts circulated by COP31 co-presidents Australia and Türkiye acknowledged the risk of exceeding 1.5°C warming but failed to bridge differences, leading to objections from several countries.
  • Longest UN climate summit. COP31 became the longest-running UN climate summit on record, extending two days beyond the scheduled two weeks due to deadlock.

[1] United Nations News

What warnings and messages did the UN Secretary-General give?

  • No ambiguity in science. The UN Secretary-General António Guterres emphasized that despite the lack of agreement, the science on climate change is clear and unambiguous.
  • Urgency of action. He warned that global temperatures are breaking records, greenhouse gas emissions remain dangerously high, and the world is on course to exceed the 1.5°C warming limit.
  • Call for fossil fuel transition. Guterres stated that fossil fuels are the primary driver of global warming and urged accelerating the transition to cleaner energy, criticizing fossil fuel interests for obstructing climate action.
  • Support for vulnerable countries. He called for stronger emissions cuts, increased adaptation finance, and support for countries least responsible for climate change but most affected.

[1] United Nations News

How did countries react to the COP31 outcome?

  • European Union objection. The EU formally objected to the final text, criticizing the omission of fossil fuel references and calling the pact the 'lowest common denominator.'
  • Other country positions. Panama, the Marshall Islands, and Pakistan expressed concerns about weakened commitments and urged continued efforts for consensus, while Saudi Arabia defended carbon capture and storage technology.
  • African Group stance. Ghana, speaking for the African Group, acknowledged shortcomings but chose not to block consensus, emphasizing the text reflected diverse views.

[1] United Nations News

What are the implications for future climate negotiations?

  • Focus on COP32. Attention now shifts to COP32 in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, where unresolved issues like fossil fuel transition and climate finance mobilization will be addressed.
  • Lessons from COP31. UN Climate Change Executive Secretary Simon Stiell urged countries to learn from the deadlock and avoid repeating the impasse, highlighting progress made on just transition and climate action initiatives.

Back to basics: UN Climate Change Conferences (COP)

The Conference of Parties (COP) is an annual meeting of countries under the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change to negotiate and advance global climate action. These summits aim to assess progress, set targets, and resolve issues related to climate change mitigation and adaptation.

What happens next?

  • Accelerate fossil fuel transition. The priority is to accelerate and finance the shift away from fossil fuels to cleaner energy sources, as emphasized by the UN Secretary-General.
  • Increase climate finance. Stronger support and substantially increased adaptation finance for vulnerable countries are needed to address climate impacts equitably.
  • Build on COP31 progress. COP32 must build on achievements like the just transition agreement and COP31 Action Agenda initiatives rather than stepping back.
