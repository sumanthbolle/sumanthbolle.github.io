KERC mandates 43.33% renewable energy use by 2030 for Escoms and industries

The Karnataka Electricity Regulatory Commission (KERC) has set a Renewable Procurement Obligation (RPO) target of 43.33% renewable energy use by 2029-30 for electricity distribution companies (Escoms) and large consumers. This phased increase from 35.95% in 2026-27 reflects a regulatory push to reduce reliance on coal and promote sustainable energy, relevant for UPSC aspirants studying energy policy and environmental governance.

KERC’s draft regulations require Escoms and large electricity consumers to progressively increase renewable energy use to 43.33% by 2029-30, supporting India’s energy transition goals.

Why is this in the news?

On November 22, 2026, KERC proposed draft regulations increasing renewable energy targets for Escoms and large consumers to 43.33% by 2029-30.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the new renewable energy targets set by KERC?

  • Phased RPO targets. KERC has set Renewable Procurement Obligation targets starting at 35.95% in 2026-27, increasing to 38.81% in 2027-28, 41.36% in 2028-29, and reaching 43.33% in 2029-30.
  • Entities covered. The targets apply to electricity distribution companies (Escoms) and obligated entities including open-access consumers with contracted demand of 1 MW or more, such as industries and commercial establishments.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why has KERC raised renewable energy targets?

  • Reducing coal dependence. Due to poor monsoon and shortages in hydropower and wind energy, Karnataka’s coal reliance has increased, prompting higher renewable targets to promote cleaner energy.
  • Alignment with national policy. KERC’s higher RPO targets align with the Union Ministry of New and Renewable Energy’s proposal to expand renewable obligations across stakeholders.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How will compliance with RPO targets be ensured?

  • Multiple compliance options. Entities can meet RPOs by consuming renewable electricity, purchasing Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs), or paying a buyout price if targets are unmet.
  • Monitoring and penalties. Entities must submit consumption details by July 31, 2027, with state agency monitoring and compliance reports due by August 31, 2027; non-compliance can lead to proceedings under Section 142 of the Electricity Act, 2003.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What renewable energy sources are included in the targets?

  • Diverse renewable sources. The RPO covers solar, wind, hydropower, biomass, municipal solid waste-based energy, and distributed renewable energy such as rooftop solar and small projects below 10 MW.
  • New projects emphasized. Wind and hydropower RPOs must be met through projects commissioned after March 31, 2024, distinguishing newer renewable capacity from older installations.

Back to basics: Renewable Procurement Obligation (RPO)

RPO is a regulatory mandate requiring electricity distribution companies and large consumers to source a specified minimum percentage of their electricity from renewable energy sources. It aims to promote clean energy adoption and reduce dependence on fossil fuels. Compliance can be achieved by consuming renewable power, purchasing Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs), or paying a penalty if targets are unmet.
