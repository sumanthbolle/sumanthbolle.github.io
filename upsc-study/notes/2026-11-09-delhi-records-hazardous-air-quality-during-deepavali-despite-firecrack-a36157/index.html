Delhi's Air Quality Turns Hazardous During Deepavali Despite Firecracker Ban

During Deepavali 2026, Delhi experienced air pollution levels over 50 times the WHO limit as many residents ignored the Supreme Court's firecracker restrictions. This highlights challenges in enforcing environmental regulations and managing urban pollution, important for understanding governance and public health issues.

Delhi's air quality deteriorated to hazardous levels during Deepavali 2026 despite a Supreme Court ban on traditional firecrackers, exposing enforcement and pollution control challenges.

Why is this in the news?

On Deepavali night 2026, Delhi recorded PM2.5 levels up to 66 times the WHO guideline as people defied the Supreme Court's firecracker ban, causing severe air pollution.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What caused hazardous air quality in Delhi during Deepavali 2026?

  • Firecracker use despite ban. Many Delhi residents burst traditional firecrackers beyond the Supreme Court's 10 p.m. deadline and green cracker restrictions, contributing heavily to pollution spikes.
  • High PM2.5 concentrations. PM2.5 levels reached up to 993 micrograms per cubic metre in Punjabi Bagh, about 66 times the WHO's 24-hour guideline of 15 micrograms per cubic metre.
  • Other pollution sources. Winter meteorological conditions, stubble burning in October-November, vehicle emissions, and dust also worsened air quality during this period.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How severe was the air pollution and what are its health implications?

  • Severe and very poor AQI levels. Delhi's 24-hour average Air Quality Index stood at 370 on November 9, categorized as 'very poor', with several areas experiencing 'severe' pollution.
  • Health risks from PM2.5. Fine particulate matter (PM2.5) can penetrate deep into lungs and bloodstream, causing respiratory and cardiovascular problems, especially in vulnerable populations.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What challenges does this situation highlight for environmental governance?

  • Enforcement gaps. Despite Supreme Court directives permitting only green crackers during specific time slots, traditional firecrackers remained available and widely used.
  • Data monitoring issues. Several Delhi Pollution Control Committee monitoring stations did not provide data during peak pollution hours, possibly underestimating the pollution spike.

Back to basics: Air Quality Index (AQI) and PM2.5 pollution

The Air Quality Index (AQI) is a measure that indicates how polluted the air is on a given day, with categories ranging from 'good' to 'severe'. PM2.5 refers to fine particulate matter with a diameter of 2.5 micrometres or less, which can penetrate deep into the lungs and bloodstream, posing serious health risks. Monitoring AQI and PM2.5 helps assess pollution levels and inform public health advisories.
