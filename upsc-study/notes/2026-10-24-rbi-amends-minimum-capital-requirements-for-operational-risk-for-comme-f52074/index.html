RBI Revises Minimum Capital Requirements for Operational Risk in Banks

The Reserve Bank of India issued the Second Amendment Directions, 2026, specifying the implementation of the Basel III Standardised Approach for operational risk capital requirements effective April 1, 2027. This update is crucial for understanding the evolving regulatory framework governing commercial banks' risk management.

RBI's 2026 amendment mandates new minimum capital norms for operational risk under Basel III, effective from April 1, 2027.

Why is this in the news?

On October 23, 2026, RBI released the Second Amendment Directions, 2026, updating minimum capital requirements for operational risk in commercial banks, following Basel Committee clarifications.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What changes did RBI make to operational risk capital requirements?

  • New amendment issued. RBI issued the Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Forthcoming Instructions) Second Amendment Directions, 2026, updating operational risk capital requirements.
  • Basel III Standardised Approach. The amendment specifies implementation of the Basel III Standardised Approach for minimum operational risk regulatory capital requirements.
  • Implementation date. The new norms will come into effect from April 1, 2027.
  • Scope of applicability. These directions apply to commercial banks excluding Small Finance Banks, Payment Banks, and Regional Rural Banks.
  • Additional clarifications. The amendment incorporates further clarifications issued by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision after the initial 2025 Directions.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Why does this matter for banking regulation and exams?

  • Risk management framework. The amendment reflects RBI's alignment with international Basel III norms, strengthening banks' capital buffers against operational risks.
  • Financial stability. By updating capital requirements, RBI aims to enhance the resilience of commercial banks to operational losses.
  • Exam relevance. Understanding RBI's regulatory updates on capital adequacy is important for questions on financial sector regulation and risk management.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

How does this relate to other RBI regulatory updates?

  • Leverage ratio update. On the same day, RBI also issued final guidelines on implementing the Leverage Ratio 2017 Standard, effective April 1, 2027.
  • Comprehensive capital norms. These concurrent updates indicate RBI's comprehensive approach to revising prudential norms for capital adequacy.

Back to basics: Capital Adequacy and Operational Risk

Capital adequacy refers to the minimum capital a bank must hold to cover its risks and protect depositors. Operational risk involves losses from failures in internal processes, people, systems, or external events. Basel III norms set international standards for banks to maintain sufficient capital against such risks, ensuring financial stability.
