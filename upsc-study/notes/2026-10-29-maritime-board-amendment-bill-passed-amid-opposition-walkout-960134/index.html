Kerala Maritime Board (Amendment) Bill Passed Amid Opposition Walkout

Kerala Assembly passed the Maritime Board (Amendment) Bill, 2026, which aims to reconstitute the board for centralized decision-making and operational efficiency. The Opposition protested, citing concerns over coastal people's rights and environmental impacts, highlighting tensions in state governance and maritime policy.

The Kerala Maritime Board (Amendment) Bill centralizes maritime governance but faces opposition over coastal rights and environmental concerns.

Why is this in the news?

The Kerala Assembly passed the Maritime Board (Amendment) Bill, 2026, on October 29, 2026, amid an Opposition walkout protesting its provisions.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What changes does the Maritime Board (Amendment) Bill introduce?

  • Board reconstitution. The Bill provides for reconstituting the Kerala Maritime Board to establish a centralized decision-making mechanism and enhance operational efficiency.
  • State Maritime Land Bank. It proposes constituting a State Maritime Land Bank comprising land under state government control within port limits for township and other development projects.
  • Inclusion of officials. The Bill includes the Revenue and Fisheries Secretaries as part of the Board, aiming to strengthen governance.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why did the Opposition walk out against the Bill?

  • Coastal people's rights. Opposition leader Pinarayi Vijayan argued the Bill threatens the right to life and livelihood of coastal people by risking eviction from lands used for fishing activities.
  • Environmental concerns. The Opposition claimed that constructions and private tourism projects under the Bill could harm the marine ecosystem and violate coastal protection laws.
  • Lack of representation. The Bill excludes representatives of fishers, coastal communities, local bodies, and relevant ministers from the Board, raising concerns about inclusivity.
  • Commercialisation fears. Charging 'waterfront royalty' and prioritizing commercial interests over traditional fishing access were seen as detrimental to coastal livelihoods.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How did the government respond to the Opposition's concerns?

  • Clarification on land use. The government clarified that only land within port limits would be part of the Land Bank, denying that all state-controlled land would be transferred.
  • Employment and development. Officials highlighted that the Bill would promote harbour and out-of-harbour development, generating employment and investment in maritime industries.
  • Board's statutory status. The Board remains a statutory government body, and the Bill does not deny fishers' right to movement or access to the sea.
  • Concessionaire explanation. The term 'concessionaire' is standard for ports, with the example of the Adani group at Vizhinjam cited as a government partner.

Back to basics: Maritime Board and State Legislation

A Maritime Board is a statutory body responsible for managing port and maritime activities within a state. State legislatures can amend laws governing such boards to improve governance, operational efficiency, and development. These amendments may affect coastal communities, environmental regulations, and economic infrastructure, highlighting the balance between development and rights protection.
