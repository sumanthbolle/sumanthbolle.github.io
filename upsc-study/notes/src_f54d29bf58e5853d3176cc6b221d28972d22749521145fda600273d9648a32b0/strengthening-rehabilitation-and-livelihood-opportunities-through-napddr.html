India implications

  • Policy should treat substance dependence as a multidimensional social-sector issue affecting health, family stability, dignity and employability.
  • District-level convergence among social justice authorities, health services, local bodies, families, rehabilitation centres and livelihood programmes can reduce gaps between treatment and reintegration.
  • Stigma can delay help-seeking and weaken recovery; confidential, non-discriminatory and community-based services are therefore relevant to effective implementation.
  • Outcome assessment should track continuity of care, relapse prevention, family functioning, social reintegration and livelihood stability, rather than counting only beneficiaries or counselling sessions.

Way forward

  • Integrate rehabilitation with accessible counselling, treatment, family support and livelihood pathways.
  • Ensure continuity of care and follow-up after institutional treatment, including relapse-prevention support.
  • Reduce stigma through confidential, community-sensitive and non-discriminatory service delivery.
  • Use outcome indicators covering sustained recovery, family functioning, social reintegration and livelihood stability.
