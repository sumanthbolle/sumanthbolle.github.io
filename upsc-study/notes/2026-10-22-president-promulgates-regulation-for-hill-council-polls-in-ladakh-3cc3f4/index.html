President Promulgates Regulation for Hill Council Polls in Ladakh

The President of India promulgated the Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Councils (LAHDC) (Amendment) Regulation, 2026, enabling elections for hill councils in all eight districts of Ladakh. This development is significant for UPSC aspirants as it highlights changes in local governance and decentralization in Union Territories.

The 2026 LAHDC amendment facilitates elections in all eight Ladakh districts, expanding local self-governance under the Union Territory administration.

Why is this in the news?

The President promulgated the LAHDC (Amendment) Regulation, 2026 on October 16, paving the way for hill council elections in all eight districts of Ladakh.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What changes did the LAHDC (Amendment) Regulation, 2026 introduce?

  • Expansion of hill councils. The amendment allows the creation of Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Councils in all eight districts of Ladakh, including the newly created Sankoo district.
  • Rationalisation of council seats. The number of constituencies in each council has been rationalised from a fixed 26 seats to a range between seven and 24 seats.
  • Increased inclusivity. The regulation provides for the nomination of four members from women and unrepresented communities to enhance participation in local governance.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this amendment matter for Ladakh's governance?

  • Strengthening grassroots democracy. The amendment aims to strengthen local representation and promote inclusivity in Ladakh's governance by expanding the hill councils.
  • Union Territory administrative control. The amendment gives the Union Territory administration greater control over the composition and functioning of the councils.
  • Context of ongoing political dialogue. The timing of the amendment has raised concerns as talks continue on Ladakh's constitutional status and demands for stronger protections for the councils.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the concerns raised about the amendment?

  • Lack of consultation. Civil society groups like the Kargil Democratic Alliance criticized the government for making substantial changes without consulting Ladakh's people and stakeholders.
  • Impact on autonomy negotiations. The changes affect institutions central to local self-government, potentially undermining ongoing negotiations for meaningful autonomy.
  • Executive discretion over councils. There is concern that the amendment increases executive discretion over representative institutions, which may weaken local self-governance.

Back to basics: Local Governance in Union Territories

Union Territories in India are administered directly by the Central Government but may have local governance bodies like Autonomous Hill Development Councils to decentralize power. These councils enable local decision-making and representation, especially in regions with distinct cultural or geographical identities. The Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Council is an example that provides administrative autonomy to districts within the Union Territory of Ladakh.
