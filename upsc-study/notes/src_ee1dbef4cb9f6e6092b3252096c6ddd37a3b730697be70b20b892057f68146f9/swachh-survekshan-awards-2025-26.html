India implications

  • Urban Local Bodies may use benchmarking and peer learning to improve service delivery, but results should be linked to measurable outcomes such as segregation, processing, wastewater management and worker welfare.
  • The focus on citizen feedback strengthens participatory governance and accountability, while school-level awareness can support long-term behavioural change.
  • Special recognition for river-front, coastal and Himalayan towns can encourage context-sensitive urban environmental management.
  • The initiative raises the governance question of whether rankings should complement, rather than substitute for, statutory municipal planning, finance, staffing and enforcement.

Way forward

  • Use rankings alongside outcome indicators for segregation, processing, wastewater treatment, faecal-sludge management and grievance resolution.
  • Ensure transparent evidence verification, independent assessment and accessible appeal mechanisms for Urban Local Bodies.
  • Protect sanitation workers through mechanisation where appropriate, occupational safety, welfare measures and meaningful inclusion of their field feedback.
  • Strengthen municipal finance, technical staffing and inter-city knowledge partnerships so that rankings reflect durable capacity rather than short-term campaigns.
