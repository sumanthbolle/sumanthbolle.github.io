Supreme Court Clarifies OBC Creamy Layer Income Criteria

The Supreme Court reaffirmed that parental salary and agricultural income should be excluded from the income test for determining OBC creamy layer status. This clarification affects reservation eligibility and has implications for social justice and constitutional law.

The Supreme Court upheld excluding parental salary and agricultural income from the OBC creamy layer income test, impacting reservation claims.

Why is this in the news?

On November 6, 2026, the Supreme Court reaffirmed its March 11, 2026 judgment upholding the exclusion of parental salary and agricultural income from the OBC creamy layer income criteria.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court clarify on OBC creamy layer criteria?

  • Reaffirmation of exclusion. The Supreme Court reaffirmed that parental salary and agricultural income should not be counted in the income/wealth test to determine whether an OBC candidate falls under the creamy layer.
  • Basis of the ruling. The Court upheld the 1993 Office Memorandum which excludes these incomes from consideration for creamy layer status.
  • Impact on reservation claims. This clarification affects the eligibility of OBC candidates claiming reservation benefits, especially in civil services examinations.

Back to basics: OBC Creamy Layer Concept

The creamy layer refers to the relatively wealthier and better-educated members of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) who are excluded from reservation benefits to ensure affirmative action reaches the genuinely disadvantaged. Income and wealth criteria are used to identify this layer, and government orders specify which incomes count for this purpose.
