Justice Sanjay Kishan Kaul on Executive Influence Over Collegium System

Former Supreme Court judge Justice Sanjay Kishan Kaul's memoir 'Without Fear or Favour' reveals the growing influence of the executive over the collegium system and the internal challenges in India's higher judiciary. This insight is crucial for understanding judicial independence and the separation of powers in India.

Justice Kaul's memoir highlights the executive's increasing role in judicial appointments, raising concerns about the collegium system's autonomy.

Why is this in the news?

Justice Sanjay Kishan Kaul published his memoir 'Without Fear or Favour' in November 2026, discussing the executive's growing influence over the collegium system and the internal dynamics of judicial appointments.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What does Justice Kaul's memoir reveal about the collegium system?

  • Executive influence. The memoir exposes the increasing role of the executive in delaying and influencing collegium recommendations for judicial appointments.
  • Judicial independence challenges. It highlights the power struggles within the judiciary and the dilemmas judges face in maintaining independence while reconciling competing interests.
  • Need for reform. Justice Kaul reflects on the necessity of systemic reforms to preserve judicial autonomy and improve the appointment process.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for the UPSC exam?

  • Separation of powers. The memoir provides a real-world example of tensions between the judiciary and executive, relevant to the constitutional principle of separation of powers.
  • Judicial appointments. Understanding the collegium system's challenges helps explain the process and controversies around judicial appointments in India.
  • Judicial independence. The insights contribute to debates on maintaining the judiciary's independence, a key theme in governance and constitutional studies.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Back to basics: Collegium System and Separation of Powers

The collegium system is a method by which judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts are appointed and transferred, primarily involving senior judges rather than the executive. Separation of powers is a constitutional principle that divides government responsibilities among the legislature, executive, and judiciary to prevent concentration of power and ensure checks and balances. Judicial independence is essential to uphold this separation and maintain the rule of law.
