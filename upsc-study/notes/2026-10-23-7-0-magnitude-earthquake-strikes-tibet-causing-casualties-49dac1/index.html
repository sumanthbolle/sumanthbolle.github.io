7.0 Magnitude Earthquake Strikes Tibet Causing Casualties

What happened during the Tibet earthquake?

  • Magnitude and timing. A 7.0 magnitude earthquake struck Tibet at 10:33 p.m. local time on October 22, 2026.
  • Epicentre location. The epicentre was in a mountainous area about 190 kilometres east-northeast of Nagqu and 390 kilometres northeast of Lhasa, at a shallow depth of 10 kilometres.
  • Casualties and damage. At least sixteen people died and 32 were injured; houses collapsed and cracks appeared in buildings, with power and telecom services disrupted.
  • Response measures. A level-II earthquake disaster emergency response was activated, and workers were sent to coordinate relief and check nearby villages.

Back to basics: Earthquake and Seismic Hazards

An earthquake is the shaking of the Earth's surface caused by the sudden release of energy in the crust, often along fault lines. Magnitude measures the energy released, while the epicentre is the point on the surface directly above the origin. Earthquakes can cause damage to infrastructure and loss of life, especially in mountainous and densely populated areas. Disaster management involves preparedness, response, and recovery to reduce the impact of such natural hazards.
