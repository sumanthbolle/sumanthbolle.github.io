RBI Proposes ₹50 Lakh Cap on Select Overseas Remittances

The Reserve Bank of India has proposed a ₹50 lakh sub-limit within the existing $250,000 annual Liberalised Remittance Scheme cap to curb dollar outflows and protect the rupee. This measure is significant for understanding India's forex management and monetary policy strategies.

RBI aims to limit certain overseas remittances to ₹50 lakh under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme to control dollar outflows and stabilize the rupee.

Why is this in the news?

On November 13, 2026, The Hindu reported that RBI proposed a ₹50 lakh sub-limit within the $250,000 annual Liberalised Remittance Scheme cap to reduce dollar outflows and protect the rupee.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the RBI's proposed change to overseas remittances?

  • Proposed sub-limit. RBI has proposed introducing a ₹50 lakh sub-limit on certain overseas remittances within the existing $250,000 annual Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) cap.
  • Purpose of the cap. The cap aims to curb massive dollar outflows from India and protect the value of the rupee.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)?

  • Scheme overview. LRS is a scheme under the Foreign Exchange Management Act introduced by RBI in 2004, allowing Indian residents to remit up to $250,000 annually for various purposes.
  • Permitted transactions. It covers both capital and current account transactions such as travel, education, medical treatment, overseas investments, opening foreign currency accounts, property purchase, interest-free loans up to one year, and investments in foreign equity, mutual funds, and listed debt securities.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for the economy and exams?

  • Forex management. The proposal reflects RBI's efforts to manage foreign exchange reserves and stabilize the rupee by controlling dollar outflows.
  • Monetary policy relevance. It highlights monetary policy tools used to influence currency stability, a key topic in Economy syllabus.
  • Diaspora and remittances. Understanding LRS and remittance limits is important for questions on India’s diaspora, international migration, and their economic impact.

Back to basics: Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)

The Liberalised Remittance Scheme allows Indian residents to send money abroad up to a specified annual limit for permitted purposes such as education, travel, and investments. It is regulated by the Reserve Bank of India under the Foreign Exchange Management Act to control foreign exchange outflows and maintain currency stability.
