Government Securities Underwriting Auction and Result on October 30, 2026

The Reserve Bank of India conducted an underwriting auction for government securities worth ₹33,000 crore on October 30, 2026. This auction and its results are important for understanding government borrowing, fiscal management, and the role of primary dealers in financial markets.

The RBI's underwriting auction on October 30, 2026, successfully allocated ₹33,000 crore in government securities with specified underwriting commissions to primary dealers.

Why is this in the news?

The Reserve Bank of India announced and conducted an underwriting auction for government securities on October 30, 2026.

[1] Reserve Bank of India[2] Reserve Bank of India

What was the underwriting auction conducted on October 30, 2026?

  • Auction details. The Government of India announced the sale of government securities worth ₹33,000 crore through an underwriting auction held on October 30, 2026.
  • Securities and amounts. The securities included 6.20% GS 2029 (₹9,000 crore), 6.57% GS 2033 (₹12,000 crore), 7.63% GS 2056 (₹9,000 crore), and 7.50% GOI SGrB 2056 (₹3,000 crore).
  • Role of Primary Dealers. Primary Dealers (PDs) had minimum underwriting commitments and bidding commitments under Additional Competitive Underwriting (ACU) for each security, ensuring market support for the auction.
  • Auction method and timing. The auction was conducted through a multiple price-based method, with PDs submitting bids electronically between 09:00 A.M. and 09:30 A.M. on the auction day.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What were the results and significance of the auction?

  • Total underwriting achieved. The total amount underwritten matched the notified amount of ₹33,000 crore, with each security fully underwritten by PDs.
  • Underwriting commission rates. The RBI set the cut-off underwriting commission rates per ₹100 at 0.92 paise for 6.20% GS 2029, 0.64 paise for 6.57% GS 2033, 0.74 paise for 7.63% GS 2056, and 1.00 paise for 7.50% GOI SGrB 2056.
  • Fiscal and market impact. This auction supports government borrowing requirements and maintains liquidity and stability in government securities markets.

Back to basics: Government Securities and Underwriting Auctions

Government securities are debt instruments issued by the government to borrow money from the public and financial institutions. Underwriting auctions involve Primary Dealers committing to buy a minimum amount of securities, ensuring the government can raise the required funds. The underwriting commission is the fee paid to these dealers for their commitment and risk.
