Karnataka HC Strikes Down Enhanced Reservation for SC/ST

The Karnataka High Court invalidated the state's enhanced reservation for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, sparking criticism from SC/ST groups. This verdict highlights ongoing legal and political challenges in India's reservation policies, relevant for understanding affirmative action debates in UPSC.

The Karnataka High Court struck down enhanced SC/ST reservation, raising issues on reservation limits and legal protections.

Why is this in the news?

On October 14, 2026, various SC/ST individuals and organisations condemned the Karnataka High Court verdict that struck down the state's enhanced reservation for these communities.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Karnataka High Court rule on enhanced reservation?

  • Verdict details. The Karnataka High Court struck down the enhanced reservation for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes introduced by the Karnataka Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Reservation of Seats in Educational Institutions and of Appointments or Posts in the Services under the State) Act, 2022.
  • Reservation ceiling issue. The court held that increasing SC/ST reservation beyond the 50% ceiling violated legal limits, despite the 10% Economically Weaker Sections (EWS) reservation not breaching this cap.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the verdict matter for reservation policy and social justice?

  • Impact on SC/ST representation. SC/ST leaders argue that the verdict undermines their share in reservations, which they say should be increased from the current allocation to address underrepresentation in education and employment.
  • Legal protection challenges. The law was not included in the Ninth Schedule by the Central Government, which would have shielded it from judicial review, exposing it to legal challenges.
  • Political and constitutional implications. Critics say political considerations favored OBC reservation over SC/ST, and that the state government should seek to include the law in the Ninth Schedule or appeal to the Supreme Court for a permanent solution.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How are SC/ST groups responding to the verdict?

  • Condemnation and protests. Various SC/ST organisations expressed deep disappointment and criticised both the state and central governments for failing to protect their reservation rights.
  • Legal strategy. The Karnataka State SC/ST Employees’ Association plans to join the Supreme Court proceedings and submit a joint memorandum to the government, seeking a strong legal challenge against the High Court verdict.
  • Data and evidence concerns. They argue the High Court did not adequately consider empirical data showing backwardness of SC/ST communities in sectors like education, healthcare, and employment.

Back to basics: Reservation policy in India

Reservation is a form of affirmative action in India designed to improve representation of historically disadvantaged groups such as Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Other Backward Classes in education and public employment. The Supreme Court has generally upheld a 50% ceiling on total reservations, except in extraordinary circumstances. Laws placed in the Ninth Schedule of the Constitution are protected from judicial review, which can shield reservation laws from being struck down by courts.

What happens next?

  • Legal appeal. The Karnataka government and SC/ST groups are considering filing a special leave petition in the Supreme Court to challenge the High Court verdict.
  • Ninth Schedule inclusion. Including the reservation law in the Ninth Schedule could provide constitutional protection against judicial invalidation.
