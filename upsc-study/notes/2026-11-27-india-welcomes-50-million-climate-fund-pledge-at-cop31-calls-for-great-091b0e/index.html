India welcomes $50 million climate fund pledge at COP31, urges more adaptation finance

At COP31 in November 2026, India welcomed a $50 million pledge to the Adaptation Fund but emphasized that this amount is inadequate given the scale of adaptation needs in developing countries. India stressed the importance of timely, accessible, and primarily grant-based adaptation finance and highlighted ongoing efforts to push for stronger global climate finance commitments.

India welcomed the $50 million Adaptation Fund pledge at COP31 but called for greater, predictable, and grant-based adaptation finance to meet developing countries' urgent needs.

Why is this in the news?

India responded to the $50 million Adaptation Fund pledge announced at COP31 in November 2026, highlighting the need for more substantial adaptation finance for vulnerable developing countries.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What was India's response to the $50 million Adaptation Fund pledge at COP31?

  • Welcome with reservations. India welcomed the $50 million pledge to the Adaptation Fund but stated that the amount is inadequate given the large adaptation needs of developing countries.
  • Call for timely and accessible funds. India emphasized that financial pledges must translate into timely and accessible resources, especially for the poorest and most climate-vulnerable countries.
  • Adaptation finance characteristics. India maintained that adaptation finance should be adequate, predictable, primarily public, and substantially grant-based to avoid burdening developing countries with debt.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does adaptation finance matter in global climate negotiations?

  • Developmental necessity. Adaptation is an immediate developmental necessity for millions of vulnerable people facing climate impacts.
  • Existing commitments. India noted the disappointment over the removal of the $120 billion adaptation finance figure from COP31's proposed cover text, reflecting difficulties in securing predictable financial commitments.
  • Monitoring progress. There is a challenge in agreeing on how to measure and monitor progress towards tripling adaptation finance by 2035.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How did India engage with other countries and groups at COP31?

  • Collaboration with LMDC and G77. India worked closely with Like-Minded Developing Countries (LMDC) and the G77 group to seek stronger outcomes on adaptation finance and implementation support.
  • Role as LMDC chair. India, as chair of the LMDCs, helped strengthen coordination among developing countries and worked with BASIC and G77 to advance common positions.
  • Addressing negotiation challenges. India stated that the failure to adopt the proposed Antalya Pact was due to substantive issues like mitigation pathways, historical responsibilities, and climate finance obligations, not lack of engagement by the G77.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are India's expectations for future climate negotiations?

  • Focus on COP32 priorities. India looks forward to working with Ethiopia, the COP32 host, to build on progress and address unresolved issues such as climate finance, just transition, technology transfer, and capacity-building.
  • Importance of Climate Finance Work Programme. Effective implementation of the Climate Finance Work Programme and provision of adequate, predictable, grant-based adaptation finance remain central priorities.
  • Second Global Stocktake. India highlighted the Second Global Stocktake as an opportunity to assess global climate action comprehensively, including mitigation ambition, historical responsibility, adaptation burden, and delivery of finance and technology.

Back to basics: Climate Finance and Adaptation

Climate finance refers to funds provided by developed countries to support developing countries in mitigating and adapting to climate change. Adaptation finance specifically helps vulnerable communities adjust to the adverse effects of climate change, such as extreme weather and rising sea levels. It is important that adaptation finance is predictable, adequate, and grant-based to avoid increasing debt burdens on developing countries.

What happens next?

  • Enhance financial support. India will continue to push for enhanced financial support, transparency in delivery, and implementation of existing climate finance commitments, especially for the poorest and most vulnerable countries.
  • Advance climate finance discussions. The Climate Finance Work Programme decision provides a platform for further discussions, workshops, and ministerial dialogues ahead of COP32 and COP33 to advance developed countries' obligations.
  • Operationalise Just Transition Mechanism. Parties agreed to extend and strengthen the Just Transition Work Programme with a phased approach towards operationalising the Just Transition Mechanism by 2029.
