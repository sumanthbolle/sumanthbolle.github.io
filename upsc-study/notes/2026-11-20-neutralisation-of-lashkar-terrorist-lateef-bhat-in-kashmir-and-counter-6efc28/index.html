Neutralisation of Lashkar Terrorist Lateef Bhat and Counter-Terrorism Conference

Lashkar's last known active local terrorist in Kashmir, Lateef Bhat, was neutralised in Kulgam, marking a milestone in counter-terrorism efforts. The Union Home Minister outlined India's unified and proactive counter-terrorism strategy at a conference in New Delhi.

The neutralisation of Lashkar terrorist Lateef Bhat signifies Kashmir is now free of India-born terrorists, reflecting India's strengthened and coordinated counter-terrorism approach.

Why is this in the news?

On 18-19 November 2026, Union Home Minister Amit Shah announced the neutralisation of Lashkar terrorist Lateef Bhat and addressed a two-day counter-terrorism conference in New Delhi.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What happened in the neutralisation operation?

  • Operation details. Lateef Bhat, Lashkar's last known active local terrorist in Kashmir, was neutralised in Kulgam in the early morning hours of 18 November 2026, along with his associate Umar Nisar the day before.
  • Agencies involved. The operation involved coordinated efforts by Jammu and Kashmir Police (JKP), Indian Army, Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF), and intelligence agencies, marked by high synergy.
  • Significance. This marks a major milestone as Kashmir is now free of locally recruited terrorists born in India after 35 years.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What are the key elements of India's counter-terrorism strategy?

  • Zero tolerance policy. Under Prime Minister Narendra Modi's leadership, India has adopted a 'Zero Tolerance' policy towards terrorism, yielding positive results.
  • Unified strategy. A system is being built where all police stations across the country operate under a unified strategy to combat terrorism.
  • Proactive and coordinated approach. The policy emphasizes a proactive offensive approach, structural and operational capability enhancement, and coordinated simultaneous assaults on terror networks.
  • Legal and institutional strengthening. The National Investigation Agency (NIA) has enhanced legal powers and expanded its presence with 16 new branch offices and two zonal offices; the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA) has been strengthened.
  • Technology and intelligence. The Multi-Agency Centre (MAC) now includes cyber security, narco-terrorism, and emerging radicalisation hotspots, supported by a National Memory Bank and strengthened cyber expert units in Anti-Terrorism Squads (ATS).

[2] Press Information Bureau

How does India address terror financing and fugitives abroad?

  • Terror financing control. A 25-point integrated action plan monitors terror funding, requiring better coordination with financial investigation agencies.
  • Bringing fugitives back. Since 2019, 303 fugitives have been brought back to India from 36 countries through scientific intelligence efforts.
  • Legal actions. NIA is empowered to protect interests of Indians abroad and investigate terror-related offences internationally.

[2] Press Information Bureau

What are notable recent counter-terrorism operations and outcomes?

  • PFI network dismantling. In September 2022, coordinated police operations across 11 states dismantled the Popular Front of India (PFI) network, serving as a global case study.
  • Shahzad Bhatti network. Declared a terrorist organisation under UAPA, over 40 modules dismantled, 100+ cases registered, and more than 271 arrests made.
  • Peace and security improvements. Peace established in three major hotspots, illegal religious structures along borders removed, and border security enhanced through a comprehensive approach.

Back to basics: Counter-terrorism strategy

Counter-terrorism involves coordinated actions by security agencies to prevent, detect, and respond to terrorist threats. It includes legal frameworks, intelligence sharing, operational capabilities, and addressing terror financing. A unified strategy ensures all agencies work together effectively, while proactive measures aim to dismantle terror networks before attacks occur.

What happens next?

  • Strengthen police station-level intelligence. Develop mechanisms to transmit actionable intelligence from police stations to higher authorities like the Director General of Police for timely interventions.
  • Enhance ATS cyber capabilities. Every state's Anti-Terrorism Squad should strengthen its cyber expert units to counter increasing use of technology in terrorism.
  • Establish regional security grids. Prioritize the creation of Quadrangular Regional Security Grids in border states to improve coordination and response.
