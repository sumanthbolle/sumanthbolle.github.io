High Court directs schools to tackle bullying on gender identity and sexual orientation

The Punjab and Haryana High Court has mandated that schools under its jurisdiction must implement Supreme Court guidelines to address bullying and harassment based on gender identity and sexual orientation. This ruling highlights the need for effective enforcement of existing protections for LGBTQIA+ students in educational institutions.

The High Court ordered schools to ensure prompt and confidential handling of bullying complaints related to gender identity and sexual orientation, emphasizing implementation over new guidelines.

Why is this in the news?

Triggered by a petition following the death of a 16-year-old bullied student in Faridabad, the Punjab and Haryana High Court issued directions on November 4, 2026, to enforce anti-bullying guidelines in schools.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the High Court direct schools to do?

  • Implement Supreme Court guidelines. Schools must comply with Supreme Court guidelines from Sukdeb Saha’s case to address bullying and harassment based on gender, gender identity, and sexual orientation.
  • Establish complaint mechanisms. Every school must have an internal committee to handle complaints of bullying and harassment related to gender identity and sexual orientation.
  • Prompt and confidential handling. Complaints must be dealt with promptly and confidentially, and cases involving cognisable offences should be reported to the police.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why did the court emphasize existing guidelines over new ones?

  • Existing guidelines are binding. The court noted that the Supreme Court’s guidelines are already binding and that framing new guidelines would not serve a useful purpose.
  • Implementation is the real issue. The court observed that guidelines remaining only on paper offer little protection, highlighting the need for effective enforcement at the school level.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered the court’s intervention?

  • Petition by victim’s mother. The court acted after a petition by the mother of a 16-year-old boy who was bullied and assaulted at school due to his gender expression and sexual orientation, leading to his death in 2022.
  • Demand for sensitisation. The petition sought guidelines for sensitising school authorities to prevent harassment of LGBTQIA+ students and called for a CBI probe into the case.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What role does the CBSE have following this order?

  • Ensure compliance and scrutiny. CBSE must call for and scrutinise annual reports from schools on compliance with Supreme Court guidelines and take action for non-compliance.

Back to basics: Anti-bullying guidelines in schools

Anti-bullying guidelines are rules and procedures designed to prevent and address bullying in educational institutions. They typically require schools to establish committees to receive complaints, ensure confidentiality, and take timely action. These guidelines aim to create safe environments for all students, including those from vulnerable groups such as LGBTQIA+ individuals.
