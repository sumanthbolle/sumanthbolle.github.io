ED raids multiple locations over alleged money laundering in Kerala and Tamil Nadu

The Enforcement Directorate (ED) conducted raids at 11 locations linked to the Bharath Lajhna Multi State Co-operative Society (BLM) in Kerala and Tamil Nadu on November 4, 2026, investigating alleged money laundering and fraud. This case highlights the role of ED under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) and the challenges in tackling financial crimes affecting economic security.

The ED's raids on BLM cooperative society locations expose alleged large-scale money laundering and fraud, illustrating enforcement under PMLA.

Why is this in the news?

On November 4, 2026, the ED raided 11 locations in Kerala and Tamil Nadu linked to BLM cooperative society over alleged money laundering.

What triggered the ED raids in Kerala and Tamil Nadu?

  • Money laundering probe. The ED launched raids under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) due to alleged diversion of crores of rupees collected from public depositors by BLM cooperative society's managing director.
  • Ongoing Crime Branch investigation. The raids followed an ongoing Crime Branch probe into complaints of misappropriation of funds and physical assault involving R. Premkumar, BLM's de facto chairman and managing director.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the allegations against BLM and its leadership?

  • Misuse of depositor funds. R. Premkumar allegedly diverted large sums collected from depositors into relatives’ accounts for personal use.
  • Criminal cases registered. The Crime Branch registered FIRs for criminal breach of trust and cheating, including a ₹1.6 crore fraud case against Premkumar.
  • Scale of operations. BLM has around 150 branches and over 15 lakh members across Kerala, Tamil Nadu, and Puducherry.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Role of ED and PMLA. The case exemplifies how the Enforcement Directorate enforces the Prevention of Money Laundering Act to investigate and curb financial crimes.
  • Financial crime and economic security. Money laundering and fraud cases impact economic governance and internal security, relevant for understanding regulatory mechanisms.
  • Inter-agency coordination. The investigation shows coordination between the Crime Branch and ED in tackling complex financial frauds.

Back to basics: Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA)

The Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) is a law aimed at preventing money laundering and confiscating property derived from or involved in money laundering. The Enforcement Directorate (ED) is the central agency responsible for investigating offenses under this Act. Money laundering involves disguising illegally obtained money as legitimate income, which impacts economic security and governance.
