India implications

  • AI-enabled screening, diagnostics and remote monitoring could extend specialist support to communities with limited healthcare access, if systems are clinically validated and connected to public-health delivery.
  • Assistive technologies can advance inclusion and well-being, but deployment should address affordability, accessibility, language, disability needs, privacy and human accountability.
  • Strengthening institutions beyond established technology hubs can distribute innovation capacity and create employment through entrepreneurship, while requiring durable funding, intellectual-property support and market linkages.

Way forward

  • Build interdisciplinary training linking AI, medicine, public health, ethics and engineering.
  • Require clinical validation, transparent performance assessment, human oversight and mechanisms for correction before deployment.
  • Design assistive solutions for affordability, accessibility, diverse users and communities with limited healthcare access.
  • Strengthen incubation, intellectual-property support, responsible investment and market linkages beyond established technology hubs.
