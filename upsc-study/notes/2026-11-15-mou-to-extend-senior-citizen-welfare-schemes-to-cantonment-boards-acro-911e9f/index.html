MoU to Extend Senior Citizen Welfare Schemes to Cantonment Boards

The Department of Social Justice and Empowerment (DoSJE) signed an MoU with the Directorate General Defence Estates (DGDE) to implement senior citizen welfare schemes under Atal Vayo Abhyudaya Yojana (AVYAY) in cantonment boards across India. This collaboration leverages cantonment infrastructure to enhance elderly care services, a key welfare initiative relevant for governance and social justice studies.

The MoU between DoSJE and DGDE aims to expand senior citizen welfare schemes to cantonment boards, strengthening elderly care infrastructure and promoting active ageing.

Why is this in the news?

On November 16, 2026, the DoSJE and DGDE signed an MoU to extend senior citizen welfare schemes to cantonment boards nationwide.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What does the MoU between DoSJE and DGDE entail?

  • Purpose of MoU. The MoU establishes a framework for cooperation to implement senior citizen welfare schemes under AVYAY in cantonment boards across India.
  • Leveraging infrastructure. It uses the administrative network of cantonment boards along with DoSJE’s financial and technical support to improve access to elderly care.
  • Key components. The partnership includes setting up new Day Care Centres, strengthening existing ones, providing Senior Citizen Homes with 25/50-bed capacity, and training youth as geriatric caregivers.
  • Promoting intergenerational bonding. The MoU promotes respect between generations via pledges and activities involving students in cantonment board schools.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why is this MoU significant for senior citizen welfare?

  • Rising elderly population. India’s population aged 60 and above is projected to grow from about 10 Crore in 2011 to around 23 Crore by 2036, increasing demand for elderly care.
  • Changing social dynamics. With urbanisation and changing family structures, community-based facilities like Day Care Centres and trained caregivers near homes are increasingly important.
  • Strengthening institutional support. The MoU aims to expand institutional care and community-based services to help senior citizens lead dignified, secure, and self-reliant lives.
  • Creating livelihood opportunities. Training youth as geriatric caregivers also opens new employment avenues while addressing elderly care needs.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How does this MoU fit into the broader welfare framework?

  • Atal Vayo Abhyudaya Yojana. AVYAY is the umbrella scheme of DoSJE for senior citizen welfare, promoting healthy, empowered, and active ageing.
  • Scheme components. Key components include Integrated Programme for Senior Citizens, Senior Citizen Homes, Geriatric Caregiver Training, National Helpline (Elderline – 14567), and Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana.
  • Whole-of-Government approach. The MoU exemplifies a coordinated effort between Defence and Social Justice ministries to extend welfare services efficiently.

Back to basics: Senior Citizen Welfare Schemes

Senior citizen welfare schemes are government initiatives aimed at ensuring elderly people lead dignified, healthy, and secure lives. These schemes often include healthcare support, institutional care, community services like day care centres, and training programs for caregivers. They address challenges posed by ageing populations and changing family structures by promoting active and inclusive ageing.
