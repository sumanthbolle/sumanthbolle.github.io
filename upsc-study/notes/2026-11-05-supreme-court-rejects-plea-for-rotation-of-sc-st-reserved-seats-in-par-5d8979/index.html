Supreme Court Upholds No Rotation for SC/ST Reserved Seats in Legislatures

The Supreme Court refused to order rotation of constituencies reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in Parliament and State Assemblies, ruling this is a legislative matter. This ruling clarifies the judiciary's role in reservation policies and electoral laws, relevant for constitutional and governance studies.

The Supreme Court ruled that rotation of SC/ST reserved constituencies is a legislative issue and cannot be mandated by courts.

Why is this in the news?

On November 4, 2026, the Supreme Court declined to interfere with a High Court judgment upholding the Delimitation Act's provision for SC/ST reserved constituencies without rotation.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court decide regarding rotation of reserved seats?

  • No court-ordered rotation. The Supreme Court held that the rotation of SC/ST reserved constituencies in Parliament and State Assemblies is a matter for the legislature and cannot be directed by the judiciary.
  • Upholding Delimitation Act. The Court refused to interfere with the Allahabad High Court's judgment upholding Section 9(1)(c) of the Delimitation Act, 2002, which identifies reserved constituencies based on higher SC/ST population concentrations.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What were the petitioner's arguments and the Court's response?

  • Petitioner's claim. The petitioner challenged the continued reservation of a constituency for nearly seven decades, arguing that absence of rotation violated Articles 14, 15, and 19 of the Constitution and limited voter choice.
  • Court's reasoning. The Court noted that voters in a multiparty democracy vote based on political ideologies rather than caste, and that candidates from the general category can contest non-reserved seats, distinguishing voting rights from contesting rights.
  • Forum for change. Chief Justice Surya Kant remarked that the petitioner approached the wrong forum and suggested taking the issue to Parliament or through public protests.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this ruling matter for constitutional law and governance?

  • Legislature's domain. The ruling affirms that decisions on reservation seat rotation fall within the legislative domain, reinforcing separation of powers.
  • Clarifies reservation policy. It clarifies that the current reservation system for SC/ST constituencies is based on demographic concentration rather than rotation, impacting electoral representation.
  • Implications for electoral choices. The judgment highlights the balance between protecting reserved constituencies and ensuring voter choice within constitutional limits.

Back to basics: Reservation of Constituencies

Reservation of constituencies is a constitutional mechanism to ensure representation of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in legislative bodies. Reserved constituencies are identified based on the proportion of SC/ST population in an area. Rotation of reserved seats means periodically changing which constituencies are reserved to prevent permanent reservation of the same seats. However, the current legal framework does not mandate rotation for Parliament and State Assemblies, unlike local bodies where rotation is provided under specific constitutional provisions.
