Supreme Court Recognises Children’s Fundamental Right to Internet Access

The Supreme Court ruled that children have a fundamental right to access and use the internet under Articles 21 and 19(1)(a) of the Constitution. This judgment balances child safety with freedom, favouring parental oversight and age-appropriate safeguards over blanket bans.

The Supreme Court affirmed that children’s right to internet access is fundamental and must be protected with intelligent safeguards rather than prohibitive restrictions.

Why is this in the news?

On October 17, 2026, the Supreme Court ruled that children have a fundamental right to access and use the internet, emphasizing the need for balanced safeguards.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court rule about children’s internet rights?

  • Fundamental right affirmed. The Court held that the right to access and use the internet is a fundamental right under Article 21 read with Article 19(1)(a), which applies equally to children irrespective of age.
  • Safety and freedom coexist. The Court stated that children's safety and freedom are not mutually exclusive; children are entitled to both simultaneously.
  • Opposition to blanket bans. It observed that blanket bans on children’s internet access are disproportional and ineffective, as children continue to access unregulated platforms without parental knowledge.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this ruling matter for child welfare and digital governance?

  • Protective mechanisms needed. The Court emphasized intelligent restraints such as parental consent, guidance, and dedicated child modes on platforms to ensure safety without denying access.
  • Encouraging reporting of online abuse. Stringent restrictions could discourage children from reporting online threats or abuse, undermining their safety and the objectives of child protection laws.
  • No evidence for increased safety from bans. No jurisdiction has demonstrated that blanket restrictions make children safer online, making such measures ineffective substitutes for stakeholder efforts.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How should children’s internet access be regulated?

  • Age-appropriate safeguards. The Court supports safeguards tailored to age, including parental oversight and content moderation, rather than outright denial of access.
  • Dedicated child mode. It suggested social media platforms implement a dedicated child mode with features designed to protect children’s safety online.
  • Stakeholder responsibility. The Court highlighted the roles of intermediaries, law enforcement, the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, and the judiciary in protecting children online.

Back to basics: Fundamental Rights and Child Welfare

Fundamental rights under the Constitution protect individual freedoms such as speech and privacy. Article 21 guarantees the right to life and personal liberty, while Article 19(1)(a) protects freedom of speech and expression. Child welfare laws aim to protect children from harm while respecting their rights. Balancing these involves ensuring children’s safety online without denying their constitutional freedoms.
