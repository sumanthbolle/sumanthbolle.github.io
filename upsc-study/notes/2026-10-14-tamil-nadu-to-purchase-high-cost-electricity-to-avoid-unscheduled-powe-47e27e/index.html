Tamil Nadu Buys High-Cost Power to Prevent Unscheduled Cuts

Tamil Nadu is purchasing electricity at over ₹30 per unit for 16 days to manage a 30% rise in peak power demand caused by prolonged summer and El Niño. This decision highlights challenges in energy supply and infrastructure relevant for UPSC exams.

Tamil Nadu is buying expensive gas power temporarily to avoid power cuts amid increased demand and reduced local generation.

Why is this in the news?

On October 13, 2026, Tamil Nadu's Energy Resources Minister announced the purchase of costly electricity to manage peak demand and avoid unscheduled power cuts.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is Tamil Nadu purchasing high-cost electricity?

  • Demand surge. The peak power demand rose from 16,000 MW last year to 21,000 MW this year, a 30% increase mainly due to the El Niño effect causing prolonged summer.
  • Reduced local generation. The Neyveli Lignite Corporation's central generating station reduced output by nearly 1,850 MW, limiting supply from local sources.
  • Limited renewable output. Although Tamil Nadu has tied up for 3,500 MW of wind power, only 500 MW is currently being generated and supplied.
  • Temporary expensive purchase. To manage evening peak demand for six hours daily from October 15 to 31, Tamil Nadu is buying gas power at over ₹30 per unit.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What challenges does this situation reveal about energy management?

  • Energy shortage nationwide. The entire country is facing an energy shortage, affecting states like Tamil Nadu.
  • Dependence on costly sources. With limited availability of cheaper power sources, the state must rely on expensive gas power to meet demand.
  • Need for forecasting. The Minister suggested establishing a nationwide energy forecasting centre to provide alerts on power shortfalls.

Back to basics: Electricity Demand and Supply Management

Electricity demand varies daily and seasonally, requiring careful balancing with supply to avoid outages. States procure power from various sources including thermal, gas, and renewable energy. When local generation is insufficient, states may buy power from other sources, sometimes at higher costs, to maintain supply reliability. Managing peak demand, forecasting shortages, and integrating renewables are key challenges in power sector management.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about Tamil Nadu's recent electricity purchase decision: 1. Tamil Nadu is purchasing electricity at over ₹30 per unit due to a 30% increase in peak demand caused by the El Niño effect. 2. The state is relying on increased wind power generation to meet the peak demand during October 15 to 31. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because despite tying up 3,500 MW of wind power, only 500 MW is being generated, so the state is not relying on increased wind power to meet peak demand.
