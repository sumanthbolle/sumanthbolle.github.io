RBI Money Market Operations and VRR Auction Results Explained

The Reserve Bank of India released detailed data on money market operations and Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRR) auctions for early November 2026. Understanding these operations is essential for grasping RBI's liquidity management and monetary policy stance, important for both Prelims and Mains preparation.

RBI uses money market operations and VRR auctions to manage liquidity and influence short-term interest rates in the economy.

Why is this in the news?

RBI published official data on money market operations and VRR auction outcomes for November 6 to 8, 2026, highlighting liquidity conditions and interest rates.

What are RBI's money market operations and their recent trends?

  • Overnight segment volumes and rates. On November 6 and 7, 2026, overnight money market transactions included call money, triparty repo, market repo, and repo in corporate bonds with volumes ranging from ₹20,970.90 crore to ₹21,872.47 crore and weighted average rates around 5.5% to 5.57%.
  • Term segment activity. Term money transactions such as notice money and triparty repo showed significant volumes, for example, triparty repo in the term segment was ₹5,40,462.10 crore on November 6, 2026, with rates around 5.61%.
  • Liquidity adjustment facility (LAF) operations. RBI conducted repo operations injecting ₹18,133 crore on November 6, 2026, at a rate of 5.49%, and marginal standing facility (MSF) operations absorbing ₹6,873 crore at 5.75%, reflecting active liquidity management.
  • Net liquidity impact. Net liquidity injected or absorbed fluctuated, with RBI absorbing ₹2,13,730 crore on November 6 and ₹1,46,453 crore on November 7, 2026, indicating RBI's efforts to manage surplus liquidity.

What is the Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRR) auction and its recent results?

  • VRR auction purpose. The VRR auction is a tool used by RBI to absorb excess liquidity from banks by offering variable interest rates, helping to keep short-term rates within the policy corridor.
  • Recent auction volumes and rates. On November 6 to 8, 2026, VRR auctions absorbed large amounts of liquidity daily, for example, ₹2,01,856 crore on November 6 and ₹1,50,425 crore on November 8, at a fixed rate of 5.25%.
  • Impact on liquidity. These auctions contributed to net liquidity absorption, complementing other RBI operations to maintain monetary stability.

Why do RBI's money market operations and VRR auctions matter for the economy?

  • Liquidity management. By injecting or absorbing liquidity through repos, reverse repos, and VRR auctions, RBI controls money supply to ensure stable financial conditions.
  • Influence on interest rates. These operations help keep short-term interest rates close to the policy repo rate, influencing borrowing costs and inflation.
  • Monetary policy implementation. Money market operations are key instruments for RBI to implement its monetary policy stance effectively.

Back to basics: Monetary Policy Tools

Monetary policy tools include instruments like repo and reverse repo operations that central banks use to manage liquidity and influence interest rates. The repo rate is the rate at which RBI lends to banks, while the reverse repo rate is the rate at which RBI borrows from banks. Auctions like the Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRR) help absorb excess liquidity by offering banks an interest rate that varies based on demand, thus managing short-term money market rates.
