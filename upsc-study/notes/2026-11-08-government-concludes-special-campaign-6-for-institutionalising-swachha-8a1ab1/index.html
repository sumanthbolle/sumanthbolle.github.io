Government Concludes Special Campaign 6 to Institutionalise Swachhata and Reduce Pendency

The Department of Justice, Department of Revenue, and Department of Land Resources successfully completed Special Campaign 6 in October 2026, focusing on cleanliness, reducing case and grievance pendency, and improving administrative efficiency. This campaign highlights government efforts to enhance transparency, accountability, and resource management in public administration.

Special Campaign 6 was implemented across key government departments to institutionalise Swachhata, reduce pendency, and optimise office resource utilisation, reflecting ongoing governance reforms.

Why is this in the news?

The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG) guided the implementation of Special Campaign 6 during October 2026 to reduce pendency and institutionalise Swachhata in government offices.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau[3] Press Information Bureau

What was Special Campaign 6 and its objectives?

  • Campaign purpose. Special Campaign 6 aimed to institutionalise Swachhata (cleanliness), reduce pendency of cases and grievances, improve records management, optimise workspace utilisation, and enhance administrative efficiency in government offices.
  • Implementation period. The campaign was conducted from 2 October to 31 October 2026 under the guidance of the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau[3] Press Information Bureau

How did key departments participate and what were the outcomes?

  • Department of Justice. Identified and disposed of all pending matters including 211 public grievances, 309 grievance appeals, references from MPs and state governments, and parliamentary assurances; auctioned 49 e-waste items raising Rs.2,41,000 and freed approximately 300 sq. feet of office space.
  • Department of Revenue. Disposed of around 1836 public grievances, addressed 5 PMO references, weeded out or closed approximately 400 files, and generated revenue of about ₹6,00,000 through e-waste and scrap disposal; conducted cleanliness drives including lawns near India Gate.
  • Department of Land Resources. Achieved 100% cleanliness campaigns, disposed of all public grievances and appeals, addressed 21 MP references, reviewed 1449 physical files and 501 e-files for record management, and organised employee wellness activities such as yoga and mental health sessions.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau[3] Press Information Bureau

Why does Special Campaign 6 matter for governance and resource management?

  • Administrative reforms. The campaign promotes transparency, accountability, and citizen-centric governance by systematically clearing pendency, improving records, and optimising office spaces.
  • Swachhata institutionalisation. Regular cleanliness drives and beautification efforts in government offices contribute to a productive and healthy work environment.
  • Resource optimisation. Disposal of e-waste and redundant materials generates revenue and frees up office space, supporting efficient resource utilisation.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau[3] Press Information Bureau

How does this relate to land resource management and revenue mobilisation?

  • Land focus for resources. State governments are developing digital dashboards to monitor land details, layout status, and auction readiness to enhance coordination and transparency in land resource mobilisation.
  • Revenue targets. Officials are directed to prepare action plans and set weekly targets to mobilise revenue through land utilisation, regularisation, and auction in industrial layouts and estates.
  • Strategic land use. Focus on land pooling, availability for future needs, and prioritising locations with good connectivity aims to strengthen financial resources and meet revenue goals.

Back to basics: Administrative Reforms and Swachhata in Government Offices

Administrative reforms aim to improve efficiency, transparency, and accountability in government functioning by streamlining processes and reducing pendency. Swachhata refers to cleanliness and hygiene, which when institutionalised in government offices, creates a healthy and productive work environment. Efficient records management and workspace optimisation are key components of these reforms to ensure better service delivery and resource utilisation.
