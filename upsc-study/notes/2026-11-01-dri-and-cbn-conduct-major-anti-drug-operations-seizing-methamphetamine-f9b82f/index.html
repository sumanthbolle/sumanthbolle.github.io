DRI and CBN Conduct Major Anti-Drug Operations Seizing Methamphetamine and Poppy Straw

The Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI) seized 50 kg of methamphetamine in North-East India, arresting four persons, while the Central Bureau of Narcotics (CBN) Madhya Pradesh unit seized 2,774.650 kg of illicit poppy straw and arrested four persons. These operations highlight ongoing efforts to combat cross-border drug trafficking and illicit narcotics production in India.

DRI and CBN's recent seizures of methamphetamine and poppy straw demonstrate India's crackdown on cross-border drug trafficking and narcotics cultivation.

Why is this in the news?

In October 2026, the DRI and CBN conducted major anti-drug operations leading to large seizures of methamphetamine and poppy straw, with multiple arrests under the NDPS Act.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What did the DRI seize and how?

  • Methamphetamine seizures. DRI seized 50 kg of methamphetamine in two operations in Mizoram and Assam, comprising 30 kg of methamphetamine tablets concealed inside office furniture and 20 kg of crystal methamphetamine hidden in tea packets.
  • Arrests and vehicles seized. Four persons were arrested under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985, and three vehicles used for transporting the drugs were seized.
  • Source of drugs. Preliminary investigations indicate the drugs were smuggled from Myanmar, highlighting cross-border synthetic drug trafficking.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What were the CBN's operations and results?

  • Poppy straw seizures. CBN Madhya Pradesh Unit seized a total of 2,774.650 kg of illicit poppy straw in three operations, including 2,308 kg concealed under chana husk in a container truck, 416.560 kg in two vehicles, and 50.090 kg in a car.
  • Arrests and vehicle seizures. Four persons were arrested and five vehicles were seized under the NDPS Act, 1985 during these operations.
  • Operational details. The operations were intelligence-led and targeted transportation routes towards Punjab and within Madhya Pradesh, reflecting CBN's vigilance against illicit cultivation and trafficking.

[2] Press Information Bureau

How is the cross-border drug network being investigated?

  • Enforcement Directorate arrests. The Enforcement Directorate arrested four accused, including a Myanmar national, linked to a cross-border 'narco-hawala' network involved in methamphetamine trafficking.
  • Financial investigations. The ED uncovered financial records showing drug payments converted into Myanmar currency and disguised as ordinary goods, with transactions exceeding ₹175 crore routed through banking and hawala channels.
  • Multi-agency crackdown. Operation Trident involved raids across Assam, Mizoram, and Manipur, freezing 991 bank accounts and arresting 18 persons, illustrating coordinated efforts to dismantle narcotics networks.

Back to basics: Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985

The NDPS Act, 1985 is the primary legislation in India that prohibits the production, manufacture, possession, sale, purchase, transport, and consumption of narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances except for medical or scientific purposes. It empowers agencies like the DRI, CBN, and NCB to enforce anti-drug laws and conduct operations against illegal drug trafficking and cultivation.
