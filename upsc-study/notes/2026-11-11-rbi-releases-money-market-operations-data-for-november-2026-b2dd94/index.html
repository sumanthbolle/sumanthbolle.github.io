RBI Money Market Operations Data Released for November 2026

The Reserve Bank of India published detailed data on money market operations for November 9 and 10, 2026, showing volumes, rates, and liquidity injections. This data is crucial for understanding RBI's monetary policy stance and liquidity management, relevant for both Prelims and Mains preparation.

RBI's November 2026 money market operations data reveals liquidity conditions and interest rates reflecting its monetary policy implementation.

Why is this in the news?

What are the key components of RBI's money market operations?

  • Overnight segment. Includes call money, triparty repo, market repo, and repo in corporate bonds, with volumes and weighted average rates indicating short-term liquidity conditions.
  • Term segment. Covers notice money, term money, triparty repo, market repo, and repo in corporate bonds for longer tenors, showing medium-term liquidity trends.
  • Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) and related tools. RBI uses repo, reverse repo, Marginal Standing Facility (MSF), and Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) operations to inject or absorb liquidity daily.
  • Standing Liquidity Facility (SLF). A refinance facility availed by banks from RBI to manage liquidity mismatches.

[1] Reserve Bank of India[2] Reserve Bank of India

How did RBI manage liquidity on November 9 and 10, 2026?

  • Liquidity injection and absorption. On November 9, RBI's net liquidity injection was negative ₹1,48,423 crore, indicating liquidity absorption; on November 10, it was negative ₹1,99,902 crore, showing increased absorption.
  • Outstanding operations impact. Outstanding operations on November 10 showed a net liquidity injection of ₹59,908.87 crore, partially offsetting the day's absorption.
  • Cash reserve position. Scheduled commercial banks held cash balances with RBI of ₹8,47,567.50 crore on November 9 and ₹8,47,268.91 crore on November 10, above the average daily cash reserve requirement.

Back to basics: Monetary Policy and Liquidity Management

Monetary policy is the process by which a central bank controls money supply and interest rates to achieve economic objectives like controlling inflation and supporting growth. Liquidity management involves the central bank injecting or absorbing money in the banking system through tools like repo and reverse repo operations to maintain stability in short-term interest rates and ensure adequate cash flow in the economy.
