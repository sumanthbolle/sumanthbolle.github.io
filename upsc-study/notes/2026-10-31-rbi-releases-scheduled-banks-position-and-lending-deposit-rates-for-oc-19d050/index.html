RBI Releases Scheduled Banks’ Position and Lending-Deposit Rates for October 2026

The Reserve Bank of India published the Scheduled Banks’ Statement of Position as on October 15, 2026, and the lending and deposit rates data for September-October 2026. These data provide insights into the banking sector’s health and interest rate trends, which are crucial for understanding monetary policy and economic conditions.

RBI’s October 2026 data show stable weighted average lending rates and a slight decline in deposit rates among scheduled commercial banks.

Why is this in the news?

RBI released key data on scheduled banks’ financial position and interest rates on October 30, 2026, reflecting current banking sector trends.

[1] Reserve Bank of India[2] Reserve Bank of India

What is the Scheduled Banks’ Statement of Position?

  • Definition and scope. The Scheduled Banks’ Statement of Position details liabilities and assets of scheduled commercial banks, including regional rural banks, small finance banks, and primary cooperative banks, as on a specific date.
  • Key components. It covers deposits from banks and others, borrowings, cash balances, investments in government securities, and bank credit including loans and advances.
  • Recent figures. As on October 15, 2026, deposits from others (non-bank) stood at ₹28,436,892.20 crore, and bank credit was ₹23,254,182.56 crore.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What are the recent trends in lending and deposit rates?

  • Lending rates stability. The weighted average lending rate (WALR) on outstanding rupee loans remained unchanged at 8.96% in September 2026 compared to August 2026.
  • Fresh loans lending rate decline. The WALR on fresh rupee loans declined to 8.51% in September 2026 from 8.61% in August 2026, indicating easing borrowing costs for new loans.
  • Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate. The 1-year median MCLR of scheduled commercial banks declined to 8.55% in October 2026 from 8.61% in September 2026.
  • Deposit rates marginal decline. The weighted average domestic term deposit rate on outstanding term deposits marginally declined to 6.55% in September 2026 from 6.56% in August 2026.
  • Fresh term deposits rate fall. The rate on fresh rupee term deposits declined to 5.61% in September 2026 from 5.67% in August 2026.

[2] Reserve Bank of India

Why does this data matter for UPSC preparation?

  • Understanding banking sector health. The Statement of Position reveals the liquidity, credit growth, and deposit trends in the banking system, which are indicators of economic activity.
  • Monetary policy insights. Interest rate trends on lending and deposits reflect the transmission of RBI’s monetary policy stance to the economy.
  • Economic analysis for mains. These data help analyze credit availability, cost of funds, and savings patterns, relevant for questions on Indian economy and banking.

Back to basics: Scheduled Banks and Interest Rates

Scheduled banks are those included in the Second Schedule of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, and meet certain criteria set by the RBI. They include commercial banks and cooperative banks that comply with RBI regulations. Lending rates are the interest rates banks charge on loans, while deposit rates are the interest paid to depositors. The Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate (MCLR) is a benchmark lending rate that banks use to set interest rates on loans. These rates reflect monetary policy transmission and influence economic activity.

Mains question (10 marks)

Explain the significance of the Reserve Bank of India's data on scheduled banks’ position and lending-deposit rates for understanding monetary policy and banking sector health.

Explain: Explain how RBI's banking data reflect monetary policy transmission and banking sector conditions.

Intro: RBI’s data on scheduled banks’ position and interest rates provide key indicators of the banking sector’s health and monetary policy impact.

  • The Statement of Position shows deposits, borrowings, investments, and credit, reflecting liquidity and credit growth in the economy.
  • Lending and deposit rates indicate the cost of borrowing and return on savings, showing how monetary policy changes affect the economy.
  • Stable or declining lending rates on fresh loans suggest easing credit conditions, while deposit rate trends affect savings behavior.
  • These data help assess the effectiveness of monetary policy transmission and the banking sector’s role in economic development.

Conclusion: Thus, RBI’s banking data are vital for analyzing economic trends and formulating policy responses.
