DRI Intensifies Crackdown on Drug Trafficking and Gold Smuggling in India

The Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI) recently seized over 7 kg of amphetamine, 3 kg of cocaine, 13 kg of foreign-origin gold worth Rs. 19.41 crore, and more than 43 lakh foreign-origin smuggled cigarettes. These operations highlight India's efforts to curb narcotics trafficking, gold smuggling, and cross-border illegal trade, which are critical for internal security and economic regulation.

DRI's coordinated intelligence-led operations have disrupted major narcotics and gold smuggling networks, reinforcing India's internal security and customs enforcement.

Why is this in the news?

On 19 October 2026, the Ministry of Finance announced DRI's intensified crackdown on drug trafficking and gold smuggling through multiple seizures and arrests across India.

What recent narcotics seizures has the DRI made?

  • Amphetamine and cocaine seizures. DRI seized around 7.1 kg of amphetamine in two operations and 3.1 kg of cocaine in a separate operation at Bengaluru, near Nagpur, and New Delhi airports, leading to the arrest of three persons.
  • Intelligence-led operations. The seizures resulted from coordinated, intelligence-led interceptions targeting passengers carrying narcotics concealed ingeniously in baggage.
  • Legal framework. All narcotics were seized under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How did the DRI dismantle the gold smuggling syndicate?

  • Syndicate operations. DRI uncovered a gold smuggling network that smuggled 13.017 kg of foreign-origin gold worth Rs. 19.41 crore through Ahmedabad and Surat airports, involving passengers, handlers, illegal melting, and jewellery sale.
  • Concealment methods. Smugglers used thin rhodium-coated gold wires, gold embedded in trolley bag linings, and gold paste concealed inside bags to evade detection.
  • Investigation and arrests. Coordinated searches over 42 hours led to the seizure of gold and incriminating evidence, resulting in the arrest of three persons under the Customs Act, 1962.

[2] Press Information Bureau

What is the significance of the cigarette smuggling seizure in Mizoram?

  • Large-scale seizure. DRI seized 43.2 lakh sticks of foreign-origin cigarettes valued at Rs. 8.64 crore on the Champhai–Aizawl Road, suspected to be smuggled from Myanmar.
  • Cross-border smuggling route. The cigarettes were intercepted en route to Aizawl, highlighting the use of the Mizoram–Myanmar border for illicit tobacco product movement.
  • Legal action. The cigarettes and the transporting truck were seized under Section 110 of the Customs Act, 1962, and the driver was arrested.

[3] Press Information Bureau

Why do these DRI operations matter for internal security and economy?

  • Disrupting narcotics supply chains. DRI's seizures prevent the movement of dangerous narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances through air and road networks, enhancing internal security.
  • Combating organised smuggling. Dismantling gold smuggling syndicates and cigarette smuggling networks curbs illegal trade that affects economic regulation and revenue.
  • Strengthening border enforcement. Operations along international borders and airports improve surveillance and enforcement against cross-border smuggling.

Back to basics: Role of Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI)

The Directorate of Revenue Intelligence is India's premier agency responsible for combating smuggling and economic offences related to customs and narcotics. It conducts intelligence-led operations to detect, prevent, and investigate illicit trade and trafficking activities, enforcing laws such as the Customs Act and the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the recent DRI operations: 1. DRI seized over 7 kg of amphetamine and 3 kg of cocaine under the NDPS Act, 1985. 2. The gold smuggling syndicate was dismantled with seizures at Ahmedabad and Surat airports, and arrests were made under the Customs Act, 1962. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. Both 1 and 2
  2. 1 only
  3. 2 only
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). A trap could be confusing the legal provisions under which the arrests were made; narcotics seizures are under the NDPS Act, while gold smuggling arrests are under the Customs Act.

Conclusion: DRI's focused enforcement plays a crucial role in safeguarding India's borders and economy from illicit trade and trafficking.
