Odisha Files Contempt Petition in Supreme Court Over Kotia Panchayat Election Dispute

Odisha challenged Andhra Pradesh's notification for elections in the disputed Kotia villages by filing a contempt petition in the Supreme Court. This case highlights constitutional and federal issues related to election jurisdiction and enforcement of judicial orders.

Odisha's contempt petition against Andhra Pradesh in the Supreme Court concerns the election in the disputed Kotia villages, raising questions on federal jurisdiction and judicial compliance.

Why is this in the news?

On October 23, 2026, Odisha filed a contempt of court petition in the Supreme Court challenging Andhra Pradesh's notification for elections in the Kotia group of villages, a territory disputed since 1955.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Kotia territorial dispute about?

  • Disputed villages. The Kotia group consists of 21 villages under Pottangi Tahasil in Odisha's Koraput district, whose jurisdiction has been disputed by Odisha and Andhra Pradesh since 1955.
  • Historical control. Odisha exercised administrative control over these villages from 1936, but Andhra Pradesh claimed revenue collection rights since 1947, leading to conflicting claims.
  • Boundary demarcation dispute. In 1959, Andhra Pradesh cited a 1942 boundary demarcation report by M. Gilby, claiming Odisha's consent, which Odisha rejected as inaccurate.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How has the dispute been legally addressed?

  • Supreme Court involvement. Odisha moved the Supreme Court under Article 131 in 1968, but the Court in 2006 held it lacked jurisdiction due to a proviso in Article 131 and directed maintaining the status quo.
  • Subsequent petitions. Odisha's review and curative petitions in 2006 and 2007 were dismissed, closing judicial scrutiny through those proceedings.
  • Recent legal actions. Odisha filed contempt petitions in 2021 and 2026 alleging Andhra Pradesh violated the Supreme Court's status quo order by notifying elections in the disputed villages.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the Kotia election dispute matter for federalism and elections?

  • Election notification controversy. Andhra Pradesh's notification for Mandal Parishad and Zilla Parishad elections in Kotia triggered protests and accusations of undermining Odisha's territorial integrity.
  • Enforcement of judicial orders. The contempt petition alleges Andhra Pradesh violated the Supreme Court's 2006 order to maintain the status quo, raising issues of judicial compliance.
  • Political and administrative impact. Both states are investing in infrastructure and welfare schemes to win over the Kotia residents, reflecting the dispute's socio-political significance.

Back to basics: Federalism and Election Jurisdiction

India's federal structure divides powers between the Union and States, including jurisdiction over elections. Disputes over territorial control affect which state can conduct elections in a region. The Supreme Court can adjudicate disputes between states under Article 131, but some limitations exist. Maintaining status quo orders prevents unilateral actions until disputes are resolved.
