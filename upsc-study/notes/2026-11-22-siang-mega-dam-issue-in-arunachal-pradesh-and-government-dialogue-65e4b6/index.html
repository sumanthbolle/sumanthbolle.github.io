Siang Mega Dam Controversy and Government Dialogue in Arunachal Pradesh

The Siang Upper Multipurpose Project, a large hydropower dam in Arunachal Pradesh, faces opposition from local Adi tribal bodies demanding halt and dialogue. The government assures development with respect for local culture and ongoing talks to find a way forward.

The Siang Upper Multipurpose Project in Arunachal Pradesh is a national priority but faces local opposition, prompting government dialogue to balance development with tribal rights and environmental concerns.

Why is this in the news?

On November 21, 2026, Union Home Minister Amit Shah addressed the Siang Upper Multipurpose Project issue amid protests by Adi tribal bodies, emphasizing dialogue and rehabilitation while highlighting the project's national importance.

[1] Press Information Bureau[4] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Siang Upper Multipurpose Project and why is it important?

  • Project details. The Siang Upper Multipurpose Project (SUMP) is a proposed 11,200 MW hydropower dam in Arunachal Pradesh, considered India's biggest dam and a project of national importance for energy security and strategic interests.
  • Government perspective. The government views SUMP as crucial for Arunachal Pradesh's development, state revenue, and India's energy security, promising to respect local culture and offer an attractive rehabilitation package.

[1] Press Information Bureau[4] The Hindu · Secondary

Why are local Adi tribal bodies opposing the dam?

  • Opposition reasons. The Adi Ba:né Kébang United and Siang Indigenous Farmers’ Forum oppose the dam due to lack of proper consultation, transparency, and rehabilitation plans, fearing submersion of settlements and loss of farmland and livelihoods.
  • Historical context. The 45-year-old dam plan was rejected twice before being revived in 2024, and locals allege intimidation and misuse of laws against protestors, demanding a halt to pre-feasibility work and withdrawal of security forces.
  • Conditional acceptance. The Adi bodies are not against development but prefer smaller dams on tributaries, which they revere as sacred.

[4] The Hindu · Secondary

How is the government addressing the concerns and what dialogue is underway?

  • Dialogue approach. Union Home Minister Amit Shah emphasized positive and constructive discussions with locals, assuring that traditions, culture, and religion will remain unharmed and that people will be taken into confidence at every stage.
  • Rehabilitation and respect. The government promises the most attractive rehabilitation package in India and urges local people to engage with the state government and Ministry of Home Affairs for talks.

[1] Press Information Bureau[4] The Hindu · Secondary

What other development initiatives are underway in Arunachal Pradesh?

  • Cooperative and infrastructure projects. Union Home Minister Amit Shah announced launch of development schemes worth ₹5,610.95 crore, including education, power, rural roads, and 199 projects under the Vibrant Villages Programme across 11 border districts.
  • Cooperative sector strengthening. Initiatives include unveiling ‘Arunachal Pradesh Cooperative Vision 2047’, launching an e-commerce webpage for rural products, inaugurating a foodgrain storage warehouse, and promoting women Self-Help Groups.

[3] Press Information Bureau

How does the Siang dam issue relate to national security and drug trafficking concerns?

  • Strategic location. Arunachal Pradesh shares a 1,863 km international border, including 1,226 km with China, making its security vital; the state is at the gateway to the Death Triangle, a narcotics trafficking route.
  • Drug control efforts. The government is committed to making Arunachal Pradesh drug-free, with successful operations like Operation Monsoon Thunder jointly with the US DEA to curb cross-border drug trafficking.

Back to basics: Hydropower Projects and Tribal Rights

Hydropower projects generate electricity by harnessing river water flow, often requiring large dams and reservoirs. Such projects can impact local ecosystems and displace indigenous communities, raising concerns about environmental sustainability and tribal rights. Proper consultation, rehabilitation, and respect for local culture are essential to balance development with social justice.

What happens next?

  • Inclusive dialogue. The government must continue constructive dialogue with local communities to address their concerns and ensure their participation in decision-making regarding the Siang dam project.
  • Transparent rehabilitation. Offering an attractive and transparent rehabilitation package that respects traditions and livelihoods is crucial for project acceptance.
  • Environmental and social impact assessment. Comprehensive impact assessments and clear communication of findings can help build trust and mitigate opposition.
