India’s Advances in Next-Generation Space Technologies

India is developing new space technologies including reusable launch vehicles, electric and green propulsion, and autonomous spacecraft docking. These innovations enhance self-reliance and support national security, communication, and scientific exploration, relevant for UPSC Science and Technology syllabus.

India’s space programme is advancing with next-generation launch vehicles, indigenous propulsion, and in-orbit servicing to strengthen self-reliance and national capabilities.

Why is this in the news?

The Press Information Bureau released a detailed backgrounder on 28 October 2026 outlining India’s new-age space technologies and their strategic importance.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are India’s new-generation launch vehicle developments?

  • Next Generation Launch Vehicle (NGLV). The Union Cabinet approved the NGLV development in September 2024 with an outlay of ₹8,240 crore, aiming for a 30-tonne payload to Low Earth Orbit, tripling current capacity.
  • Reusable launch vehicle technology. ISRO is developing partially reusable launch vehicles to reduce costs, demonstrated by autonomous runway landing experiments completed by June 2024.
  • Traditional launch vehicles. ISRO’s existing vehicles include PSLV, GSLV with indigenous cryogenic upper stage, LVM3 for heavy payloads, and SSLV for small satellites.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How is India advancing propulsion and space hardware technologies?

  • Electric propulsion. ISRO completed a 1,000-hour life test of an indigenous 300 mN stationary plasma thruster in March 2025, offering six times the efficiency of conventional systems.
  • Green propulsion. Development of environmentally friendly propellants like ISROSENE kerosene and green monopropellant thrusters by Bellatrix Aerospace supports sustainable space missions.
  • Radioisotope power collaboration. ISRO and the Department of Atomic Energy collaborate on Radioisotope Heater Units and Thermoelectric Generators for future lunar and Mars missions.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the key achievements in in-space operations and navigation?

  • Autonomous spacecraft docking. India became the fourth country to demonstrate autonomous docking and undocking of spacecraft in orbit through the SpaDeX mission in 2025.
  • NavIC navigation system. India’s indigenous regional satellite navigation system provides positioning and timing over India and 1,500 km beyond, supporting civilian and strategic uses.
  • Communication satellites. Satellites like GSAT-24, GSAT-N2, and CMS-03 enhance broadcasting, broadband connectivity, and secure naval communications.

Back to basics: Space Technology and Indigenisation

Space technology involves the development and use of satellites, launch vehicles, and related systems for communication, navigation, earth observation, and scientific exploration. Indigenisation means developing these technologies within the country to reduce dependence on foreign suppliers, enhance self-reliance, and build national capabilities in space science and technology.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about India’s space technology advancements: 1. The Next Generation Launch Vehicle (NGLV) aims for a payload capacity of 30 tonnes to Low Earth Orbit. 2. India became the third country to demonstrate autonomous spacecraft docking in orbit. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because India became the fourth country, not the third, to demonstrate autonomous spacecraft docking.

Mains question (15 marks)

Examine how India’s advancements in space technology contribute to national development and self-reliance.

Examine: Analyze the role of new space technologies in India's development and self-reliance goals.

Intro: India’s space programme is evolving with new technologies that enhance national capabilities and self-reliance.

  • Discuss the development of next-generation launch vehicles including reusable systems and their impact on cost and capacity.
  • Explain advancements in indigenous propulsion technologies like electric and green propulsion and their environmental and operational benefits.
  • Describe achievements in in-space operations such as autonomous docking and their significance for future missions.
  • Highlight the role of satellite navigation and communication systems in supporting civilian and strategic sectors.

Conclusion: These technological advancements position India as a self-reliant space power contributing to national development.
