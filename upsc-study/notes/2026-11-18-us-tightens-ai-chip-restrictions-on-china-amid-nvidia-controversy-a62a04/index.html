US Tightens AI Chip Export Controls Targeting China Amid Nvidia Controversy

The US Commerce Department announced new rules restricting Chinese firms' remote access to advanced AI chips via overseas data centres, aiming to close loopholes in export controls. This development is crucial for understanding global technology governance, US-China relations, and implications for India's tech policy.

The US has imposed stricter controls on AI chip access by Chinese companies through foreign data centres to prevent circumvention of export restrictions amid a scandal involving Nvidia.

Why is this in the news?

The US Commerce Department announced new export control rules on November 16, 2026, restricting Chinese companies' remote access to advanced AI chips through overseas data centres following scrutiny of Nvidia and related export control violations.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the new US AI chip export restrictions?

  • Scope of restrictions. The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule requiring government authorization for overseas data centres providing significant computing capacity to Chinese companies or their parent organisations.
  • Threshold and coverage. The rule sets a threshold of more than 12 million aggregate Total Processing Performance (TPP) for restricted remote access and covers computing capacity distributed across interconnected data centres.
  • Additional licensing requirements. Exports of advanced semiconductors to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand, and Brazil now require additional licenses due to higher risk of chip diversion.
  • Exemptions. Commercial recommendation systems used by e-commerce and social media companies are exempt from these restrictions.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why did the US tighten these restrictions now?

  • Loophole in export controls. Chinese companies have circumvented direct shipment bans by renting servers in third countries like Malaysia and Singapore to access advanced American AI chips.
  • Nvidia and Megaspeed controversy. The resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang followed revelations about his relationship with Alice Huang, linked to Singapore-based Megaspeed, which is under investigation for possible export control violations.
  • Concerns over military use. The US fears that advanced AI chips could enhance China's military and intelligence capabilities, including cyber-espionage and weapons development.
  • Evidence of chip diversion. Research suggests Chinese AI models may have used Nvidia's export-restricted Blackwell chips, indicating export controls have not fully prevented access.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary[2] The Hindu · Secondary

How has China responded to the US restrictions?

  • Accusations of economic coercion. China's Foreign Ministry accused the US of abusing export controls and overextending national security claims to interfere with normal trade and technology cooperation.
  • Impact on global supply chains. China's Ministry of Commerce stated the US actions undermine market rules, disrupt global semiconductor supply chains, and affect industry stability.
  • Commitment to safeguard interests. China vowed to protect its legitimate rights and interests in response to the US measures.

Back to basics: Export Controls and Technology Transfer

Export controls are government-imposed restrictions on the shipment of sensitive technologies to other countries to protect national security and economic interests. They often target advanced technologies that could be used for military or intelligence purposes. Technology transfer can occur directly via shipments or indirectly through services like remote computing, which export controls seek to regulate.
