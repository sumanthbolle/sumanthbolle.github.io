Indian Navy Commissions Indigenous Anti-Submarine and Multi-Purpose Warships

The Indian Navy commissioned INS Mangrol, an indigenous Anti-Submarine Warfare Shallow Water Craft, on 12 October 2026 and is set to commission INS Samarthak, an indigenous Multi-Purpose Warship, on 16 October 2026. These developments highlight India's progress in defence self-reliance and naval capabilities, important for maritime security and defence production topics in UPSC.

The Indian Navy's commissioning of INS Mangrol and INS Samarthak reflects India's growing indigenous warship design and construction capabilities under the Make in India initiative.

What are the features of INS Mangrol and its significance?

  • Ship details. INS Mangrol is the third Mahe-class Anti-Submarine Warfare Shallow Water Craft, designed and built indigenously by Cochin Shipyard Limited with over 80 percent indigenous content.
  • Operational role. It is designed for operations in coastal and shallow waters, capable of Anti-Submarine Warfare, underwater surveillance, Low-Intensity Maritime Operations, and mine warfare-related tasks.
  • Technological features. Equipped with advanced sensors, sonar, weapon systems, and powered by three diesel engine-driven waterjet propulsion units, INS Mangrol offers high manoeuvrability and rapid response.
  • Strategic importance. The ship enhances India's maritime domain awareness and anti-submarine warfare capabilities, contributing to coastal defence and regional stability.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the features of INS Samarthak and its role?

  • Ship details. INS Samarthak is the first of four indigenous Multi-Purpose Vessels built by Larsen and Toubro Ltd under the Make in India initiative.
  • Operational capabilities. Primarily designed for maritime surveillance and patrol, it can also undertake Low Intensity Maritime Operations, target deployment, act as an unmanned/autonomous system mothership, and support humanitarian assistance and disaster relief.
  • Additional functionalities. It can be converted into a limited hospital ship and assist in salvage operations, making it a versatile utility ship for strategic operations.
  • Technological integration. INS Samarthak features an integrated combat suite with indigenous sensors, weapons, and electronic systems optimized for maritime environments.

[2] Press Information Bureau

Why do these indigenous warships matter for India’s defence and security?

  • Self-reliance in defence. Both ships reflect India’s commitment to Aatmanirbhar Bharat and Make in India, showcasing growing indigenous capabilities in warship design, construction, and systems integration.
  • Strengthening maritime security. They enhance India's ability to safeguard coastal approaches, improve maritime domain awareness, and counter underwater and maritime threats in the Indian Ocean Region.
  • Boost to defence industries. The projects highlight contributions from Indian defence industries and MSMEs, strengthening the domestic defence manufacturing ecosystem.
  • Strategic and operational preparedness. The induction of these vessels supports the Indian Navy’s operational readiness and technological advancement in a challenging maritime security environment.

Back to basics: Make in India and Defence Indigenisation

Make in India is a government initiative to encourage domestic manufacturing, including defence production, to reduce reliance on imports. Defence indigenisation involves designing, developing, and producing military equipment within the country, enhancing self-reliance and strategic autonomy. Indigenous warship construction strengthens maritime security by enabling tailored capabilities suited to India's strategic needs.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about INS Mangrol and INS Samarthak: 1. INS Mangrol is an Anti-Submarine Warfare Shallow Water Craft built by Cochin Shipyard Limited with over 80 percent indigenous content. 2. INS Samarthak is the first of four Multi-Purpose Vessels built by Larsen and Toubro Ltd and is designed primarily for maritime surveillance and patrol. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). A trap would be confusing the builder or the primary role of either ship; INS Mangrol is built by Cochin Shipyard Limited, not Larsen and Toubro, and INS Samarthak is primarily for maritime surveillance and patrol.
