RBI Report Highlights Finances of Panchayati Raj Institutions

The Reserve Bank of India released its second biennial report on the finances of Panchayati Raj Institutions, focusing on local finances and capacity building in rural India. This report is important for understanding fiscal decentralisation and rural governance finance in India.

The RBI report assesses Gram Panchayat finances, showing dependence on intergovernmental transfers and expenditure aligned with national schemes, highlighting challenges in fiscal autonomy and capacity building.

Why is this in the news?

The Reserve Bank of India released the second edition of its biennial Report titled 'Finances of Panchayati Raj Institutions' on November 19, 2026.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What does the RBI report on Panchayati Raj finances reveal?

  • Report scope. The report draws upon a balanced panel of 2.25 lakh Gram Panchayats covering 2022-23 to 2024-25 to assess their finances and role in rural socio-economic development.
  • Revenue sources. Gram Panchayats remain heavily dependent on intergovernmental transfers, which constitute about 90% of their total receipts, while own source revenues account for about 10%, mainly from non-tax sources like interest receipts, programme funds, and user charges.
  • Expenditure focus. Expenditure primarily reflects mandates of the Fifteenth Finance Commission and convergence with national schemes, with water supply and sanitation accounting for about 40% of total expenditure.
  • Progress and disparities. There has been notable progress in rural connectivity, electrification, water supply, sanitation, and community assets, but inter-State disparities persist in access, quality, and functionality.
  • Challenges in transition. As some large Gram Panchayats transition to small urban local bodies, their functional responsibilities may expand faster than their fiscal and administrative preparedness, requiring transitional fiscal support and capacity building.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Why does this matter for UPSC preparation?

  • Fiscal decentralisation. The report provides insights into fiscal decentralisation at the grassroots level, highlighting the dependence on transfers and the need to strengthen own source revenues.
  • Rural governance. Understanding the financial status and challenges of Panchayati Raj Institutions is key to evaluating rural governance and development schemes.
  • Link to Finance Commission. Expenditure patterns reflect the Fifteenth Finance Commission’s mandates, linking local governance finance with broader fiscal federalism.

Back to basics: Fiscal decentralisation and Panchayati Raj Institutions

Fiscal decentralisation refers to the process of transferring financial powers and resources from the central or state governments to local bodies like Panchayats. Panchayati Raj Institutions are local self-government bodies in rural areas responsible for planning and implementing development programs. Their financial health depends on both intergovernmental transfers and own source revenues, which determine their capacity to deliver services.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the finances of Gram Panchayats as per the RBI report: 1. About 90% of their total receipts come from intergovernmental transfers. 2. Water supply and sanitation account for about 40% of their total expenditure. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. Both 1 and 2
  2. 1 only
  3. 2 only
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). The trap is confusing the share of own source revenues with intergovernmental transfers; the report states that intergovernmental transfers are about 90%, not own source revenues.

Mains question (15 marks)

Discuss the challenges and significance of fiscal decentralisation in strengthening Panchayati Raj Institutions in India.

Discuss: Explain the financial challenges faced by Panchayati Raj Institutions and their implications for rural governance.

Intro: Fiscal decentralisation empowers local bodies but faces challenges in India’s Panchayati Raj Institutions.

  • Dependence on intergovernmental transfers limits financial autonomy and flexibility.
  • Own source revenues are low, indicating weak local revenue generation mechanisms.
  • Expenditure priorities align with national schemes but may not address local needs fully.
  • Capacity building and transitional fiscal support are needed as Panchayats take on expanded roles.

Conclusion: Strengthening fiscal decentralisation is essential for effective rural governance and sustainable development.
