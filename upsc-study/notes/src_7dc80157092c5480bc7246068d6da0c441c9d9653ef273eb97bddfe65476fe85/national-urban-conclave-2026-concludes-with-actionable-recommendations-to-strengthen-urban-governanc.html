India implications

  • Urbanisation will require stronger coordination among Union, State and local governments because city outcomes depend on linked decisions concerning land use, transport, housing, water and waste.
  • Empowered smaller cities and stronger municipal own-source revenues can support more balanced regional growth and reduce excessive dependence on state capitals.
  • Affordable housing near employment centres and public transport can improve access to livelihoods, but implementation must prevent displacement and exclusion.
  • Scientific remediation of legacy waste and prevention of fresh accumulation can support public health, land recovery and resource efficiency.

Way forward

  • Clarify functions and strengthen devolution of powers, finances and functionaries to urban local bodies, with accountable city leadership.
  • Shift monitoring from expenditure and asset completion to measurable service-quality outcomes, maintenance and citizen experience.
  • Integrate land-use, housing, transport, pedestrian infrastructure, water, waste and climate resilience in city planning.
  • Build municipal capacity in planning, finance, procurement, data use and project management, with special support for smaller and weaker cities.
