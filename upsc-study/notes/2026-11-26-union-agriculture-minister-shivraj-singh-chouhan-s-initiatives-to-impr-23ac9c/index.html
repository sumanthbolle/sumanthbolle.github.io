Shivraj Singh Chouhan's Initiatives to Boost Soil Health and Farmers' Income

Union Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan visited Punjab farms to promote sustainable farming practices and urged implementation of key schemes like Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana. He also held a high-level meeting in Madhya Pradesh approving rural infrastructure projects and scientific agricultural plans to enhance productivity and farmers' welfare.

Shivraj Singh Chouhan emphasized soil health, stubble management, crop insurance, and rural infrastructure to improve farmers' income and agricultural sustainability.

Why is this in the news?

On 24-25 November 2026, Union Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan visited Punjab and held a meeting in Madhya Pradesh to promote soil health, stubble management, crop insurance, and rural infrastructure development.

[1] Press Information Bureau[3] Press Information Bureau

What initiatives did Shivraj Singh Chouhan promote in Punjab to improve soil health?

  • Stubble management without burning. Farmers in Booh Haveliyaan village have not burned stubble for six years, adopting Happy Seeder and Direct Seeding, which the Minister praised as a new direction for sustainable farming.
  • Concerns over soil fertility. Excessive use of chemical fertilizers, pesticides, and stubble burning is deteriorating soil health, killing beneficial insects and earthworms, risking future agricultural productivity.
  • Call for Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana. The Minister expressed disappointment over Punjab's reluctance to implement the crop insurance scheme, emphasizing its importance for compensating flood and disaster losses.
  • Agricultural machinery funding. The Centre provides approximately ₹286 crore for Crop Residue Management machinery, but the state government must ensure quality checks and repair facilities for farmers.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What rural development and agricultural plans were approved for Madhya Pradesh?

  • Infrastructure funding. The Centre approved ₹643.14 crore for constructing 223 bridges and discussed road construction proposals worth about ₹600 crore to improve rural connectivity.
  • Scientific Agricultural Roadmap. A dedicated roadmap will be developed with agricultural scientists to enhance productivity and sustainability in Madhya Pradesh.
  • Agriculture Clusters and Centres of Excellence. Two Agriculture Clusters and two Centres of Excellence will be established to promote modern farming and value addition.
  • Clean Plant Programme and irrigation support. Specialized nurseries will be developed under the Clean Plant Programme, and extensive assistance will be provided under the Per Drop More Crop scheme to support micro-irrigation.

[3] Press Information Bureau

How does Shivraj Singh Chouhan link these initiatives to farmers' welfare?

  • Commitment to pro-farmer policies. He reiterated the Modi government's commitment to record procurement at Minimum Support Price (MSP) for paddy, wheat, pulses, and cotton.
  • Integrated farming approach. He emphasized increasing production, reducing costs, and adopting integrated farming including crop diversification, animal husbandry, fisheries, and poultry.
  • Serving farmers as a value. Chouhan described serving farmers as equivalent to worship, inspired by Guru Nanak Dev ji's message of humility and service.

Back to basics: Minimum Support Price (MSP) and Crop Insurance

Minimum Support Price is a government-guaranteed price for certain crops to protect farmers from price fluctuations. Crop insurance schemes like Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana compensate farmers for losses due to natural disasters, helping stabilize their income and encourage investment in agriculture.

What happens next?

  • State implementation of crop insurance. Punjab government needs to implement the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana to ensure farmers receive compensation for flood and disaster losses.
  • Quality assurance for agricultural machinery. State governments must ensure quality checks and repair arrangements for machinery provided under central subsidies to benefit farmers effectively.
