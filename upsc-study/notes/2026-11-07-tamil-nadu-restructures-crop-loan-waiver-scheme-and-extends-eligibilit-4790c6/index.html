Tamil Nadu Restructures Crop Loan Waiver Scheme and Extends Eligibility

Tamil Nadu government revised its crop loan waiver scheme by increasing the waiver limit and extending the eligibility period by a month. This change is important for understanding state welfare measures addressing agrarian distress and their fiscal implications.

Tamil Nadu expanded its crop loan waiver scheme by raising waiver limits and extending the loan eligibility period to support more farmers.

Why is this in the news?

On November 7, 2026, Tamil Nadu government announced restructuring of its crop loan waiver scheme and extended the eligibility period by a month.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What changes were made to the crop loan waiver scheme?

  • Waiver limit increased. Farmers with loans exceeding ₹75,000 from cooperative institutions now receive a flat waiver of ₹75,000, whereas previously those with loans between ₹75,000 and ₹1 lakh got a maximum waiver of ₹75,000 and those above ₹1 lakh got ₹35,000.
  • Full waiver for smaller loans. Farmers who obtained crop loans up to ₹75,000 continue to receive a full waiver of the loan amount.
  • Eligibility period extended. The scheme now covers loans taken by farmers up to March 31, 2026, extended from the earlier cutoff of February 28, 2026.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Who benefits from the revised scheme and what is the fiscal impact?

  • Number of beneficiaries. Under the revised scheme, 5,90,621 farmers with loans up to ₹75,000 will get full waiver, and 7,34,260 farmers with loans above ₹75,000 will get ₹75,000 waiver.
  • Additional beneficiaries. The scheme also extends benefits to 10,810 farmers who had fully repaid their loans before the waiver announcement.
  • Financial sanction and expenditure. An additional ₹2,842.77 crore was sanctioned, making the total estimated expenditure ₹9,062.83 crore, of which ₹6,220.056 crore has already been released.
  • Interest reimbursements. The revised order includes provisions for various interest-related reimbursements to eligible farmers.

Why does this matter for UPSC preparation?

  • State welfare schemes. The restructuring illustrates how state governments design and modify welfare schemes to address agrarian distress and farmer indebtedness.
  • Fiscal federalism. The scheme’s financial implications highlight the role of states in resource mobilisation and expenditure within the federal structure.
  • Agriculture sector policy. Understanding such schemes helps in analyzing government interventions in agriculture credit and rural economy.

Back to basics: Crop Loan Waiver Schemes

Crop loan waiver schemes are government initiatives that forgive farmers' outstanding agricultural loans to relieve financial distress. These schemes aim to reduce the burden of debt on farmers, often due to crop failure or low income. They are typically implemented by state governments and involve budgetary allocations to cover the waived amounts.
