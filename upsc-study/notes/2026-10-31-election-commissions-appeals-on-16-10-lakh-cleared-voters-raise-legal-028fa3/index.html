Legal Questions Over Election Commission Appeals on 16.10 Lakh Voters

The Election Commission of India filed appeals against the inclusion of 16.10 lakh voters in West Bengal whose names had been cleared by judicial officers during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls. This raises constitutional and legal issues about the authority of officials to file such appeals under the Representation of the People Act.

The Election Commission's appeals against 16.10 lakh judicially cleared voters in West Bengal question the legal authority of officials under the Representation of the People Act.

Why is this in the news?

On October 26, 2026, the Election Commission of India filed appeals challenging the inclusion of 16.10 lakh voters in West Bengal, raising questions about the authority of officials who filed these appeals under the Representation of the People Act.

What triggered the Election Commission's appeals against 16.10 lakh voters?

  • Judicial clearance of voters. During the West Bengal Special Intensive Revision (SIR), judicial officers cleared 16.10 lakh voters whose names had earlier been flagged for logical discrepancies.
  • Appeals filed by officials. The Election Commission filed appeals against the inclusion of these voters, but the officials who filed these appeals may not have had the legal authority under the Representation of the People Act.
  • Opposition response. Opposition parties in West Bengal demanded scrapping of these appeals and called for a judicial probe into the matter.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Who has the authority to manage electoral rolls and file appeals?

  • Authorized officials under the Act. The Representation of the People Act recognizes the Chief Electoral Officer (CEO), District Electoral Officer (DEO), Electoral Registration Officer (ERO), and Assistant Electoral Registration Officer (AERO) as the authorities responsible for electoral rolls.
  • Dispute over appellants' authority. The appeals were filed by 'officers of the office of CEO West Bengal' but it is unclear if these were the authorized officials since the Act does not mention any appellate tribunals created by the Supreme Court.
  • Supreme Court's role. The Supreme Court formed Appellate Tribunals during its supervision of the SIR to adjudicate disputed cases, but these tribunals are not recognized authorities under the Act.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the background and scale of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR)?

  • Scope of SIR. The SIR involved reviewing roughly 60 lakh 'doubtful and pending' voter cases in West Bengal.
  • Judicial officers' decisions. Judicial officers cleared 32,77,360 voters and rejected 27.23 lakh, of which 22 lakh appealed to the Appellate Tribunals.
  • Bulk appeals and pending cases. Alongside individual appeals, 16,10,311 bulk appeals challenged the inclusion of voters restored by judicial officers, resulting in over 38 lakh cases pending in the tribunals.
  • Election outcome. West Bengal recorded a historic voter turnout of 92.47% in the Assembly elections held after the SIR process.

Back to basics: Representation of the People Act and Electoral Roll Management

The Representation of the People Act governs the preparation and revision of electoral rolls in India. It designates specific officials such as the Chief Electoral Officer and Electoral Registration Officers as authorities responsible for maintaining accurate voter lists. Electoral rolls are periodically revised to add eligible voters and remove ineligible or duplicate entries, ensuring free and fair elections. The Act does not recognize judicial or tribunal bodies as authorities for electoral roll decisions unless specifically empowered.
