India's Climate Diplomacy and Initiatives at COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye

At COP31 in Antalya, India emphasized a country-driven just transition mechanism, highlighted big cat conservation for climate mitigation, and launched LeadIT 3.0 for industrial decarbonisation. These initiatives showcase India's approach to climate action and international cooperation, relevant for UPSC aspirants studying environment and international relations.

India at COP31 advocated practical, country-specific climate solutions, combining conservation, industrial transition, and partnerships with vulnerable nations.

Why is this in the news?

India's Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav presented key climate initiatives and partnerships at the UNFCCC COP31 held in Antalya, Türkiye in November 2026.

What climate proposals did India present at COP31?

  • Just Transition Mechanism. India called for a two-tier, party-guided just transition mechanism that respects national sovereignty and diverse circumstances, focusing on employment, social protection, adaptation, poverty eradication, and energy access.
  • LeadIT 3.0 Launch. India and Sweden launched LeadIT 3.0 to accelerate low-carbon industrial transition from 2027 to 2030, focusing on scaling projects, mobilising capital, and delivering tangible outcomes in steel, cement, and transport sectors.
  • Climate Engagement with Small Island Nations. India offered scientific and technological expertise to small island developing states for renewable energy, disaster preparedness, resilient infrastructure, and climate adaptation, building on existing programmes like IRIS under CDRI.

[1] Press Information Bureau[3] Press Information Bureau[4] The Indian Express · Secondary

How does India link wildlife conservation to climate change mitigation?

  • Big Cat Conservation Role. India highlighted big cat conservation as a pathway to protect carbon-rich ecosystems, enhance climate resilience, and support community livelihoods, showcased by its tiger conservation model.
  • Tiger Reserves and Ecosystem Services. India's 58 Tiger Reserves covering about 85,000 sq. km protect forests that provide carbon sequestration, water purification, soil conservation, and groundwater recharge, benefiting over 600 rivers.
  • Governance and Community Participation. Project Tiger's success is attributed to political commitment, legislation, institutional mechanisms, and community involvement, generating about 6 million man-days of green employment annually.

[2] Press Information Bureau

Why are India's climate actions significant for global cooperation?

  • Respect for National Priorities. India stresses that climate mechanisms should avoid uniform obligations and enable countries to pursue just transition pathways aligned with their national priorities.
  • South-South Cooperation. LeadIT's expansion to 20 countries, including Brazil joining as a member, strengthens the Global South's voice and opportunities for cooperation in industrial decarbonisation.
  • Inclusive and Practical Implementation. India advocates moving from dialogue to practical, country-driven implementation of climate actions that create opportunities for workers, communities, and developing economies.

Back to basics: Just Transition and Nature-based Climate Solutions

Just transition means shifting to a low-carbon economy in a way that is fair and inclusive, addressing employment, social protection, and energy access. Nature-based solutions use ecosystem conservation and restoration to mitigate climate change by protecting carbon sinks like forests and wetlands, while supporting biodiversity and livelihoods.

What happens next?

  • Operationalising Just Transition. India supports moving from dialogue to practical implementation of just transition mechanisms that are country-driven and respect national sovereignty, requiring adequate finance, technology transfer, and capacity building.
  • Scaling Industrial Projects. LeadIT 3.0 aims to shift focus from partnerships to scaling industrial decarbonisation projects, mobilising capital, and delivering tangible outcomes by 2030.
  • Enhancing Small Island Partnerships. India seeks to broaden cooperation with small island developing states in coastal risk assessment, ecosystem protection, technology partnerships, and capacity building to strengthen climate resilience.
