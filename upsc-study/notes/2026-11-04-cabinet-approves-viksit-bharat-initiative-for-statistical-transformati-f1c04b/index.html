Cabinet Approves VISTAR Scheme for Statistical Transformation 2026-31

The Union Cabinet approved the Viksit Bharat Initiative for Statistical Transformation, Analytics & Research (VISTAR) scheme with a budget of Rs.4,371.10 crore for 2026-27 to 2030-31. This scheme aims to modernize India's official statistical system through enhanced data collection, AI integration, and state-level capacity building, which is crucial for evidence-based policymaking.

The VISTAR scheme will upgrade India's official statistics by expanding surveys, digitizing processes, and strengthening data use for policy decisions from 2026-27 to 2030-31.

Why is this in the news?

On 4 November 2026, the Union Cabinet chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi approved the VISTAR scheme to transform India's statistical system for the period 2026-27 to 2030-31.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the VISTAR scheme and its objectives?

  • Scheme overview. VISTAR is a central sector scheme with an outlay of Rs.4,371.10 crore for 2026-27 to 2030-31, aligned with the Viksit Bharat 2047 vision to build a dynamic, real-time, and future-ready official statistical architecture.
  • Key objectives. It aims to enhance timeliness, data granularity, interoperability, and AI readiness of datasets, enabling flexible resource allocation for surveys, research, digital transformation, and national data governance.
  • Scope. The scheme covers all statistical and data management activities of the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) to upgrade the official statistical system.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What are the main components of the VISTAR scheme?

  • Component 1: Statistical Operations. MoSPI will conduct 59 surveys during the 16th Finance Commission cycle, up from 43 previously, filling critical data gaps and improving policy decisions.
  • Component 2: Support for Statistical Strengthening. Funds will be provided to States and UTs under a redesigned framework (SSS 2.0) to build capacity for producing high-quality, granular data at district and city levels and embed data use in local policymaking.
  • Component 3: Economic Census. The 8th Economic Census will collect detailed data on non-farm establishments and workers up to Gram Panchayat and Ward levels, assessing the unorganized sector and forming the baseline for the National Statistical Business Register.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

How will VISTAR improve data quality and usage?

  • Digital transformation. MoSPI will digitize statistical processes end-to-end and integrate Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning for data processing, validation, and analysis.
  • New metrics and data harmonisation. New indices like the Headline Index of Services Production and Index of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises will be introduced, supported by data cataloguing, machine readability, interoperability, and privacy by design.
  • Alternate data sources. The scheme will mainstream alternative data sources such as mobile phone, scanner, and e-commerce data to complement traditional surveys, reduce respondent burden, and fill data gaps.
  • Capacity building. Opportunities for youth and researchers will be enhanced through internships and research proposals from academic institutions.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

Why does VISTAR matter for governance and policy?

  • Evidence-based policymaking. Improved and timely data will enable targeted policy interventions and better decision-making at national and sub-national levels.
  • Unorganized sector assessment. Economic Census data will help estimate the contribution of the unorganized sector to GDP, aiding planners and policymakers.
  • State-level empowerment. By strengthening statistical capabilities at the state and district levels, VISTAR supports localized policymaking and indigenous data processing.

Back to basics: Official Statistical System

An official statistical system collects, processes, and disseminates data to inform government policy and public knowledge. It includes surveys, censuses, and data management practices. Modern systems aim for data accuracy, timeliness, granularity, and interoperability to support evidence-based decision-making.
