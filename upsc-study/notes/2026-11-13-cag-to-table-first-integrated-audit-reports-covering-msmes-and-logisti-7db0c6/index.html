CAG to Table First Integrated Audit Reports on MSMEs and Logistics

The Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG) will present its first integrated audit reports in December 2026, covering the MSME sector and logistics. This new approach combines audits of interconnected sectors to provide a comprehensive assessment of government performance, relevant for understanding governance and economic oversight.

The CAG's integrated audit reports will assess MSMEs and logistics together to improve transparency and accountability in these key economic sectors.

Why is this in the news?

The CAG announced in November 2026 that it will table its first integrated audit reports in December, focusing on MSMEs and logistics sectors.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the integrated audit approach by the CAG?

  • Integrated audit concept. The CAG has adopted an integrated audit approach that combines audits of interconnected sectors and government programmes to provide a comprehensive assessment of their performance.
  • Moving beyond isolated audits. Instead of auditing individual sectors like railways or roads separately, the integrated audit brings together findings across departments in one report to analyse overall outcomes.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What sectors are covered in the first integrated audit reports?

  • Focus areas. The initial reports will focus on ease of doing business with emphasis on MSMEs at the State level and logistics, including railways, shipping, and road transport departments.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this integrated audit matter for governance and economy?

  • Improved accountability. By auditing interconnected sectors together, the CAG provides a clearer picture of government performance and resource utilisation, enhancing transparency.
  • Relevance to economic oversight. MSMEs and logistics are crucial for economic growth and ease of doing business, so auditing them together helps identify systemic issues and policy gaps.

Back to basics: Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG)

The CAG is a constitutional authority responsible for auditing government expenditure and ensuring accountability. It conducts audits of various government departments and programmes to assess financial propriety and performance. Traditionally, audits were sector-specific, but integrated audits combine related sectors to provide a broader view of outcomes.
