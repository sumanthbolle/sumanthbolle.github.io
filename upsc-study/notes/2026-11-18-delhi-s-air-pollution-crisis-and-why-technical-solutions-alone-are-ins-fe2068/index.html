Delhi's Air Pollution: Limits of Technical Solutions and Policy Measures

Delhi's air quality has recently improved from 'Severe' to 'Poor/Very Poor' category, leading to a downgrade in emergency pollution control measures by the Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM). Despite technical interventions like air purifiers gaining popularity, experts highlight their limited ability to fully protect against health effects, emphasizing the need for comprehensive policy and citizen action.

Delhi's air pollution control requires combined regulatory action and public cooperation beyond technical fixes like air purifiers.

Why is this in the news?

On 17 November 2026, CAQM revoked the Stage-III Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) for Delhi-NCR as the Air Quality Index improved, while public demand for air purifiers surged amid ongoing pollution concerns.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current air quality situation and government response in Delhi?

  • Improved AQI status. Delhi's Air Quality Index (AQI) improved to 204 on 17 November 2026, classified as 'Poor/Very Poor', prompting CAQM to revoke the Stage-III ('Severe') GRAP order and revert to Stage-II measures.
  • Continued restrictions. Despite revocation of Stage-III, restrictions on dust-generating demolition and outdoor construction activities remain in force, and offices operate with up to 50% physical attendance until 20 November.
  • Citizen guidelines. CAQM urges citizens to follow measures like vehicle maintenance, use of public transport, waste segregation, avoiding open burning, and eco-friendly festivals to sustain air quality improvements.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why are technical solutions like air purifiers insufficient alone?

  • Limited filtration scope. Air purifiers can filter indoor pollutants but cannot remove all harmful components of outdoor air pollution or provide complete protection against prolonged exposure.
  • Surge in demand. Following worsening air quality, sales of air purifiers surged in Delhi-NCR, but consumers often lack clarity on their effectiveness and limitations.
  • Need for broader action. Experts emphasize that technical devices must be complemented by regulatory measures and public behavioral changes to effectively mitigate health risks.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What should citizens and authorities do to manage air pollution effectively?

  • Strict implementation. Authorities must maintain vigilance and intensify measures under GRAP Stages I and II to prevent reverting to severe pollution levels.
  • Public participation. Citizens should adhere to vehicle upkeep, avoid open waste burning, use public transport, and participate in awareness campaigns to reduce pollution sources.
  • Sustained monitoring. Continuous monitoring of air quality and meteorological forecasts is essential to timely adjust pollution control actions.

Back to basics: Air Quality Index and Graded Response Action Plan

The Air Quality Index (AQI) is a numerical scale used to communicate how polluted the air is, with categories ranging from 'Good' to 'Severe'. The Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) is a set of pollution control measures triggered at different AQI stages to mitigate air pollution in Delhi-NCR, involving restrictions on construction, vehicle use, and industrial activities.
