India Urges Unified Action Against Terrorism at 25th SCO Heads Meeting

At the 25th SCO Council of Heads of Government Meeting in October 2026, India's Defence Minister Rajnath Singh called for dismantling the entire ecosystem of terrorism, emphasizing no justification or double standards in addressing it. This is crucial for UPSC aspirants to understand India's counter-terrorism diplomacy and its role in regional security cooperation within the SCO framework.

India advocates for collective dismantling of terrorism's ecosystem and stresses inclusive economic cooperation at the 25th SCO Heads of Government Meeting.

Why is this in the news?

India's Defence Minister Rajnath Singh addressed the 25th SCO Council of Heads of Government Meeting on October 20, 2026, calling for unified efforts to dismantle terrorism and promoting economic cooperation among member states.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What did India emphasize about terrorism at the SCO meeting?

  • Unified action against terrorism. India called upon SCO member states to dismantle the entire ecosystem of terrorism, describing it as a grave threat to peace and development.
  • No justification or double standards. India stressed that there can be no justification for terrorism, no double standards in addressing it, and no place for those who finance, support, or provide safe havens to terrorists.
  • Impact of terrorism. Terrorism results in loss of innocent lives, disrupts investment and commerce, radicalizes societies, and deprives youth of opportunities.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

How does India view economic cooperation and connectivity within SCO?

  • Inclusive and mutually beneficial engagement. India believes economic engagement in SCO should be inclusive and mutually beneficial for all member states, adhering to sovereign equality, consensus, and mutual respect.
  • Action Plan for 2026-2030. India supports the adoption of the Multilateral Trade and Economic Cooperation Programme Action Plan for 2026-2030 to strengthen trade, investment, and technology cooperation.
  • Connectivity initiatives. India supports projects like the International North-South Transport Corridor and Chabahar Port to connect Central Asia with broader markets, emphasizing that connectivity must respect sovereignty and territorial integrity.
  • Beyond physical connectivity. India advocates simplifying customs, promoting digital documentation, improving logistics, and adopting interoperable digital solutions to reduce business costs across the region.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What are India's contributions and initiatives highlighted at the SCO meeting?

  • Digital Public Infrastructure. India highlighted its Digital Public Infrastructure that transformed public service delivery and enhanced financial inclusion.
  • Start-up ecosystem and women-led development. India has built one of the world’s largest start-up ecosystems and emphasizes women-led development as key to shaping growth.
  • Global initiatives on clean energy and climate. India’s initiatives like the International Solar Alliance, Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure, Mission LiFE, and Global Biofuel Alliance promote clean energy and sustainable development.
  • SCO institutional developments. India welcomed the establishment of the Universal Centre in Tashkent and Anti-Drug Centre in Dushanbe as permanent SCO bodies, stressing effective coordination and timely execution.

Back to basics: Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO)

The SCO is a Eurasian political, economic, and security alliance that promotes cooperation among member states in areas such as security, trade, and cultural exchange. It aims to enhance regional stability and economic development through consensus-based decision-making and respect for sovereignty. India became a full member in 2017 and actively participates in its mechanisms.
