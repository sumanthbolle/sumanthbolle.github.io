RBI Underwrites ₹33,000 Crore Government Securities Auction on November 20, 2026

The Reserve Bank of India conducted an underwriting auction for Government Securities worth ₹33,000 crore on November 20, 2026. This auction is crucial for understanding government borrowing, the role of primary dealers, and RBI's monetary policy operations.

RBI's underwriting auction on November 20, 2026, facilitated the sale of ₹33,000 crore Government Securities through primary dealers, impacting government borrowing and liquidity management.

Why is this in the news?

RBI announced and conducted an underwriting auction for Government Securities on November 20, 2026, as part of its debt management and monetary policy operations.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What was the underwriting auction conducted by RBI on November 20, 2026?

  • Auction details. RBI conducted an underwriting auction for the sale of Government Securities worth ₹33,000 crore, comprising two securities: 6.53% GS 2031 (₹19,000 crore) and 7.71% GS 2066 (₹14,000 crore).
  • Primary Dealers' role. Primary Dealers (PDs) were required to meet Minimum Underwriting Commitment (MUC) and could participate in Additional Competitive Underwriting (ACU) auctions, bidding electronically via RBI's e-Kuber system.
  • Auction method and timing. The auction used a multiple price-based method and was held between 9:00 A.M. and 9:30 A.M. on November 20, 2026.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What were the results and significance of the underwriting auction?

  • Auction outcome. The total amount underwritten matched the notified amount of ₹33,000 crore, with ₹19,000 crore for 6.53% GS 2031 and ₹14,000 crore for 7.71% GS 2066.
  • Commission rates. RBI set the cut-off underwriting commission rates payable to Primary Dealers at 0.39 paise per ₹100 for 6.53% GS 2031 and 0.78 paise per ₹100 for 7.71% GS 2066.
  • Monetary policy impact. Such auctions help RBI manage government borrowing requirements and influence liquidity in the financial system.

[3] Reserve Bank of India

How are State Government Securities auctioned and why does it matter?

  • State securities auction. Separate auctions for State Government Securities amounting to ₹31,950 crore were scheduled for November 23, 2026, involving multiple states and various tenors.
  • Bidding process. Both competitive and non-competitive bids are accepted electronically via RBI's e-Kuber system, with non-competitive bids also accepted through the Retail Direct portal.
  • Investment and regulatory aspects. State Government Securities qualify as eligible investments for banks under the Statutory Liquidity Ratio and are governed by the Government Securities Act, 2006.

Back to basics: Government Securities and Underwriting Auctions

Government securities are debt instruments issued by the government to borrow money from the public. Underwriting auctions involve primary dealers committing to buy a minimum amount of securities to ensure full subscription, helping the government raise funds efficiently. The Reserve Bank of India conducts these auctions electronically, setting commission rates for primary dealers who facilitate the sale. These securities also play a role in banks' liquidity management and regulatory requirements.

Mains question (15 marks)

Explain the role of the Reserve Bank of India in government securities auctions and its impact on monetary policy.

Explain: Describe how RBI conducts government securities auctions and how these auctions influence monetary policy and government borrowing.

Intro: RBI conducts auctions of government securities to facilitate government borrowing and manage liquidity.

  • RBI organizes underwriting and competitive auctions involving primary dealers to ensure full subscription of government debt.
  • The auction process sets interest rates on government securities, influencing borrowing costs and market liquidity.
  • Government securities form part of banks' Statutory Liquidity Ratio, affecting credit availability and monetary policy transmission.

Conclusion: Thus, RBI's auction mechanism is a key tool for managing government finances and monetary policy.
