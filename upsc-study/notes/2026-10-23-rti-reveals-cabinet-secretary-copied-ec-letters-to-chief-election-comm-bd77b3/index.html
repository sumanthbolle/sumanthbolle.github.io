RTI Reveals Cabinet Secretary Copied EC Letters to Chief Election Commissioner

An RTI response disclosed that the Cabinet Secretary acknowledged letters from two Election Commissioners by copying them to the Chief Election Commissioner for information. This incident highlights issues of administrative procedure and transparency in communication within constitutional bodies.

The Cabinet Secretary acknowledged letters from two Election Commissioners by copying them to the Chief Election Commissioner, raising questions about communication protocols within the Election Commission.

Why is this in the news?

The RTI response dated October 23, 2026, revealed that the Cabinet Secretary copied letters from two Election Commissioners to the Chief Election Commissioner for information, following concerns raised about a senior deputy election officer.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What happened with the Cabinet Secretary and Election Commissioners' letters?

  • RTI disclosure. The Cabinet Secretary, T.V. Somanathan, acknowledged letters sent by Election Commissioners S.S. Sandhu and Vivek Joshi regarding the conduct of Senior Deputy Election Commissioner Maneesh Garg, copying the Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar for his information.
  • Nature of letters. The letters were from the Election Commissioners in their individual capacity and not formal communications from the Election Commission as a whole.
  • Administrative caution. The Cabinet Secretariat noted that acting on these letters without official commission communication could be seen as executive interference in the constitutional body's functioning.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for governance and transparency?

  • Independence of Election Commission. The Election Commission is a constitutional body insulated from executive interference to ensure independent and transparent functioning.
  • Communication protocols. The incident highlights the importance of formal communication channels within constitutional bodies to avoid perceptions of interference.
  • Role of RTI. The Right to Information Act enabled public access to administrative details, promoting accountability in government communication.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the key administrative details revealed?

  • Work allocation issue. The letters concerned an order issued by Senior Deputy Election Commissioner Maneesh Garg on work allocation without prior discussion with the commission or officers.
  • Acknowledgement process. The Cabinet Secretariat sent acknowledgements to both Election Commissioners with a copy to the Chief Election Commissioner and also marked the Secretary of the Department of Personnel and Training.
  • No recorded response. It is unclear from the RTI whether the Chief Election Commissioner responded to or acknowledged the information received.

Back to basics: Right to Information (RTI) and Constitutional Bodies

The Right to Information Act allows citizens to request information from public authorities to promote transparency and accountability. Constitutional bodies like the Election Commission function independently of the executive to ensure impartiality, and their communications follow formal protocols to maintain this independence.
