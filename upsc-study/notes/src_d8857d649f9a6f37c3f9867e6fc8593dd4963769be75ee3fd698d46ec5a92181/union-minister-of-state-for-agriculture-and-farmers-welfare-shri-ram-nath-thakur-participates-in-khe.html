India implications

  • Improved soil health can strengthen long-term agricultural productivity and livelihood security, especially where repeated or unbalanced input use has weakened soil quality.
  • Soil-test-based fertiliser advice can reduce wasteful input use while preserving productivity, provided Soil Health Cards are understood and acted upon.
  • KVKs can serve as the bridge between policy messaging and farm-level adoption through demonstrations, technical guidance and extension.
  • Implementation should remain locally adapted: crop, soil, water availability and climate conditions should determine the appropriate mix of natural, organic and mineral inputs.

Way forward

  • Make Soil Health Card recommendations crop-specific, locally understandable and easier to implement through demonstrations.
  • Use KVKs and state extension systems to test and communicate natural, organic and mineral input combinations under local conditions.
  • Support transition periods with training, reliable organic-input supply chains and risk-management measures where required.
  • Monitor adoption through soil-health indicators, nutrient-use efficiency, productivity, farm profitability and farmer feedback.
