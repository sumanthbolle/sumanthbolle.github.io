Supreme Court and Political Protests on Chief Election Commissioner and Special Intensive Revision

A plea filed by Rahul Gandhi and opposition leaders challenges the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, alleging irregularities and voter deletions. The Supreme Court has not yet listed the plea for hearing due to procedural defects, while political protests continue over the Election Commission's actions.

The Supreme Court is set to hear a petition challenging the SIR of electoral rolls amid political protests against the Chief Election Commissioner.

Why is this in the news?

On October 13, 2026, the Supreme Court did not list Rahul Gandhi’s petition challenging the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls due to procedural defects, while political protests against the Chief Election Commissioner continue.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Supreme Court petition about?

  • Petition details. Rahul Gandhi and 10 opposition leaders filed a plea alleging large-scale voter deletions and irregularities in the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls.
  • Relief sought. The plea seeks suspension of the ongoing SIR, constitution of an independent audit committee headed by a retired apex court judge, and directions to ensure no elector is deleted without notice and hearing.
  • Parties involved. The Election Commission of India, Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, and Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi are respondents in the case.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why has the Supreme Court not heard the petition yet?

  • Procedural defects. The Supreme Court registry flagged 14 defects in the petition, including missing clear ID copies and illegible or missing pages, which have yet to be cured.
  • Court’s assurance. The Chief Justice of India assured that the matters will be listed for hearing once the defects are rectified.
  • Separation of powers. The CJI declined a request for a meeting to submit a memorandum, citing that addressing sub judice issues outside court would breach judicial propriety.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the key legal and constitutional issues raised?

  • Article 324 powers. The petition questions whether decisions related to the SIR comply with Article 324, which vests superintendence, direction and control of elections in the Election Commission.
  • Statutory compliance. It challenges compliance with the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners Act, 2023, and the Representation of the People Act, 1950.
  • Transparency demands. The plea demands publication of booth-wise deleted electors, reasons for deletions, and a mechanism to restore eligible voters.

Back to basics: Election Commission and Electoral Rolls

The Election Commission of India is a constitutional body responsible for administering elections and maintaining electoral rolls. Article 324 of the Constitution grants it superintendence, direction, and control over elections. Electoral rolls are periodically revised to include eligible voters and remove ineligible ones, with processes like the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) aimed at updating voter lists comprehensively.
