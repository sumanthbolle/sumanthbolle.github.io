India Advances Space Debris Removal with Vision for 2030

India has emerged as a pioneer in active space debris removal with the inauguration of Cosmoserve Space’s Base 1 facility in Hyderabad. This initiative and the announced vision for debris-free space by 2030 highlight India's growing leadership in space technology and its commitment to sustainable space environment.

India is among the early global leaders in space debris removal, aiming for a debris-free space environment by 2030 through advanced private sector initiatives.

Why is this in the news?

On 5 November 2026, Union Minister Dr. Jitendra Singh inaugurated Cosmoserve Space’s Base 1 facility in Hyderabad, marking a significant step in India's active space debris removal efforts and announcing a vision for debris-free space by 2030.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is India’s new initiative in space debris removal?

  • Cosmoserve Space facility. Cosmoserve Space inaugurated Base 1, a 14,800 square feet state-of-the-art facility in Hyderabad where space robots are designed, built, tested under space conditions, and operated through mission control.
  • Technological capabilities. The facility includes orbital rendezvous and capture labs, space robotics innovation labs, microgravity simulation platforms, and satellite integration clean rooms, advancing India's private sector space capabilities.
  • Mission EMBRACE. Within a year of formation, Cosmoserve Space conducted an in-space test of soft robotic petals in July 2026, demonstrating rapid progress in active debris removal technology.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does India’s space debris removal matter for the exam?

  • Global leadership in space technology. India’s emerging capabilities in active space debris removal reflect its growing global leadership and commitment to a safe and sustainable space environment benefiting all humanity.
  • Space economy growth. India’s space economy has grown to nearly USD 9 billion and is projected to reach USD 40–45 billion in the coming decade, showing the sector’s expanding economic importance.
  • Startup ecosystem expansion. The space startup ecosystem has expanded from single digits to nearly 450 enterprises due to transformative reforms, highlighting the role of private sector innovation.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How is India achieving this vision and what partnerships support it?

  • Collaborative model. Cosmoserve Space leverages experience from ISRO and the Gaganyaan programme, translating it into private sector innovation and collaboration.
  • Partnerships. Key partnerships include Pixel for satellite systems, Dhruv Aerospace Netherlands for propulsion, and Skyroot Aerospace for launch services, fostering end-to-end space technology collaboration.
  • Government reforms. Reforms such as the establishment of NewSpace India Limited and IN-SPACe have reshaped the space ecosystem, enabling private sector growth and technological self-reliance.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What broader impacts do India’s space capabilities have?

  • Applications on Earth. India’s space technologies contribute to Gati Shakti, smart cities, disaster preparedness, and other areas directly benefiting citizens.
  • Geopolitical stature. Initiatives like Cosmoserve Space strengthen India’s global leadership in space technology and enhance its geopolitical position.
  • Vision for humanity. The initiative embodies the spirit of Vishwa Bandhu Bharat, extending India’s commitment to space sustainability beyond national boundaries for humanity’s benefit.

Back to basics: Space Debris and Active Debris Removal

Space debris consists of defunct satellites, spent rocket stages, and fragments orbiting Earth, posing collision risks to operational spacecraft. Active debris removal (ADR) involves technologies to capture and remove such debris to ensure a safer and sustainable space environment. This is important for maintaining the long-term usability of outer space.
