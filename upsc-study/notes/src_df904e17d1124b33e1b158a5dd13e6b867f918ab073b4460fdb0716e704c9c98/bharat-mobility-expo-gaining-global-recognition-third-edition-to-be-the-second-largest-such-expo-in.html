India implications

  • Greater integration of manufacturers, suppliers and service providers can strengthen domestic value chains and improve opportunities for MSME participation.
  • Export orientation can encourage Indian firms to meet global quality, cost and delivery standards, but requires reliable logistics, standards infrastructure, skills and competitive input costs.
  • Partnerships with Japanese, European and other international firms may support investment and market access, while India must safeguard policy space and build domestic technological capability.
  • A whole-of-government approach can reduce departmental silos, but measurable outcomes should include exports, investment realised, domestic value addition, jobs and technology absorption rather than visitor numbers alone.

Way forward

  • Set outcome indicators beyond footfall, including export orders, investment realised, technology partnerships, domestic value addition, MSME participation and jobs.
  • Create post-expo facilitation mechanisms linking firms with buyers, testing laboratories, export agencies, financiers and State authorities.
  • Strengthen standards, certification, logistics, skills, design capability and research support across the mobility value chain.
  • Ensure sustainable mobility, circularity and clean technologies remain integrated into industrial strategy.
