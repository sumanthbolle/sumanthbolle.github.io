Cabinet Approves Largest-Ever FDI in India’s Financial Sector via IDBI Bank Sale

The Union Cabinet approved the strategic restructuring of IDBI Bank by selling a 60.72% stake to Fairfax Financial Holdings for ₹52,878 crore. This transaction marks the largest foreign direct investment in India's financial sector, impacting banking reforms and investment climate.

The government and LIC sold a combined 60.72% stake in IDBI Bank to Fairfax for ₹52,878 crore, marking the largest FDI in India's financial sector.

Why is this in the news?

On October 14, 2026, the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs approved the sale of a 60.72% stake in IDBI Bank to Fairfax Financial Holdings, finalizing the largest-ever FDI in India's financial sector.

[1] Press Information Bureau[3] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the background and reason for the IDBI Bank sale?

  • Poor financial health. IDBI Bank faced poor financial health requiring repeated capital infusion by the Government of India and LIC, and was placed under RBI's Prompt Corrective Action framework in 2017 due to high non-performing assets.
  • Improvement and restructuring. RBI lifted restrictions in 2021 after financial improvements, leading to the Cabinet's approval of strategic restructuring involving sale of 60.72% equity to a strategic buyer.

[1] Press Information Bureau[3] The Hindu · Secondary

How was the sale process conducted and who is the buyer?

  • Competitive bidding. A two-stage competitive bidding process was conducted with eligibility criteria including RBI's 'Fit and Proper' assessment and security clearance by the Ministry of Home Affairs.
  • Qualified bidders. Out of five initial parties, four qualified and two submitted techno-financial bids; Fairfax Financial Holdings (through M/s 10955230 Canada Inc.) was selected as the strategic buyer.
  • Purchase consideration. The approved purchase consideration was ₹52,878.0806181 crore for acquiring 60.72% stake, split equally between the Government of India (30.48%) and LIC (30.24%).

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau[3] The Hindu · Secondary

How was the valuation of IDBI Bank determined?

  • Valuation methods. Two independent professional agencies used the Market Multiple Method and Asset Valuation Method based on March 31, 2026 accounts to determine fair market and net asset values.
  • Benchmark price. The average of the two valuations, ₹75.99 per share, served as the benchmark price; the winning bid of ₹81 per share was above this benchmark and deemed reasonable.
  • Stock price role. Volatile stock market prices were disregarded as they misaligned with intrinsic value; valuation relied on accounting-based methods.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What are the implications of this transaction for the banking sector and economy?

  • Largest FDI in financial sector. This transaction is the largest foreign direct investment in India's financial sector, expected to boost investor confidence and economic reforms.
  • Post-privatization benefits. IDBI Bank is expected to generate funds without government assistance, with Fairfax bringing private capital, technology, and management practices to enhance competitiveness.
  • Residual shareholding. The Government of India retains 15% and LIC retains 19% residual stakes, with plans for minority stake sales through open market mechanisms.
  • Regulatory oversight. RBI continues full regulatory and prudential supervision; depositor interests and priority sector lending norms remain unaffected.

Back to basics: Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) in Banking

Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) refers to investment from a foreign entity into a domestic company, allowing ownership or control. In banking, FDI can bring capital, technology, and management expertise, but is regulated to protect financial stability. The Reserve Bank of India oversees banks, including those with foreign investment, ensuring prudential norms and depositor protection.
