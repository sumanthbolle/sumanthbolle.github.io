Joint Review by Union Agriculture and Environment Ministers on Stubble Management

Union Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan and Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav held a high-level meeting on October 13, 2026, to review and accelerate stubble management efforts during the paddy harvesting season. This is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights government strategies to curb stubble burning, a major environmental and agricultural challenge.

The Union Ministers emphasized urgent field-level action and financial support to prevent stubble burning and promote sustainable crop residue management.

Why is this in the news?

On October 13, 2026, the Union Agriculture and Environment Ministers conducted a joint review meeting to implement crop residue management in mission mode during the ongoing paddy harvesting season.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What was decided in the joint review meeting on stubble management?

  • Urgent field action. Ministers directed district collectors to take immediate ground-level action to prevent stubble burning, emphasizing that very little time remains between harvesting and sowing of rabi crops.
  • Use of machinery. The government has provided over 3.54 lakh crop residue management machines, including 26,109 new machines in 2026–27, and stressed repairing old machines and activating Custom Hiring Centres to ensure availability to small farmers at subsidised rates.
  • Financial assistance. ₹4,266.47 crore was released to states under the Crop Residue Management scheme between 2018–19 and 2025–26, with ₹592.57 crore allocated for 2026–27; Punjab was urged to submit Utilisation Certificates promptly to receive further funds.
  • Alternative uses of stubble. Stubble can be converted into organic manure, bio-CNG, bio-ethanol, electricity, and animal fodder, providing farmers additional income and improving soil health.
  • Monitoring and enforcement. The Environment Minister called for intensified ground-level monitoring, especially in vulnerable districts, combining satellite data with field inspections and strict enforcement, including during satellite observation gaps after 3 PM.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does stubble management matter for environment and agriculture?

  • Air pollution and soil health. Stubble burning causes air pollution and destroys beneficial soil organisms and nutrients, turning them into ash, which harms soil fertility.
  • Farmer income and sustainability. Proper management of crop residue can enhance farmers' income through alternative uses and contribute to sustainable agricultural practices.
  • Policy implementation challenge. Despite reductions in stubble burning in some states, incidents have risen in Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan, requiring special action plans and continuous vigilance.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What roles do central and state governments play in stubble management?

  • Central support. The Central Government provides financial, technical, and administrative assistance, including funding, machinery, and policy guidance.
  • State responsibility. State governments and district collectors are responsible for ground-level implementation, micro-planning, monitoring, and ensuring machinery availability.
  • Coordination necessity. Effective stubble management requires coordinated efforts among Centre, states, local administrations, and Krishi Vigyan Kendras.

Back to basics: Crop Residue Management and Environmental Impact

Crop residue or stubble is the leftover plant material after harvesting crops like paddy. Burning stubble releases harmful pollutants causing air pollution and damages soil health by destroying nutrients and beneficial organisms. Crop residue management involves alternative uses such as converting stubble into organic manure, biofuels, or fodder, which helps protect the environment and supports sustainable agriculture.
