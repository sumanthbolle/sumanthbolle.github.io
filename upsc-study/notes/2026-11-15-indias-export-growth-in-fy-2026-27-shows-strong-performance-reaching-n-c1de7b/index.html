India’s Export Growth in FY 2026-27 Reaches New Milestones

India’s exports grew 16.66% to US$ 568.84 billion during April-October FY 2026-27, with gains across merchandise and services sectors. This growth reflects India’s broader economic progress and expanding global trade partnerships, making it a key indicator for economic development and trade policy.

India’s exports in FY 2026-27 showed strong growth across sectors and markets, supporting the country’s economic expansion and global trade integration.

Why is this in the news?

The Press Information Bureau reported on November 14, 2026, that India’s exports grew 16.66% during April-October FY 2026-27, reaching US$ 568.84 billion and setting new records.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the key features of India’s export growth in FY 2026-27?

  • Overall export growth. India’s total exports increased 16.66% to US$ 568.84 billion during April-October FY 2026-27 compared to the previous year, with merchandise exports growing 21.96% and services exports 10.93%.
  • Monthly performance. Exports recorded positive year-on-year growth every month, including a 29.90% rise in October 2026, with merchandise exports surging 45.06% and services exports growing about 15.2%.
  • Sectoral growth. Seven of the top ten export categories showed double-digit growth, including other cereals (109.74%), petroleum products (47.71%), electronic goods (46.92%), and engineering goods (22%).
  • Services sector strength. Services exports grew at a compound annual rate of 10.3% between 2015 and 2025, outpacing the global average, and accounted for about 45.7% of total exports in April-October FY 2026-27.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How has India’s export footprint expanded globally?

  • Growth across markets. Eight of the ten largest merchandise export destinations recorded double-digit growth, with Singapore (107.50%), Malaysia (106.87%), Spain (88.38%), and South Africa (62.20%) among the top performers.
  • New product-market pairs. India added 1,985 new product-country export pairs during April-September FY 2026-27, generating US$ 622.1 million in exports across 116 product groups and 229 countries.
  • Regional specialties. Local products like organic palmyra jaggery powder, Sinduri pomegranates, and GI-tagged Tandur red gram reached new international markets such as Bulgaria, Dubai, and Australia.
  • Trade agreement partners. Exports to trade agreement partners grew strongly, with ASEAN exports rising 58%, SAFTA members 22%, and significant increases to Singapore, Malaysia, Sri Lanka, Japan, and South Korea.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does India’s export growth matter for economic development?

  • Complement to GDP growth. Export growth supports India’s broader economic progress, complementing a 7.8% GDP growth in April-June FY 2026-27 and contributing to employment and investment.
  • Employment and labour market. Labour force participation and employment rates improved in both rural and urban areas during 2026, with unemployment declining to 5.1% in July-September.
  • Poverty reduction. Economic growth, supported by exports, contributed to reducing poverty from 57.7% in 2011 to 16.1% in 2023 for those living under US$ 4.20 a day.
  • Foreign investment and reserves. Net foreign direct investment inflows rose to US$ 8.8 billion in April-September 2026, and foreign exchange reserves stood at US$ 719.55 billion, strengthening India’s external position.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What policy measures support India’s export competitiveness?

  • Legal framework. The Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act, 1992 and the Foreign Trade Policy 2023 provide the legal and policy foundation for export promotion.
  • Export facilitation schemes. Schemes like Advance Authorisation, Duty Free Import Authorisation, and Export Promotion Capital Goods allow duty-free imports of inputs and capital goods for exports.
  • Tax remission schemes. RoDTEP and RoSCTL refund unrebated taxes and levies on exports, benefiting thousands of exporters, especially MSMEs.
  • Districts as Export Hubs. The DEH initiative promotes district-level export planning to enhance competitiveness and market access for local enterprises.
  • Export Promotion Mission. Launched in November 2025 with an outlay of ₹25,060 crore, EPM integrates financial and non-financial support to strengthen export competitiveness and generate employment.

Back to basics: Export Growth and Economic Development

Exports refer to goods and services sold by a country to foreign markets. Export growth helps expand domestic production, create jobs, and earn foreign exchange, which supports overall economic development. Trade policies and export promotion schemes aim to enhance competitiveness, reduce costs, and open new markets for exporters.
