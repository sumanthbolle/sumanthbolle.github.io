PIL Challenges Tamil Nadu's Decision to Abandon Parandur Airport Project

A public interest litigation has been filed in the Madras High Court contesting the Tamil Nadu government's 2026 decision to abandon the Parandur greenfield airport project without supporting technical or economic studies. This case raises important questions about constitutional governance and judicial review of state infrastructure decisions.

The Madras High Court is hearing a PIL that challenges the constitutionality of Tamil Nadu's abandonment of the Parandur airport project due to lack of supporting data and procedural irregularities.

Why is this in the news?

The PIL was filed in November 2026 in the Madras High Court challenging the Tamil Nadu government's decision to abandon the Parandur airport project.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Parandur airport project and why was it abandoned?

  • Project purpose. The ₹27,000 crore Parandur greenfield airport was planned to address a proven aviation capacity crunch at the existing Meenambakkam airport handling about 30 million passengers annually.
  • Land acquisition. The government had spent ₹680 crore to consolidate over 1,700 acres (48% of targeted land) in Kancheepuram district for the airport, with sanctioned acquisition of 1,527.32 hectares of private land and 798.12 hectares of government land.
  • Abandonment decision. In August 2026, the new Tamil Nadu government announced abandoning the project citing concerns over agricultural land acquisition and farmers' livelihoods, without presenting technical data or economic studies.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the legal and procedural issues raised by the PIL?

  • Constitutionality challenged. The PIL argues the abandonment decision is unconstitutional as it lacked any technical dataset, expert committee report, or comparative economic study.
  • Procedural irregularity. The Chief Minister made the announcement under Rule 110 of the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly Rules, which does not permit immediate debate, and the petitioner contends this use was illegal.
  • Interim reliefs sought. The petitioner seeks court directions to produce all internal files and reports related to the decision, an interim stay on the abandonment, and to restrain reclassification or transfer of acquired lands.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Judicial review example. The case highlights judicial oversight of state government decisions on infrastructure projects, illustrating the balance of powers and constitutional accountability.
  • Governance and transparency. It raises issues of transparency, evidence-based policy making, and procedural propriety in government decisions affecting public resources and livelihoods.
  • Federalism and state autonomy. The PIL touches upon state autonomy in development decisions and the role of courts in reviewing such executive actions.

Back to basics: Judicial Review

Judicial review is the power of courts to examine the constitutionality and legality of executive and legislative actions. It ensures that government decisions comply with constitutional provisions and principles of fairness, transparency, and reasoned decision-making. This mechanism acts as a check on arbitrary or unlawful use of power by the state.
