Spiralling Food Prices and Rising Inflation in India

India is experiencing rising inflation, with retail inflation at 5.54% in September 2026, driven largely by food prices. This matters for UPSC aspirants as it affects economic stability, rural livelihoods, and monetary policy decisions.

Rising food inflation, especially in rural areas, is pushing overall inflation above RBI targets, influencing monetary policy and economic conditions.

Why is this in the news?

September 2026 retail inflation reached 5.54%, the highest in almost two years, prompting RBI to raise the repo rate in October 2026.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the current trends in inflation and food prices?

  • Retail inflation rate. Retail inflation in September 2026 was 5.54%, marking the fourth consecutive month above the RBI’s 4% target.
  • Rural inflation impact. Rural inflation was higher at 6.08%, with rural food inflation rising sharply to 8.06% between August and September.
  • Key food price drivers. Prices of onion (108.09%), garlic (54.62%), and ginger (62.85%) surged, while potato and tomato prices fell compared to the previous year.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is inflation rising and why does it matter?

  • Fuel and geopolitical factors. Fuel prices rose due to a 41% increase in the Indian crude basket price from July to September 2026, influenced by the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran.
  • Currency depreciation. The rupee weakened about 7% against the dollar over the year to early October 2026, adding to inflationary pressures.
  • Broader price pressures. Core inflation, excluding food and fuel but including precious metals, rose to 4.6% in September, indicating broadening inflation.
  • Impact on rural households. Rural households face higher inflation amid monsoon uncertainties, El Niño risks, and high temperatures, affecting incomes and expenses.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How is the Reserve Bank of India responding to inflation?

  • Monetary policy action. The RBI’s Monetary Policy Committee raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% in October 2026, ending a pause since February 2023.
  • Future rate outlook. Another rate increase in December 2026 is possible if inflationary pressures continue to broaden.
  • Currency support measures. RBI’s special dollar facility for oil marketing companies aims to ease rupee pressure temporarily but does not address underlying import dependence.

Back to basics: Inflation and Monetary Policy

Inflation is the rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services rises, reducing purchasing power. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) controls inflation primarily through monetary policy tools like the repo rate, which influences borrowing costs and liquidity in the economy. When inflation rises above target levels, the RBI may raise the repo rate to reduce demand and stabilize prices.
