RBI Conducts Variable Rate Repo Auction and MoSPI Updates Key Economic Data Series

On November 23, 2026, the Reserve Bank of India conducted a Variable Rate Repo auction to manage liquidity, while the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation held a conference on revised GDP, CPI, and IIP data series. These developments are crucial for understanding monetary policy tools and updated economic indicators relevant for UPSC exams.

The RBI's Variable Rate Repo auction manages short-term liquidity, and MoSPI's revised GDP, CPI, and IIP series reflect updated economic measurement methodologies important for policy and analysis.

Why is this in the news?

On November 23, 2026, RBI conducted a Variable Rate Repo auction under the Liquidity Adjustment Facility, and MoSPI, with RBI as Knowledge Partner, held a conference on new GDP, CPI, and IIP series to explain methodological changes and implications.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Reserve Bank of India

What is the Variable Rate Repo auction conducted by RBI?

  • Purpose of VRR auction. The RBI conducts Variable Rate Repo auctions to manage short-term liquidity in the banking system by borrowing excess cash from banks at variable interest rates.
  • Details of the November 23, 2026 auction. On November 23, 2026, RBI conducted a 4-day VRR auction with a notified amount of ₹50,000 crore, receiving bids worth ₹1,51,982 crore and allotting ₹50,005 crore at a cut-off rate of 5.62%.
  • Auction mechanics. The auction tenor was 4 days, with the window open from 10:30 AM to 11:00 AM, and the amount borrowed was reversed on November 25, 2026.

[2] Reserve Bank of India[3] Reserve Bank of India

What are the key changes in the new GDP, CPI, and IIP data series?

  • Conference purpose. MoSPI organised a Data Users Conference on November 23, 2026, to familiarise users with the methodological changes, data sources, and improvements in the revised GDP, CPI, and IIP series.
  • GDP revisions. The new GDP series with base year 2022-23 includes double deflation for manufacturing, use of administrative data like MCA-21 filings, and improved measurement of unincorporated sectors and private consumption.
  • CPI revisions. The CPI series rebased to 2024=100 uses updated consumption baskets from the 2023-24 Household Consumption Expenditure Survey, expanded coverage, COICOP 2018 classification, and incorporates administrative and e-commerce data.
  • IIP revisions. The IIP series rebased to 2022-23 broadens sector coverage by adding water supply, sewerage, waste management, and gas supply sectors, revises weights using National Accounts and ASI data, and uses Output PPI for deflation.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why do these updates matter for monetary policy and economic analysis?

  • Improved data quality. The revisions incorporate new data sources and methodologies that better reflect structural changes in the economy, consumption patterns, and production processes.
  • Policy implications. More accurate GDP, CPI, and IIP estimates help the RBI and policymakers make informed decisions on inflation targeting, growth assessment, and liquidity management.
  • RBI's role. RBI, as a committed user and knowledge partner, emphasises stable estimates with smaller revisions and continued dialogue with MoSPI for clarity and predictability.

Back to basics: Monetary Policy and Economic Indicators

Monetary policy involves managing liquidity and interest rates to control inflation and support growth. The Reserve Bank of India uses tools like the Variable Rate Repo auction to borrow excess funds from banks temporarily. Economic indicators such as GDP measure overall economic output, CPI tracks retail inflation, and IIP gauges industrial production. Accurate and updated data series are essential for effective policy formulation and economic analysis.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the Variable Rate Repo (VRR) auction conducted by RBI on November 23, 2026: 1. The notified amount for the auction was ₹50,000 crore with a tenor of 4 days. 2. The cut-off rate for allotment was 5.62%, and the total bids received were less than the notified amount. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the total bids received were ₹1,51,982 crore, which is more than the notified amount of ₹50,000 crore.
