India’s Trade Deficit with China Crosses $100 Billion in 2026

India’s trade deficit with China has crossed $100 billion in 2026, with two-way trade rising 25.4% to $144.45 billion between January and September. This widening trade imbalance is a key economic and diplomatic issue for India, relevant for understanding trade policy and bilateral relations with China.

India’s trade deficit with China crossed $100 billion in 2026 amid rising imports and growing exports, highlighting challenges in trade balance and market access.

Why is this in the news?

Data released by China’s General Administration of Customs in October 2026 showed India’s trade deficit with China crossing $100 billion, with exports rising 40.7% and imports up 23.3% in the first three quarters.

What are the recent trends in India-China trade?

  • Trade volume growth. Two-way trade between India and China increased by 25.4% to $144.45 billion from January to September 2026.
  • Rising imports. India’s imports from China rose 23.3% to $125.19 billion, continuing the trend of high import dependence.
  • Exports growth. Indian exports to China grew robustly by 40.7% to $19.26 billion, reaching last year’s total within nine months.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the trade deficit with China matter?

  • Widening trade imbalance. India’s trade deficit with China reached $105.93 billion in 2026, surpassing the previous year’s record of $116.12 billion.
  • Economic concerns. The large deficit reflects India’s reliance on Chinese finished goods and intermediates, especially electronics and machinery.
  • Diplomatic implications. The deficit was discussed by Prime Minister Modi and President Xi Jinping at the BRICS summit, highlighting its significance in bilateral relations.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What challenges does India face in trade with China?

  • Market access issues. India faces limited market access in China, which contributes to the lopsided trade relationship.
  • Export controls by China. China has imposed export controls on sectors like fertilisers and rare earths, affecting India’s supply chains.
  • Dependence on Chinese imports. India continues to rely heavily on Chinese imports for manufacturing intermediates and finished goods.

Back to basics: Trade Deficit and Balance of Trade

Trade deficit occurs when a country's imports exceed its exports, leading to a negative balance of trade. It reflects economic dependence on foreign goods and affects currency stability and domestic industries. Understanding trade deficits helps analyze a country's economic health and international relations.
