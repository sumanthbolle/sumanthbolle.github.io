Supreme Court warns Rajasthan on Jojari river pollution, threatens CBI probe

The Supreme Court reprimanded the Rajasthan government for facilitating illegal mining and pollution in the Jojari river system and warned of a CBI investigation if corrective actions are not taken. This case exemplifies judicial intervention in environmental protection and enforcement of pollution control laws.

The Supreme Court has held Rajasthan accountable for illegal mining and pollution in the Jojari river, emphasizing state responsibility and threatening a CBI probe.

Why is this in the news?

On October 26, 2026, the Supreme Court initiated suo motu proceedings against Rajasthan following reports of severe pollution in the Jojari river, warning of a CBI probe if the state failed to act.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered the Supreme Court's intervention on Jojari river pollution?

  • Suo motu action. The Supreme Court took suo motu cognisance last year after a news report highlighted alarming pollution levels in the Jojari river, which supports nearly two million people.
  • Illegal mining and pollution. The Court found that Rajasthan officials facilitated illegal mining in the Jojari-Luni-Bandi river system and regularised mining leases despite violations.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the Supreme Court's directions and concerns?

  • Remedial steps demanded. The Court directed the state to seal and dismantle all illegal discharge points into the river and initiate proceedings against responsible landowners and officials.
  • Financial proposal for treatment plants. Rajasthan was asked to submit a concrete financial plan for common effluent treatment plants with zero-liquid-discharge technology.
  • Polluter pays principle. The Court emphasized that the state must share the cost of pollution control and cannot leave restoration solely to industries and municipalities.
  • Ban on construction near rivers. In August, the Court prohibited construction within 100 metres of the Jojari river banks and banned hazardous industries within 500 metres of flood lines.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for environmental governance and federal accountability?

  • State responsibility. The Court questioned the bona fides of state officials who regularised illegal mining and held them equally responsible for pollution.
  • Judicial oversight. The Supreme Court's active monitoring and threat of a CBI probe underscore the judiciary's role in enforcing environmental laws.
  • Impact on residents. The Court prioritized the health and environment of 20 lakh people over the economic interests of a few involved in mining and industries.

Back to basics: Polluter Pays Principle

The polluter pays principle is an environmental policy concept where the party responsible for pollution bears the costs of managing it to prevent damage to human health or the environment. It ensures accountability and incentivizes pollution control by making polluters financially liable for the harm they cause.

What happens next?

  • State compliance and monitoring. The Supreme Court has set a deadline for Rajasthan to submit a workable restoration plan and financial proposal for pollution control, highlighting the need for strict state compliance and monitoring.
