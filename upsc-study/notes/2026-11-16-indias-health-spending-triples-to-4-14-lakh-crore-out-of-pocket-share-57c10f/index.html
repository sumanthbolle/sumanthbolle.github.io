India’s Government Health Spending Triples to ₹4.14 Lakh Crore by 2023–24

India’s National Health Accounts 2023–24 reveal that government health expenditure has tripled since 2013–14, reaching ₹4.14 lakh crore, while out-of-pocket expenditure by households has dropped from 64.2% to 42.7%. This shift marks progress towards affordable and financially protective healthcare, important for understanding India’s health financing and welfare policies.

India’s public health spending has tripled in a decade, reducing the financial burden on households and strengthening primary healthcare.

Why is this in the news?

The Union Ministry of Health and Family Welfare released the National Health Accounts 2023–24 on November 15, 2026, showing a tripling of government health expenditure and a sharp decline in out-of-pocket spending by households.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the key changes in India’s health expenditure from 2013-14 to 2023-24?

  • Government spending tripled. Government health expenditure increased from ₹1.30 lakh crore in 2013-14 to ₹4.14 lakh crore in 2023-24, reflecting sustained public investment in healthcare.
  • Out-of-pocket expenditure declined. Households’ out-of-pocket expenditure as a share of total health expenditure fell sharply from 64.2% to 42.7%, a reduction of 21.5 percentage points.
  • Per capita government spending rose. Per capita government health expenditure rose nearly threefold from ₹1,042 to ₹2,959, indicating increased public spending per citizen.
  • Social security share increased. Social security expenditure on health nearly doubled as a share of total health expenditure, rising from 6% to 11%, strengthening financial protection.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

How has the government focused on primary healthcare and financial protection?

  • Primary healthcare investment. Government expenditure on primary healthcare reached ₹1.53 lakh crore, supporting community-level services and early disease detection.
  • Flagship initiatives. Programs like Ayushman Bharat, free drugs and diagnostics, Jan Aushadhi, and AMRIT pharmacies improve access to affordable healthcare and medicines.
  • Financial protection schemes. Government-financed health insurance schemes, including Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY and state schemes, have expanded social security financing and reduced direct household spending.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this transformation in health financing matter for India’s healthcare system?

  • Shift towards affordability. The decline in out-of-pocket spending reduces financial hardship on families and increases healthcare accessibility.
  • Stronger public role. Increased government spending signals a shift in healthcare financing from households to public resources, supporting universal health coverage goals.
  • Improved health outcomes. Greater investment in primary healthcare and financial protection mechanisms can lead to early disease prevention and better health equity.

Back to basics: Health Financing and Out-of-Pocket Expenditure

Health financing refers to how funds are collected, pooled, and spent to provide healthcare services. Out-of-pocket expenditure is the direct payment made by individuals to healthcare providers at the time of service, which can cause financial hardship. Reducing out-of-pocket spending through increased public investment and social security schemes improves affordability and access to healthcare.

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only

  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because out-of-pocket expenditure actually decreased from 64.2% to 42.7%, not increased.
