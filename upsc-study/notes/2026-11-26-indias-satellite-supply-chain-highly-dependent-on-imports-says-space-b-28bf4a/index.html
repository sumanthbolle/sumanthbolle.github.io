India’s Satellite Supply Chain Highly Dependent on Imports

A report by IN-SPACe highlights that India relies heavily on imported electronic and specialized components for satellite manufacturing, with limited private sector involvement beyond assembly. This matters for UPSC aspirants as it relates to India's science and technology policy, self-reliance, and strategic autonomy in the space sector.

India's satellite manufacturing depends largely on imports for critical components, limiting its global competitiveness and self-reliance.

Why is this in the news?

On November 25, 2026, IN-SPACe released a report revealing India's high dependence on imported components in satellite manufacturing and the limited role of the private sector.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current state of India’s satellite supply chain?

  • High import dependence. Nearly 60% of satellite costs come from electronic, electrical, electromechanical components, and specialized materials, most of which are imported.
  • Limited private sector role. Private companies mainly contribute to structural assemblies and propulsion components, with most critical parts supplied by public sector and established private firms.
  • Supply chain challenges. Critical components often take 12-16 months to procure, causing project delays and reducing responsiveness to market opportunities.
  • Skill and infrastructure gaps. There is a shortage of trained personnel in satellite systems engineering and limited testing and certification facilities.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for India’s space sector and economy?

  • Growth potential. India’s space economy is valued at nearly $9 billion and is projected to grow to $40-45 billion within a decade.
  • Private sector expansion. The private space sector is expected to grow from a few startups to 400-500 companies in the coming years.
  • Global competitiveness. Limited indigenous production and testing facilities weaken India’s prospects in global satellite markets.
  • Strategic autonomy. Dependence on imports for critical satellite components affects India’s self-reliance and strategic capabilities in space technology.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the recommendations and future directions?

  • Policy interventions. Addressing supply chain gaps through strategic investments, policy support, and international collaborations is necessary.
  • Demand generation. Creating strong demand for space products and services is essential to sustain capacity and investments.
  • Enhancing capabilities. Increasing testing, certification facilities, and skilled workforce will improve quality and global acceptance.

Back to basics: Indigenisation in Science and Technology

Indigenisation refers to developing and producing technology and components within the country to reduce dependence on imports. It is important for strategic autonomy, economic growth, and building domestic capabilities. In the space sector, indigenisation involves manufacturing satellite components, testing, and assembling satellites using domestic resources and expertise.

What happens next?

  • Strengthen domestic supply chains. India needs strategic investments and policy support to develop indigenous manufacturing and testing capabilities for satellite components.
  • Expand private sector role. Encouraging private companies to move beyond assembly to manufacturing and exporting satellite subsystems can boost the space economy.
  • Skill development. Addressing the shortage of skilled personnel in satellite engineering and quality assurance is critical for growth.
Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about India's satellite manufacturing supply chain: 1. Nearly 60% of satellite costs are from imported electronic and specialized components. 2. The private sector currently leads in manufacturing critical satellite components. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the private sector's role is limited mainly to assembly and some propulsion components, while critical components are supplied by public sector and established firms.
