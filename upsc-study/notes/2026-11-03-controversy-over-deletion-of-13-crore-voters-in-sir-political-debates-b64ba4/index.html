Controversy Over Deletion of 13 Crore Voters in SIR and Political Debates

Union Home Minister Amit Shah claimed that the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) exercise identified 13 crore deleted voters, including 3 crore illegal infiltrators, sparking political debates and demands for a special Parliament session. This issue raises important questions on electoral roll accuracy, voter rights, and parliamentary accountability relevant for UPSC exams.

The SIR exercise led to deletion of 13 crore voters, including alleged infiltrators, causing political controversy and calls for parliamentary debate.

Why is this in the news?

On November 2, 2026, Union Home Minister Amit Shah publicly defended the SIR voter deletion exercise and challenged opposition leaders to a special Parliament session on the issue.

What is the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) and what happened?

  • SIR exercise. The Election Commission's Special Intensive Revision (SIR) exercise involved reviewing and updating electoral rolls, resulting in deletion of about 13 crore names from voter lists.
  • Reasons for deletion. Among the 13 crore deleted, 4.25 crore were registered in two locations, 3 crore were deceased, 1 crore were duplicate entries, and 3 crore were alleged illegal infiltrators from Bangladesh.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is the deletion controversial and politically significant?

  • Allegations of infiltrators. Union Home Minister Amit Shah claimed the 3 crore deleted voters were illegal infiltrators and accused opposition parties of protecting them as vote banks.
  • Opposition's stance. Opposition leaders, including Rahul Gandhi, alleged that 13 crore genuine voters lost their votes unfairly, opposing the SIR exercise.
  • Political debates and challenges. Amit Shah challenged Rahul Gandhi to a special Parliament session on SIR, infiltrators, and EVMs, accusing him of obstructing Parliament and denying the legitimacy of the deletions.

What are the implications for elections and parliamentary democracy?

  • Electoral roll accuracy. The SIR aims to improve electoral roll accuracy by removing duplicate, deceased, and non-existent voters, which affects the integrity of elections.
  • Parliamentary accountability. The demand for a special Parliament session highlights the role of parliamentary debate in resolving electoral controversies and ensuring transparency.
  • Federal and political tensions. The controversy reflects tensions between the central government and opposition parties, impacting federal relations and democratic processes.

Back to basics: Electoral Rolls and Voter List Revision

Electoral rolls are official lists of eligible voters maintained by the Election Commission. Periodic revision exercises like the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) update these lists to remove duplicate, deceased, or ineligible voters to ensure free and fair elections. Accurate rolls prevent electoral fraud and uphold democratic integrity.
