Supreme Court Notice on Bail Pleas of Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam in UAPA Case

The Supreme Court issued notice on the bail pleas of Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam, accused in the 2020 Delhi riots case under UAPA, and decided to form a three-judge Bench to resolve conflicting bail law interpretations. This case highlights the judiciary's role in balancing stringent anti-terror laws and fundamental rights, relevant for understanding constitutional protections and judicial processes.

The Supreme Court is examining whether prolonged incarceration and trial delays can override strict bail restrictions under UAPA in the Delhi riots conspiracy case.

Why is this in the news?

On November 25, 2026, the Supreme Court issued notice on bail pleas by Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam in the 2020 Delhi riots case and decided to constitute a three-judge Bench to address conflicting interpretations of bail law under UAPA.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Supreme Court's current stance on the bail pleas?

  • Notice issued. The Supreme Court issued notice on the bail pleas of Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam but declined interim bail, deciding to form a three-judge Bench to examine if prolonged incarceration and trial delays can override UAPA's stringent bail curbs.
  • Bench composition. A Bench comprising Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana sought the Delhi Police’s response by December 14 and directed the Registry to place the matter before the CJI for a larger Bench constitution.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is the Supreme Court forming a larger Bench on this issue?

  • Conflicting interpretations. Different Benches have interpreted bail law under UAPA divergently, with a May 22, 2026 referral to the CJI to ensure parity and consistency in applying precedents, especially regarding prolonged incarceration and bail eligibility.
  • Key precedent. The 2021 ruling in Union of India v. K. A. Najeeb held that stringent bail restrictions under UAPA could be relaxed if prolonged incarceration and trial delays infringe fundamental rights.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the case specifics and legal challenges raised?

  • Case background. Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam are accused in the 2020 Delhi riots conspiracy case under UAPA and IPC provisions, linked to communal violence during protests against the Citizenship (Amendment) Act.
  • Delay and detention. Both activists have been in custody for over six years, with trial proceedings delayed and protected witnesses yet to be examined, leading to claims that continued detention is punitive and violates Article 21 rights.
  • High Court ruling. The Delhi High Court rejected their fresh bail pleas, citing judicial discipline and conditions set by the Supreme Court in January 2026, which allowed bail renewal only after one year or examination of protected witnesses.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for constitutional law and civil liberties?

  • Balancing rights and security. The case tests the balance between stringent anti-terror laws like UAPA and fundamental rights such as the right to life and liberty under Article 21, especially concerning bail and prolonged detention.
  • Judicial consistency. Resolving conflicting judicial interpretations ensures uniform application of law, protecting accused persons' rights while addressing national security concerns.

Back to basics: Bail under UAPA and Fundamental Rights

Bail is the temporary release of an accused awaiting trial, balancing the presumption of innocence with public safety. The Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA) imposes stringent bail conditions to prevent terrorism-related crimes. However, prolonged detention without trial can violate the fundamental right to life and liberty under Article 21 of the Constitution, requiring courts to balance security concerns with individual rights.
