CSIR Transfers Indigenous Technologies to Industry for Sustainable Development

The Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) held a Technology Transfer event to accelerate commercialization of indigenous technologies addressing infrastructure, construction, and road materials. This initiative is crucial for UPSC aspirants as it illustrates the role of scientific research in sustainable industrial growth and technology adoption in India.

CSIR's technology transfer initiative promotes sustainable development by enabling industry adoption of indigenous innovations in infrastructure and materials.

Why is this in the news?

CSIR organised a Technology Transfer and MoU signing event on 19 November 2026 to promote commercialisation and deployment of indigenous technologies developed by its laboratories.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What technologies did CSIR transfer to industry and why?

  • Infrastructure and construction solutions. CSIR-Structural Engineering Research Centre (CSIR-SERC) transferred technologies including the Emergency Retrieval System for rapid restoration of power lines, recycled aggregate-based eco-friendly bricks, hollow blocks, paver blocks, and Ultra-High-Performance Concrete pavement technology to industry partners.
  • Sustainable road construction materials. CSIR-Central Road Research Institute (CSIR-CRRI), in collaboration with CSIR-AMPRI and CSIR-IIP, transferred MicroReAct-RAP™ technology using up to 40% reclaimed asphalt pavement and Bio-Bitumen technology converting agricultural residues into biomass-derived bitumen substitutes.
  • Industrial and environmental benefits. These technologies reduce dependence on natural aggregates, promote circular economy by utilising construction and demolition waste, and offer durable, low-maintenance infrastructure solutions.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How does CSIR facilitate industry collaboration and technology adoption?

  • Monthly technology transfer initiative. CSIR organises monthly events to strengthen the interface between its laboratories and industry, accelerating the journey of innovations from laboratory to market.
  • Memoranda of Understanding (MoUs). CSIR-CRRI signed MoUs with Global Automotive Research Centre (GARC) and Pārakhi Skill Systems Private Limited to enhance joint R&D, technical cooperation, and industry engagement.
  • Focus on commercialisation and scale-up. CSIR leadership emphasises that scientific research must translate into tangible economic, industrial, and societal benefits through effective mechanisms for industrial adoption and commercialisation.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this matter for sustainable development and UPSC preparation?

  • Promotes sustainable industrial growth. The transfer of indigenous technologies supports sustainable infrastructure development by reducing resource consumption and waste, aligning with environmental conservation goals.
  • Illustrates science-policy-industry linkage. CSIR's initiative exemplifies how scientific research institutions contribute to economic development and technology dissemination, a key theme in science and technology governance.
  • Relevant for environment and economy syllabus. Understanding such technology transfer mechanisms helps answer questions on sustainable development, resource utilisation, and industrial innovation in UPSC exams.

Back to basics: Technology Transfer

Technology transfer is the process by which scientific research outputs are shared with industry for commercialisation and practical application. It bridges the gap between laboratory innovations and market-ready products, enabling economic and societal benefits. This process often involves agreements such as MoUs to facilitate collaboration and adoption.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about CSIR's recent technology transfer initiative: 1. CSIR transferred technologies like Emergency Retrieval System for power lines and Bio-Bitumen to industry partners. 2. The MoU signed with Global Automotive Research Centre aims to enhance joint R&D and shared testing facilities. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). Statement 1 is correct but the Bio-Bitumen technology transfer involved RAACS Solutions and SAR Parivahan, not the Global Automotive Research Centre; Statement 2 correctly describes the MoU purpose.

Quick revision

What is the Emergency Retrieval System transferred by CSIR?
It is an indigenous, lightweight modular system for rapid restoration of electricity transmission lines.
Which technology uses up to 40% reclaimed asphalt pavement?
MicroReAct-RAP™ technology is a sustainable cold micro-surfacing solution incorporating up to 40% reclaimed asphalt pavement.
What is Bio-Bitumen technology developed by CSIR?
Bio-Bitumen converts agricultural residues like rice straw into biomass-derived material partially replacing petroleum-based bitumen.
