Challenges in India’s Rice Economy: Exports, Water, Emissions, Groundwater

India is the world’s largest rice exporter, but its rice production heavily depends on water and natural resources, raising sustainability concerns. Understanding the environmental and economic dimensions of rice cultivation is crucial for UPSC aspirants focusing on agriculture, environment, and resource management.

India’s rice export competitiveness must shift from volume to sustainable value by optimizing water use, reducing emissions, and enhancing farmer income.

Why is this in the news?

The Hindu published an analysis on November 16, 2026, highlighting the sustainability challenges in India’s rice economy related to exports, water use, emissions, and groundwater depletion.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current status of India’s rice production and exports?

  • Production volume. India produced an estimated 154.02 million tonnes of rice in 2025-26, with major producing states including Uttar Pradesh, Telangana, West Bengal, Punjab, and others.
  • Export leadership. India has been the world’s largest rice exporter since 2012, accounting for around 30-35% of global rice exports in recent years.
  • Export growth. Rice exports increased from 17.73 million tonnes in 2020-21 to 21.57 million tonnes in 2025-26, with export value rising from $8.82 billion to $11.54 billion.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is India’s rice export policy considered paradoxical?

  • Export composition. India’s export basket has two segments: premium Basmati and high-volume non-Basmati rice; non-Basmati accounts for more than twice the export volume but only marginally higher export value.
  • Virtual water trade. Each tonne of rice exported carries embedded water use, especially blue water from aquifers and rivers, which is scarce and depleting in key states like Punjab, Haryana, and western Uttar Pradesh.
  • Environmental cost. Rice cultivation generates significant greenhouse gases, including methane from flooded fields and emissions from nitrogen-based fertilizers, adding a climate dimension to the export sustainability challenge.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What changes are suggested for sustainable rice production and export?

  • Shift to value over volume. Policy should focus on more crop per drop, higher income per tonne, and lower energy and resource use per unit exported rather than just increasing export volume.
  • Align cultivation with conditions. Rice cultivation should be aligned with agro-climatic conditions, water availability, and groundwater status, promoting crop diversification in water-stressed areas.
  • Adopt sustainable practices. Technologies like alternate wetting and drying, direct-seeded rice, and integrated nutrient management can reduce greenhouse gas emissions and improve resource-use efficiency.
  • Measure sustainability. Policymakers should track metrics such as export value per cubic metre of blue water, groundwater use per tonne, irrigation productivity, emissions per kilogram of rice, and farmer income per unit of water.

Back to basics: Virtual Water Trade and Sustainable Agriculture

Virtual water trade refers to the hidden flow of water when commodities like rice are traded internationally, representing the water used in their production. Sustainable agriculture aims to optimize resource use such as water and energy while minimizing environmental impacts like greenhouse gas emissions, ensuring long-term viability of farming systems.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about India’s rice economy: 1. India has been the world’s largest rice exporter since 2012, with exports increasing in volume and value by 2025-26. 2. Non-Basmati rice exports generate significantly higher export value than Basmati rice despite lower volumes. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because non-Basmati rice exports have more than twice the volume of Basmati but only marginally higher export value, not significantly higher.

Mains question (15 marks)

Examine the challenges and sustainability concerns in India’s rice economy with reference to exports, water use, and environmental impact.

Examine: Analyze the issues related to India’s rice production and export, focusing on water resource management and environmental sustainability.

Intro: India is the world’s largest rice exporter, but its rice economy faces sustainability challenges related to water use and emissions.

  • Discuss the scale and significance of India’s rice production and exports.
  • Explain the paradox of export volume versus environmental costs including virtual water trade and greenhouse gas emissions.
  • Analyze policy suggestions for shifting from volume-based to value-based rice export strategy emphasizing sustainable water and resource use.

Conclusion: Sustainable rice production and export require integrated policies balancing economic gains with environmental and water resource conservation.
