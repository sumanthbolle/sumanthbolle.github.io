Karnataka High Court Limits Enforcement Directorate’s Powers

The Karnataka High Court ruled that the Enforcement Directorate (ED) is not a 'super cop' and cannot file private complaints independently under the Prevention of Money-Laundering Act (PMLA). This ruling clarifies the scope of ED’s powers and the principle of checks and balances in law enforcement, important for understanding constitutional governance and agency accountability.

The Karnataka High Court held that the ED’s investigative powers under the PMLA are derivative, not original, and it cannot initiate private complaints when other agencies refuse to act.

Why is this in the news?

On November 7, 2026, the Karnataka High Court ruled that the Enforcement Directorate cannot act as a 'super cop' by filing private complaints independently under the PMLA.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Karnataka High Court rule about the ED’s powers?

  • Not a super cop. The Court held that the ED is not a 'super cop' and must exercise only those powers expressly conferred by the PMLA, rejecting its attempt to file private complaints independently.
  • Derivative powers. The ED’s power under the PMLA is 'derivative and not original', meaning it can act only after a predicate offence is established by competent agencies.
  • Private complaints disallowed. Filing private complaints under Section 200 Cr.P.C. or Section 223 BNSS by the ED is not contemplated under the PMLA and was quashed by the Court.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What was the background of the case?

  • Land scam allegations. The case originated from allegations of irregularities in land grants by the Malur Land Grant Committee, leading to an ED investigation under the PMLA.
  • Collapse of predicate offence. The original 'land scam' case was closed by the police and dismissed by the trial court due to lack of sanction to prosecute.
  • ED’s separate complaint. After the Lokayukta police did not act on ED’s information about a related 'job scam', the ED filed a private complaint, which the Court found impermissible.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this ruling matter for law enforcement and governance?

  • Checks and balances. The ruling reinforces constitutional principles by ensuring that the ED cannot bypass other agencies and act without proper authority.
  • Limits on investigative powers. It clarifies that the ED’s investigation must be based on a predicate offence established by competent authorities, preventing abuse of power.
  • Separation of powers. The Court emphasized that acts must be done in the manner prescribed by law, upholding the separation of powers and preventing overreach.

Back to basics: Separation of powers and checks and balances

The separation of powers divides government responsibilities among different branches to prevent concentration of power. Checks and balances ensure that no single agency or branch can act beyond its legal authority, maintaining accountability and rule of law. Law enforcement agencies must operate within powers granted by statutes and cannot override other competent authorities.
