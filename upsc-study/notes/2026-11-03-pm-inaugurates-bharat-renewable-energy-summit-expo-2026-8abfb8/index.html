PM Inaugurates Bharat Renewable Energy Summit & Expo 2026 in Delhi

Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated the four-day Bharat Renewable Energy Summit & Expo 2026 on 2 November at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, with participation from over 130 countries and 40 ministers. The summit focuses on investment, innovation, and international cooperation in clean energy, highlighting India's progress and ambitious targets in renewable energy.

The Bharat Renewable Energy Summit & Expo 2026 showcases India's leadership in clean energy with global partnerships and ambitious targets to expand non-fossil fuel capacity.

Why is this in the news?

What is the Bharat Renewable Energy Summit & Expo 2026?

  • Summit details. The four-day summit held from 2 to 5 November 2026 at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, has the theme ‘Invest. Innovate. Inspire.’ and includes participants from over 130 countries, 40 ministers, policymakers, investors, and industry leaders.
  • Global and national focus. It serves as a global platform for clean energy investment, industry engagement, and technology partnerships, coinciding with the 9th International Solar Alliance Assembly and the 4th International Conference on Green Hydrogen.
  • State participation. Thirteen Indian states and union territories showcase their renewable energy potential, investment opportunities, and manufacturing capabilities.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are India's renewable energy achievements and targets?

  • Installed capacity milestone. India achieved the Paris pledge of meeting half of its installed power capacity through non-fossil sources in 2025, five years ahead of schedule, with over 50 percent of installed capacity now non-fossil.
  • Capacity targets. India has nearly 300 gigawatts of non-fossil fuel capacity installed and aims to add another 500 gigawatts over the next 10 to 11 years.
  • Global ranking. India ranks among the top three countries worldwide in solar and renewable energy capacity.

[2] Press Information Bureau[3] Press Information Bureau

How is India promoting renewable energy adoption and innovation?

  • PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana. Launched in February 2024, this scheme aims to install rooftop solar panels on one crore homes; over 65 lakh households have joined, reducing electricity bills by about 76 percent and enabling many to earn by selling surplus power.
  • Solar integration in public and agricultural sectors. More than 44,000 government buildings use solar energy saving nearly ₹700 crore annually, and over 30 lakh farmers benefit from solar-powered irrigation under the KUSUM scheme.
  • Green hydrogen and infrastructure. India is advancing the National Green Hydrogen Mission with a target of producing five million tonnes by 2030 and developing Green Energy Corridors to efficiently transmit renewable power nationwide.
  • Manufacturing growth. Solar module manufacturing capacity has increased from 2 gigawatts a decade ago to over 200 gigawatts today, reflecting Atmanirbhar Bharat and Make in India successes.
  • Innovation call. The Prime Minister urged startups, IITs, and entrepreneurs to develop solar power models meeting maximum household needs, including solar cooking and electric vehicle charging.

[2] Press Information Bureau[3] Press Information Bureau

Why does this summit matter for India's energy security and global climate goals?

  • Energy and economic security link. Recent global crises have shown that energy security is directly linked to economic security, and renewable energy offers a stable, unlimited alternative to finite fossil fuels.
  • Investment and job creation. Over the past decade, ₹15 lakh crore has been invested in renewables, with ₹6 lakh crore invested since 2024; projected investments by 2030 exceed ₹20 lakh crore, creating thousands of green jobs.
  • International cooperation. India promotes global clean energy cooperation through platforms like the International Solar Alliance and proposes initiatives such as a $200 million Africa Solar Facility.

Back to basics: Renewable Energy and Energy Security

Renewable energy comes from sources that are naturally replenished, such as solar, wind, and hydro power. Energy security means ensuring reliable and affordable energy supply to support economic growth and national security. Transitioning from fossil fuels to renewable energy reduces dependence on limited and geopolitically concentrated fossil reserves, helping stabilize economies and address climate change.
