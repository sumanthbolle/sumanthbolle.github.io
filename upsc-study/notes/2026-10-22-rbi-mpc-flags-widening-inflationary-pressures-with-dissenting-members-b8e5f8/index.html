RBI MPC Flags Broadening Inflation with Divergent Views on Policy

The Reserve Bank of India's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.5% in October 2026 amid widening inflationary pressures. Two external members dissented, preferring to keep the policy stance neutral rather than shift to calibrated tightening, highlighting debates on balancing inflation control and growth support.

The RBI MPC raised rates due to broadening inflation but faced dissent over tightening stance reflecting concerns on growth and inflation risks.

Why is this in the news?

The RBI MPC released minutes on October 21, 2026, revealing concerns about inflation spreading beyond food and fuel and differing views on monetary policy stance.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the RBI MPC decide on inflation and policy stance?

  • Rate hike decision. The MPC unanimously voted to raise the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.5% at its October 5-7 meeting.
  • Shift in policy stance. The committee shifted the policy stance from neutral to calibrated tightening, signaling no near-term rate cuts and potential future hikes or pauses.
  • Dissenting opinions. Two external members, Dr Nagesh Kumar and Prof Ram Singh, dissented, preferring to retain the neutral stance to avoid signaling an extended rate hike cycle that could undermine growth-supportive policies.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does inflation concern the RBI MPC?

  • Broadening inflation pressures. Inflation is spreading beyond food and fuel, with the share of commodities in the CPI basket inflating above 4% rising from 20% in March to 37% in August.
  • Core inflation rise. Core inflation increased to 4.2% in August from 3.9% in July, with underlying inflation expected to rise above the target in coming months.
  • Inflation projections. Headline CPI is projected to reach the upper tolerance band of 6% in the third quarter and remain significantly above 5% for the next two quarters.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What risks and factors influenced MPC members' views?

  • External risks. Members acknowledged risks from the West Asia conflict, rising oil prices, rupee depreciation, and elevated precious-metal prices impacting inflation.
  • Supply-side inflation. Inflation is largely driven by supply-side factors without signs of economic overheating, but inflationary expectations have risen sharply.
  • Need for vigilance. Members emphasized monitoring inflation generalization and second-round effects of supply shocks to decide the pace and extent of rate hikes.

Back to basics: Monetary Policy and Inflation Control

Monetary policy involves managing interest rates and money supply to achieve economic objectives like controlling inflation and supporting growth. The repo rate is the rate at which the central bank lends to commercial banks; raising it makes borrowing costlier, helping to reduce inflation. Inflation targeting involves keeping inflation within a set tolerance band to maintain price stability and economic confidence.
