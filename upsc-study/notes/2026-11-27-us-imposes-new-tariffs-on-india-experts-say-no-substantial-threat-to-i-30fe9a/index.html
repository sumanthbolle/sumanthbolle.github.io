US Imposes New Tariffs on India Amid Trade Deal Pressure

The US Trade Representative proposed a 25% tariff on India citing alleged excess manufacturing capacity, with a reduced 17% tariff if India signs a trade deal. This move is seen as a pressure tactic rather than a substantial threat to India’s trade interests.

The US tariffs on India aim to pressure a trade deal but do not reflect excess manufacturing capacity or pose a major threat to India’s exports.

Why is this in the news?

On November 26, 2026, the US Trade Representative proposed new tariffs on India, renewing trade tensions and prompting analysis of their impact on India-US relations.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the new US tariffs on India and their rationale?

  • Tariff rates imposed. The US proposed a 25% tariff on Indian imports, citing India's alleged use of strategic excess manufacturing capacity harming American industry.
  • Pressure for trade deal. The tariff could be reduced to 17% if India signs a trade deal with the US, indicating the tariffs are a pressure tactic rather than purely punitive.
  • Comparison with previous tariffs. The 25% rate matches tariffs imposed by the US in 2025 and is close to rates on competitors like Indonesia and Malaysia.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why do experts say the tariffs pose no substantial threat to India?

  • No excess manufacturing capacity. India denies having excess manufacturing capacity, supported by data showing manufacturing's GDP share has declined and import dependence remains.
  • Tariffs unlikely to reduce exports. Key Indian export sectors like generic pharmaceuticals, telecom equipment, and renewable energy have developed expertise, making them resilient to tariff disadvantages.
  • Context of Russian oil tariffs. The US has also imposed 100% tariffs on Indian imports of Russian oil, which have been temporarily paused but add complexity to trade relations.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What should India consider regarding the trade deal?

  • Assessing benefits versus costs. India has so far refrained from signing a trade deal and should proceed only if the benefits at least equal the costs.

Back to basics: Tariffs and Trade Policy

Tariffs are taxes imposed on imported goods to protect domestic industries or as a tool in trade negotiations. They can raise the cost of foreign products, influencing trade flows and diplomatic relations. Trade deals often aim to reduce tariffs to facilitate smoother exchange of goods and services between countries.
