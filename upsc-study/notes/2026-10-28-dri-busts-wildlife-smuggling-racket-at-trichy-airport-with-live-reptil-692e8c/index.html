DRI Busts Wildlife Smuggling Racket at Trichy Airport with Live Reptiles Seized

The Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI) intercepted 45 live reptiles smuggled through Trichy International Airport on 26 October 2026, arresting two passengers. This case highlights India's efforts to combat wildlife trafficking, relevant for understanding enforcement of wildlife protection and internal security.

DRI foiled a wildlife smuggling attempt at Trichy Airport by seizing 45 live reptiles concealed in food packets from passengers arriving from Bangkok via Colombo.

Why is this in the news?

On 26 October 2026, DRI intercepted two passengers at Trichy Airport smuggling 45 live reptiles concealed in food packets, arresting them under the Customs Act.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What happened in the wildlife smuggling case at Trichy Airport?

  • DRI operation. The Directorate of Revenue Intelligence, in coordination with the Air Intelligence Unit at Trichy Airport, intercepted two Indian passengers arriving from Bangkok via Colombo on 26 October 2026.
  • Seizure details. A total of 45 live reptiles were recovered, concealed in 27 food packets and found sedated during baggage examination.
  • Species involved. The reptiles included 23 Corn Snakes, 16 Ball Pythons, 5 Argentine Black and White Tegus, and 1 Snow White Iguana.
  • Legal action. The two passengers were arrested under the Customs Act, 1962, and the seized wildlife was handed over to the Air Intelligence Unit for further action.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this wildlife smuggling bust matter for UPSC preparation?

  • Wildlife trafficking enforcement. The case exemplifies intelligence-led enforcement against illegal wildlife trade, a key challenge in biodiversity conservation.
  • Role of DRI and Customs. It highlights the role of the Directorate of Revenue Intelligence and Customs authorities in preventing illegal movement of wildlife through airports.
  • Link to internal security. Wildlife smuggling is linked to organized crime and poses threats to biodiversity and national security, relevant for internal security topics.

Back to basics: Wildlife Trafficking and Enforcement

Wildlife trafficking involves illegal trade or smuggling of animals and plants protected by law. Enforcement agencies like the Directorate of Revenue Intelligence work with Customs and other units to detect and prevent such crimes, which threaten biodiversity and violate national and international laws.
