Cabinet Approves Continuation and Renaming of National Social Assistance Programme till 2030-31

The Union Cabinet approved the continuation of the National Social Assistance Programme (NSAP) with a budget of ₹53,428 crore for five years from 2026-27 to 2030-31, including renaming the scheme and revising eligibility and assistance for disability pensions. This matters for UPSC aspirants as it reflects government social welfare policy updates, budgetary allocations, and changes in scheme design affecting vulnerable populations.

The Cabinet extended NSAP till 2030-31 with ₹53,428 crore, renamed it Pradhan Mantri Samajik Sahayata Yojana, and revised disability pension norms to widen coverage.

Why is this in the news?

On October 14, 2026, the Union Cabinet chaired by the Prime Minister approved the continuation of NSAP with funding and policy changes.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the key changes approved for the National Social Assistance Programme?

  • Scheme continuation and funding. NSAP will continue for five years from 2026-27 to 2030-31 with a total outlay of ₹53,428 crore.
  • Renaming of scheme and sub-schemes. NSAP is renamed as Pradhan Mantri Samajik Sahayata Yojana; old age, widow, disability, and family benefit schemes are renamed accordingly, dropping the 'Indira Gandhi' prefix.
  • Disability pension revisions. Eligibility for disability pension now requires 40% benchmark disability instead of 80%, with assistance rates of ₹375 for ages 0-79 and ₹625 for 80+, and minimum age criterion set since birth.
  • Other pension schemes. Pension schemes for old aged, widows, and family benefit will continue in their existing form.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

Why do these changes matter for social welfare and policy?

  • Uniform central assistance. The scheme provides 100% central assistance to ensure uniform support to beneficiaries across all States and UTs.
  • Coverage and beneficiary impact. Approximately 3.20 crore beneficiaries across 36 States and UTs will benefit from the scheme continuation and revisions.
  • Criticism of fixed budget and coverage. Critics argue the fixed five-year budget does not account for rising beneficiary numbers or inflation, potentially limiting adequacy.
  • State-level pension schemes. Due to limited central coverage capped at 3.09 crore, many States run their own pension schemes, increasing total coverage to about 8.95 crore beneficiaries.

[1] Press Information Bureau[3] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the background and legal basis for NSAP?

  • Launch and constitutional basis. NSAP was launched on 15 August 1995 to assist poor households under Article 41 of the Directive Principles of State Policy.
  • Rights of Persons with Disability Act 2016. The Act required linking disability pension eligibility to 40% benchmark disability and mandated disability assistance to be 25% higher than similar schemes.

Back to basics: Social Welfare Schemes in India

Social welfare schemes are government initiatives aimed at providing financial support and social security to vulnerable and marginalized populations. These schemes often include pensions, subsidies, and direct transfers to improve living conditions and reduce poverty. The National Social Assistance Programme is one such scheme providing pensions to elderly, widows, disabled persons, and families of deceased breadwinners, funded fully by the central government to ensure uniformity across states.
