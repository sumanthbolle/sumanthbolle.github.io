RBI Auction of State Government Securities for ₹24,400 Crore

The Reserve Bank of India announced an auction of State Government Securities totaling ₹24,400 Crore across multiple states with varying tenors and interest rates. Understanding this auction process is important for UPSC aspirants as it relates to state fiscal management, debt market operations, and fiscal federalism.

The RBI conducts auctions of State Government Securities to help states raise funds, impacting fiscal federalism and debt management.

Why is this in the news?

On October 15, 2026, the RBI announced an auction of State Government Securities amounting to ₹24,400 Crore to be conducted on October 19, 2026.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What are State Government Securities and how are they auctioned?

  • Definition. State Government Securities (SGS) are debt instruments issued by state governments to raise funds, governed by the Government Securities Act, 2006 and related regulations.
  • Auction process. The RBI conducts auctions electronically via its Core Banking Solution (E-Kuber) system where competitive and non-competitive bids are submitted by investors.
  • Bid types and allotment. Competitive bids specify yield or price and are accepted based on RBI's determination of maximum yield or minimum price; non-competitive bids allow individuals and institutions to participate with a capped allocation.
  • Tenor and interest. The auction includes securities with various tenors (from 4 to 24 years) and interest rates fixed either at re-issue or determined at auction.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Which states and securities are involved in the auction?

  • States participating. Eleven states including Andhra Pradesh, Goa, Gujarat, Jammu and Kashmir, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Meghalaya, Punjab, Rajasthan, Telangana, and Tripura are offering securities.
  • Aggregate amount. The total amount to be raised is ₹24,400 Crore through re-issues and new issuances of SGS with varying interest rates from 7.53% to 8.08%.
  • Tenor range. The securities have tenors ranging from 4 years to 24 years, reflecting different maturity periods for investors.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Why does the auction of State Government Securities matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Fiscal federalism. SGS auctions illustrate how states mobilize resources independently, reflecting the fiscal autonomy and debt management within India's federal structure.
  • Debt market understanding. Knowledge of SGS auctions helps understand government borrowing mechanisms and their impact on the economy and banking sector.
  • Investment and regulation. SGS qualify as eligible investments for banks under the Statutory Liquidity Ratio, linking them to banking regulations and monetary policy.

Back to basics: Government Securities Auction

Government securities are debt instruments issued by the government to borrow money from the public. Auctions are the process through which these securities are sold to investors, typically involving competitive bidding where investors specify the yield or price they are willing to accept. These securities help governments finance their fiscal deficit and are important for monetary policy and financial market stability.
