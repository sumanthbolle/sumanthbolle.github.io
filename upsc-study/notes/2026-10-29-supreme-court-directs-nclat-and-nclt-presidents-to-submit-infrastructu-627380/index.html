Supreme Court directs NCLAT and NCLT presidents to submit infrastructure and staff proposals

The Supreme Court expressed concern over the Ministry of Corporate Affairs controlling staff appointments at the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) and directed the tribunal presidents to submit proposals on infrastructure and staffing. This development is important for understanding judicial autonomy and reforms in dispute resolution mechanisms.

The Supreme Court emphasized the autonomy of NCLT and NCLAT by directing their presidents to propose infrastructure and staffing needs, highlighting concerns over Ministry interference.

Why is this in the news?

On October 29, 2026, the Supreme Court orally expressed concern about the Ministry of Corporate Affairs 'hijacking' staff appointments at the NCLT and directed the presidents of NCLT and NCLAT to submit comprehensive proposals on infrastructure and staffing.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court observe about NCLT staff appointments?

  • Ministry interference. The Supreme Court observed that the Ministry of Corporate Affairs has taken over staff appointments at the NCLT, which undermines the tribunal's autonomy.
  • Autonomy of appointments. The Court stated that the power to make staff appointments must remain with the NCLT president to maintain an employer-employee relationship between staff and the tribunal.
  • Outsourcing concerns. Senior Advocate Meenakshi Arora highlighted that the appointment process has been outsourced, allowing contractors to influence staffing without regard for judicial or technical qualifications.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What directions did the Supreme Court give regarding infrastructure and staffing?

  • Proposal submission. The Court directed the presidents of NCLT and NCLAT to submit comprehensive proposals within four weeks on infrastructure, buildings, and staffing needs to improve daily functioning.
  • Infrastructure issues. The Court was informed that the NCLT Principal Bench infrastructure is deteriorating, with two floors closed and courts operating at half strength.
  • Data collection. The Court ordered the NCLT Principal Bench Bar Association to collect and tabulate data on infrastructure at all regional benches.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for judicial autonomy and dispute resolution?

  • Preserving tribunal independence. Maintaining appointment powers with tribunal presidents ensures institutional autonomy and prevents external interference from the Ministry.
  • Effective dispute redressal. Improved infrastructure and adequate staffing are essential for the efficient functioning of tribunals that handle corporate law disputes.
  • Government's role. The Court noted tribunals are vital government institutions, highlighting the need for proper support and autonomy to uphold justice delivery.

Back to basics: Judicial autonomy and tribunal infrastructure

Judicial autonomy means that courts and tribunals operate independently without undue influence from the executive branch. Tribunals like the NCLT and NCLAT resolve specialized disputes and require adequate infrastructure and staffing to function efficiently. Maintaining appointment powers within the tribunal preserves this autonomy and ensures accountability.
