PM Modi Criticizes Industry on Wages, Employment, and Manufacturing Quality

On November 19, 2026, PM Modi addressed industry leaders at FICCI's Annual General Meeting, criticizing stagnant wages, lack of job growth, and poor manufacturing standards. This is important for UPSC aspirants studying labour economics, employment policy, and industrial development challenges.

PM Modi urged Indian industry to raise wages, increase employment, improve product quality, and better utilize Free Trade Agreements for economic growth.

Why is this in the news?

PM Modi's remarks at FICCI's Annual General Meeting on November 19, 2026, highlighted concerns over stagnant wages, employment growth, and manufacturing standards.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What criticisms did PM Modi make about Indian industry?

  • Static wages and employment. PM Modi criticized industry leaders for failing to increase employee wages and expand employment despite tax cuts and economic growth.
  • Manufacturing standards. He condemned the practice of exporting best-quality products while selling lower-quality goods domestically, urging improvement in product quality for Indian consumers.
  • Utilization of Free Trade Agreements. PM Modi pointed out that Indian industry is not adequately prepared to exploit opportunities from recent Free Trade Agreements with major economies.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does PM Modi link current issues to historical context?

  • Reference to Gandhi's Swadeshi. PM Modi recalled Gandhi's 1930 call for self-reliance and Swadeshi, highlighting that despite 100 years, manufacturing progress remains limited.
  • Challenge to industry. He questioned whether the lack of progress over a century reflects success or failure of Indian industry.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the implications for economic policy and growth?

  • Tax benefits not passed on. Despite corporate tax reductions to 22% and income tax relief for individuals earning up to ₹12 lakh, industries have not increased wages or employment proportionately.
  • Need for quality improvement. Improving domestic product quality is essential to meet the expectations of India's 1.4 billion consumers and reduce reliance on export-quality standards.
  • Seizing trade opportunities. Indian industries must proactively plan to leverage Free Trade Agreements to boost exports and economic growth.

Back to basics: Labour wages and employment growth

Wages refer to the monetary compensation paid to workers for their labour. Employment growth indicates the increase in the number of jobs available in the economy. Both are key indicators of economic development and inclusive growth, reflecting how benefits of growth reach workers and improve living standards.
