Election Commission Assures Supreme Court on Electoral Roll Accuracy

The Election Commission of India (ECI) filed a supplementary affidavit on November 14, 2026, assuring the Supreme Court that it is actively ensuring all eligible voters find their names on the electoral rolls. This update is important for UPSC aspirants to understand ongoing electoral reforms and the role of constitutional bodies in safeguarding voter rights.

The ECI has assured the Supreme Court that it is taking measures to include all eligible voters in the electoral rolls and has clarified procedures for voters who have shifted within states.

Why is this in the news?

The Election Commission of India filed a supplementary affidavit in the Supreme Court on November 14, 2026, detailing measures to ensure eligible voters are included in electoral rolls.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What measures has the ECI taken to ensure voters find their names on electoral rolls?

  • Active inclusion efforts. The ECI assured the Supreme Court that it remains alert to the need to include all eligible voters, especially those wrongly left out as absent, shifted, dead, or duplicate in draft electoral rolls.
  • Submission of forms simplified. Voters who have shifted within the same state need not submit Form 8; instead, they can submit an Enumeration Form (Annexure A) with a statement of their new address and proof to the Electoral Registration Officer (ERO) or Assistant ERO.
  • Online and offline options. Voters can submit forms in person, through family members, or online with e-sign on the ECINET portal, including a facility to book a call with Booth Level Officers (BLOs).
  • Processing and re-inclusion. Enumeration Forms submitted by left-out electors are processed according to the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960, with the same Elector Photo Identity Card (EPIC) number reassigned to them.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How is the ECI addressing concerns about electoral roll accuracy and oversight?

  • Directions to States and UTs. The ECI issued directions on November 6, 2026, to Chief Electoral Officers to allow submission of Enumeration Forms during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls.
  • Display of left-out voters. Booth-wise lists of left-out electors marked as absent, shifted, dead, or duplicate are displayed with probable reasons for non-inclusion to enhance transparency.
  • Oversight of IT Division. A senior Deputy Election Commissioner (DEC) was assigned oversight of the ECI’s IT Division following a new work order on November 4, 2026, addressing concerns about bypassing DEC-level supervision.
  • Avoiding duplication. A mechanism is in place to ensure no eligible elector is deprived of inclusion while avoiding duplication in enrolment.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What controversy did the ECI clarify regarding voter re-inclusion procedures?

  • Petitioners’ concerns. Activists raised concerns that previously registered voters excluded in draft rolls were being forced to submit Form 6, meant for new voters, risking criminal prosecution under the Representation of the People Act, 1950.
  • ECI’s clarification. The ECI clarified that voters who had already submitted Form 6 need not submit the Enumeration Form again and their applications would be processed without fear of prosecution.

Back to basics: Electoral Roll and Voter Registration

An electoral roll is an official list of eligible voters in a constituency. Voter registration involves verifying eligibility and including names in the roll to enable participation in elections. The Election Commission of India oversees the preparation and revision of electoral rolls to ensure accuracy and inclusiveness, using forms like Form 6 for new registrations and Form 8 for shifting addresses within constituencies.

Answer and trap

Answer: (b). Statement 1 is incorrect because voters who have shifted within the state do not need to submit Form 8; they submit an Enumeration Form instead.

Quick revision

Who was assigned oversight of the ECI’s IT Division in November 2026?

No, they do not need to submit Form 6 again; their applications will be processed without fear of prosecution.
