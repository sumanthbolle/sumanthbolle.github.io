Supreme Court directs increase in tribunals for West Bengal SIR appeals

The Supreme Court ordered the Election Commission of India and Calcutta High Court to increase tribunals hearing appeals related to the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls in West Bengal. This ruling is important for understanding judicial oversight in election dispute resolution and the functioning of federal institutions.

The Supreme Court mandated more tribunals and priority hearings for voter deletion appeals after West Bengal's SIR.

Why is this in the news?

On November 23, 2026, the Supreme Court ordered the Election Commission of India and Calcutta High Court to increase tribunals for appeals related to West Bengal's Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court order regarding tribunals for West Bengal SIR appeals?

  • Increase tribunals. The Supreme Court directed the Election Commission of India and the Calcutta High Court to increase the number of tribunals hearing appeals about inclusion and exclusion of names in West Bengal's electoral rolls after the Special Intensive Revision.
  • Segregate appeals. Tribunals must segregate appeals filed by deleted voters from others and give urgent, first-priority hearings to the deleted voters' appeals.
  • Defer inclusion appeals. Appeals filed against inclusion of names in the voter list can be deferred by the tribunals.
  • Priority for Kolkata appeals. Appeals filed by excluded voters from the Kolkata Municipal Corporation area, which is going to polls before December, must be identified and heard on priority within a week.
  • Change tribunal locations. The Supreme Court also ordered that tribunal premises be shifted to central Kolkata and near the airport for better accessibility.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for election dispute resolution and federal institutions?

  • Judicial oversight. The Supreme Court's intervention ensures timely and efficient resolution of electoral roll disputes, which is critical for free and fair elections.
  • Role of Election Commission. The Election Commission of India is directed to cooperate with the judiciary to enhance the capacity of tribunals, reflecting federal institutional coordination.
  • Impact on electoral process. Prioritizing deleted voters' appeals helps protect voter rights and maintains the integrity of the electoral rolls before upcoming elections.

Back to basics: Election Tribunals and Electoral Rolls

Election tribunals are quasi-judicial bodies that hear disputes related to electoral rolls, such as inclusion or deletion of voter names. Electoral rolls are official lists of eligible voters in a constituency, and their accuracy is crucial for fair elections. The Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is a process to update and verify these rolls before elections. Judicial oversight ensures disputes are resolved promptly to uphold electoral integrity.
