Union Health Ministry Launches Drug De-addiction Capacity Building Programme

On October 26, 2026, Union Health Minister Shri J.P. Nadda launched a capacity building training programme for medical and allied healthcare professionals engaged in drug de-addiction services. This initiative aims to train 10,000 service providers by 2028-29 to strengthen treatment and management of substance use disorders, supporting the national goal of a Nasha Mukt Bharat by 2029.

The Union Health Ministry's capacity building programme trains healthcare professionals to improve drug de-addiction services, aligning with the Vision Document on Narcotic Control: 2026-2029.

Why is this in the news?

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the Capacity Building Programme for Drug De-addiction Services?

  • Programme launch and aim. The programme was launched on October 26, 2026, to strengthen the capacity of healthcare professionals in prevention, identification, assessment, treatment, and management of substance use disorders across India.
  • Training targets. It aims to train 10,000 service providers including doctors, nurses, counsellors, and allied healthcare personnel by March 2029.
  • Training methods. The programme uses a blended learning approach with online live sessions, self-paced online modules, and offline in-person training.
  • Institutions involved. Six central government institutions under the Drug De-addiction Programme (DDAP), coordinated by the National Drug Dependence Treatment Centre (NDDTC), AIIMS, implement the training.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this programme matter for public health and governance?

  • National goal of Nasha Mukt Bharat. The programme supports the Government of India's commitment to achieve a Nasha Mukt Bharat by 2029 through coordinated multi-ministerial efforts.
  • Whole-of-government approach. Effective substance abuse control requires synergy between healthcare, social welfare, and law enforcement agencies to address prevention, treatment, enforcement, and trafficking.
  • Addressing coordination gaps. The Vision Document on Narcotic Control: 2026-2029 identifies gaps caused by fragmented departmental efforts and calls for sustained inter-ministerial coordination and accountability.
  • Capacity building as a key strategy. Strengthening treatment services and healthcare professional capacity is central to improving evidence-based addiction care and expanding access.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How is the training programme structured and implemented?

  • Training curriculum. The curriculum covers various substances including alcohol, cannabis, opioids, sleeping tablets, and inhalants, and addresses specific groups such as women and adolescents.
  • Training delivery. Doctors receive weekly 12-week online live sessions combining theory and clinical case discussions; nurses, CHOs, and ANMs have self-paced online modules; offline training is also provided.
  • Participant nomination. Participants are nominated by State Governments through the National Health Mission and by Central Government services including Armed Forces and Central Armed Police Forces.
  • Certification and resources. Certificates are issued upon completion, and resources like the second edition of 'Substance Use Disorder: Manual for Physicians' guide clinical practice.

Back to basics: Capacity Building in Public Health

Capacity building in public health involves training and equipping healthcare professionals with knowledge and skills to effectively prevent, diagnose, and manage health conditions. It ensures quality service delivery and strengthens health systems by expanding workforce competence and resources. In the context of drug de-addiction, capacity building enables healthcare workers to provide evidence-based treatment and support rehabilitation efforts.
