INDIA Bloc Plans Public Impeachment of Chief Election Commissioner

The INDIA political alliance announced a public impeachment proceeding against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar on October 30, 2026. This event highlights constitutional provisions for the removal of the CEC and raises questions on electoral governance and accountability.

The INDIA bloc's public impeachment proceeding against the CEC spotlights constitutional removal procedures and electoral oversight issues.

Why is this in the news?

On October 24, 2026, Congress leader K.C. Venugopal announced that the INDIA bloc will hold a public impeachment proceeding against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar on October 30, 2026.

What is the INDIA bloc's action against the Chief Election Commissioner?

  • Public impeachment proceeding. The INDIA bloc plans to hold a public impeachment proceeding of CEC Gyanesh Kumar on October 30, 2026, as part of their demand for his removal.
  • Follow-up protest march. After the proceeding, a 'Dilli Chalo March' against alleged vote theft will culminate at Ramleela Maidan on November 1, 2026, with expected participation of at least a million citizens.
  • Political coordination. Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge has requested all INDIA bloc parties to be present in Delhi in full strength during this period.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What constitutional provisions govern the removal of the Chief Election Commissioner?

  • Removal grounds and process. Article 324(5) of the Constitution provides that the CEC can be removed on grounds and by a procedure similar to that of a Supreme Court judge.
  • Difference from impeachment of President. The term 'impeachment' under Article 61 applies only to the President, not the CEC.

Back to basics: Removal of Chief Election Commissioner

The Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) is appointed to oversee elections in India and ensure their fairness. The Constitution provides that the CEC can be removed only through a process similar to that of a Supreme Court judge, which requires proven misbehavior or incapacity and a parliamentary process. This safeguards the independence of the Election Commission as a constitutional body.

Common mistake. The removal of the Chief Election Commissioner is not through impeachment under Article 61, which applies only to the President.
