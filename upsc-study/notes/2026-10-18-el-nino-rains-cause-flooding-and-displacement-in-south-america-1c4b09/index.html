El Nino Causes Flooding and Displacement in South America

Heavy rains linked to El Nino have caused flooding in Argentina, Paraguay, and Uruguay, forcing thousands to evacuate and damaging livestock. This event highlights the global impact of El Nino on weather patterns and disaster management, relevant for UPSC exams.

El Nino-driven heavy rains have led to severe flooding and displacement in parts of South America, demonstrating the climate phenomenon's wide-reaching effects.

Why is this in the news?

In October 2026, heavy rains linked to El Nino caused flooding in Argentina, Paraguay, and Uruguay, leading to evacuations and disruptions.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What caused the flooding in South America?

  • El Nino phenomenon. The flooding was caused by heavy rains linked to El Nino, a natural climate event where warmer-than-usual Pacific Ocean waters disrupt global weather patterns.
  • Affected rivers. Three major rivers—the Parana, Uruguay, and Iguazu—originating in Brazil and flowing south through the region, swelled and overflowed their banks.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the impacts of the floods?

  • Evacuations and displacement. Thousands of people were forced to evacuate their homes, especially in Paraguay's border city of Ayolas and Argentina's Corrientes province.
  • Livestock and agriculture. Ranchers had to move cattle to higher ground as pastures flooded, with calves struggling against strong currents.
  • Tourism disruption. Iguazu Falls, a major tourist attraction on the Argentina-Brazil border, closed its Devil's Throat walkway due to high river levels.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for UPSC preparation?

  • Climate change and disaster management. Understanding El Nino's effects helps explain how climate phenomena influence weather extremes and require disaster preparedness.
  • Global environmental impact. El Nino's disruption of weather patterns affects multiple continents, illustrating interconnected environmental challenges.

Back to basics: El Nino Phenomenon

El Nino is a climate pattern involving the periodic warming of sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern Pacific Ocean. This warming alters atmospheric circulation and disrupts normal weather patterns globally, often causing heavy rains in some regions and droughts in others. It is a key driver of climate variability and impacts agriculture, water resources, and disaster management.

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only

Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the flooding affected northeastern Argentina, southern Paraguay, and western Uruguay, not mainly northern Brazil.
