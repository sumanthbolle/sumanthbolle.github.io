Cyclone Arnab: Very Severe Cyclonic Storm Threatens Andhra Pradesh

Cyclone Arnab is approaching Andhra Pradesh as a Very Severe Cyclonic Storm, the first since Titli in 2018. Understanding its intensity and impact is important for disaster management and climate studies in the region.

Cyclone Arnab is the 11th Very Severe Cyclonic Storm to threaten Andhra Pradesh since 1970, highlighting the state's vulnerability to intense cyclones.

Why is this in the news?

Cyclone Arnab is approaching Andhra Pradesh in October 2026 as a Very Severe Cyclonic Storm, prompting preparations and concerns about damage.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the intensity and history of cyclones affecting Andhra Pradesh?

  • Cyclone classification. The India Meteorological Department classifies cyclones by wind speed: Cyclonic Storm (62-88 kmph), Severe Cyclonic Storm (89-117 kmph), Very Severe Cyclonic Storm (118-167 kmph), Extremely Severe Cyclonic Storm (168-221 kmph), and Super Cyclonic Storm (above 221 kmph).
  • Past cyclone occurrences. Since 1970, Andhra Pradesh has experienced 61 cyclonic storms, with 10 classified as Very Severe Cyclonic Storms before Arnab.
  • Notable past storms. Major storms include Phailin (2000 kmph winds), Hudhud (181.6 kmph winds), and Titli (2018), the last Very Severe Cyclonic Storm before Arnab.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does Cyclone Arnab matter for disaster management?

  • Potential impact. Arnab is expected to have maximum wind speeds around 140 kmph, making it a Very Severe Cyclonic Storm capable of causing significant damage.
  • Preparedness. Andhra Pradesh is bracing for Arnab, the first VSCS in nearly eight years, highlighting the need for effective disaster response and mitigation.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does Arnab fit into the pattern of cyclones in the region?

  • Frequency of VSCS. Arnab would be the 11th Very Severe Cyclonic Storm to affect Andhra Pradesh since 1970, showing a pattern of recurring intense cyclones.
  • Recent cyclone trends. Recent cyclones like Montha and Michaung were less intense, classified only as Severe Cyclonic Storms, unlike Arnab.

Back to basics: Cyclone classification and disaster management

Cyclones are classified by their wind speeds, which indicate their intensity and potential damage. Disaster management involves preparing for such storms through early warnings, evacuations, and infrastructure readiness to reduce loss of life and property.
