Cyclone Arnab's Landfall and Impact on Andhra Pradesh and Odisha

Cyclone Arnab, a very severe cyclonic storm, made landfall near the Andhra Pradesh coast on October 28, 2026, causing heavy rains, strong winds, and disruption in multiple states. The Union Home Minister assured full central assistance, with rescue operations underway and impact assessments planned, highlighting disaster management and inter-state coordination.

Cyclone Arnab's landfall near Andhra Pradesh triggered large-scale evacuations and rescue efforts, demonstrating the central government's role in disaster response and the vulnerability of India's eastern coast to severe cyclones.

What was the nature and path of Cyclone Arnab?

  • Cyclone classification. Cyclone Arnab was classified as a 'very severe cyclonic storm' with maximum sustained winds of 125-135 kmph gusting to 150 kmph.
  • Landfall location and timing. The cyclone made landfall near the Andhra Pradesh coast between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam, close to south of Yanam, starting around 1:20 a.m. on October 28, 2026.
  • Movement and impact area. The cyclone moved at 18-23 kmph, with its northern spiral band entering land and affecting coastal districts of Andhra Pradesh and southern Odisha.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

How did the states and central government respond to Cyclone Arnab?

  • Central government coordination. Union Home Minister Amit Shah spoke with Chief Ministers of Andhra Pradesh, Puducherry, Odisha, and Chhattisgarh, assuring full assistance and deploying NDRF and SDRF teams for rescue and relief.
  • State-level preparedness. Andhra Pradesh evacuated about 89,000 people from coastal areas, set up cyclone shelters, and mobilized sanitation and road-clearing teams; Odisha deployed 60 teams of ODRAF and put police on high alert.
  • Impact mitigation measures. Authorities warned of severe waterlogging, power outages, and falling trees; flights and trains were cancelled to ensure safety; fishermen were advised to stay ashore.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What were the immediate impacts and challenges caused by Cyclone Arnab?

  • Rainfall and wind damage. Visakhapatnam recorded over 100 mm of rain in a day, with 206 trees uprooted and roads blocked; heavy rains caused flooding and power cuts in multiple districts.
  • Casualties and safety issues. One man died after coming into contact with a snapped live wire in Visakhapatnam; reluctance to evacuate was noted in Yanam despite warnings.
  • Transport disruptions. Numerous flights were cancelled at Vijayawada and Visakhapatnam airports, and train services were affected in Telangana and neighboring states.

Back to basics: Cyclones and Disaster Management in India

A cyclone is a large-scale air mass that rotates around a strong center of low atmospheric pressure, often causing heavy rains and strong winds. India’s eastern coast is vulnerable to cyclones originating in the Bay of Bengal. Disaster management involves preparedness, timely evacuation, rescue operations by agencies like NDRF and SDRF, and coordination between central and state governments to reduce loss of life and property.
