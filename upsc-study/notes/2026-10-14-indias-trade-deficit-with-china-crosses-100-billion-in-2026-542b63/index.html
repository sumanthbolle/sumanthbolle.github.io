India’s Trade Deficit with China Surpasses $100 Billion in 2026

India’s trade deficit with China crossed $100 billion in 2026, continuing a trend of widening imbalance despite growth in exports. This matters for UPSC aspirants as it highlights key economic and diplomatic challenges in India-China relations.

India’s trade deficit with China exceeded $100 billion in 2026 due to rising imports and a lopsided trade relationship.

Why is this in the news?

Data released by China’s General Administration of Customs in October 2026 showed India’s trade deficit with China crossed $100 billion, highlighting ongoing trade imbalances.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the recent trade figures between India and China?

  • Trade volume growth. Two-way trade between January and September 2026 increased by 25.4% to $144.45 billion.
  • Import rise. India’s imports from China rose by 23.3% to $125.19 billion in the same period.
  • Export growth. India’s exports to China grew by 40.7% to $19.26 billion, reaching last year’s total within nine months.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the trade deficit with China matter?

  • Widening imbalance. The trade deficit stood at $105.93 billion in 2026, surpassing the previous year’s record of $116.12 billion and reflecting a heavily lopsided trade relationship.
  • Concerns for India. India is concerned about lack of market access for its firms, reliance on finished goods imports, and Chinese export controls on key sectors like fertilisers and rare earths.
  • Diplomatic discussions. The trade deficit was a key topic in talks between Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Chinese President Xi Jinping at the BRICS summit in 2026.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are India’s major exports and imports with China?

  • Exports. India’s major exports to China include seafood products, naphtha, and printed circuit boards, some of which India previously imported in bulk from China.
  • Imports. India continues to import large quantities of electronics, machinery, and intermediates necessary for manufacturing from China.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How are India and China addressing trade issues?

  • Bilateral talks. Both countries agreed to address structural trade imbalances, supply chain issues, and facilitate meaningful market access during the 2026 BRICS summit.
  • Cultural and business ties. The leaders emphasized promoting cultural exchanges, business linkages, and greater mobility to improve bilateral relations.

Back to basics: Trade Deficit

A trade deficit occurs when a country imports more goods and services than it exports, resulting in a negative balance of trade. It indicates that more money is leaving the country to pay for imports than is coming in from exports. Persistent trade deficits can affect a country’s currency value, foreign exchange reserves, and economic policy decisions.
