India implications

  • Prevention messaging can support healthier youth behaviour and reduce stigma around seeking help, provided it is linked to accessible and confidential services.
  • Educational institutions and community volunteers can act as early-contact points for awareness, referral and community mobilisation.
  • Policy evaluation should distinguish inputs such as quizzes, pledges and activities from outcomes such as treatment completion, recovery, reintegration and relapse prevention.
  • Digital delivery may widen participation but can exclude persons with weak connectivity, low digital literacy or limited access to smartphones.

Way forward

  • Integrate digital awareness with confidential counselling, screening, referral and accessible treatment services.
  • Use schools, colleges, families, community organisations and trained local volunteers for sustained prevention and early support.
  • Publish disaggregated monitoring indicators covering reach, referrals, treatment continuity, rehabilitation, reintegration and relapse prevention.
  • Design multilingual, low-bandwidth and offline alternatives so digital campaigns do not exclude vulnerable groups.
