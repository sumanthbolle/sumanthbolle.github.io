Supreme Court on West Bengal SIR Appeals: Passport Validity and Tribunal Hearings

The Supreme Court observed that deleted voters with valid Indian passports will face fewer hurdles in proving their cases for re-inclusion in West Bengal's electoral rolls during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR). This affects the priority hearing of appeals in appellate tribunals and the electoral justice process.

The Supreme Court directed priority hearing for appeals of voters deleted in West Bengal's SIR, noting valid passports ease re-inclusion challenges.

Why is this in the news?

On November 27, 2026, the Supreme Court made observations on the priority hearing of appeals related to West Bengal's SIR electoral roll revisions and the role of valid passports in easing re-inclusion.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the issue with West Bengal's Special Intensive Revision (SIR) appeals?

  • Deleted voters' appeals. Over 22 lakh voters were deleted from West Bengal's electoral roll during the SIR exercise, leading to appeals for re-inclusion in appellate tribunals.
  • Tribunal hearing priority. The Supreme Court ordered tribunals to prioritize hearing appeals filed by deleted voters seeking re-inclusion, deferring appeals challenging inclusions.
  • Scope of priority hearing. Petitioners requested extending priority hearing beyond Kolkata Municipal Corporation to six other Municipal Corporations where elections are due.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does passport validity affect the appeals process?

  • Passport as identity proof. The Supreme Court noted that deleted voters possessing valid Indian passports will face fewer hurdles in proving their case before appellate tribunals.
  • Police involvement concerns. The West Bengal government deployed police for house-to-house checks of appellants, which was seen as interference with the Supreme Court's process.
  • Court's response. The Court emphasized the importance of election and inclusion, suggesting tribunals could dispose of appeals within 30 days.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for electoral justice and governance?

  • Large scale appeals. Over 38 lakh appeals were filed in 19 tribunals in West Bengal, with 22 lakh by deleted voters and 16.10 lakh opposing certain inclusions.
  • Election Commission's role. The Election Commission clarified it was not involved in police actions and acknowledged the appeals filed from within the West Bengal CEO's office.
  • Ensuring fair elections. Prioritizing appeals of deleted voters and ensuring valid identity proofs like passports help uphold electoral rights and smooth election conduct.

Back to basics: Electoral Roll Revision and Appellate Tribunals

Electoral rolls are periodically revised to ensure accurate voter lists. Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is a focused exercise to update these rolls. Appellate tribunals are quasi-judicial bodies that hear appeals related to inclusion or exclusion of names in electoral rolls, ensuring electoral justice and fair participation in elections.
