Sheikh Hasina's Return to Bangladesh Faces Legal and Political Hurdles

Sheikh Hasina seeks travel documents to return to Bangladesh amid a death sentence by the International Crimes Tribunal for a police crackdown. This situation impacts India-Bangladesh relations and regional political stability.

Sheikh Hasina's attempt to return to Bangladesh is complicated by legal charges and extradition demands, affecting bilateral ties with India.

Why is this in the news?

On November 9, 2026, Sheikh Hasina applied for a new passport and national identity card from Bangladesh, prompting the government to seek her extradition under a prisoner transfer process.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What legal challenges does Sheikh Hasina face for returning to Bangladesh?

  • Death sentence by ICT. Sheikh Hasina has been sentenced to death by the International Crimes Tribunal in Dhaka for ordering the police crackdown during July-August 2024.
  • Extradition demand. The Bangladesh government has sought her extradition through an acceptable prisoner transfer process after she applied for travel documents.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does Sheikh Hasina want to return to Bangladesh?

  • Political leadership. Sheikh Hasina aims to return as a political leader to reclaim control over her party, the Awami League.
  • Facing justice dilemma. She faces the challenge of returning to face legal charges while maintaining her political position.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does this issue affect India-Bangladesh relations?

  • India's role in travel documents. Sheikh Hasina has been in India since August 5, 2024, and her efforts to obtain travel documents involve diplomatic considerations between India and Bangladesh.
  • Regional political stability. The legal and political complexities surrounding her return have implications for bilateral ties and regional security dynamics.

Back to basics: Extradition and International Crimes Tribunal

Extradition is a formal process where one country surrenders a suspected or convicted criminal to another country. The International Crimes Tribunal is a domestic court in Bangladesh that tries individuals for crimes against humanity and war crimes, issuing sentences such as death penalties. Understanding these concepts helps in grasping the legal and diplomatic challenges in cross-border political cases.
