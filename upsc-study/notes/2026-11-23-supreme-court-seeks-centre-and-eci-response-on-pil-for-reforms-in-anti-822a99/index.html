Supreme Court Seeks Responses on PIL for Anti-Defection Law Reforms

The Supreme Court on November 23, 2026, sought responses from the Centre, Election Commission of India, Law Commission, and relevant ministries on a PIL demanding reforms in the anti-defection law. These reforms aim to address misuse of resignations by legislators to evade disqualification and manipulate government stability, a critical issue for governance and legislative ethics.

The Supreme Court has initiated a process to examine reforms in the anti-defection law to prevent strategic resignations that undermine legislative discipline and government stability.

Why is this in the news?

The Supreme Court issued notices on November 23, 2026, seeking responses on a PIL filed for comprehensive reforms in the anti-defection law to curb misuse of resignations by elected representatives.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What issues does the PIL raise about the anti-defection law?

  • Misuse of resignations. The PIL highlights that elected representatives misuse resignations to avoid disqualification proceedings under the Tenth Schedule and influence government survival.
  • Strategic resignations. Legislators facing or anticipating disqualification often resign strategically before the Speaker or Chairman can decide on their cases, enabling political realignments and altering assembly strength.
  • Need for adjudication reforms. The PIL seeks time-bound, reasoned, and transparent adjudication of resignations and disqualification proceedings, and institutional review of the Speaker-centred adjudicatory mechanism.
  • Majority testing in hung assemblies. It demands reaffirmation that government majority in a hung assembly must be tested on the floor of the House after members are sworn in, not through extra-parliamentary letters or demonstrations.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What directions does the PIL seek from the Supreme Court?

  • Non-termination of proceedings. Resignations tendered when disqualification proceedings are pending or imminent should not automatically terminate those proceedings.
  • Speaker/Chairman role. Speakers and Chairpersons should record reasoned orders based on objective material on the voluntariness and genuineness of resignations and independently adjudicate pending disqualification petitions.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why do these reforms matter for governance and elections?

  • Preventing political manipulation. Reforms aim to stop elected representatives from evading accountability by resigning strategically to avoid disqualification.
  • Ensuring legislative discipline. Transparent and timely adjudication strengthens the anti-defection framework, promoting ethical conduct among legislators.
  • Protecting government stability. Clear rules on majority testing and resignation handling prevent undue influence on government formation and survival.

Back to basics: Anti-Defection Law

The anti-defection law aims to prevent elected legislators from switching parties after elections, which can destabilize governments. It provides grounds for disqualification of members who defect from their party. The law's adjudication is typically handled by the Speaker or Chairman of the legislature, who decides on disqualification petitions. However, concerns about misuse and delays have led to calls for reforms.
