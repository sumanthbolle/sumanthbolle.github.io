RBI Bulletin Highlights Broad-Based Pressure on Food Prices in October 2026

The Reserve Bank of India’s November 2026 Bulletin reports that headline CPI inflation rose to 6.1% in October 2026, driven mainly by broad-based increases in food prices. This development is important for understanding inflation dynamics and monetary policy challenges in India.

The RBI Bulletin shows that food inflation was the primary driver pushing CPI inflation above the 6% upper tolerance level in October 2026.

Why is this in the news?

The RBI released its November 2026 Bulletin on November 25, highlighting rising food price inflation and its impact on overall CPI inflation.

[1] Reserve Bank of India[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current inflation scenario in India?

  • Headline CPI inflation rise. CPI inflation increased to 6.1% year-on-year in October 2026 from 5.5% in September 2026, slightly above the RBI’s upper tolerance limit of 6%.
  • Food inflation as main driver. The rise in inflation was primarily driven by broad-based pressure on food prices, including cereals, pulses, and edible oils.
  • Other inflation components. Fuel and core inflation also firmed up, with 11 of the 12 CPI divisions registering sequential increases in October.

[1] Reserve Bank of India[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Which food items contributed to inflationary pressures?

  • Cereals and pulses. Prices of all major cereals and pulses recorded increases, contributing to food inflation.
  • Edible oils. Edible oil prices saw a broad-based increase, led by mustard oil.
  • Vegetables. Vegetable prices showed mixed trends: onion prices rose moderately, tomato prices corrected sharply, and potato prices increased.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the geographical and external factors influencing inflation?

  • Rural and urban inflation. Inflation increased in both rural and urban areas, with food and beverages inflation above 6% in both segments.
  • State-level inflation. Most States and Union Territories recorded inflation in the 4-6% range, but the number reporting inflation above 6% increased.
  • Global factors. Geopolitical instability in West Asia and volatile crude oil prices contributed to uncertainty and inflationary pressures.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

How is the Indian economy responding amid inflationary pressures?

  • Economic resilience. Despite global challenges and monetary tightening by major central banks, India’s domestic economic activity showed resilience supported by strong credit growth.
  • Exports and financial markets. Sustained double-digit export growth and robust corporate earnings supported equity markets and foreign portfolio inflows.
  • Credit inflows. Net inflows through external commercial borrowings and NRI deposits remained strong, supporting economic expansion.

Back to basics: Consumer Price Index (CPI) and Inflation

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) measures the average change over time in the prices paid by consumers for a market basket of goods and services. Inflation refers to the rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services rises, eroding purchasing power. The RBI targets inflation within a tolerance band to maintain price stability while supporting growth.
