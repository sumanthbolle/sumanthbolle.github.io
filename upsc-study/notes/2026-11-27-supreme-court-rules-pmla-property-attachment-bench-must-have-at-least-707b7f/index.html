Supreme Court mandates judicial member in PMLA property attachment benches

The Supreme Court ruled that the Adjudicating Authority (AA) under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) must include at least one judicial member when deciding on property attachments. This ruling clarifies the legal composition of the AA and emphasizes judicial oversight in anti-money laundering enforcement, relevant for both Prelims and Mains.

The Supreme Court held that PMLA property attachment benches must have at least one judicial member to ensure legal objectivity in adjudication.

Why is this in the news?

On November 26, 2026, the Supreme Court ruled on petitions regarding the composition of the Adjudicating Authority under the PMLA, emphasizing the need for judicial members on benches deciding property attachment.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Supreme Court's ruling on PMLA Adjudicating Authority composition?

  • Judicial member required. The Supreme Court held that the Adjudicating Authority (AA) under the PMLA must have at least one judicial member on the bench adjudicating property attachment cases.
  • Role of the AA. The AA adjudicates whether a citizen's property is 'proceeds of crime' and confirms attachment, functioning through benches composed of judicial, administrative, and financial members.
  • Legal objectivity. Presence of a judicial member ensures legal objectivity and proper interpretation of law and precedents in decisions involving property attachment.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this ruling matter for anti-money laundering enforcement?

  • Pre-trial property attachment. The PMLA permits the State to attach a citizen's property even before guilt is proven, making the AA's role critical in protecting civil rights.
  • Substantial consequences. Adjudication under Section 8 of the PMLA can lead to dispossession of property until trial conclusion, affecting constitutional rights.
  • Need for judicial competence. The court emphasized that adjudication requires a legal mind to ensure fair and lawful decisions on property attachment.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What changes did the Court suggest regarding the PMLA framework?

  • Original multiple AAs. Originally, the PMLA provided for multiple Adjudicating Authorities, but a 2009 amendment centralized it into a single body.
  • Repeal recommendation. The Supreme Court urged the Union government to consider repealing the 2009 amendment and revert to multiple AAs to handle rising complaints and attachments.
  • Workload concerns. A single AA may be overwhelmed by the increasing number of complaints and provisional attachments each year.

Back to basics: Adjudicating Authority under PMLA

The Adjudicating Authority (AA) under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) is a quasi-judicial body that decides whether property is proceeds of crime and can be attached or confiscated. It functions through benches composed of judicial, administrative, and financial members. The AA's decisions affect the possession rights of individuals even before conviction, making its composition and legal competence crucial.

What happens next?

  • Reconsider amendment. The Supreme Court recommended that the government consider repealing the 2009 amendment to restore multiple Adjudicating Authorities to manage workload effectively.
