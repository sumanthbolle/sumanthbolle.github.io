Special Parliament Session to Consider Amended Delimitation Bill in 2026

The Indian government has called a special Parliament session from November 30 to December 3, 2026, to table and discuss the amended Delimitation Bill. This session is significant for UPSC aspirants as it involves constitutional amendments affecting electoral representation and federal balance.

The amended Delimitation Bill aims to redraw parliamentary constituencies using the 2011 Census and extend the freeze on seat allocation based on the 1971 Census, impacting state representation in the Lok Sabha.

Why is this in the news?

The government announced a special Parliament sitting starting November 30, 2026, to pass the amended Delimitation Bill after over 100 days since the last session adjourned.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the purpose of the special Parliament session?

  • Session dates and agenda. Parliament will meet from November 30 to December 3, 2026, to consider the amended Delimitation Bill, which proposes changes to the allocation and redrawing of Lok Sabha seats.
  • Bill clearance. The Union Cabinet is scheduled to meet on November 27, 2026, to clear the amended legislation before it is tabled in Parliament.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the key changes proposed in the amended Delimitation Bill?

  • Uniform seat increase. The Bill proposes a uniform 50% increase in the share of Lok Sabha seats across states.
  • Extension of freeze on seat allocation. It extends the freeze on using the 1971 Census for determining the proportional strength of states in the Lok Sabha by 25 years.
  • Use of 2011 Census for redrawing. The Bill proposes using the 2011 Census data to redraw constituencies within each state.
  • Replication of 2001 amendment. These changes replicate the formula introduced by the Constitution (Eighty-fourth Amendment) Act, 2001.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the Delimitation Bill matter for federalism and electoral representation?

  • Impact on state representation. If the freeze on seat allocation lapses after 2026, states like Tamil Nadu could lose Lok Sabha seats based on new Census data, affecting their political influence.
  • Political arithmetic. The government expects to secure support from various parties to achieve the two-thirds majority needed to pass the Bill.
  • Opposition concerns. Opposition leaders have criticized the timing and intent of the Bill, calling it an assault on the Constitution and democratic processes.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How are political parties responding to the Bill?

  • Government alliances. The NDA is confident of support from allied parties and some breakaway factions, aiming for a two-thirds majority.
  • Opposition stance. Congress and other opposition parties have expressed strong opposition, alleging constitutional violations.
  • DMK's position. DMK has not publicly opposed the Bill yet, with some leaders concerned about potential loss of seats if the freeze is lifted.

Back to basics: Delimitation and its Constitutional significance

Delimitation is the process of redrawing the boundaries of electoral constituencies to reflect changes in population. It ensures fair representation in Parliament by adjusting seat allocation among states based on Census data. The Constitution allows Parliament to amend rules on delimitation, but such changes affect federal balance and political representation.
