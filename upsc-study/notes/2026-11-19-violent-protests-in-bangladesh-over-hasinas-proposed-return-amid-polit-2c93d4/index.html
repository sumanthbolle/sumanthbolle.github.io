Violent Protests in Bangladesh Over Hasina’s Proposed Return

Supporters of deposed Bangladesh Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina staged violent protests in her hometown Gopalganj in November 2026 amid tensions over her planned return from India. The protests and legal-political developments are significant for India-Bangladesh relations and regional security.

Sheikh Hasina’s planned return to Bangladesh from India triggered violent protests and legal-political tensions affecting bilateral relations.

Why is this in the news?

The protests erupted in November 2026 as Sheikh Hasina announced her intention to return to Bangladesh from India, where she has been staying since fleeing in August 2024 after being ousted.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered the protests in Bangladesh?

  • Hasina’s return plan. Sheikh Hasina announced her intention to return to Bangladesh in December 2026 despite a death sentence against her by Bangladesh’s International Crimes Tribunal.
  • Protests in hometown. Supporters of Hasina’s Awami League party staged violent protests in Gopalganj, attacking police and pulling down a monument marking her government’s ouster.
  • Political tensions. The protests occurred amid heightened political tensions due to Hasina’s legal status and the government’s efforts to extradite her from India.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the legal and political context of Hasina’s return?

  • Hasina’s exile and sentence. Hasina fled to India in August 2024 after a student-led uprising toppled her government and was sentenced to death in absentia in November 2025 for crimes against humanity.
  • Extradition efforts. Bangladesh has sought Hasina’s extradition from India under a bilateral treaty, completing legal processes but awaiting India’s decision.
  • Passport and travel documents. Bangladesh declined Hasina’s request for a passport, and India stated that a travel document would be needed for her return.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How are the Bangladesh government and India responding?

  • Government security measures. Bangladesh deployed paramilitary Border Guard Bangladesh and riot police in Gopalganj to control unrest and withdrew local officials amid protests.
  • Political leadership actions. Prime Minister Tarique Rahman chaired a Cabinet meeting to discuss the situation and urged party activists to remain active on the ground.
  • India’s stance. India acknowledged receiving extradition requests but has not announced a decision, treating the matter as a legal issue.

Back to basics: Extradition and bilateral relations

Extradition is a legal process where one country requests another to surrender a person accused or convicted of crimes. It is governed by treaties and involves legal and diplomatic considerations. Such cases can affect bilateral relations, especially when political leaders are involved.

Common mistake. The protests are not simply about public dissent but are linked to complex legal and political issues involving Hasina’s extradition and return.
