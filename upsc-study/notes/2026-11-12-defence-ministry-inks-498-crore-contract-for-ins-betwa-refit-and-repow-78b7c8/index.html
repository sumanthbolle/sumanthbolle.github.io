Defence Ministry Signs ₹498 Crore Contract for INS Betwa Refit

The Ministry of Defence signed a ₹498.58 crore contract with Cochin Shipyard Limited on November 11, 2026, for the normal refit and repowering of INS Betwa, a Brahmaputra-class frigate. This contract involves major propulsion system transformation and combat capability upgrades, reinforcing indigenous naval maintenance and supporting 'Aatmanirbhar Bharat'.

The Defence Ministry contracted Cochin Shipyard for ₹498.58 crore to refit and repower INS Betwa, enhancing its propulsion and combat systems while boosting indigenous ship repair capabilities.

What does the INS Betwa refit contract entail?

  • Contract details. The Ministry of Defence signed a ₹498.58 crore contract with Cochin Shipyard Limited for the normal refit and repowering of INS Betwa, a Brahmaputra-class frigate.
  • Propulsion system transformation. The refit involves replacing INS Betwa's existing steam-based propulsion system with a diesel-engine-based propulsion system.
  • Combat capability upgrade. The ship's combat capabilities will be enhanced through the installation of a modernised weapon suite to improve operational effectiveness.

Why does this contract matter for India's defence and industry?

  • Indigenous capability strengthening. The project will strengthen indigenous ship repair and maintenance capabilities, especially for complex propulsion system transformations and naval platform modernisation.
  • Employment and MSME involvement. More than 50 MSMEs are expected to be involved, generating significant employment opportunities across Indian shipbuilding and ancillary industries.
  • Support for government initiatives. This contract supports the Government's vision of 'Aatmanirbhar Bharat' and 'Make-in-India' by reinforcing domestic naval maintenance ecosystems.

Back to basics: Naval ship refit and repowering

A naval ship refit involves comprehensive maintenance and upgrades to extend the vessel's operational life and improve capabilities. Repowering refers to replacing the ship's propulsion system, often to enhance efficiency, reliability, or performance. Such projects are critical for maintaining naval readiness and often involve complex engineering and indigenous industrial participation.
