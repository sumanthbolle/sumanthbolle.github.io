Karnataka mandates gynaecological check-ups and sex education in schools

Karnataka government issued a joint circular mandating gynaecological check-ups for girls and sex education in all schools and colleges to address rising child marriage and teenage pregnancy. This policy also includes child protection measures like CCTV installation and counselling, relevant for social welfare and adolescent health topics in UPSC.

Karnataka mandates mandatory gynaecological check-ups for schoolgirls and sex education to combat child marriage and teenage pregnancy.

Why is this in the news?

The Karnataka government issued a joint circular in November 2026 mandating health and protection measures for students in educational institutions.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the key measures mandated by Karnataka for schools and colleges?

  • Mandatory gynaecological check-ups. All girls in schools must undergo mandatory check-ups by a gynaecologist to monitor adolescent health.
  • Sex education and awareness. Sex education is mandated, including awareness on child rights, teenage pregnancy, child marriage, POCSO Act, and child helpline 1098.
  • Child Protection Committees. Schools must form Child Protection Committees comprising parents, children, and teachers that meet quarterly to address student welfare.
  • CCTV installation and counselling. Installation of CCTV cameras is compulsory, and qualified counsellors must be appointed to address mental health and abuse issues.
  • Legal action for negligence. Failure to prevent or address child marriage, teenage pregnancy, or POCSO cases will lead to legal and disciplinary action against officials.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why were these measures introduced by Karnataka?

  • Rising child marriage and teenage pregnancy. The circular is a response to increasing cases of child marriage and teenage pregnancy in the state.
  • Protecting adolescent health and rights. The measures aim to safeguard adolescent girls' health, prevent abuse, and ensure continued education until age 18.
  • Preventing exploitation and abuse. The policy includes steps to prevent child labour, trafficking, sexual abuse, and substance abuse among students.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How will the policy be implemented and monitored?

  • Regular meetings and grievance redressal. Child Protection Committees will meet every three months, and complaint boxes will be installed to address grievances.
  • Tracking absenteeism. If a girl student is absent for more than three days without notice, the principal must inform the gram panchayat and child helpline.
  • Counselling and re-enrolment efforts. Counsellors will address mental health challenges, and teachers must persuade parents to re-enrol girls who dropped out.
  • Legal measures against illegal abortion. The Health Department will take legal action against shops selling abortion pills without prescription and unauthorized abortion providers.

Back to basics: Adolescent health and education policy

Adolescent health policies focus on ensuring physical and mental well-being of teenagers through preventive healthcare and education. Sex education in schools helps students understand bodily changes, reproductive health, and rights, reducing risks like early pregnancy and abuse. Child protection mechanisms in educational institutions aim to create safe environments for students to learn and grow.
