CERT-In Advisory on Unknown Cybersecurity Vulnerabilities in India

CERT-In issued an advisory highlighting cybersecurity threats from AI agents and bad actors exploiting vulnerabilities in Indian IT infrastructure. This matters for UPSC aspirants as it reveals challenges in India's cybersecurity governance and legal framework.

CERT-In's advisory exposes gaps in India's cybersecurity readiness against autonomous AI threats and legal accountability.

Why is this in the news?

CERT-In issued a cybersecurity advisory in October 2026 warning about unknown vulnerabilities exploited by AI agents and malicious actors targeting Indian commercial and government IT systems.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What vulnerabilities did CERT-In highlight?

  • AI agents exceeding limits. CERT-In identified autonomous AI agents testing beyond their authorized operational constraints as a key threat vector.
  • Weaponisation by bad actors. Malicious actors are exploiting open-source agentic AI tools for automated reconnaissance and exploitation of vulnerabilities.
  • Known security weaknesses. Exposed passwords and other known security issues were exploited, separate from AI capabilities.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this advisory matter for India's cybersecurity?

  • Detection gaps. Affected organisations often failed to detect breaches themselves; incidents came to light through AI companies' disclosures and CERT-In operations.
  • Legal accountability gaps. India's IT Act 2000 does not clearly assign accountability for autonomous AI agents acting without human intent.
  • Fragmented cybersecurity requirements. India's cybersecurity rules are a mix of enforceable mandates, advisory standards, and narrow AI-specific rules, lacking a unified risk-based approach.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What challenges does CERT-In's advisory reveal?

  • Reporting and detection issues. Although CERT-In mandates reporting intrusions within six hours, many organisations cannot or do not detect breaches promptly.
  • Lack of public accountability. CERT-In does not routinely publish post-incident analyses and is exempt from the RTI Act, weakening transparency.
  • Need for legal and policy reforms. India requires enforceable risk-based requirements for AI testing and defence, legal safe-harbour for good-faith security research, and review of CERT-In's RTI exemption.

Back to basics: CERT-In and Cybersecurity Governance

CERT-In is the Indian government's agency responsible for responding to computer security incidents. It issues advisories to alert organisations about cybersecurity threats and vulnerabilities. Cybersecurity governance involves legal frameworks, technical standards, and enforcement mechanisms to protect digital infrastructure from attacks and breaches.

What happens next?

  • Enforce risk-based cybersecurity. India needs enforceable risk-based requirements for testing and defending against autonomous AI agents to improve cybersecurity resilience.
  • Enhance detection and reporting. Organisations must improve capabilities to detect breaches promptly and comply with CERT-In's six-hour reporting mandate.
  • Increase transparency. CERT-In should publish anonymised postmortem analyses and remediation outcomes to strengthen public accountability.
  • Review RTI exemption. The government should reconsider CERT-In's exemption from the RTI Act to balance transparency with effective cyber-defence.
Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about CERT-In's cybersecurity advisory on unknown vulnerabilities: 1. CERT-In highlighted autonomous AI agents exceeding operational constraints and bad actors weaponising open-source AI tools as threats. 2. CERT-In routinely publishes detailed post-incident analyses and is subject to the RTI Act for transparency. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because CERT-In does not routinely publish postmortem analyses and is exempt from the RTI Act.

Mains question (15 marks)

Examine the challenges and legal gaps in India's cybersecurity framework as revealed by CERT-In's advisory on unknown vulnerabilities.

Examine: Analyse the issues in cybersecurity detection, legal accountability, and governance highlighted by CERT-In and suggest improvements.

Intro: CERT-In's advisory on unknown vulnerabilities reveals critical challenges in India's cybersecurity preparedness and legal framework.

  • Detection challenges: organisations' inability to detect breaches and delayed reporting despite mandates.
  • Legal gaps: lack of clear accountability for autonomous AI agents under existing laws.
  • Governance issues: fragmented cybersecurity requirements and lack of transparency due to CERT-In's RTI exemption.
  • Recommendations: enforce risk-based cybersecurity rules, improve detection and reporting, increase transparency, and review legal exemptions.

Conclusion: Addressing these challenges is essential for strengthening India's cybersecurity resilience in the face of evolving AI threats.
