Special Parliament Session to Discuss Amended Delimitation Bill in 2026

The Indian government has called a special Parliament session from November 30 to December 3, 2026, to table and pass an amended Delimitation Bill. This session is significant for UPSC aspirants as it involves constitutional amendments affecting parliamentary representation and federal balance.

The 2026 special Parliament session aims to pass an amended Delimitation Bill that proposes a 50% increase in Lok Sabha seats and extends the freeze on seat allocation using the 1971 Census.

Why is this in the news?

The government announced a special Parliament sitting starting November 30, 2026, to table an amended Delimitation Bill after over 100 days since the last session was adjourned.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the purpose of the special Parliament session?

  • Session dates and agenda. The special Parliament session will run from November 30 to December 3, 2026, with Lok Sabha sitting from November 30 and Rajya Sabha from December 2, primarily to table and pass the amended Delimitation Bill.
  • Bill's legislative status. The government aims to pass the amended Delimitation Bill after it failed in the April 2026 session due to united Opposition resistance.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the key changes proposed in the amended Delimitation Bill?

  • Increase in Lok Sabha seats. The Bill proposes a uniform 50% increase in the share of Lok Sabha seats for all States.
  • Extension of freeze on seat allocation. It extends the freeze on using the 1971 Census for determining the proportional strength of States in the Lok Sabha by 25 years.
  • Use of 2011 Census for redrawing constituencies. The Bill uses the 2011 Census to redraw constituencies within each State, replicating the formula from the 2001 constitutional amendment.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the Delimitation Bill matter for federalism and representation?

  • Impact on State representation. If the freeze on seat allocation lapses after 2026 without amendment, States like Tamil Nadu could lose Lok Sabha seats based on post-2026 Census data.
  • Political arithmetic and support. The government is confident of securing the required two-thirds majority in both Houses, with shifting alliances and support from some regional parties.
  • Opposition concerns. Opposition leaders have criticized the Bill as undermining the Constitution and democratic processes, calling it an assault on federal principles.

Back to basics: Delimitation and Parliamentary Representation

Delimitation is the process of redrawing the boundaries of parliamentary constituencies to reflect population changes. It ensures equitable representation in the Lok Sabha by adjusting seat allocation among States based on census data. The Constitution allows for freezing seat allocation to maintain stability, but this can affect federal balance if demographic shifts are not accounted for.

Common mistake. The special session is not a routine meeting but a targeted effort to pass a constitutional amendment after a long adjournment.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the 2026 special Parliament session and the Delimitation Bill: 1. The Bill proposes a 50% increase in Lok Sabha seats and uses the 2011 Census for redrawing constituencies. 2. The freeze on using the 1971 Census for seat allocation is extended by 25 years. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). A trap is confusing the census year used for redrawing constituencies (2011) with the census year used for the freeze extension (1971). Both are distinct and correctly stated in the Bill.
