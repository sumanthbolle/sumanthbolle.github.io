MoRTH-Bihar MoU for Scientific Desilting and Silt Use in Highways

The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) and Bihar government signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) for scientific desilting of rivers and water bodies and using the excavated silt in National Highway projects. This initiative integrates river restoration, flood management, agricultural conservation, and infrastructure development, making it relevant for UPSC aspirants studying infrastructure and environmental sustainability.

The MoRTH-Bihar MoU aims to restore water bodies through scientific desilting and use the excavated silt as construction material in National Highway projects to reduce flood risks and construction costs.

Why is this in the news?

On November 3, 2026, MoRTH and the Bihar government signed an MoU for scientific desilting of water bodies and utilisation of silt in National Highway projects under the 'Nadi Sanjivani' initiative.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the MoU between MoRTH and Bihar about?

  • Scientific desilting. The MoU focuses on scientific desilting of identified rivers and water bodies in Bihar to restore their water-carrying capacity and improve drainage.
  • Silt utilisation. Suitable excavated silt from desilting will be used as a sustainable construction material in ongoing and future National Highway projects in Bihar.
  • Integrated approach. The initiative integrates river restoration, flood management, agricultural conservation, and highway infrastructure development.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Indian Express · Secondary

Why does this initiative matter for Bihar and infrastructure development?

  • Flood and water challenges. Bihar faces recurring floods and water scarcity due to silt accumulation in rivers, which reduces their carrying capacity and damages agricultural land.
  • Resource efficiency. Using excavated silt reduces dependence on fresh earth materials, conserves agricultural soil, lowers construction costs, and speeds up highway project completion.
  • Expanded highway network. Bihar's National Highway network has expanded from 4,615 km in 2014 to about 6,500 km, with many ongoing and proposed projects requiring large quantities of construction material.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Indian Express · Secondary

How will the initiative be implemented and monitored?

  • Institutional framework. The Road Construction Department of Bihar will be the nodal agency, supported by a State-level Coordination Committee to facilitate approvals and monitor progress.
  • Scientific methods and safeguards. Desilting will follow detailed technical assessments, with extraction only at approved locations to protect river ecosystems and infrastructure.
  • Transparency and accountability. Joint measurement, digital record-keeping, regular monitoring, and periodic reconciliation of silt quantities will ensure transparency.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the scale and scope of silt utilisation in highway projects?

  • Current and future projects. 243 lakh cubic metres of silt will be used in 16 ongoing National Highway projects, and 835 lakh cubic metres is proposed for 27 future projects, subject to approvals.
  • Project coverage and investment. Currently, 39 projects covering about 1,561 km with ₹58,340 crore investment are underway, and 11 projects covering 1,982 km with ₹1,85,200 crore proposed.
  • Potential benefits. Utilising silt will meet a significant part of material requirements, reduce costs, and facilitate timely execution of highway works.

Back to basics: Scientific Desilting and Infrastructure Development

Scientific desilting involves the planned removal of accumulated silt from rivers and water bodies to restore their water-carrying capacity and reduce flood risks. The excavated silt can be repurposed as construction material, reducing the need to extract fresh soil and conserving agricultural land. This approach links environmental management with infrastructure development to promote sustainable growth.
