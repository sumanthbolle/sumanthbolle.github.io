Supreme Court directs States to fund women advocates' bar rooms and washrooms

The Supreme Court on October 26, 2026, directed all States and Union Territories to allocate funds for constructing bar rooms, washrooms, and basic amenities for women advocates in district and taluk courts. This directive highlights the judiciary's role in ensuring gender-inclusive infrastructure, a key issue for gender justice and institutional reforms in the legal profession.

The Supreme Court mandated States and UTs to provide dedicated facilities for women advocates to promote inclusiveness and accessibility in courts.

Why is this in the news?

What did the Supreme Court direct regarding women advocates' facilities?

  • Mandatory allocation of funds. The Supreme Court directed all States and Union Territories to allocate funds to build bar rooms, washrooms, and other basic amenities for women advocates in district and taluk courts.
  • Facilities regardless of numbers. The Court disapproved of the absence of ladies' bar rooms and emphasized that facilities must be provided regardless of the number of women advocates practicing.
  • High Courts and State governments' duty. It is the duty of all High Courts and State governments to ensure exclusive women's bar rooms with all facilities wherever women members are practicing.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for gender justice and the judiciary?

  • Promoting inclusiveness. The directive addresses the accessibility and inclusiveness of women lawyers in the legal profession by ensuring they have safe and adequate facilities.
  • Long-term sustainability. Providing such infrastructure supports the long-term sustainability of women advocates' participation in the judiciary.
  • Judiciary's role in gender equality. The Supreme Court's order reflects its role in promoting gender justice through institutional reforms within the judiciary.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How will the directive be implemented and monitored?

  • Compliance mandatory. The Court stated that compliance is mandatory and must be ensured even if additional funds or land are required.
  • Next hearing scheduled. The matter was posted for further hearing on December 2, 2026, indicating ongoing judicial oversight.

Back to basics: Gender justice in judiciary

Gender justice in the judiciary involves ensuring equal opportunities, safety, and inclusiveness for women lawyers and judges. It includes providing necessary infrastructure like separate bar rooms and washrooms to support women advocates' professional needs and participation. Institutional reforms by courts can address systemic barriers to gender equality in the legal profession.
