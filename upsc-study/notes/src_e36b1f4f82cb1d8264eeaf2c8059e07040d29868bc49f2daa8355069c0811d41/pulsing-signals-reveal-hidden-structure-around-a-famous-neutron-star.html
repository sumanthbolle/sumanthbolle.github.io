Background

  • An X-ray binary pulsar comprises a highly magnetised neutron star and a companion star bound by gravity.
  • The neutron star can pull material from its companion; the infalling material produces X-rays.
  • Pulsar radiation originates at magnetic poles and sweeps past an observing satellite as the neutron star rotates.
  • Surrounding structures can absorb and re-emit this primary radiation as secondary radiation, whose variation can help infer system geometry and magnetic-field-related parameters.
  • In high-mass X-ray binaries, accretion generally occurs through direct capture of stellar wind; an accretion disk is therefore an unusual possibility in this context.
