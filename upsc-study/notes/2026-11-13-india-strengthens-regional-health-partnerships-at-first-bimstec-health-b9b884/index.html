India Advances Regional Health Cooperation at First BIMSTEC Health Ministers’ Meeting

India led the inaugural BIMSTEC Health Ministers’ Meeting in Colombo, where member countries adopted a Joint Ministerial Statement and a Revised Plan of Action on Public Health (2026–2030). This meeting marks a significant step in strengthening regional health security and cooperation in the Bay of Bengal region, relevant for both international relations and health governance.

The first BIMSTEC Health Ministers’ Meeting in 2026 established a regional framework for health cooperation, with India playing a key leadership role.

Why is this in the news?

The first BIMSTEC Health Ministers’ Meeting was held in Colombo on 11 November 2026, where India led efforts to adopt a Joint Ministerial Statement and a Revised Plan of Action on Public Health for 2026–2030.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What were the key outcomes of the first BIMSTEC Health Ministers’ Meeting?

  • Joint Ministerial Statement. The meeting unanimously adopted a Joint Ministerial Statement reaffirming commitment to accessible, affordable, quality healthcare and regional health security across BIMSTEC countries.
  • Revised Plan of Action. The Revised BIMSTEC Plan of Action on Public Health (2026–2030) was adopted, outlining cooperation in disease surveillance, pandemic preparedness, digital health, traditional medicine, One Health, and healthcare workforce development.
  • Regional Centres of Excellence. The meeting welcomed initiatives including the BIMSTEC Regional Centre of Excellence for Public Health in Sri Lanka, the Centre of Excellence on Tropical Medicine in Thailand, and India’s commitment to host a Centre of Excellence in Traditional Medicine at ITRA, Jamnagar.
  • Bilateral Health Initiatives. India and Sri Lanka exchanged an MoU for 100 additional ambulances under Indian grant assistance of approximately USD 5.28 million and India announced support for 50 Medical Kiosks/Health ATMs to strengthen primary healthcare in Sri Lanka.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How is India contributing to regional health cooperation?

  • Leadership and Expertise Sharing. India’s Union Health Minister Shri J.P. Nadda led the meeting and offered to share India’s expertise in primary healthcare, digital health, and public health infrastructure with BIMSTEC countries.
  • Healthcare Transformation. India highlighted its progress towards Universal Health Coverage, including over 1.85 lakh Ayushman Arogya Mandirs and the Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana covering over 60 crore people with cashless hospitalisation up to ₹5 lakh.
  • Digital Health Initiatives. India’s National Telemedicine Service, eSanjeevani, has crossed 50 crore teleconsultations, and the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission is building an interoperable digital health ecosystem.
  • Traditional Medicine Centre. India committed to hosting the BIMSTEC Centre of Excellence in Traditional Medicine at ITRA, Jamnagar, to promote research, academic exchange, and preservation of traditional knowledge.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this regional health cooperation matter for India and BIMSTEC?

  • Strengthening Regional Health Security. The cooperation enhances disease surveillance, emergency preparedness, and coordinated response to cross-border health threats under the One Health approach.
  • Promoting Sustainable Development Goals. The Joint Ministerial Statement aligns with Universal Health Coverage and Sustainable Development Goal 3, focusing on equitable health access and resilient systems.
  • Deepening Bilateral Relations. India’s bilateral engagements with Sri Lanka and Nepal during the meeting reinforce longstanding partnerships and support regional prosperity and stability.
  • Supporting Inclusive Development. The initiatives reflect India’s vision of “One Earth, One Family, One Future,” emphasizing inclusive development and shared prosperity in the Bay of Bengal region.

Back to basics: Regional Health Cooperation

Regional health cooperation involves countries working together to improve public health outcomes through shared strategies, resources, and information. It helps in disease surveillance, pandemic preparedness, and strengthening health systems, especially for cross-border health threats. Such cooperation promotes Universal Health Coverage and Sustainable Development Goals by ensuring accessible and affordable healthcare for all in the region.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the first BIMSTEC Health Ministers’ Meeting: 1. The meeting adopted a Joint Ministerial Statement and a Revised Plan of Action on Public Health for 2026–2030. 2. India committed to hosting the BIMSTEC Centre of Excellence on Tropical Medicine in Thailand. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because India committed to hosting the Centre of Excellence in Traditional Medicine at ITRA, Jamnagar, not the Centre of Excellence on Tropical Medicine in Thailand.
