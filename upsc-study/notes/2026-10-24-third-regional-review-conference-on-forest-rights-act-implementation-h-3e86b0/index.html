Third Regional Review Conference on Forest Rights Act Implementation in Srinagar

The Ministry of Tribal Affairs held the Third Regional Review Conference in Srinagar on 23 October 2026 to assess the implementation of the Forest Rights Act across seven States and UTs. This review focused on pending claims, community forest resource rights, and livelihood convergence, emphasizing time-bound action plans, which is crucial for understanding tribal rights and forest governance in India.

The conference reviewed progress and challenges in implementing the Forest Rights Act, stressing transparent claim disposal, community participation, and integration with livelihood schemes.

Why is this in the news?

What was the purpose of the Third Regional Review Conference on FRA?

  • Review implementation progress. Senior officials from Maharashtra, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Goa, Rajasthan, and Jammu & Kashmir assessed the status of forest rights claims, community forest resource rights, and livelihood convergence.
  • Identify challenges and priorities. The conference discussed obstacles in claim disposal and emphasized the need for transparent, timely processes and stronger interdepartmental coordination.
  • Emphasize time-bound action plans. States were urged to prepare district-wise plans to expedite pending claims and review rejected cases, ensuring rights recognition advances alongside conservation and sustainable development.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the current status of Forest Rights Act implementation?

  • Claims and titles statistics. As of 31 August 2026, about 54 lakh claims were received nationally, with 43.56 lakh disposed of, 25.42 lakh titles distributed over nearly 238 lakh acres, 10.45 lakh claims pending, and 18.13 lakh rejected.
  • Community Forest Resource Rights. Recognition and sustainable management of Community Forest Resource Rights (CFRR) were highlighted, with calls for convergence of CFR Management Plans with Forest Department Working Plans.
  • Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs). Habitat rights have been recognized for only 11 of the 75 PVTGs in India, and States were urged to expedite their recognition and develop PVTG-wise Habitat Rights roadmaps.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How are States progressing in FRA implementation?

  • Maharashtra's progress. Distributed 2,03,510 individual and 8,821 community titles; CFR rights cover about 13.87 lakh acres with 7,518 CFR Committees and 1,875 management plans.
  • Gujarat's efforts. Distributed 1,00,747 individual and 3,879 community titles; satellite verification and FRA Cells address pendency; PVTGs identified with a Habitat Rights Action Plan to be submitted.
  • Jammu & Kashmir's status. Received 48,287 claims, approved 6,191, and distributed 6,178 titles covering approximately 3,960 hectares, mainly community rights; has SLMC under Chief Secretary and directives to prevent eviction before due process.
  • Other States' updates. Himachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Goa, and Rajasthan have made varying progress in title distribution, record incorporation, and committee meetings, with ongoing efforts to resolve pending and rejected claims.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the key recommendations and next steps from the conference?

  • District-wise action plans. States and UTs must prepare detailed plans identifying delays, responsible authorities, and stages of pending claims.
  • Review rejected claims. Rejections must be supported by reasoned orders with appeal rights preserved, and a one-time window to review rejected claims is suggested.
  • Digitisation and integration. Complete digitisation and incorporation of recognised rights into revenue and forest records are to be done as per the advisory dated 24 June 2026.
  • Capacity building and convergence. States should strengthen Gram Sabhas, raise awareness, provide post-claim support, facilitate CFR Management Plans, and align with livelihood and welfare schemes.
  • Monitoring and reporting. Quarterly reviews through State Level Monitoring Committees chaired by Chief Secretaries and regular submission of reconciled Monthly Progress Reports are mandated.

Back to basics: Forest Rights Act and Community Forest Resource Rights

The Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006, recognizes the rights of forest-dwelling communities over forest land and resources. It includes Individual Forest Rights (IFR) and Community Forest Resource Rights (CFRR), which empower communities to manage and conserve forests sustainably. The Act mandates Gram Sabha participation in claim recognition and aims to balance forest conservation with tribal livelihoods.

What happens next?

  • Expedite pending claims. States must prepare district-wise plans to identify and address delays in claim disposal with accountability for responsible authorities.
  • Strengthen Gram Sabhas. Enhance awareness, capacity building, and post-claim support to empower Gram Sabhas for effective forest rights management.
  • Integrate rights with welfare schemes. Incorporate recognized forest rights into revenue records to facilitate access to schemes like PM-KISAN and livelihood programs.

Mains question (15 marks)

Discuss the significance of the Forest Rights Act implementation review conferences in strengthening tribal rights and forest governance in India.

Discuss: Explain how review conferences aid in addressing challenges and improving the implementation of the Forest Rights Act for tribal welfare and sustainable forest management.

Intro: The Forest Rights Act aims to recognize and protect the rights of forest-dwelling communities, and review conferences assess its implementation.

  • Review conferences provide a platform for States and UTs to share progress, challenges, and best practices in claim disposal and rights recognition.
  • They emphasize transparency, timely processing of claims, and stronger Gram Sabha participation, which are crucial for tribal empowerment.
  • The conferences promote convergence of forest rights with livelihood schemes and sustainable forest management through Community Forest Resource Rights.
  • They facilitate monitoring through State Level Monitoring Committees and encourage digitisation and integration of records for better governance.

Conclusion: Thus, these conferences play a vital role in ensuring effective implementation of the FRA, balancing tribal rights with conservation goals.
