CAG Embraces Technology and Reforms to Enhance Public Accountability

The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) of India has emphasized technology adoption and institutional reforms to strengthen public accountability and align audits with national development goals. This is important for UPSC aspirants to understand the evolving role of constitutional bodies in governance and transparency.

The CAG is leveraging technology and thematic audits to improve public accountability and support India's development objectives.

Why is this in the news?

On November 16, 2026, CAG K. Sanjay Murthy highlighted reforms and technology use at the Audit Divas event to strengthen public accountability.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What reforms has the CAG implemented to strengthen public accountability?

  • Breaking departmental silos. The CAG has started working across departmental silos to conduct audits covering cross-sectoral issues, enabling a more integrated audit approach.
  • Thematic audits. Audits now focus on themes like ‘Ease of Living’ and ‘Ease of Doing Business’, aligning with national development goals and assessing reforms at the grassroots level.
  • Focus on local bodies. The CAG has expanded audits of local governance, prioritizing quality of accounts, uniform grant reporting, timely audits, and coverage of the top 500 cities.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How is technology being used by the CAG to improve audit processes?

  • AI tools for audits. The CAG is developing audit-specific artificial intelligence tools to enhance data security and efficiency in audits.
  • Data-led audits. Pilot projects using data-led audits for establishment expenditure, social-sector Direct Benefit Transfers, and public works reduced the audit cycle from six to eight months to three months.
  • Capacity building. The institution is investing in developing officials skilled in AI through the AI Champions and AI Enablers programme, involving collaboration with start-ups.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the scale and impact of the CAG’s audit work?

  • Audit reports and recommendations. In the last year, the CAG tabled 40 Union government and 174 State government audit reports, making 3,742 audit recommendations, with nearly one-third accepted.
  • Units audited. Over 34,000 units were audited and more than 27,000 inspection reports issued, covering Union and State governments, PSUs, autonomous bodies, and externally aided projects.
  • Horizontal audits. Pan-India horizontal audits include ‘Ease of Doing Business’ for MSMEs and urban mobility audits in 101 major cities under the ‘Ease of Living’ theme.

Back to basics: Role of the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)

The CAG is a constitutional authority responsible for auditing the accounts of the Union and State governments and public sector entities. It ensures public money is used efficiently and transparently, thus strengthening accountability. The CAG reports to the Parliament and State Legislatures, helping them oversee government financial management.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the recent reforms and initiatives by the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) of India: 1. The CAG has initiated pan-India horizontal audits on themes like ‘Ease of Doing Business’ and ‘Ease of Living’. 2. The CAG has reduced the audit cycle time from six to eight months to three months through data-led audits and AI tools. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). A trap could be confusing the audit themes or the timeline of audit cycle reduction; both statements are supported by the evidence.

Mains question (15 marks)

Discuss how technological reforms and thematic audits by the CAG contribute to enhancing public accountability and governance.

Discuss: Explain the role of CAG reforms and technology use in improving audit effectiveness and public trust.

Intro: The CAG plays a key role in public accountability by auditing government finances and operations.

  • Technological reforms like AI tools and data-led audits have shortened audit cycles and improved data security.
  • Thematic audits on ‘Ease of Doing Business’ and ‘Ease of Living’ align audit focus with national development goals.
  • Breaking departmental silos and expanding audits to local bodies enhance comprehensive oversight.
  • Capacity building through AI training equips officials to handle modern audit challenges.

Conclusion: These reforms strengthen the CAG’s ability to ensure transparency and foster public trust in governance.
