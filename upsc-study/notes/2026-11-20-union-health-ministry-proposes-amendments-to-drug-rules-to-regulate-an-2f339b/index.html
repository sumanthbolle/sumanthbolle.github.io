Health Ministry Proposes Amendments to Regulate Antimicrobials and Bulk Drug Wholesale

The Union Health Ministry has proposed amendments to the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019, and the Drugs Rules, 1945, to strengthen regulatory oversight of antimicrobials and introduce separate licensing for bulk drug wholesale. These changes aim to address antimicrobial resistance and improve accountability in pharmaceutical supply chains, important for public health governance.

The Health Ministry's proposed amendments bring antimicrobials under central regulation and create a distinct licensing framework for bulk drug wholesalers to combat antimicrobial resistance and enhance drug quality oversight.

Why is this in the news?

On November 19, 2026, the Union Health Ministry issued draft notifications proposing amendments to drug rules to include antimicrobials under central regulatory oversight and to create a separate licensing framework for bulk drug wholesale.

What changes are proposed for antimicrobials under the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019?

  • Inclusion in new drug definition. The amendment proposes to include 'antimicrobials' within the definition of 'New Drug' under Rule 2(1)(w)(v) to bring them under the regulatory framework of new drugs.
  • Central Licensing Authority role. Manufacturers of antimicrobials would need to obtain permission from the Central Licensing Authority before applying for a manufacturing licence from the State Licensing Authority.
  • Addressing antimicrobial resistance. This change aims to ensure quality, safety, and efficacy of antimicrobials by requiring studies like bioavailability, bioequivalence, and stability, thereby helping to combat antimicrobial resistance.

[1] Press Information Bureau[4] The Hindu · Secondary

How will the licensing framework for bulk drug wholesale be modified?

  • Separate licence forms introduced. The Drugs Rules, 1945, will be amended to introduce a separate application form (Form 19-BC) and licence form (Form 20-BC) specifically for wholesale sale of bulk drugs, distinct from formulations.
  • Focused regulatory oversight. This dedicated licensing framework will enable better control and accountability in the wholesale distribution of bulk drugs, including those under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act.
  • Supervision and compliance. Wholesale sale of bulk drugs will be carried out under the personal supervision of a competent person to strengthen accountability in distribution and handling.
  • Transition period for existing licensees. Existing licensees will have time to comply, with new requirements applying after their current retention period expires or 12 months after final amendment publication, whichever is later.

[2] Press Information Bureau[4] The Hindu · Secondary

What other regulatory amendments are proposed for specific drug categories?

  • Regulatory flexibility for certain drugs. Amendments to the Drugs Rules, 1945, propose regulatory flexibility under revised Schedule M for medicinal gases, empty gelatin capsules, and disinfectant fluids.
  • Exemptions continuation. The Drugs Technical Advisory Board recommended continuing exemptions available under the pre-revised Schedule M for these categories, as some revised provisions are not relevant to their manufacturing.
  • Maintaining quality standards. The amendments aim to align regulatory requirements with manufacturing processes without diluting quality, safety, or oversight standards.

Back to basics: Drug Regulatory Framework in India

India regulates drugs through the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and Rules, which include the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019, and the Drugs Rules, 1945. The Central Licensing Authority grants permissions for manufacturing new drugs, while State Licensing Authorities issue manufacturing licenses. Bulk drugs are active or inactive substances used in formulations. Regulatory oversight ensures drug quality, safety, and efficacy to protect public health and prevent issues like antimicrobial resistance.

Quick revision

Why are antimicrobials being included under the definition of 'New Drug'?
To ensure central regulatory oversight and require quality studies, addressing antimicrobial resistance concerns.
What is the purpose of introducing separate licensing for bulk drug wholesale?
To enable focused regulatory control and accountability in the distribution of bulk drugs distinct from formulations.
How long is the transition period for existing bulk drug wholesalers to comply with new licensing rules?
Up to 12 months after the final publication of the amendment or expiry of their current retention period, whichever is later.
