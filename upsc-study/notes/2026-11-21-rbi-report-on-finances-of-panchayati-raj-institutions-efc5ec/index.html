RBI Report Highlights Finances of Panchayati Raj Institutions

The Reserve Bank of India released its second biennial report assessing the finances of Gram Panchayats from 2022-23 to 2024-25, focusing on their fiscal health and role in rural development. This is important for UPSC aspirants to understand rural governance, fiscal decentralisation, and challenges in local self-government financing.

Gram Panchayats rely heavily on intergovernmental transfers for their finances, with limited own revenue sources, impacting their capacity for rural development.

Why is this in the news?

On November 19, 2026, the Reserve Bank of India released the second edition of its biennial report titled 'Finances of Panchayati Raj Institutions', presenting an assessment of rural local bodies' finances and capacity.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What does the RBI report on Panchayati Raj finances reveal?

  • Report scope. The report analyses a balanced panel of 2.25 lakh Gram Panchayats covering 2022-23 to 2024-25, focusing on their finances and role in rural socio-economic development.
  • Revenue sources. Gram Panchayats depend on intergovernmental transfers for about 90% of their receipts, while own source revenues constitute only about 10%, mainly from non-tax sources like interest receipts, programme funds, and user charges.
  • Expenditure priorities. Expenditure is largely aligned with the Fifteenth Finance Commission mandates and national schemes, with water supply and sanitation accounting for about 40% of total spending.
  • Progress and disparities. There has been progress in rural connectivity, electrification, water supply, sanitation, and community assets, but inter-State disparities in access, quality, and functionality persist.
  • Challenges ahead. As some large Gram Panchayats transition to Town Panchayats, their functional responsibilities may outpace fiscal and administrative readiness, requiring fiscal support, capacity building, and improved own revenue collection through better property tax and license fee rationalisation.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Why does this report matter for UPSC preparation?

  • Decentralisation insight. The report provides empirical data on fiscal decentralisation and the financial autonomy of rural local bodies, a key topic in governance and federalism.
  • Rural governance challenges. Understanding the dependence on transfers and limited own revenues highlights challenges in empowering Panchayati Raj Institutions for sustainable rural development.
  • Fiscal policy relevance. The report links local government finances with national fiscal policies like the Fifteenth Finance Commission, relevant for questions on fiscal federalism and budgeting.

Back to basics: Panchayati Raj Institutions and Fiscal Decentralisation

Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs) are rural local self-government bodies responsible for planning and implementing development programs at the village level. Fiscal decentralisation refers to the process of transferring financial powers and resources from central and state governments to local bodies like PRIs, enabling them to raise revenues and manage expenditures to meet local needs effectively.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the finances of Gram Panchayats as per the RBI report: 1. Gram Panchayats derive about 90% of their receipts from intergovernmental transfers. 2. Own source revenues constitute the majority of Gram Panchayat receipts. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because own source revenues account for only about 10% of receipts, not the majority.

Mains question (15 marks)

Explain the challenges faced by Panchayati Raj Institutions in achieving fiscal autonomy and effective rural governance.

Examine: Examine the financial dependence of Panchayati Raj Institutions on intergovernmental transfers and the implications for rural governance.

Intro: Panchayati Raj Institutions play a crucial role in rural governance but face challenges in fiscal autonomy.

  • Dependence on intergovernmental transfers limits financial independence and decision-making capacity.
  • Own source revenues are low, primarily from non-tax sources, restricting resource mobilisation.
  • Expenditure priorities are influenced by central mandates and schemes, limiting local flexibility.
  • Transition of large Gram Panchayats to urban local bodies requires enhanced fiscal and administrative capacity.

Conclusion: Strengthening fiscal autonomy through improved revenue collection and capacity building is essential for effective rural governance.
