DST’s ESTIC Session on Autonomous Systems, Physical AI and Robotics

The Department of Science and Technology (DST) held a session at ESTIC 2026 discussing how autonomous systems, physical AI, and robotics will transform scientific research and innovation. This is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights India's strategic focus on emerging technologies and interdisciplinary collaboration for technological advancement.

DST’s ESTIC 2026 session emphasized collaboration among academia, industry, and researchers to advance autonomous systems, physical AI, and robotics for societal benefits.

Why is this in the news?

The DST’s session on Autonomous Systems, Physical AI and Robotics was held during ESTIC 2026 at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, from October 27 to 29, 2026.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What was discussed at the DST’s ESTIC session?

  • Focus areas. The session focused on how autonomous systems, physical AI, and robotics will change scientific research and technological innovations in the near future.
  • Collaboration importance. Prof. Umesh V. Waghmare emphasized that collaboration among industry, academia, researchers, start-ups, and technology users is crucial for advancing these technologies and translating scientific ideas into societal solutions.
  • National Mission on Cyber-Physical Systems. DST’s National Mission on Interdisciplinary Cyber-Physical Systems has established technology innovation hubs at 25 leading academic institutions to facilitate academia-industry interaction, with Phase I concluding in December 2027.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does Physical AI pose unique challenges?

  • Systems engineering challenge. Prof. Abhay Karandikar stated that Physical AI is not just an AI challenge but also a systems engineering challenge involving sensors, actuators, computing, control algorithms, communication, and hardware.
  • Safety and reliability. Unlike conversational AI, errors in physical AI systems like robots or autonomous vehicles can have immediate physical consequences, making reliability, safety, security, and trustworthiness central to research.
  • Interdisciplinary collaboration. Progress requires collaboration among experts in embedded systems, AI models, and algorithms to address research challenges in autonomous systems.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What opportunities and future plans were highlighted?

  • India’s capabilities. India can develop autonomous systems by leveraging its strengths in systems engineering, electronics, manufacturing, embedded systems, design, telecommunications, and software.
  • Integration potential. Integrating intelligence and advanced algorithms into robotics, autonomous vehicles, and cyber-physical systems can lead to more reliable, safe, and trustworthy autonomous technologies.
  • Physical AI Mission proposal. Stakeholder consultations have considered a dedicated Physical AI Mission, potentially as part of the next phase of the National Mission on Interdisciplinary Cyber-Physical Systems.

Back to basics: Physical AI and Autonomous Systems

Physical AI refers to artificial intelligence applied to physical systems like robots and autonomous vehicles, which interact with the real world and require reliable sensors, actuators, and control systems. Autonomous systems operate independently with minimal human intervention, relying on real-time computing and communication. Understanding these concepts is essential to grasp how emerging technologies are transforming science and innovation.

What happens next?

  • Formulating Phase II plans. Feedback from the ESTIC session will help inform the formulation of plans for the second phase of the National Mission on Interdisciplinary Cyber-Physical Systems.
  • Physical AI Mission development. There is a proposal under consideration for a dedicated Physical AI Mission to build on existing capabilities and address research challenges in autonomous systems.
