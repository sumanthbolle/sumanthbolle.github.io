Constitutional Provisions and Implications of Proroguing Parliament

The Constitution mandates that the President, on the Council of Ministers' advice, summons and prorogues Parliament sessions, ensuring no more than six months between sessions. Understanding prorogation is crucial for grasping parliamentary functioning and executive-legislative relations.

Article 85 requires at least two sessions of Parliament annually, with prorogation marking the formal end of a session on the President's advice.

Why is this in the news?

Parliament was adjourned sine die on August 13, 2026, but has not been prorogued since, marking the longest unprorogued period on record, raising questions about constitutional procedure.

What does the Constitution say about proroguing Parliament?

  • Article 85 provisions. Article 85 of the Constitution empowers the President to summon and prorogue each House of Parliament based on the Council of Ministers' advice.
  • Session frequency. The Constitution mandates that the gap between two sessions of Parliament should not exceed six months, ensuring at least two sessions annually.
  • Prorogation purpose. Prorogation formally ends a parliamentary session, distinct from adjournment, and is necessary before a new session can begin.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the implications of not proroguing Parliament?

  • Unprorogued period significance. The current longest unprorogued period since the last adjournment sine die raises questions about parliamentary procedure and executive discretion.
  • Parliamentary reconvening. Without prorogation, the Houses cannot formally reconvene for a new session, affecting legislative business and government accountability.

Back to basics: Prorogation of Parliament

Prorogation is the formal ending of a parliamentary session by the President on the advice of the Council of Ministers. It differs from adjournment, which is a temporary suspension of sitting. Prorogation clears the agenda of the current session and is necessary before a new session can be summoned. The Constitution requires at least two sessions annually, with no more than six months between sessions.

Explain the constitutional provisions related to proroguing Parliament and discuss its significance in parliamentary democracy.

Explain: Explain the constitutional basis of prorogation and its role in parliamentary functioning.

Intro: Prorogation is a constitutional mechanism for ending a parliamentary session, governed by Article 85.

  • Article 85 empowers the President to summon and prorogue Parliament on the Council of Ministers' advice.
  • The Constitution requires at least two sessions annually with no more than six months gap, ensuring regular legislative oversight.
  • Prorogation formally ends a session, distinct from adjournment, and is necessary for starting a new session.
  • The practice affects legislative business and executive accountability, as seen in prolonged unprorogued periods.

Conclusion: Thus, prorogation is essential for orderly parliamentary functioning and maintaining the balance of powers.
