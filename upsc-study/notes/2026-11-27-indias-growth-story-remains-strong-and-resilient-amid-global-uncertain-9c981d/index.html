India’s Growth Story Remains Strong and Resilient Amid Global Uncertainties

Union Minister of Commerce and Industry Shri Piyush Goyal highlighted India's robust economic growth, sustained reforms, and expanding trade opportunities at the India Debt Capital Market Summit 2026. This official affirmation is crucial for UPSC aspirants to understand India's economic resilience and policy direction amid global challenges.

India's economy showed 7.8% growth in Q1 FY 2026-27, with strong export performance and credit growth, reflecting resilience despite global uncertainties.

Why is this in the news?

Union Minister Shri Piyush Goyal addressed the India Debt Capital Market Summit 2026 on November 27, 2026, highlighting India's economic performance and growth prospects amid global uncertainties.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the current status of India’s economic growth?

  • Growth rate. India recorded 7.8% GDP growth in the first quarter of the current financial year and is expected to maintain this momentum in the second quarter.
  • Capital formation. Gross fixed capital formation increased by about 12%, indicating strong investment activity.
  • Export performance. Exports reached USD 570 billion in the first seven months, on track to achieve the USD 1 trillion export target set in May 2026.
  • Credit growth. Credit growth stood at about 19%, reflecting robust credit markets supporting economic expansion.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How has India maintained economic resilience amid global challenges?

  • Global uncertainties. India demonstrated resilience despite two ongoing wars, oil price shocks, tariff uncertainties, disruptions to sea routes, and sporadic attacks affecting air travel.
  • Policy reforms. Continuous reforms, improved ease of doing business, and an open-door policy for businesses have supported sustained growth.
  • Debt sustainability. India maintains among the lowest national debt levels globally, with external debt being a small portion of total debt.
  • Economic fundamentals. Higher foreign exchange reserves, remittances, and foreign direct investment inflows strengthen India’s economic stability.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the government’s plans and priorities for future growth?

  • Long-term vision. The government plans for the next 20 to 25 years with the vision of Viksit Bharat 2047 and aims to become a USD 30 trillion economy.
  • Trade agreements. India is finalising several free trade agreements, including India-EU and India-US FTAs, to expand market access.
  • Infrastructure development. Massive infrastructure projects are underway, including construction of more buildings in the next 30 years than currently exist.
  • Debt market role. Debt markets are critical for financing growth, with calls for innovative financing instruments and broader credit ecosystems.
  • Manufacturing and exports. Focus on export growth, manufacturing investments, value addition, and making India a global manufacturing hub.

Back to basics: Economic Growth and Resilience

Economic growth refers to the increase in a country's production and consumption of goods and services, measured by GDP. Resilience in an economy means its ability to withstand and recover from external shocks such as wars, price shocks, or global disruptions. Factors like investment, exports, credit availability, and policy reforms contribute to sustained growth and resilience.
