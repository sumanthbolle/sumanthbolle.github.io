Supreme Court PIL Questions Voter Enumeration and Electoral Roll Updates

A public interest litigation in the Supreme Court seeks disclosure of how many voters removed under absent, shifted, dead and duplicate (ASDD) categories have refilled enumeration forms to re-enter electoral rolls. This issue highlights concerns about electoral roll accuracy and voter rights, relevant for electoral reforms and democratic processes.

The Supreme Court is examining the process and transparency of voter re-enumeration for those removed from electoral rolls under ASDD categories.

Why is this in the news?

On November 17, 2026, transparency activists filed a PIL in the Supreme Court seeking data on voters removed under ASDD categories who submitted enumeration forms to be restored to electoral rolls.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the issue with voter enumeration and electoral rolls?

  • ASDD voter removal. Voters marked as absent, shifted, dead, or duplicate (ASDD) were deleted from draft electoral rolls during a Special Intensive Revision (SIR).
  • Submission of enumeration forms. Some ASDD voters submitted enumeration forms to be restored to the rolls, but the exact number is unknown and sought by petitioners.
  • Form 6 misuse. Form 6, meant for new voter registration, was used by excluded voters to re-enter rolls, which the Supreme Court called an 'error' since it requires applicants to declare they are first-time voters.
  • Election Commission directive. The Election Commission allowed ASDD voters in seven states, including Delhi, to submit enumeration forms again instead of using Form 6.
  • Concerns over Form 7 misuse. The petition also raised concerns about misuse of Form 7 for targeted deletions and requested an audit and public disclosure of all Form 7 applications.
  • Transparency in political party role. It was requested that booth-level agents filing Form 7 identify themselves as party representatives rather than individuals.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for electoral reforms and democracy?

  • Voter rights protection. Ensuring voters removed under ASDD categories can restore their names protects their right to vote and prevents disenfranchisement.
  • Electoral roll accuracy. Accurate and updated electoral rolls are essential for free and fair elections, preventing fraud and errors.
  • Transparency and accountability. Public disclosure of enumeration and deletion data, and transparency about political party involvement, strengthen electoral integrity.
  • Legal clarity on forms. Clarifying correct use of forms like Form 6 and Form 7 prevents procedural errors and misuse in voter registration and deletion.

Back to basics: Electoral Roll and Voter Enumeration

The electoral roll is an official list of eligible voters maintained by the Election Commission. Voter enumeration is the process of registering voters and updating the roll to reflect changes such as deaths, shifts, or duplicates. Forms like Form 6 are used for new voter registration, while Form 7 is used for corrections or deletions. Accurate rolls ensure fair elections by preventing fraud and disenfranchisement.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about voter enumeration and electoral roll updates in the context of the recent Supreme Court PIL: 1. Form 6 is intended for new voter registration but was misused by voters removed under ASDD categories to re-enter rolls. 2. The Election Commission allowed voters removed under ASDD categories in seven states to submit enumeration forms instead of Form 6. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). A trap is confusing the role of Form 7 with Form 6; Form 7 relates to corrections or deletions, not new registrations.

Quick revision

What are ASDD categories in electoral rolls?
ASDD stands for absent, shifted, dead, and duplicate voters whose names are removed from electoral rolls.
Why was the use of Form 6 by excluded voters called an error?
Because Form 6 is meant for first-time voter registration, and excluded voters were not first-time applicants.
What did the Election Commission allow for ASDD voters in seven states?
They allowed these voters to submit enumeration forms again instead of using Form 6.
