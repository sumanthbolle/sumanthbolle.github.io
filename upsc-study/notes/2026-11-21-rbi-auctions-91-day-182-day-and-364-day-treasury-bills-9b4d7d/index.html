RBI Auctions 91-Day, 182-Day and 364-Day Treasury Bills

The Reserve Bank of India announced the auction of Government of India Treasury Bills with tenures of 91, 182, and 364 days, totaling ₹23,000 crore. This auction is important for understanding government debt instruments and their role in fiscal management and monetary policy.

The RBI conducts price-based auctions of Treasury Bills to manage short-term government borrowing and liquidity in the economy.

Why is this in the news?

On November 20, 2026, the RBI announced the auction of 91-day, 182-day, and 364-day Treasury Bills totaling ₹23,000 crore.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What are the details of the RBI Treasury Bill auction?

  • Auction amounts and dates. The RBI announced auctions for 91-day and 182-day Treasury Bills at ₹8,000 crore each, and 364-day Treasury Bills at ₹7,000 crore, with the auction date on November 25, 2026, and settlement on November 26, 2026.
  • Auction method. The auction uses a price-based multiple price method where bids are submitted electronically via RBI’s Core Banking Solution (E-Kuber system).
  • Participation. State Governments, Union Territories with legislature, eligible Provident Funds, designated Foreign Central Banks, and specified persons or institutions can participate non-competitively; individuals can also participate as retail investors with a maximum allocation of 5% of the notified amount.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Why does the Treasury Bill auction matter for the exam?

  • Government borrowing tool. Treasury Bills are short-term government securities used to raise funds for meeting temporary mismatches in the government's cash flow.
  • Monetary policy and liquidity. Auctions of Treasury Bills help the RBI manage liquidity in the banking system and influence short-term interest rates.
  • Retail investor participation. The inclusion of retail investors through the Retail Direct portal reflects efforts to widen the investor base and promote financial inclusion.

Back to basics: Treasury Bills

Treasury Bills (T-Bills) are short-term debt instruments issued by the government to meet short-term funding needs. They are issued at a discount and redeemed at face value on maturity, with tenures typically ranging from a few weeks to one year. T-Bills are considered safe investments and are used by the central bank to manage liquidity in the economy.

Mains question (10 marks)

Explain the role of Treasury Bills in government borrowing and monetary policy management in India.

Explain: Explain how Treasury Bills function as instruments of short-term government borrowing and their significance in monetary policy.

Intro: Treasury Bills are short-term government securities used to manage temporary funding needs and liquidity.

  • They help the government raise funds for short durations without long-term debt commitments.
  • The RBI uses Treasury Bill auctions to regulate liquidity and influence short-term interest rates in the economy.
  • Participation by retail investors through platforms like the Retail Direct portal broadens the investor base and promotes financial inclusion.

Conclusion: Thus, Treasury Bills are vital tools for both fiscal management and monetary policy implementation.
