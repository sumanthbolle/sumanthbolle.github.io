Political Violence in Bangladesh and Ex-PM Sheikh Hasina’s Planned Return

Political violence has erupted in Bangladesh following former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina’s announcement to return from exile in December 2026. This development impacts India-Bangladesh relations and regional security, making it relevant for UPSC aspirants studying India’s neighbourhood policy.

Sheikh Hasina’s planned return from exile has triggered political violence in Bangladesh, highlighting challenges in its democratic process and bilateral ties with India.

Why is this in the news?

The political unrest in Bangladesh intensified after ex-PM Sheikh Hasina announced her return from India in December 2026, prompting violent protests and diplomatic tensions.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered the recent political violence in Bangladesh?

  • Hasina’s return announcement. The catalyst for the unrest was former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina’s announcement to return to Bangladesh in December 2026 after living in exile in India since 2024.
  • Protests and attacks. Following the announcement, there were attacks on police stations and Bangladesh National Party workers in Awami League strongholds, retaliatory arson on AL-linked properties, and violent protests across at least 28 districts.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does Sheikh Hasina’s return matter for Bangladesh’s politics?

  • Extradition and legal status. Bangladesh’s government has vowed to arrest and jail Hasina upon her return, seeking her extradition from India under an existing treaty, as she faces a death sentence and her party is banned following the 2024 uprising.
  • Democratic challenges. The violence reflects longstanding issues in Bangladesh’s politics, including the ruling class’s failure to accept opposition parties as legitimate political actors and the tendency to label opponents as foreign agents, which stifles political dialogue.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary[2] The Hindu · Secondary

How does this situation affect India-Bangladesh relations?

  • Diplomatic tension. Bangladesh expects India to honor the extradition treaty and return Hasina as a convict, while India has not confirmed any action, creating diplomatic friction.
  • Need for stable ties. Despite tensions, both countries have interests in cooperation on visas, energy, and river water sharing, and the political fallout must not derail these ties.

Back to basics: India and its neighbourhood relations

India’s neighbourhood policy involves managing diplomatic, security, and economic relations with neighbouring countries to ensure regional stability and mutual growth. Political developments in neighbouring countries like Bangladesh directly impact India’s security and bilateral cooperation, making it essential to understand these dynamics for UPSC preparation.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the recent political situation in Bangladesh: 1. Sheikh Hasina fled Bangladesh in 2024 and plans to return in December 2026. 2. Bangladesh’s government has accepted India’s refusal to extradite Hasina. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because Bangladesh expects India to honor the extradition treaty and has not accepted any refusal.
