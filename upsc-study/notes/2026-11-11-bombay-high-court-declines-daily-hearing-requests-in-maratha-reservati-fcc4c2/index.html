Bombay High Court refuses daily hearings in Maratha reservation case

The Bombay High Court on November 5, 2026, declined a request to conduct daily hearings on petitions challenging the 10% Maratha reservation quota in Maharashtra. This case involves constitutional questions on reservation limits and the judiciary's role in social justice, relevant for both Prelims and Mains.

The Bombay High Court decided to hear Maratha reservation petitions intermittently, reflecting judicial management of sensitive reservation disputes.

Why is this in the news?

On November 5, 2026, the Bombay High Court refused a petitioner's request for daily hearings in the case challenging the Maratha reservation quota in Maharashtra.

What is the current status of the Maratha reservation petitions in Bombay High Court?

  • Court's decision on hearing schedule. The Bombay High Court declined the request to hear the Maratha reservation petitions daily and decided to continue hearings intermittently, with the next session scheduled for November 27, 2026.
  • Bench details. A Bench of Justices Makarand Karnik, Nijamoodin Jamadar, and Sandeep Marne is hearing the petitions since September 18, 2026.
  • Petition background. The petitions challenge the 10% reservation quota for Marathas in education and employment under the State, enacted by the Maharashtra legislature in February 2024.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the legal and constitutional issues involved in the Maratha reservation case?

  • Reservation beyond 50% limit. The case questions the validity of reservation for Marathas beyond the 50% ceiling established by the Supreme Court in the 1992 Indra Sawhney judgment.
  • Previous Supreme Court rulings. In May 2021, the Supreme Court struck down the earlier SEBC Act, 2018, which granted 16% reservation to Marathas, citing lack of justification and questioning the data on backwardness.
  • Review petitions. The Maharashtra government’s review petition against the Supreme Court verdict was rejected in April 2023, and further relief petitions are pending.

Why does this matter for UPSC aspirants?

  • Judiciary's role in reservation policy. The case illustrates the judiciary's critical role in balancing social justice through reservation with constitutional limits and data-backed policy.
  • Understanding reservation limits. It highlights the constitutional principle that reservations generally should not exceed 50%, except in extraordinary circumstances.
  • Social empowerment and legal challenges. The petitions reflect ongoing debates on caste-based reservations and their impact on social empowerment and federal governance.

Back to basics: Reservation and Constitutional Limits

Reservation is a constitutional mechanism to provide affirmative action for socially and educationally backward classes. The Supreme Court has set a general ceiling of 50% on total reservations to maintain equality. Exceptions require strong justification based on backwardness data and social circumstances. Courts review such laws to ensure they comply with constitutional principles.
