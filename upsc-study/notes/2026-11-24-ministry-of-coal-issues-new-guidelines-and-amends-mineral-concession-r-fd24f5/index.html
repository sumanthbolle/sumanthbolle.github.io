Ministry of Coal Updates Guidelines and Rules for Coal and Lignite Mining

The Ministry of Coal issued new guidelines for mining and mine closure plans for coal and lignite mines and amended the Mineral Concession Rules to extend accreditation periods for key agencies. These reforms aim to streamline mining operations, improve regulatory efficiency, and support energy security, relevant for UPSC aspirants studying resource management and governance.

The Ministry of Coal's 2026 reforms enhance scientific mine planning, accelerate mine operationalization, and extend agency accreditation to improve coal sector governance.

Why is this in the news?

On 20 November 2026, the Ministry of Coal issued new guidelines for mining plans and mine closure plans, and on 17 November 2026, it amended the Mineral Concession Rules extending accreditation periods for agencies.

What are the new guidelines for mining and mine closure plans?

  • Guidelines issued. The Ministry of Coal issued the “Guidelines for preparation of Mining Plan and Mine Closure Plan for Coal and Lignite Mines, 2026” on 20 November 2026, effective 30 days later.
  • Framework purpose. These guidelines provide an outcome-oriented framework for systematic and scientific mine planning and closure, complementing existing statutory provisions and avoiding redundant compliance.
  • Time-bound processes. Defined timelines are set for scrutiny and approval stages, ensuring faster decision-making and reducing reimbursement time from escrow accounts for mine closure works to one month.
  • Technological recognition. The guidelines recognize Underground Coal Gasification as a mining method and include specific guidelines for its mining plan preparation and approval.
  • Operational flexibility. Project proponents are given greater flexibility aligned with prevailing conditions to enable faster operationalization of mines.
  • Expected impact. The guidelines aim to improve Ease of Doing Business, accelerate mine operationalization, support energy security, and promote scientific mine closure benefiting local communities.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How have the Mineral Concession Rules been amended?

  • Amendment notified. The Ministry of Coal notified the Mineral Concession (Third Amendment) Rules, 2026 on 17 November 2026.
  • Accreditation period extended. The accreditation cycle for Accredited Prospecting Agencies (APA) and Mining Plan Preparing Agencies (MPPA) has been extended from three to five years.
  • Benefits of extension. This reduces the frequency of assessments and re-accreditations, saving time and ensuring continuity in preparing geological reports and mining plans necessary for coal and lignite block operationalization.
  • Governance impact. The reform supports Ease of Doing Business and trust-based governance in the coal sector.

Back to basics: Mineral Concession Rules and Mining Plans

Mineral Concession Rules regulate the grant of mining leases and prospecting licenses in India. Mining plans are detailed documents prepared by accredited agencies outlining how mining operations will be conducted, including extraction methods, environmental safeguards, and closure plans. These plans ensure scientific and sustainable mining practices and are prerequisites for operationalizing mining blocks.
