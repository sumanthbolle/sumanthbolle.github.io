Nepal Begins Process to Declare 213 Missing Indians Presumed Dead After Flash Floods

Following the devastating flash floods in Nepal in August 2026, the Nepal government has started the official process to declare 213 missing Indian nationals presumed dead. This development is important for understanding India-Nepal disaster cooperation and cross-border humanitarian responses.

Nepal has initiated the presumption of death process for 213 missing Indians after the 2026 flash floods, highlighting India-Nepal disaster management collaboration.

Why is this in the news?

On November 25, 2026, the Indian Embassy in Nepal announced that the Nepal government has begun the process to declare 213 missing Indian nationals presumed dead following the August 26 flash floods.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current status of missing Indians after Nepal flash floods?

  • Presumption of death process started. Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA) has published a list of 213 missing Indian nationals and begun the formal process to declare them presumed dead.
  • Invitation for objections. The NDRRMA has invited objections or corrections to the list, allowing family members to contact the Indian Embassy in Nepal via email or phone before December 2, 2026.
  • Next steps after objections. After finalizing the list, Nepal will formally issue death declarations, and the Indian Embassy will obtain death certificates from local authorities.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What caused the flash floods and what was their impact?

  • Cause of floods. The flash floods were triggered by an ice-rock avalanche near the Nepal-Tibet border on August 26, 2026.
  • Extent of damage. The floods devastated towns and villages in northern and central Nepal, killing at least 1,453 people and leaving more than 5,000 missing.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How is the Indian Embassy supporting affected families?

  • Communication channels. The Embassy has provided an email (eoikathmandufloodsupport@gmail.com) and a phone number (+9779851316807) for family members to raise concerns or objections.
  • Documentation support. During the process, the Embassy may seek additional details and documents from families and will update them accordingly.

Back to basics: Disaster Management and Cross-Border Humanitarian Cooperation

Disaster management involves coordinated efforts to prepare for, respond to, and recover from natural or man-made disasters. When disasters affect multiple countries or foreign nationals, cross-border humanitarian cooperation becomes essential to provide timely assistance, share information, and manage casualties. Embassies play a key role in coordinating with local authorities and supporting their nationals during such crises.
