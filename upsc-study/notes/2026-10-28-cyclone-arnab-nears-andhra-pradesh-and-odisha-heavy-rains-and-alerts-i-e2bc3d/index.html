Cyclone Arnab Approaches Andhra Pradesh and Odisha with Heavy Rain Alerts

Cyclone Arnab is nearing the coast near Yanam or Kakinada, prompting red alerts and evacuation orders in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha. This event highlights the importance of disaster preparedness and management for UPSC aspirants.

Cyclone Arnab, a very severe cyclonic storm, is expected to make landfall near Yanam-Kakinada, causing heavy rains and prompting large-scale evacuations in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha.

Why is this in the news?

Cyclone Arnab's approach to Andhra Pradesh and Odisha in October 2026 has triggered extensive alerts and preparedness measures by state governments and the IMD.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current status and forecast of Cyclone Arnab?

  • Cyclone intensity and location. Cyclone Arnab is classified as a very severe cyclonic storm moving west-northwestwards over the Bay of Bengal, expected to make landfall near Yanam or Kakinada with wind speeds gusting up to 140 kmph.
  • Rainfall predictions. Isolated extremely heavy rainfall of 200 mm or more is forecasted in seven districts of Andhra Pradesh, with heavy to very heavy rainfall (70 mm to 200 mm) expected in several other districts.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How are Andhra Pradesh and Odisha preparing for Cyclone Arnab?

  • Evacuations and vulnerable groups. Andhra Pradesh has ordered evacuations from flood-prone areas, prioritizing vulnerable residents including 4,711 pregnant women who will be shifted to safer healthcare facilities.
  • Disaster response deployment. Seven NDRF and eight SDRF battalions have been deployed in Andhra Pradesh, while Odisha has prepared over 1,700 shelters and deployed eight NDRF teams and 40 Odisha Disaster Rapid Action Force teams.
  • Coordination and monitoring. Andhra Pradesh has opened multiple divisional and circle-level control rooms to monitor the situation, and Odisha is coordinating with the Centre for additional disaster response forces and assistance from the Army and Navy if needed.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the potential secondary impacts and concerns?

  • River discharge challenges. Odisha officials are concerned about the Mahanadi River's spate due to heavy rains and full moon conditions, which may complicate water discharge into the sea.
  • Impact on neighbouring regions. Heavy rains triggered by the cyclone are expected to affect Chhattisgarh when the cyclone curves away, requiring preparedness for flooding and water management.

Back to basics: Tropical Cyclones and Disaster Management

Tropical cyclones are intense circular storms that originate over warm ocean waters and can cause heavy rainfall, strong winds, and flooding when they make landfall. Disaster management involves preparedness, early warning, evacuation, and response measures to reduce the impact of such natural hazards on people and infrastructure.
