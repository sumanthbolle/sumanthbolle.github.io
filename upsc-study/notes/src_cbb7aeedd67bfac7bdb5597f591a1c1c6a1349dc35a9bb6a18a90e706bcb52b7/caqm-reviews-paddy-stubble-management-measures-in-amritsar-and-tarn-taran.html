India implications

  • Punjab’s district-level residue-management capacity affects the practical balance between agricultural operations and air-pollution control.
  • Farmer facilitation becomes a governance test: information about facilities is insufficient unless machinery and services reach farmers in time.
  • Industrial demand for processed residue can support a circular-resource model, but utilisation must remain linked to feasible sectors and compliance monitoring.

Way forward

  • Ensure machinery and residue-management facilities reach farmers at the operationally relevant time, not merely that their availability is announced.
  • Strengthen coordination among district administrations, farmers, Custom Hiring Centres, machinery providers, aggregators and industrial users.
  • Assign sector-wise nodal officers to verify actual residue utilisation and report compliance.
  • Combine in-situ and ex-situ pathways according to local feasibility, with productive use in suitable thermal power plants, brick kilns and industries.
