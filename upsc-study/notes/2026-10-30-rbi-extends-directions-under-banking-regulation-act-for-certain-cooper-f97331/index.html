RBI Extends Regulatory Directions for Three Cooperative Banks

The Reserve Bank of India has extended its directions under Section 35A read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, for three cooperative banks until January 30, 2027. This extension reflects RBI's ongoing regulatory oversight to ensure public interest and financial stability in the cooperative banking sector.

RBI extended regulatory directives for Sonpeth Nagari, Lokapavani Mahila, and Irinjalakuda Town cooperative banks to maintain oversight under the Banking Regulation Act.

Why is this in the news?

On October 28 and 29, 2026, RBI issued official extensions of its regulatory directions under the Banking Regulation Act for three cooperative banks, citing public interest and financial oversight.

What are the RBI directions under the Banking Regulation Act?

  • Legal basis. RBI issues directions under Section 35A read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, to regulate cooperative banks when necessary for public interest.
  • Purpose of directions. These directions impose operational restrictions or oversight to protect depositors and ensure financial stability in cooperative banks.

Which cooperative banks had their directives extended and why?

  • Banks involved. The Sonpeth Nagari Sahakari Bank Maryadit (Parbhani), Lokapavani Mahila Sahakari Bank Niyamitha (Mandya), and The Irinjalakuda Town Co-operative Bank Ltd. (Kerala) had their directives extended.
  • Extension details. Each bank's directive was extended for three months from October 30, 2026, to January 30, 2027, subject to review.
  • Reason for extension. RBI stated the extensions were necessary in the public interest but clarified this does not imply satisfaction with the banks' financial positions.

Back to basics: Banking Regulation Act and RBI's supervisory role

The Banking Regulation Act, 1949, empowers the Reserve Bank of India to regulate banking companies including cooperative banks. Under this Act, RBI can issue directions to banks to ensure their sound functioning and protect depositors' interests. Sections 35A and 56 allow RBI to impose restrictions or conditions on cooperative banks when their financial position or management raises concerns. This regulatory oversight helps maintain financial stability and public confidence in the banking system.
