Special Summary Revision of Electoral Rolls: Issues and Remedies

The Election Commission of India conducted a Special Summary Revision (SSR) of electoral rolls in several states to update voter lists after a Special Intensive Revision (SIR). This process has raised concerns about deletion of electors and the adequacy of remedies, which is important for understanding electoral reforms and democratic governance.

The SSR aimed to update electoral rolls but revealed flaws in deletion and re-enrolment processes, highlighting challenges in maintaining accurate voter lists.

Why is this in the news?

Following the SSR by the Election Commission of India in some states after the SIR, issues with deletion of electors and corrective measures have come to light in November 2026.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Special Summary Revision (SSR) and why was it conducted?

  • Purpose of SSR. The SSR was conducted to update electoral rolls in states that had undergone the Special Intensive Revision (SIR), adding back some electors and correcting the rolls.
  • Electorate changes. After the SIR, the electorate in 13 states shrunk from 49.61 crore to 45.22 crore but increased to 45.96 crore after the SSR added about 74 lakh names.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What issues arose from the Special Intensive Revision (SIR)?

  • Large deletions. The SIR led to a draft deletion of 13.37 crore electors, with deletions averaging 17% in Phase 3 and reaching 32.8% in Delhi, causing concern over accuracy.
  • Flaws in deletion process. The SIR required electors to provide details from the last SIR and subjected them to 'logical discrepancy' checks that resembled a citizenship test, raising legal and procedural issues.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What remedies has the Election Commission provided for deleted electors?

  • Corrective measures. The ECI allowed electors deleted by the SIR in some states to return to the rolls by explaining delays or submitting proof of new address, restoring their old EPIC numbers.
  • Limitations of remedies. These remedies cover about 4.5 crore of the 13.37 crore deleted electors and do not apply to those who moved out of their states or where SIR has concluded.
  • Supreme Court observation. The Supreme Court noted that forcing deleted electors to re-enrol as new voters was an error, prompting the ECI to use existing registration rules for corrections.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why do these issues matter for electoral reforms and democratic governance?

  • Electoral integrity. Accurate and inclusive electoral rolls are essential for fair elections and democratic legitimacy.
  • Need for better revision methods. The 2002-03 special revision, which enumerated shifted voters door to door, is suggested as a better model to avoid exclusion and errors.
  • Legal and procedural clarity. Addressing the legality of deletion criteria and simplifying re-enrolment can strengthen voter confidence and participation.

Back to basics: Electoral Roll Revision

Electoral roll revision is the process of updating voter lists to include new eligible voters and remove ineligible or deceased voters. It ensures the accuracy and integrity of electoral rolls, which is critical for free and fair elections. Revisions can be periodic or special, involving verification and correction of voter details.

What happens next?

  • Adopt door-to-door enumeration. The Supreme Court should direct the ECI to return to the door-to-door enumeration model used in 2002-03 to better capture shifted voters and reduce errors.
Common mistake. The SSR does not require proof of citizenship for enrolment, but deletion processes have imposed checks that resemble citizenship tests, which is legally questionable.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the Special Summary Revision (SSR) and Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls: 1. The SSR added about 74 lakh names to the electoral rolls after the SIR reduced the electorate. 2. The Supreme Court upheld the requirement for deleted electors to re-enrol using Form 6 as new voters. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the Supreme Court observed that forcing deleted electors to re-enrol as new voters was an error.
