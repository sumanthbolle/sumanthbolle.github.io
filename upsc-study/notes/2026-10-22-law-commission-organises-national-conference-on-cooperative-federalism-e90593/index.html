Law Commission Hosts National Conference on Cooperative Federalism and Law Reforms

The 23rd Law Commission of India organised a National Conference on 22 October 2026 to discuss cooperative federalism and law reforms involving State Law Commissions and national legal experts. This event highlights the constitutional balance of legislative powers and the need for coordinated legal reforms between the Centre and States, a key topic for UPSC exams.

The Law Commission's conference emphasized cooperative federalism and collaborative law reforms to balance Union and State legislative powers within India's constitutional framework.

Why is this in the news?

The Law Commission of India organised a National Conference on ‘Co-operative Federalism and Law Reforms: National and State Perspectives’ on 22 October 2026 at Vigyan Bhawan, New Delhi.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What was the purpose of the National Conference on Cooperative Federalism and Law Reforms?

  • Conference objective. The conference aimed to strengthen coordination among States and institutions engaged in law reform while respecting State autonomy and ensuring reforms respond to national and regional needs.
  • Participants involved. It brought together State Law Commissions, jurists, legal scholars, policymakers, former judges, members of the Bar, and academia from across India.
  • Key themes. Discussions focused on law reforms, cooperative federalism, constitutional governance, institutional frameworks for justice, and collaboration between Union and States within the Seventh Schedule division of powers.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What were the key messages from the Vice-President and Union Minister at the conference?

  • Vice-President's emphasis. Vice-President Shri C. P. Radhakrishnan highlighted India’s unity in diversity, the need for consultation and coordination between Centre and States, and urged States to repeal obsolete laws for efficient governance.
  • Cooperative federalism clarified. He stressed that cooperative federalism does not mean uniformity in all matters but requires working together where constitutional responsibilities converge while preserving State-specific needs.
  • Union Minister's priorities. Union Minister Shri Arjun Ram Meghwal outlined four priorities for law reform: continuous institutional dialogue, evidence-based and participatory reform, technology with human oversight, and citizen-centric legal design.
  • Law Commission Chairperson's proposal. Justice Dinesh Maheshwari proposed a 'citizen friction test' to assess laws based on their impact on time, cost, and procedural burden on citizens.

How does this conference relate to India's federal structure and law reform process?

  • Constitutional framework. The conference drew from the constitutional distribution of legislative powers across the Union, State, and Concurrent Lists in the Seventh Schedule.
  • Balancing autonomy and coordination. It sought to balance State autonomy with national objectives by promoting cooperative law reforms that respect regional diversity and local needs.
  • Institutional collaboration. The event aimed to create a structured framework for collaboration between the Law Commission of India, State Law Commissions, and equivalent bodies through joint studies, consultations, and sharing best practices.
  • Legal reform as a continuous process. The conference emphasized the need for continuous review and adaptive legislation responsive to social, economic, and technological changes.

Back to basics: Cooperative Federalism

Cooperative federalism is a concept where the Union and State governments work together to achieve common goals while respecting each other's constitutional powers and autonomy. It involves consultation, coordination, and shared responsibility rather than uniformity in all matters. This approach helps balance national objectives with regional diversity, especially in legislative and administrative functions.

What happens next?

  • Strengthen institutional dialogue. The conference proposed continuous institutional dialogue between national and State law-reform bodies to improve coordination and law reform implementation.
  • Adopt citizen friction test. Implementing the 'citizen friction test' can help assess laws for reducing procedural burdens on citizens, making legal processes more accessible and efficient.
  • Promote evidence-based reforms. Law reforms should be evidence-based, participatory, and incorporate technology with human oversight to ensure citizen-centric legal design.
