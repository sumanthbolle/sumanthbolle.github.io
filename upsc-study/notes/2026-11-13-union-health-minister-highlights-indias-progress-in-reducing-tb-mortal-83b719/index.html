India's Progress in Reducing TB Mortality and TB-Mukt Bharat Resolve

India has achieved a 38% reduction in tuberculosis (TB) mortality since 2015, surpassing the global decline, and maintains a 94% treatment success rate. The government’s intensified TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan campaign uses technology, community participation, and patient-centric care to accelerate TB elimination.

India’s intensified TB elimination efforts have led to its highest-ever reduction in TB mortality and a strong treatment success rate, advancing the goal of a TB-Mukt Bharat.

Why is this in the news?

On November 13, 2026, Union Health Minister J.P. Nadda highlighted India's significant progress in reducing TB mortality and reiterated the government’s commitment to the TB-Mukt Bharat campaign following the WHO Global Tuberculosis Report 2026.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What progress has India made in reducing TB mortality?

  • Mortality reduction. India recorded a 38% reduction in TB mortality since 2015, outperforming the global decline of 35%, with the mortality rate dropping from 28 deaths per lakh population in 2015 to 18 deaths per lakh in 2025.
  • Treatment success. India achieved a 94% treatment success rate among patients with drug-susceptible TB, reflecting strengthened diagnosis, timely treatment, and comprehensive patient care.
  • WHO recognition. The WHO South-East Asia Regional Office acknowledged India’s significant contribution to reducing TB mortality across the region.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How does the TB-Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan work?

  • Campaign scale. The TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan has covered over 25 crore people through intensified TB screening, conducting more than 11 crore chest X-rays and 3 crore molecular tests.
  • Early detection. Nearly 15 lakh asymptomatic TB cases were detected through proactive screening, enabling early diagnosis and timely treatment initiation.
  • Technology and community. The campaign uses AI-enabled diagnostics, rapid molecular tests, the Ni-kshay digital platform, nutritional support under Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana, and community support via Ni-kshay Mitras.
  • Community participation. In 2025, 67,933 Panchayats were declared TB-free, and initiatives like JOSH Teams involve government officials, NCC cadets, and volunteers to support local awareness and mobilisation.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this matter for health policy and governance?

  • National ownership. India meets 96% of its TB programme funding requirements through domestic resources, showing strong national investment and ownership.
  • Comprehensive approach. The approach integrates technology, nutrition, community engagement, and patient-centric care, strengthening the National TB Elimination Programme (NTEP).
  • Jan Andolan. Under Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s leadership, TB elimination has become a nationwide movement involving multiple stakeholders to ensure no patient is left behind.

Back to basics: Tuberculosis and Public Health Campaigns

Tuberculosis (TB) is a contagious bacterial infection primarily affecting the lungs and is a major public health challenge. Public health campaigns against TB focus on early detection, timely treatment, and patient support to reduce transmission and mortality. Strategies include mass screening, use of diagnostic technologies, nutritional support, and community involvement to ensure adherence and reduce stigma.
