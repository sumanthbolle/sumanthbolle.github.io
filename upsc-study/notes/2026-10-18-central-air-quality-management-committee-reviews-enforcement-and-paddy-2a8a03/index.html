CAQM Reviews Enforcement and Paddy Stubble Management in Punjab and NCR

The Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) conducted inspections and reviews in Punjab's districts and the National Capital Region (NCR) to assess enforcement against stubble burning and industrial pollution. This matters for UPSC aspirants as it highlights India's institutional mechanisms for air pollution control and sustainable agricultural residue management.

CAQM's enforcement and monitoring efforts in Punjab and NCR aim to reduce air pollution by preventing paddy stubble burning and ensuring industrial compliance.

Why is this in the news?

In October 2026, CAQM led by Dr. T.S. Panwar reviewed enforcement measures in Punjab's Moga, Firozpur, and Faridkot districts and compliance status across NCR to prevent stubble burning and control pollution.

What enforcement actions did CAQM review in the NCR?

  • Inspections conducted. Between 25.09.2026 and 09.10.2026, CAQM's Enforcement Task Force conducted 124 inspections across NCR, including 54 industrial units, 6 construction and demolition sites, and 64 diesel generator sets.
  • Violations and actions. These inspections found 55 violations leading to proposed closures of 2 C&D projects and 3 industrial units, sealing of 12 units, and issuance of compliance orders and environmental compensation.
  • Flying Squads' role. CAQM's Flying Squads inspected 28,868 units across NCR, issued 1,837 closure directions, and 1,491 resumption orders after compliance verification.
  • Focus sectors. Inspections prioritized Red and Orange category industries, especially those with furnaces and boilers, to effectively mitigate air pollution.
  • Coordination and monitoring. CAQM emphasized strengthening enforcement, timely compliance verification, and close coordination among agencies for pollution control.

[2] Press Information Bureau

How is CAQM addressing paddy stubble burning in Punjab?

  • District visits and reviews. CAQM teams visited Moga, Firozpur, and Faridkot districts to assess ground-level implementation of stubble burning prevention during the paddy harvesting season.
  • Management measures. The Commission reviewed in-situ and ex-situ management of paddy residue, including availability and deployment of Crop Residue Management (CRM) machinery and field surveillance.
  • Technology use. Deployment of drones and low-cost sensors was reviewed for monitoring and trend analysis of stubble burning incidents.
  • Farmer facilitation. Efforts to sensitize farmers about the harmful effects of stubble burning and to facilitate their access to CRM machinery were emphasized.
  • Coordination among stakeholders. CAQM stressed better coordination among farmers, aggregators, industries, and district administrations to prevent stubble burning.

Back to basics: Air Quality Management and Stubble Burning

Air quality management involves monitoring and controlling pollutants to protect public health and the environment. Paddy stubble burning is a major source of air pollution in northern India, releasing particulate matter and harmful gases. Managing crop residue through in-situ (on-field) and ex-situ (off-field) methods, supported by machinery and enforcement, helps reduce pollution and improve air quality.
