India implications

  • Policy should treat substance dependence through an integrated continuum: prevention, treatment, rehabilitation, family support, after-care and social reintegration.
  • District-level access matters because timely voluntary help can prevent dependence from worsening into family disruption, financial instability and loss of livelihood.
  • Outcome monitoring should distinguish treatment access from sustained recovery, including relapse prevention, family functioning, livelihood continuity and dignity.
  • Coordination among the Ministry, state authorities, treatment centres, families and livelihood-support systems can make rehabilitation more durable.

Way forward

  • Expand accessible, voluntary and non-stigmatising rehabilitation and counselling services at district level.
  • Build continuity between treatment, family counselling, after-care, relapse prevention and livelihood support.
  • Use measurable indicators such as retention, relapse prevention, family functioning, livelihood continuity and beneficiary well-being.
  • Publish independent evaluations while protecting confidentiality and dignity, especially in beneficiary narratives.
