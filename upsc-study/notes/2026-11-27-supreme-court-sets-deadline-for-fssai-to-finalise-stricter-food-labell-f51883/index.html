Supreme Court directs FSSAI to finalise stricter food labelling norms in 4 months

The Supreme Court has mandated the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) to implement a single-phase front-of-pack nutrition labelling system with prominent red warning labels on packaged foods high in sugar, saturated fat, or salt. This judicial directive highlights the role of the judiciary in public health governance and consumer protection.

The Supreme Court ordered FSSAI to finalise stricter front-of-pack nutrition labelling within four months to protect consumers' right to health.

Why is this in the news?

On November 26, 2026, the Supreme Court set a four-month deadline for FSSAI to finalise draft regulations for stricter front-of-pack nutrition labelling on packaged foods.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court direct FSSAI to do?

  • Single-phase labelling regime. The Court directed FSSAI to formulate a single-phase front-of-pack nutrition labelling (FoPNL) regime mandating prominent red warning labels on packaged foods high in sugar, saturated fat, or salt.
  • Nutrients of concern. FSSAI was asked to treat 'total sugars' and 'saturated fat', rather than 'added sugar' and 'added fat', alongside salt, as the nutrients of concern for labelling.
  • Design and size of labels. The Court prescribed separate red hexagon warning labels for each nutrient with pictorial representation and text such as 'HIGH FAT', 'HIGH SUGAR', or 'HIGH SALT', occupying 4% to 5% of the package's principal display panel.
  • Deadline and transition period. FSSAI must finalise the draft regulations within four months and allow a 365-day transition period for compliance, with safeguards to prevent misuse such as stockpiling unlabelled products.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for public health and consumer rights?

  • Right to health obligation. The Court observed that the right to health obliges the state to regulate industry practices detrimental to public health, including controlling exposure to harmful substances in food.
  • Consumer protection. Mandatory warning labels help consumers make informed choices by clearly indicating high levels of sugar, fat, or salt in packaged foods.
  • Preventing label fatigue. The Court cautioned against setting thresholds too low or too high to avoid desensitising consumers or allowing unhealthy products to escape scrutiny.
  • Addressing double standards. The Court highlighted concerns about multinational food companies applying different standards in India compared to other countries, emphasizing equal protection for Indian consumers.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does the judgment address nutritional literacy and enforcement?

  • Nutritional literacy in schools. The Court emphasized early nutritional education as preventive healthcare and directed the Centre to ensure compliance with existing regulations and enhance school curricula with interactive teaching methods.
  • Enforcement safeguards. FSSAI was instructed to prevent misuse of the transition period by planning how to phase out unlabelled products already in circulation and control excessive manufacturing without labels.

Back to basics: Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) and Food Labelling

FSSAI is the regulatory body responsible for setting standards and regulating food safety in India. Food labelling provides consumers with information about the nutritional content of packaged foods, helping them make informed choices. Front-of-pack nutrition labelling (FoPNL) uses clear, visible labels on the front of food packages to indicate high levels of nutrients of concern such as sugar, fat, and salt.

What happens next?

  • Prevent misuse of transition period. FSSAI must develop clear plans to phase out unlabelled products already manufactured and prevent excessive production of such products during the transition period.
  • Enhance nutritional education. The Centre should ensure that nutritional literacy is effectively integrated into school curricula with practical teaching methods to promote healthy dietary habits from childhood.

  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the transition period allowed is 365 days, not 60 days; 60 days is the public comment period for the draft regulations.
