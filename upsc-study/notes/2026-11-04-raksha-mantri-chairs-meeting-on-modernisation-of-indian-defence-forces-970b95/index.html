Raksha Mantri Chairs Meeting on Modernisation of Indian Defence Forces

On November 3, 2026, Raksha Mantri Shri Rajnath Singh chaired a Consultative Committee meeting in Jaisalmer focusing on modernising the Indian Defence Forces. The meeting discussed enhancing military capabilities through technology, indigenous production, and jointness, which are key for India's defence preparedness and self-reliance.

The Indian government is accelerating defence modernisation by boosting indigenous production, technology integration, and joint operational capabilities to meet evolving security challenges.

Why is this in the news?

Raksha Mantri chaired a meeting on November 3, 2026, to deliberate on modernisation of Indian Defence Forces, focusing on technology, indigenous manufacturing, and operational preparedness.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What were the key focus areas of the Defence Forces modernisation meeting?

  • Capability development. The meeting emphasized accelerating induction of next-generation platforms, weapon systems, and technologies to enhance combat effectiveness and operational preparedness.
  • Jointness and integration. Strengthening tri-service integration, joint operational planning, and synergized application of capabilities across multiple domains was highlighted.
  • Indigenous manufacturing. Boosting Make-in-India and Aatmanirbhar Bharat through indigenous research, design, development, and manufacturing with greater participation of domestic industry, start-ups, and academia.
  • Emerging technologies. Harnessing artificial intelligence, autonomous systems, unmanned platforms, cyber, space, electronic warfare, and advanced communications to prepare for future conflicts.
  • Research and innovation. Strengthening collaboration among Defence Forces, DRDO, Defence PSUs, private industry, and academia to accelerate transition from innovation to operational capability.
  • Resource optimisation. Establishing priorities, institutional mechanisms, and measurable milestones to ensure modernisation initiatives yield tangible operational outcomes.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How is the government supporting defence modernisation and self-reliance?

  • Budget and capital acquisition. The defence budget for FY 2026-27 was increased to a record Rs 7.85 lakh crore, with Rs 2.19 lakh crore earmarked for modernisation and capital procurement.
  • Growth in defence production. Defence production increased from Rs 46,429 crore in FY 2014-15 to Rs 1.78 lakh crore in FY 2025-26, with an aim to reach Rs 3 lakh crore by 2029.
  • Defence exports expansion. Defence exports grew from less than Rs 2,000 crore in 2014 to approximately Rs 40,000 crore in 2025-26, targeting Rs 50,000 crore by 2029.
  • Private sector participation. The government is enabling greater private sector involvement, including MSMEs and start-ups, in defence manufacturing and R&D.
  • Human capital focus. Modernisation includes enhancing personnel capabilities and preparedness, with new Indian Army formations adapting to contemporary operational needs.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

Why is indigenous production and technological self-reliance important for India’s defence?

  • Technological sovereignty. Self-reliance means ensuring critical defence needs are not dependent on external sources by possessing design, manufacturing, intellectual property, and sustainment capabilities.
  • Strategic autonomy. Indigenous capabilities reduce strategic dependence on foreign countries and safeguard national security.
  • Global competitiveness. Expanding defence exports and collaborations enhances India’s position in the global defence market and fosters sustainable industrial ecosystems.
  • Innovation ecosystem. Balancing operational requirements with research freedom encourages innovation, experimentation, and transition of technologies from labs to operational use.
  • International partnerships. Future collaborations focus on co-development, co-production, shared innovation, and meaningful Indian participation in design and technology sharing.

[2] Press Information Bureau

How is the character of modern warfare influencing India’s defence reforms?

  • Multi-domain battlefields. Warfare now extends beyond land, sea, and air to space, cyberspace, and digital domains, requiring new operational approaches.
  • Blurring warfare types. The distinction between conventional and unconventional warfare is diminishing, with drones and autonomous systems challenging traditional platforms.
  • Need for rapid innovation. The ability to innovate quickly, manufacture at scale, and replenish capabilities is as important as possessing advanced weapon systems.
  • Defence reforms focus. Reforms emphasize jointness, integration, emerging technologies, innovation, and strengthening the domestic defence industrial ecosystem.

Back to basics: Defence Modernisation and Aatmanirbharta

Defence modernisation involves upgrading military capabilities through new technology, equipment, and operational strategies to meet evolving security challenges. Aatmanirbharta in defence means achieving self-reliance by developing indigenous design, manufacturing, and sustainment capabilities, reducing dependence on foreign sources. This approach strengthens national security and supports strategic autonomy.
