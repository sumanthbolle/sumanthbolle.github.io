India implications

  • India can compare the EU’s approach while refining its own securitisation framework to balance credit expansion with investor protection and systemic-risk control.
  • Greater use of securitisation in major economies may influence cross-border capital allocation and the competitive environment for Indian financial institutions; the precise effect depends on the final EU rules and market response.
  • India–EU financial dialogue could examine standards for disclosure, risk retention, prudential treatment and supervision, without assuming that an EU model is directly transferable.

Way forward

  • Publish clear and comparable information on underlying assets, cash flows, servicing and performance.
  • Require proportionate due diligence and meaningful risk retention so originators do not externalise all incentives.
  • Align capital treatment with actual risk and strengthen supervision across banks, non-bank institutions and market intermediaries.
  • Develop crisis-management and resolution arrangements for interconnected securitisation structures.
