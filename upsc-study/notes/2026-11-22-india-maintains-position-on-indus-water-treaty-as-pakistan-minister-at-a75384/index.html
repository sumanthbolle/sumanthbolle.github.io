India Maintains Stance on Indus Water Treaty Amid Pakistan Minister's SCO Visit

India reiterated its unchanged position on the Indus Water Treaty during the visit of Pakistan's Climate Change Minister Musadik Malik to the SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum in Delhi in November 2026. This event marks a rare diplomatic engagement since the suspension of the treaty in 2025 following a terror attack, highlighting ongoing tensions and the treaty's significance in India-Pakistan relations.

India's firm stance on the Indus Water Treaty remains unchanged despite Pakistan's participation in the SCO forum hosted in Delhi.

Why is this in the news?

The Ministry of External Affairs clarified India's position on the Indus Water Treaty on November 20, 2026, coinciding with Pakistan's minister attending the SCO forum in Delhi.

What is India's position on the Indus Water Treaty during the SCO forum?

  • Unchanged stance. India's Ministry of External Affairs stated on November 20, 2026, that there has been absolutely no change in its position on the Indus Water Treaty.
  • Context of suspension. India suspended the treaty in 2025 as a punitive measure against Pakistan following the April 22 Pahalgam terror attack that caused 26 deaths.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does Pakistan's minister attend the SCO forum in India?

  • SCO multilateral event. Pakistan's Climate Change Minister Musadik Malik attended the SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum in Delhi, an event hosted by India and open to all SCO members.
  • Diplomatic significance. This was possibly the first time a Pakistani minister was invited to an event in India since the military hostilities in May 2025, signaling a rare diplomatic engagement.
  • Cultural role. Mr. Malik emphasized culture's key role in fostering better ties between India and Pakistan during the forum.

What is the significance of the SCO forum for India-Pakistan relations?

  • Platform for dialogue. The SCO forum serves as a multilateral platform where India and Pakistan can engage despite bilateral tensions.
  • Invitation policy. India extended invitations to all SCO members, including Pakistan, maintaining established diplomatic practice.
  • Potential for improved ties. Experts view Pakistan's participation as an opportunity to explore improved relations within a multilateral framework.

Back to basics: Indus Water Treaty

The Indus Water Treaty is a water-sharing agreement between India and Pakistan that allocates the use of the Indus River system's waters. It was designed to manage water resources between the two countries and prevent disputes. The treaty is significant for bilateral relations, especially given the shared river basins and regional security concerns.
