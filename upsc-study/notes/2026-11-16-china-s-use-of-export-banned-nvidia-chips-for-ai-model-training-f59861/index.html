China's Use of Export-Banned Nvidia Chips for AI Model Training

An investigation found Nvidia Blackwell GPU fingerprints in advanced Chinese AI models despite US export restrictions. This raises questions about the effectiveness of US export controls and the challenges in enforcing technology denial regimes.

China's leading AI companies appear to have used Nvidia's export-banned Blackwell GPUs to train advanced AI models, highlighting enforcement gaps in US semiconductor export controls.

Why is this in the news?

On November 16, 2026, The Indian Express published an investigation revealing that Chinese AI models show hardware fingerprints of Nvidia's Blackwell GPUs, which are banned for export to China by the US government.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

What did the investigation find about Nvidia chips in Chinese AI models?

  • Hardware fingerprints detected. Researchers found distinctive Nvidia Blackwell GPU fingerprints in six AI models developed by four Chinese companies, indicating use of advanced US-made chips despite export bans.
  • Methodology used. The investigation analyzed publicly released AI model weights, identifying mathematical patterns linked to the GPU hardware used for initial model training.
  • Scale of usage. Analysis estimated that around 12,000 Blackwell GPUs were collectively used in five training runs, with a plausible range of 7,000 to 22,000 chips.
  • Limitations. The fingerprints identify hardware used to initialize model weights but do not conclusively prove the entire training was done on these GPUs or confirm physical import violations.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

How might China have accessed these restricted Nvidia chips?

  • Third-country routes. Evidence suggests restricted Nvidia processors entered China through intermediaries in countries like Malaysia, with trade data indicating possible smuggling worth over $3 billion.
  • Circumventing export controls. Chinese AI firms may be using overseas data centers and sophisticated systems to access advanced hardware and American AI technology despite US restrictions.
  • US government concerns. US officials have accused Chinese companies like Moonshot AI of acquiring restricted Nvidia GPUs via servers in Thailand and other locations.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

Why does this matter for US-China technology relations and global AI governance?

  • Effectiveness of export controls questioned. The findings highlight challenges in enforcing semiconductor export restrictions, as China may still access advanced AI chips indirectly.
  • Strategic implications. US export controls aim to slow China's AI progress and prevent military applications, but enforcement gaps could undermine these goals.
  • Political controversy in the US. Disputes within the US administration have emerged over enforcement failures and the role of companies suspected of aiding China, complicating policy coherence.

Back to basics: Export Controls on Semiconductor Technology

Export controls are government measures that restrict the sale or transfer of sensitive technologies to certain countries to protect national security. Semiconductor export controls limit the shipment of advanced computer chips to prevent their use in military or strategic applications by rival nations. These controls rely on licensing and monitoring but face enforcement challenges when goods are routed through third countries or re-exported.
