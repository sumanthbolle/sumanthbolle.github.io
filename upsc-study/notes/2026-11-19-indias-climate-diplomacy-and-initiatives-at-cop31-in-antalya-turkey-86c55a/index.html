India’s Climate Diplomacy and Initiatives at COP31 in Antalya, Turkey

At COP31 in Antalya, India highlighted its climate diplomacy focusing on equity, climate justice, and sustainable development while advancing mechanisms like the India-Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism and promoting disaster-resilient infrastructure. These developments are crucial for UPSC aspirants to understand India’s international climate stance and its domestic and global climate action strategies.

India at COP31 emphasized equity-based climate action, operationalized the India-Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism, and promoted disaster-resilient infrastructure to support sustainable development and climate justice.

Why is this in the news?

India’s Environment Minister Shri Bhupender Yadav presented India’s climate diplomacy and initiatives including the India-Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism and the Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure at UNFCCC COP31 held in Antalya, Turkey in November 2026.

What key climate initiatives did India highlight at COP31?

  • India-Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism. India operationalized the India-Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism (JCM) under Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement, signed in August 2025, to mobilise finance, facilitate technology transfer, and advance low-carbon development aligned with India’s Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) and Long-Term Low Emission Development Strategy (LT-LEDS).
  • Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure. India positioned the Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI) as a global platform to strengthen infrastructure resilience through international cooperation, emphasizing knowledge sharing, capacity building, and affordable sustainable finance.

[1] Press Information Bureau[3] Press Information Bureau

What principles and demands did India emphasize in its climate diplomacy?

  • Equity and Climate Justice. India called for climate action rooted in equity, climate justice, and the principle of Common but Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC), stressing that developing countries should not bear the burden of historical emissions.
  • Climate Finance Obligations. India urged developed countries to honestly and expeditiously fulfil their climate finance obligations and cautioned against unilateral trade measures penalizing developing countries under the guise of climate action.
  • Development and Climate Action. India emphasized that developing countries must not choose between climate action and their right to development, decent living standards, health, and education, and insisted on equitable access to the remaining carbon budget.

How did India address transboundary water issues and bilateral ties at COP31?

  • Indus Waters Treaty Context. India stated that sharing of transboundary river waters cannot be based on 'archaic mechanisms' or divorced from the broader bilateral relationship, indirectly referring to the Indus Waters Treaty being in abeyance since 2025 due to cross-border terrorism concerns.
  • Need for Contemporary Cooperation. India highlighted the importance of considering climatic, demographic, and technological changes in transboundary water cooperation and reiterated that constructive cooperation can lead to shared progress and prosperity.

Back to basics: Common but Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC)

CBDR-RC is a principle under international environmental law recognizing that while all countries are responsible for addressing global environmental destruction, they have different capabilities and differing responsibilities based on their historical contributions to the problem. It underpins climate negotiations by emphasizing equity and fairness in climate action commitments.

What happens next?

  • Strengthening Domestic Capacities. India plans to strengthen domestic validation and verification capacities under the JCM to ensure robust monitoring, reporting, and verification of emission reductions.
  • Translating Commitments into Action. India emphasized the need to translate international climate commitments into practical outcomes on adaptation, mitigation, and resilience, supported by stronger international cooperation and finance.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about India's climate initiatives at COP31: 1. The India-Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism was established under Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement to support low-carbon development. 2. The Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI) focuses on promoting disaster-resilient infrastructure through global cooperation. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). A trap could be confusing the JCM with other bilateral mechanisms or misunderstanding CDRI as a domestic-only initiative rather than a global platform.

Quick revision

What is the India-Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism?
It is a mechanism established under Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement to mobilise finance, transfer technology, and support low-carbon development between India and Japan.
What principle did India emphasize for climate action at COP31?
India emphasized the principle of Common but Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC) for equity and climate justice.
What is the Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI)?
CDRI is a global initiative led by India to promote international cooperation for strengthening disaster-resilient infrastructure.
