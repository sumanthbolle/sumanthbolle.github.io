India’s Renewable Energy Journey: Progress and Policies

India has rapidly expanded its renewable energy capacity to enhance energy security and support environmental goals. This transformation includes solar, wind, hydro, bio-energy, geothermal, and green hydrogen, backed by strong policy and investment.

India’s renewable energy capacity reached around 560 GW in 2026, with non-fossil sources accounting for 55%, reflecting a major shift towards clean energy.

Why is this in the news?

On 13 November 2026, the Press Information Bureau released an official backgrounder detailing India’s renewable energy progress and policies.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the current status of India’s renewable energy capacity?

  • Installed capacity growth. India’s total installed power capacity reached around 560 GW in October 2026, up from 249 GW in 2014.
  • Non-fossil share. Non-fossil fuel capacity stood at 308 GW as of 31 July 2026, accounting for 55% of total installed capacity.
  • Global ranking. India ranks third globally in renewable energy installed capacity.

[1] Press Information Bureau

How is India expanding its solar and wind energy sectors?

  • Solar capacity expansion. Installed solar capacity reached approximately 175 GW by 31 October 2026, up from 2.8 GW in 2014, making solar the largest renewable component.
  • Solar growth market. India became the world’s second-largest solar growth market in 2025, adding over 37 GW of solar capacity.
  • PM-Surya Ghar scheme. Launched in February 2024 with an outlay of ₹75,021 crore, it benefits 65.26 lakh households with rooftop solar installations and has commissioned 19.1 GW of rooftop capacity.
  • Wind energy status. Installed wind capacity was 59.20 GW as of 29 October 2026, with a record addition of 6.05 GW in FY 2025–26, and around 28 GW under implementation.
  • Wind manufacturing. Wind turbine manufacturing capacity rose to about 24 GW by March 2026, with 70–80% indigenisation of key components.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What other renewable energy sources and initiatives are significant?

  • Bio-energy capacity. Bio-power capacity stood at 11.75 GW as of 29 October 2026, with 839 compressed biogas plants registered.
  • Hydropower capacity. Cumulative hydropower capacity was 57.49 GW as of 29 October 2026, including large and small hydro projects.
  • Green hydrogen mission. The National Green Hydrogen Mission, launched in 2023 with an outlay of ₹19,744 crore, targets 5 MMT of green hydrogen production by 2030.
  • Geothermal energy policy. Notified in September 2025, it supports exploration and development of geothermal resources, with an estimated potential of 10,600 MW.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What policy and investment support underpins India’s renewable energy growth?

  • Investment scale. India invested USD 63.7 billion in renewable energy during July 2024–June 2026.
  • Bharat Renewable Energy Summit. Held in November 2026, it brought together global and Indian stakeholders to discuss financing, manufacturing, storage, and emerging technologies.
  • Government schemes. Schemes like PM-Surya Ghar, Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana, and GOBARdhan support solar rooftop, floating solar projects, and bioenergy respectively.
  • Offshore wind support. The Union Cabinet approved a ₹7,453 crore Viability Gap Funding scheme in June 2024 for 1 GW of offshore wind projects.

Back to basics: Renewable Energy and Energy Transition

Renewable energy comes from natural sources that replenish quickly, such as sunlight, wind, water, and biomass. Energy transition refers to shifting from fossil fuels to cleaner energy sources to reduce environmental impact and enhance sustainability. This transition involves policy support, technology development, infrastructure, and investment to increase renewable energy capacity and integration into the power grid.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about India’s renewable energy sector: 1. India’s total installed power capacity reached around 560 GW in October 2026, with non-fossil sources accounting for 55%. 2. The National Green Hydrogen Mission targets 5 MMT of green hydrogen production by 2030 with an outlay of ₹19,744 crore. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). The trap is confusing the total installed capacity with only renewable capacity or mixing up the outlay and target figures for the Green Hydrogen Mission.
