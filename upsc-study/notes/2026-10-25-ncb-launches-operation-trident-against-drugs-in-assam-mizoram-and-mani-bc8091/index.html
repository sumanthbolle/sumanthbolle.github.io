NCB launches Operation Trident against drug cartel in Northeast India

The Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB) launched Operation Trident on 23 October 2026, targeting an international drug trafficking cartel across Assam, Mizoram, and Manipur. This operation marks a strategic shift from seizure-centric to network-centric and financial disruption approaches, important for understanding India's internal security and narcotics control policies.

Operation Trident is a coordinated multi-agency crackdown dismantling a transnational drug cartel trafficking narcotics through the Northeast, reflecting India's resolve to eradicate drug networks by 2029.

Why is this in the news?

On 23 October 2026, the NCB led a massive multi-agency crackdown called Operation Trident across 38 locations in Assam, Mizoram, and Manipur, targeting a transnational narco-cartel.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What was Operation Trident and how was it executed?

  • Operation overview. Operation Trident was launched by the NCB in coordination with Mizoram Police and the Enforcement Directorate to simultaneously strike 38 locations across Assam, Mizoram, and Manipur on 23 October 2026.
  • Multi-agency coordination. The operation involved 38 composite teams acting under a unified command, covering key urban hubs and sensitive border areas including Aizawl, Champhai, Zokhawthar, Churachandpur, and the Cachar/Assam transit corridor.
  • Strategic approach. It exemplified a shift from seizure-centric to network-centric and financial disruption tactics, aiming to dismantle both the logistics and financial backbone of the drug syndicate.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Who was targeted and what were the key outcomes?

  • Cartel background. The operation targeted a transnational syndicate led by Myanmar-based kingpin Thangcintuang, trafficking methamphetamine and heroin across the Myanmar–Mizoram–Manipur–Assam–Tripura corridor into India and Bangladesh.
  • Financial disruption. The crackdown uncovered ₹100 crore in hawala transactions, froze 1,424 bank accounts, and attached properties worth ₹17.82 crore linked to the cartel.
  • Seizures and arrests. Authorities seized 500 detonators, ₹12 lakh in cash, and arrested 18 people involved in the narcotics network.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does Operation Trident matter for India's internal security?

  • Transnational threat. The operation addressed drug trafficking through the 'Death Triangle Corridor' spanning India, Myanmar, and Bangladesh, a major conduit for narcotics in the region.
  • Policy alignment. It aligns with the Vision Document on Narcotics Control (2026-2029) and India's mission to eradicate every drug network by 31 December 2029.
  • Financial and network focus. The focus on financial disruption and network dismantling represents a strategic evolution in narcotics control, enhancing the effectiveness of enforcement agencies.

Back to basics: Narcotics Control and Enforcement

Narcotics control involves coordinated efforts by agencies like the Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB) to prevent drug trafficking and abuse. Enforcement includes intelligence gathering, financial investigations, and coordinated raids to dismantle drug networks. A shift from seizure-centric to network-centric approaches focuses on disrupting entire criminal syndicates and their financial channels, rather than just confiscating drugs.
