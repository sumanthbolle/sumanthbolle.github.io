Core Sector Growth Hits Series-High of 10.9% in October 2026

India's core industrial sectors grew by 10.9% in October 2026, driven mainly by infrastructure-related production and a low base effect from the previous year. This growth is crucial for understanding recent industrial momentum and infrastructure development in the economy.

Core sector growth surged to 10.9% in October 2026 due to infrastructure push and statistical low base effects.

Why is this in the news?

The Ministry of Commerce and Industry released data on November 20, 2026, showing core sector growth at a series-high of 10.9% in October 2026.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What drove the core sector growth to 10.9% in October 2026?

  • Infrastructure sectors' strong growth. Infrastructure-related sectors like iron ore, steel, and cement showed robust growth without relying on low base effects, with iron ore growing 23.4%, steel production up 7.3%, and cement at 15.4%.
  • Coal and electricity surge. Coal production increased by 23.05% and electricity generation by 21.6%, though both benefited from a low base effect since these sectors had contracted in October 2025.
  • Low base effect impact. The growth rate was partly inflated by a low base, as core sector output had grown only 0.8% in October 2025, influenced by a shift in the festive calendar.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Which sectors lagged despite overall growth?

  • Fuel sector contraction. Crude oil production contracted by 4.1%, marking 22 consecutive months of decline, while natural gas production fell 7.6%, the worst since the new index series began.
  • Refinery products slowdown. Growth in refinery products slowed to a three-month low of 3.4% in October 2026.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this growth matter for the economy and UPSC preparation?

  • Indicator of industrial momentum. Core sector growth reflects the health of key industries like coal, cement, steel, and electricity, which are foundational for economic development and infrastructure.
  • Insight into infrastructure push. Strong growth in infrastructure-related sectors indicates government and market focus on building physical assets, relevant for questions on economic growth and development.
  • Understanding statistical effects. Recognizing the role of low base effects helps critically analyze economic data trends rather than taking growth figures at face value.

Back to basics: Core Sector and Its Economic Significance

The core sector comprises key industries such as coal, crude oil, natural gas, refinery products, steel, cement, electricity, and fertilizers that are fundamental to the economy's infrastructure and industrial activity. Growth in these sectors is a leading indicator of economic health because they supply essential inputs to other industries. The Index of Core Industries measures monthly production changes in these sectors to track industrial momentum and infrastructure development.
