Static anchor

Crop Residue Management (CRM) machinery comprises equipment used to manage crop residue either in the field through in-situ treatment or by supporting collection and removal for ex-situ management. In the reported review, CAQM treated timely availability and farmer access as implementation priorities.

Background

  • Paddy harvesting creates a narrow interval for residue disposal before the next crop is sown. Burning is therefore linked not only to environmental harm but also to the availability, affordability and timely deployment of alternatives.
  • In-situ management treats or incorporates residue in the field, whereas ex-situ management removes straw for collection and utilisation outside the field. A practical strategy requires both routes and coordination across the supply chain.
  • The issue involves environmental governance and agricultural administration together: district preparedness, farmer facilitation, machinery access, monitoring and enforcement must operate simultaneously.

India implications

  • Air-pollution control requires implementation in source regions, not only episodic response in affected urban areas.
  • District-level coordination can improve convergence between environmental authorities, agriculture departments, farmers, aggregators and industries.
  • Better residue utilisation can convert an environmental liability into an input for productive uses, subject to viable collection and market arrangements.
  • Technology-assisted surveillance can improve accountability, but should complement—not replace—farmer support and field-level facilitation.

Way forward

  • Ensure district-level machinery planning matches the harvesting window and local demand.
  • Provide farmer-facing facilitation so available CRM machines can be accessed and used in time.
  • Build dependable coordination between farmers, aggregators and industries for ex-situ management.
  • Combine hotspot surveillance, drones and low-cost sensors with field verification and responsive administration.
