NHRC Issues Advisory on Women’s Safety at Work and Public Spaces

The National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) of India issued an advisory on October 27, 2026, addressing women’s safety in workplaces and public spaces. This advisory targets multiple Union Ministries, the Railway Board, and State authorities, urging action on eight key areas to improve safety and security for women.

The NHRC advisory highlights persistent challenges in women’s safety despite laws, urging coordinated action across government bodies to ensure safer workplaces and public spaces.

Why is this in the news?

On October 27, 2026, the NHRC issued an advisory to key Union Ministries, the Railway Board, and State Chief Secretaries on women’s safety at work and in public spaces, seeking action and progress reports within two months.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What prompted the NHRC advisory on women’s safety?

  • Safety concerns persist. Despite stringent laws protecting women’s rights, safety at workplaces and public spaces remains a concern, with about 4.4 lakh cases of crimes against women reported in 2026.
  • Labour force participation. Women’s participation in the labour force was 34.6% as per the Periodic Labour Force Participation Survey in September 2026, highlighting the need for safe environments to enable full workforce participation.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the key focus areas of the NHRC advisory?

  • Eight key areas. The advisory emphasizes raising awareness on women’s safety and sexual harassment, improving infrastructure, encouraging bystander intervention, enhancing police roles, providing survivor support, addressing cybercrime against women, implementing long-term reforms, and other measures.
  • Targeted authorities. The advisory was sent to Secretaries of ten Union Ministries including Women and Child Development, Education, Home Affairs, and others, as well as the Railway Board Chairperson and State Chief Secretaries/Administrators.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does the NHRC consider women’s safety vital?

  • Constitutional entitlement. Women’s safety is essential to ensure their constitutional rights to justice and equal opportunities.
  • Workforce participation. Safe workplaces and public spaces are necessary conditions for women to participate fully in the workforce and realize their potential.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What follow-up does the NHRC require from authorities?

  • Action Taken Reports. The NHRC has urged all concerned authorities to implement the advisory’s recommendations and submit progress reports within two months.

Back to basics: National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)

The NHRC is a statutory body established to protect and promote human rights in India. It issues advisories and monitors the implementation of laws related to human rights, including women’s rights. The NHRC can direct government bodies to take action and report on progress to ensure rights are upheld.
