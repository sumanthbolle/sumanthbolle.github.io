China Imposes Export Controls on Solar, AI, Biotech Technologies

China announced draft rules restricting exports of over 40 advanced technologies including solar manufacturing, AI, and biotech citing national security concerns. This affects countries like India aiming to reduce dependence on Chinese technology and has implications for trade and security policies.

China's new export controls on key technologies reflect its national security priorities and impact global supply chains, including India's technology sectors.

Why is this in the news?

On October 16, 2026, China announced a draft plan titled 'Announcement No. 48' to restrict exports of 40 key technologies in AI, biomedicine, and photovoltaics citing national security.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the new export controls imposed by China?

  • Scope of controls. China's draft plan proposes a complete export ban on 14 technologies and restrictions on 26 others, covering next-generation solar manufacturing, AI models, and biotech platforms.
  • Technologies affected. The banned technologies include perovskite and tandem solar-cell manufacturing, AI tools for generating malicious code, and cell and gene therapy platforms.
  • Reason for controls. China cites national security concerns, noting these technologies have applications in aerospace, defense, unmanned systems, and sensitive sectors.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How do these controls impact India and global trade?

  • Effect on Indian solar industry. Controls on next-generation solar technologies and polysilicon engineering could delay Indian manufacturers' production and affect efforts to reduce dependence on China.
  • Investment and collaboration challenges. Chinese firms face constraints on solo investments, joint ventures, and licensing deals in India for affected technologies due to these export restrictions.
  • Global supply chain implications. The controls may disrupt international technology cooperation and supply chains in AI, biotech, and solar sectors.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does China justify these export controls?

  • National security safeguard. China aims to prevent risks from loss of control over sensitive technologies and their misuse by malicious actors threatening critical infrastructure and defense.
  • International practice. China aligns its controls with global export control practices to regulate technologies with dual-use or sensitive applications.
  • Balance with cooperation. Technologies already public or open-source and overseas chip fabrication activities remain largely exempt to maintain technological cooperation.

Back to basics: Export Controls and National Security

Export controls are government-imposed restrictions on the export of certain technologies or goods to protect national security and foreign policy interests. They often target dual-use technologies that can have both civilian and military applications. Countries use export controls to prevent sensitive technologies from falling into the hands of adversaries or being used in ways that threaten their security. These controls can affect international trade, technology transfer, and global supply chains.
