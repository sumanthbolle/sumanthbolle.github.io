10th National Convention of Agrivision 2026 and Mahila Kisan Diwas Highlight Agricultural Progress and Women’s Role

The 10th National Convention of Agrivision was held in New Delhi on October 13-14, 2026, focusing on shifting Indian agriculture from production-centric to value-chain approaches. Concurrently, Mahila Kisan Diwas 2026 was celebrated nationally to honour women farmers’ contributions and empowerment in agriculture.

The Agrivision convention emphasized market-aligned agricultural development, while Mahila Kisan Diwas recognized women farmers’ vital role and promoted their empowerment in Indian agriculture.

Why is this in the news?

The 10th National Convention of Agrivision was held on October 13-14, 2026, and Mahila Kisan Diwas was celebrated nationally on October 15, 2026, both events organized by the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare.

What was the focus of the 10th National Convention of Agrivision 2026?

  • Event details. Held at ICAR-NASC Complex, New Delhi, the convention gathered around 450 students from 25 states, scientists, officials, entrepreneurs, and farmers to discuss agricultural education, research, market-oriented agriculture, and farmer-centric development.
  • Shift in agricultural approach. Dr. M. L. Jat emphasized moving beyond a production-centric approach to adopt a broader agri-food system and value-chain perspective, linking production with marketing and value addition to improve farmer profitability.
  • Encouragement for entrepreneurship. Speakers encouraged students to pursue innovation, entrepreneurship, and employment generation in agriculture, highlighting opportunities in teaching, research, and extension.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What key announcements and issues did Union Agriculture Minister highlight?

  • Agricultural growth and self-reliance. Shri Shivraj Singh Chouhan noted a 49% increase in foodgrain production from 2014 to 2024, with India becoming the world’s largest rice producer and second in wheat production, marking a shift from scarcity to self-reliance.
  • Regulation of agricultural education. He announced plans to establish an Agriculture Council to regulate private agricultural colleges, curb fake degrees, and ensure quality education safeguarding students’ futures.
  • Integrated farming for small farmers. To improve incomes of small and marginal farmers with an average landholding of 0.96 hectares, diversification through integrated farming involving fruits, vegetables, medicinal plants, animal husbandry, fisheries, beekeeping, and natural farming was promoted.
  • Technology adoption. The minister emphasized accelerating the use of AI, machine learning, and robotics in agriculture to modernize practices and improve efficiency.
  • Policy formulation based on expert input. He invited students and farmers to contribute ideas at the convention, promising government responsibility for policy formulation and implementation based on these inputs.

[2] Press Information Bureau

Why is Mahila Kisan Diwas 2026 significant and what does it highlight?

  • Recognition of women farmers. Mahila Kisan Diwas 2026, celebrated nationally on October 15, recognizes the significant contributions of women farmers to Indian agriculture and rural development.
  • Women’s role in agriculture. Women contribute across the agricultural value chain including sowing, harvesting, livestock management, seed conservation, natural resource management, and value addition, using traditional knowledge and sustainable practices.
  • Workforce participation. According to the Periodic Labour Force Survey 2025, females constituted 59.8% of the total workforce engaged in agricultural activities.
  • International recognition. The year 2026 is declared the International Year of the Woman Farmer by the United Nations, providing a global platform to promote women farmers’ empowerment.
  • Theme and activities. The theme ‘Role of Women Farmers in Building Developed Agriculture and a Developed India’ highlights women’s leadership in adopting modern technologies, crop diversification, livestock management, and natural resource conservation.
  • Inclusive participation. Celebrations include women farmers from tribal regions, agricultural officers, extension functionaries, and officials, facilitating experience sharing and encouraging leadership.

Back to basics: Agricultural value chain and women’s role in agriculture

The agricultural value chain includes all steps from production to processing, marketing, and consumption, emphasizing value addition beyond mere crop production. Women play a crucial role across this chain, contributing labor, traditional knowledge, and entrepreneurship, which are vital for sustainable and resilient agricultural development.

Mains question (15 marks)

Discuss the importance of shifting from a production-centric approach to a value-chain perspective in Indian agriculture and the role of women farmers in this transformation.

Discuss: Explain how adopting a value-chain approach can improve farmer incomes and how empowering women farmers contributes to agricultural development.

Intro: Indian agriculture is transitioning from focusing solely on production to integrating value addition and market orientation.

  • Value-chain approach links production with marketing and processing, enhancing profitability and sustainability for farmers.
  • The 10th National Convention of Agrivision emphasized this shift and encouraged entrepreneurship and innovation among agricultural students and farmers.
  • Women constitute a significant portion of the agricultural workforce and contribute across the value chain through labor, knowledge, and enterprise.
  • Mahila Kisan Diwas 2026 highlighted women’s leadership in adopting modern technologies and sustainable practices, essential for building developed agriculture.

Conclusion: Integrating value-chain strategies and empowering women farmers are key to advancing Indian agriculture and rural prosperity.
