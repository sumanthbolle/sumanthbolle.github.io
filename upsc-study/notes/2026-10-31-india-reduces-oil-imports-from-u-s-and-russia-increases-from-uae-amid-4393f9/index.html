India Cuts U.S. and Russian Oil Imports, Boosts UAE Supply Amid Sanctions

In September 2026, India stopped importing oil from the U.S. and sharply reduced imports from Russia following the U.S. Russia Sanctions Act, while increasing oil imports from the UAE. This shift highlights India's adaptation to international sanctions and its impact on energy security and foreign relations.

India adjusted its oil import sources in response to U.S. sanctions by eliminating U.S. oil imports, reducing Russian oil, and increasing UAE imports, concentrating 60% of its oil supply from Russia and the UAE.

Why is this in the news?

India's oil import pattern changed in September 2026 after the U.S. passed the Russia Sanctions Act, which imposed tariffs on countries importing Russian oil, prompting India to reduce imports from the U.S. and Russia and increase imports from the UAE.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What changes occurred in India's oil imports in September 2026?

  • U.S. oil imports stopped. India completely stopped importing oil from the U.S. in September 2026, after U.S. sanctions were enacted.
  • Russian oil imports reduced. India cut its Russian oil imports by nearly 18% compared to August 2026, reducing Russia's share from 44% in August to 33% in September.
  • UAE oil imports increased. India increased its oil imports from the UAE by 67% compared to August 2026, raising the UAE's share to 26.5%, an all-time high.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why did India shift its oil import sources?

  • Response to U.S. sanctions. The U.S. Russia Sanctions Act empowered the U.S. to impose tariffs up to 100% on countries importing large quantities of Russian oil, prompting India to reduce imports from Russia and the U.S.
  • Sanctions temporarily waived. Although the tariffs were temporarily waived by a U.S. executive order in October 2026, India had already adjusted its imports in September.
  • Energy security considerations. India maintained a concentrated oil import pattern, relying on Russia and the UAE for 60% of its crude oil, balancing sanctions compliance and energy needs.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does this shift affect India's economy and foreign relations?

  • Rising import bill. Despite only a 4.1% increase in oil volume from September 2025 to 2026, India's oil import bill rose by 33% due to elevated crude prices.
  • Impact on India-U.S. relations. The U.S. sanctions were viewed by some as an affront to India's sovereignty, complicating bilateral ties.
  • Strategic ties with UAE. Increasing imports from the UAE strengthens India's energy partnership with a key West Asian country amid regional instability.

Back to basics: Energy Security and International Sanctions

Energy security means ensuring a stable and affordable supply of energy resources like oil. Countries often import crude oil from multiple sources to diversify risk. International sanctions can restrict trade with certain countries, forcing importers to adjust their sources. Tariffs are taxes on imports that can make buying from certain countries more expensive, influencing trade decisions.
