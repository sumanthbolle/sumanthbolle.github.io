RBI Extends Regulatory Directions for Two Cooperative Banks

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has extended the period of regulatory directions under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 for The Pusad Urban Co-operative Bank Ltd. and Sri Guru Raghavendra Sahakara Bank Niyamitha. This matters for UPSC aspirants as it highlights RBI's role in cooperative bank regulation and financial stability.

RBI extended directions under Sections 35A and 56 of the Banking Regulation Act for two cooperative banks to ensure continued regulatory oversight.

Why is this in the news?

On November 5 and 6, 2026, RBI extended directions under Section 35A read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 for two cooperative banks to continue regulatory control.

[1] Reserve Bank of India[2] Reserve Bank of India

What are the RBI directions extended for the cooperative banks?

  • Extension for Pusad Urban Co-operative Bank. RBI extended the regulatory directions for The Pusad Urban Co-operative Bank Ltd., Maharashtra, for three months from November 7, 2026, to February 7, 2027, under Section 35A read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act.
  • Extension for Sri Guru Raghavendra Sahakara Bank. RBI extended the regulatory directions for Sri Guru Raghavendra Sahakara Bank Niyamitha, Bengaluru, for six months from November 10, 2026, to May 10, 2027, under the same provisions.
  • Nature of directions. These directions are regulatory measures imposed by RBI to oversee the banks’ operations due to concerns about their financial position.

[1] Reserve Bank of India[2] Reserve Bank of India

Why does RBI issue and extend such directions under the Banking Regulation Act?

  • Legal basis. RBI exercises powers under sub-section (1) of Section 35A read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, to issue directions to cooperative banks for their proper functioning.
  • Public interest and financial stability. Extensions are made when RBI is satisfied that it is necessary in the public interest to continue regulatory oversight to protect depositors and maintain financial stability.
  • No implication of financial health. RBI clarifies that extension of directions does not imply satisfaction with the bank’s financial condition.

  • Banking Regulation Act provisions. Understanding Sections 35A and 56 of the Banking Regulation Act is important for questions on financial sector governance and regulatory frameworks.
  • Financial sector governance. The extensions reflect ongoing regulatory challenges in cooperative banking, relevant for governance and economic stability topics.

Back to basics: Banking Regulation Act and RBI's supervisory role

The Banking Regulation Act, 1949 empowers the Reserve Bank of India to regulate and supervise banking institutions, including cooperative banks, to ensure their sound functioning and protect depositors. Sections 35A and 56 allow RBI to issue directions to banks in public interest, including restrictions or operational controls, to maintain financial stability and confidence in the banking system.
