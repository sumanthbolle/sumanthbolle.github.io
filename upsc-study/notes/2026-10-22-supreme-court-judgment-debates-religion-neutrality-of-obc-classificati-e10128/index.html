Supreme Court Judgment Questions Religion-Neutrality of OBC Status

The Supreme Court ruled that a Hindu OBC person loses reservation benefits upon converting to another religion, distinguishing the source of social backwardness between Hindu and non-Hindu OBCs. This judgment challenges the long-held view of religion-neutral OBC classification, impacting reservation policy and social justice debates.

The Supreme Court clarified that OBC reservation entitlement depends on the social order linked to religion, affecting converts and the principle of religion-neutral backwardness.

Why is this in the news?

The Supreme Court's October 15, 2026 judgment clarified the religion-neutrality of OBC classification and reservation entitlement for converts.

What did the Supreme Court decide about OBC reservation and religion?

  • Loss of reservation on conversion. The Court ruled that a Hindu OBC individual loses entitlement to reservation benefits if they convert to another religion, as their social backwardness source changes.
  • Religion-neutrality challenged. While OBC classification has been religion-neutral since the 1992 Indra Sawhney judgment, this ruling distinguishes between Hindu and non-Hindu OBCs based on social order.
  • Non-Hindu OBCs recognized. The Court clarified that non-Hindu communities can be classified as OBC, but the basis of backwardness differs from Hindu OBCs.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does the judgment interpret social backwardness and caste?

  • Source of backwardness matters. The Court emphasized that the 'source of social backwardness' must be considered, linking Hindu OBC entitlement to membership in the Hindu social order.
  • Caste's role in Hindu society. Caste is the foundational institution determining backwardness in Hindu society, unlike in other religions where social stratification differs.
  • Difference from Indra Sawhney. Unlike the 1992 judgment, which treated caste as a common factor across religions, this ruling distinguishes social stratification mechanisms between Hindu and non-Hindu communities.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for reservation policy and social justice?

  • Impact on converts' reservation rights. The judgment affects whether converts retain reservation benefits, influencing social justice for those changing religion.
  • Re-examination of OBC criteria. It opens debate on whether OBC classification should remain religion-neutral or consider social order differences.
  • Constitutional and policy implications. The ruling challenges existing frameworks on affirmative action and may lead to policy adjustments regarding backward class identification.

Back to basics: Reservation and Social Backwardness

Reservation in India is a constitutional provision to promote social justice by providing affirmative action to historically disadvantaged groups. OBC classification identifies communities that are socially and educationally backward based on criteria like social, educational, and economic indicators. Traditionally, OBC classification has been religion-neutral, unlike Scheduled Castes which are religion-specific. Caste is a key social stratification in Hindu society influencing backwardness.
