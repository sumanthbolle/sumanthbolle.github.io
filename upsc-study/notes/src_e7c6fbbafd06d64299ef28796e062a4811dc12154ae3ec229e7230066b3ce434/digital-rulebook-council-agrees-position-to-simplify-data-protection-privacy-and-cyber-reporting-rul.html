India implications

  • Indian firms serving EU users may need to monitor changes in EU compliance, privacy and incident-reporting obligations.
  • A clearer reporting architecture could lower procedural friction for Indian technology and digital-service exporters, but divergence between EU and Indian rules may still require parallel compliance.
  • India can draw lessons for interoperable cyber-incident reporting while preserving independent regulatory oversight and data-subject safeguards.

Way forward

  • Separate procedural simplification from substantive rights: reduce duplicated filings without lowering privacy or security standards.
  • Adopt clear single-entry reporting protocols with defined authority, timelines, confidentiality and escalation rules.
  • Preserve independent supervision, internal documentation and auditability so that easier reporting does not become weaker accountability.
  • For India, promote interoperable cyber-incident reporting and regulatory coherence while aligning export-facing compliance support with domestic privacy safeguards.
