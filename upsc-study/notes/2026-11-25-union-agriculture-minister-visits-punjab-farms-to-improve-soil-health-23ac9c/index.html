Union Agriculture Minister Visits Punjab Farms to Promote Soil Health and Farmer Income

Union Agriculture Minister Shri Shivraj Singh Chouhan visited farms in Punjab's Tarn Taran district to engage with farmers on soil health and income improvement. He emphasized the need for implementing the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana and highlighted government support for crop residue management and procurement at MSP.

The Union Agriculture Minister's visit to Punjab focused on promoting sustainable farming practices, insurance coverage, and assured procurement to improve farmers' income and soil health.

Why is this in the news?

On 24 November 2026, Union Agriculture Minister Shri Shivraj Singh Chouhan visited farms in Punjab to discuss soil health and farmer income improvement with local farmers and panchayats.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What were the key messages from the Agriculture Minister's visit?

  • Focus on soil health. The Minister highlighted the deterioration of soil health due to excessive chemical fertilizer use and stubble burning, warning of future crises if soil fertility is not preserved.
  • Promotion of sustainable practices. He praised farmers of Booh Haveliyaan village for not burning stubble for six years and adopting techniques like Happy Seeder and Direct Seeding as part of the 'Khet Bachao Abhiyan'.
  • Call for insurance implementation. The Minister expressed disappointment over Punjab's reluctance to implement the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana, stressing its importance for compensating flood and disaster-related losses.
  • Government support for machinery. The Centre provides about ₹286 crore annually for Crop Residue Management machinery, but the state government must ensure quality procurement and repair services.
  • Assured procurement at MSP. The Minister assured farmers that the Central Government procures the entire quantity of paddy, wheat, pulses, and cotton at Minimum Support Price, with procurement quantities higher than under the previous UPA government.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does this visit matter for agriculture policy and farmer welfare?

  • Soil health and sustainability. Improving soil health is critical to sustaining agricultural productivity and preventing long-term damage from chemical overuse and stubble burning.
  • Insurance for risk mitigation. Implementing crop insurance schemes like Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana helps protect farmers from income losses due to natural disasters, enhancing their financial security.
  • Integrated farming promotion. The Minister emphasized integrated farming including crop diversification, animal husbandry, fisheries, and poultry to increase profitability and reduce costs.
  • Government procurement role. Guaranteed procurement at MSP ensures farmers receive fair prices, supporting income stability and incentivizing production.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What are the responsibilities of the state government in this context?

  • Implement insurance schemes. The Punjab government is urged to implement the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana to provide compensation for crop losses.
  • Ensure machinery quality. While the Centre funds Crop Residue Management machinery, the state government must ensure quality procurement, distribution, and repair services for farmers.

Back to basics: Minimum Support Price (MSP) and Crop Insurance

Minimum Support Price (MSP) is a government-guaranteed price at which it procures certain crops from farmers to ensure income stability. Crop insurance schemes like the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana provide financial compensation to farmers for losses due to natural disasters, helping reduce the risk in farming.

What happens next?

  • State implementation of insurance. Punjab must implement the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana to compensate farmers for flood and disaster losses as urged by the Union Minister.
  • Quality assurance in machinery. The state government should ensure quality checks and repair arrangements for agricultural machinery funded by the Centre.
