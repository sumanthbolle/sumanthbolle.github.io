India Continues Russian Crude Imports After U.S. Sanctions Waiver

India is expected to maintain its imports of Russian crude oil near current levels after U.S. President Donald Trump waived sanctions mandated by a new U.S. law targeting major buyers of Russian oil. This development is important for UPSC aspirants studying India's energy security and international relations involving sanctions.

India remains the largest importer of Russian seaborne crude, continuing purchases after the U.S. waived sanctions that could have imposed tariffs and restrictions.

Why is this in the news?

On October 16, 2026, U.S. President Donald Trump waived sanctions and tariffs mandated by a new U.S. law targeting major buyers of Russian crude oil, allowing India to continue its imports.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the U.S. sanctions waiver on Russian crude imports?

  • Sanctions law details. The Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act authorised tariffs up to 100% on goods from the five largest importers of Russian crude and gas and sanctions on Russia's shadow fleet of tankers.
  • Waiver issued. President Trump issued an executive order on October 16, 2026, waiving these sanctions and tariffs before the 30-day enforcement deadline expired.
  • Scope of waiver. The waiver is not specific to India and also benefits other major buyers like China, with the administration able to reconsider the waiver later.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How has India’s Russian crude import pattern changed?

  • India as top importer. India is the largest importer of Russian seaborne crude, averaging 1.8 million barrels per day (bpd) in 2026, ahead of China at 1.2 million bpd.
  • Import volumes. In September 2026, India imported about 1.82 million bpd of Russian crude, with October expected to hold at 1.8-1.9 million bpd.
  • Peak imports. Indian imports peaked at 2.6 million bpd in June and 2.57 million bpd in July 2026.
  • Shift after Ukraine invasion. Before the 2022 Ukraine invasion, Russian crude was about 2% or less of India’s imports; after Western sanctions on Russia, India rapidly increased purchases due to steep discounts.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for India’s energy security and economy?

  • Discounted Russian crude. Russian crude was available at steep discounts, sometimes around $23 per barrel cheaper than Brent crude in September 2026, making it an attractive alternative to West Asian supplies.
  • Refining and exports. Indian refiners processed Russian crude into fuels like diesel for domestic use and exports, creating a lucrative export stream.
  • Impact of sanctions waiver. The U.S. waiver, including a diesel sale allowance until April 7, 2027, reduces the risk of sanctions enforcement, encouraging continued imports.

Back to basics: International Sanctions and Energy Security

International sanctions are penalties imposed by countries to influence the behavior of other nations, often restricting trade. Energy security refers to a country's ability to secure reliable and affordable energy supplies. When sanctions target energy exports, countries dependent on those supplies may seek alternative sources or negotiate waivers to maintain their energy needs without violating international rules.

Common mistake. The U.S. sanctions waiver does not mean sanctions on Russian crude imports are permanently lifted; the waiver can be reconsidered later.
