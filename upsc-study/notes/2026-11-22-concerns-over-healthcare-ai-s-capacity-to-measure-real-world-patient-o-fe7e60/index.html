Challenges in Measuring Real-World Outcomes of Healthcare AI

A French expert highlighted that most hospitals worldwide lack the capacity to evaluate whether AI systems improve patient outcomes, raising concerns about the clinical and economic value of healthcare AI. This issue is critical for understanding the governance and policy challenges in integrating AI into healthcare.

Hospitals struggle to measure real-world patient outcomes from AI, questioning the actual benefits of healthcare AI technologies.

Why is this in the news?

On November 22, 2026, Emmanuel Bacry, Scientific Director of France’s Health Data Hub, warned at the Bengaluru Tech Summit that hospitals worldwide are ill-equipped to assess the real-world impact of AI systems on patient outcomes.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why do hospitals struggle to measure AI's impact on patient outcomes?

  • Data integration challenges. Hospitals find it difficult to access and integrate patient information after discharge, such as recovery rates and mortality, which hampers tracking the real-world impact of AI-based medical technologies.
  • Limited evaluation capacity. Only about six or seven hospitals worldwide currently have the ability to evaluate medical devices deployed in their systems, including AI technologies.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What risks arise from inadequate evaluation of healthcare AI?

  • Unintended consequences. An AI alert system for severe kidney failure doubled deaths in some US hospitals, possibly due to lack of tested response protocols among staff, showing that AI can have harmful effects if not properly integrated.
  • Misleading accuracy scores. Laboratory accuracy scores of AI systems may not translate into better clinical performance; a system with 95% accuracy might not outperform a doctor with 90% accuracy in real-life settings.
  • Black-box problem. AI can detect patterns unknown even to its developers, such as predicting ethnicity or sex from medical images, which may introduce bias and affect medical decisions without clinician understanding.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is Western bias in healthcare AI data a concern?

  • Skewed training data. Approximately 70% of global healthcare AI training data comes from male, white, Western populations, leading to an 'Occidental AI' that may not perform well in other demographic contexts.
  • Need for local adaptation. Smaller, locally tailored AI models trained on representative population data are advocated to avoid disastrous consequences in developing countries.

Back to basics: Healthcare AI and patient outcome measurement

Healthcare AI refers to artificial intelligence technologies applied to medical diagnosis, treatment, and patient management. Measuring patient outcomes means tracking real-world effects of treatments or technologies on recovery, mortality, and quality of life after hospital discharge. Accurate outcome measurement requires integrating diverse patient data over time, which is essential to assess the true clinical value of AI tools beyond laboratory accuracy.

Laboratory accuracy scores of AI systems always reflect their clinical performance in hospitals. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because laboratory accuracy scores do not necessarily translate into better clinical outcomes, as highlighted by the expert.
