Joint Workshop Highlights Role of Agriculture and Rural Development in Poverty Eradication

A joint workshop chaired by Union Minister Shri Shivraj Singh Chouhan reviewed the contributions of agriculture and rural development sectors in reducing India's extreme poverty from 27.1% to 2.6%. This event underscored the importance of inter-ministerial coordination and scientific innovations in sustaining rural progress and targeting the remaining poor.

India's poverty rate has dropped dramatically due to coordinated efforts in agriculture, rural development, and scientific research, as highlighted in a 2026 joint ministry workshop.

Why is this in the news?

The joint workshop held on 23 November 2026 in Delhi, chaired by Union Minister Shri Shivraj Singh Chouhan, assessed the role of agriculture and rural development ministries in eradicating poverty.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What were the key outcomes of the joint workshop?

  • Poverty reduction achievement. India reduced extreme poverty from 27.1% to 2.6%, lifting nearly 500 million people out of poverty, a feat acknowledged by the World Bank.
  • Agriculture sector growth. The agriculture growth rate reached a historic 4.45%, surpassing Green Revolution-era levels, supported by initiatives like PM-KISAN and digital tools such as AgriStack.
  • Rural development impact. Schemes like Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana, PMAY-G, PMGSY, and Viksit Bharat - G RAM G have improved rural livelihoods, housing, roads, and employment.
  • Scientific research returns. ICAR innovations delivered an economic return of ₹13.85 for every rupee invested, with 3,436 new crop varieties released since 2014, 93% of which are climate-resilient.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

How do agriculture and rural development schemes contribute to poverty eradication?

  • Direct financial support. More than ₹4.66 lakh crore was provided through PM-KISAN to farmers, boosting incomes and agricultural productivity.
  • Women empowerment. Over 10 crore women joined self-help groups under Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana, with 3.76 crore becoming 'Lakhpati Didis', enhancing rural economic participation.
  • Infrastructure development. 3.99 crore permanent rural houses and 7.99 lakh kilometres of rural roads were constructed, improving living standards and connectivity.
  • Watershed and land resource management. Watershed development under PMKSY covered 3.40 crore hectares, benefiting 61.15 lakh farmers and increasing irrigated areas and milk production.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] Press Information Bureau

What are the future goals and strategies discussed?

  • Sustaining poverty reduction. The government aims to prevent those lifted out of poverty from falling back and to bring the remaining 2.6 to 2.7% of the population above the poverty line.
  • Global market integration. The next objective is to enable Indian agricultural products to compete successfully in global markets through Free Trade Agreements.
  • Inclusive policy improvement. Union Minister encouraged officers at all levels to suggest improvements in budgets and schemes to further increase farmers' incomes.
  • Effective communication. There is emphasis on taking departmental achievements beyond files to the public, ensuring people understand the impact of government initiatives.

Back to basics: Poverty Eradication through Agriculture and Rural Development

Poverty eradication involves improving income and living standards of the poor, especially in rural areas where agriculture is the main livelihood. Government schemes provide financial support, infrastructure, and technology to farmers and rural communities. Scientific research in agriculture enhances productivity and resilience. Rural development programs focus on housing, roads, employment, and empowerment of vulnerable groups like women. Coordinated efforts across ministries ensure these benefits reach the intended populations effectively.

What happens next?

  • Inclusive policy feedback. Union Minister Shri Shivraj Singh Chouhan invited suggestions from officers at all levels to improve schemes and budgets to better increase farmers’ incomes.
  • Public outreach. There is a need to communicate government achievements effectively to the public to ensure awareness and participation in development programs.
  • Global competitiveness. Efforts will focus on enabling Indian agricultural products to compete in global markets through Free Trade Agreements.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the joint workshop on poverty eradication chaired by Union Minister Shri Shivraj Singh Chouhan: 1. The agriculture growth rate reached 4.45%, surpassing the Green Revolution era. 2. The workshop exclusively involved senior officials and excluded lower-level employees. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). Statement 2 is incorrect because the workshop included employees from Section Officers to Secretaries to recognize contributions at all levels.
