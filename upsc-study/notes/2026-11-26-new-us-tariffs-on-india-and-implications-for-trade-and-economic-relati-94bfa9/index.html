New US Tariffs on India Under Section 301 and Trade Deal Implications

The US has proposed a new tariff regime under Section 301, imposing a 25% tariff on India that could reduce to 17% after a trade deal. This tariff structure offers India a competitive edge over Southeast Asian rivals but requires India to accept significant concessions in trade and market access.

The US Section 301 tariffs impose a 25% duty on Indian goods, reducible to 17% post-trade deal, reflecting strategic trade negotiations affecting India’s exports and imports.

Why is this in the news?

On November 26, 2026, The Indian Express reported the US Trade Representative's proposal of a new tariff structure on India under Section 301, following a Supreme Court ruling against previous tariffs.

What is the new US tariff regime on India under Section 301?

  • Tariff rates proposed. The US has proposed a 25% tariff on Indian goods, which could reduce to 17% once India signs a trade deal, offering India a tariff advantage over ASEAN countries.
  • Comparison with other countries. The proposed 17% rate for India is better than the 18% offered to Bangladesh and ASEAN countries like Indonesia and Malaysia, and the 20% rate for Vietnam after trade deals.
  • Basis of tariffs. The tariffs are based on a Section 301 investigation citing India's 'forced labour' concerns and 'excess capacity' in manufacturing, which the US claims distort fair competition.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

Why does the US target India’s trade practices and imports from China?

  • Dependence on China. India’s imports of manufacturing inputs from non-market economies like China have surged, increasing by $91 billion or 152% between 2017 and 2025, distorting competition.
  • Pharmaceutical exports. India supplies about one-third of US pharmaceutical imports, including 47% of generic prescriptions and 61% of generic solid oral drugs, but remains dependent on APIs from China.
  • Industrial policies. US criticizes India’s industrial plans like Make in India and production-linked incentives for promoting excess capacity and production, affecting market fairness.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

What has India agreed to under the US-India trade deal?

  • Tariff reductions. India agreed to eliminate or reduce tariffs on US industrial goods and a wide range of agricultural products including dried distillers’ grains, red sorghum, tree nuts, and soybean oil.
  • Market access improvements. India will address barriers to US medical devices, remove restrictive import licensing on US ICT goods, and consider US or international standards for exports within six months of the deal.
  • Purchase commitments. India intends to purchase $500 billion worth of US energy products, aircraft, precious metals, technology products, and coking coal over the next five years.
  • Technology cooperation. The deal includes plans to significantly increase trade in technology products like GPUs and expand joint technology cooperation.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

How has the US adjusted its demands on sensitive sectors like dairy and GM products?

  • Dairy imports. The US had cited Indian regulations on dairy imports as non-tariff barriers, but recent indications suggest easing of demands on dairy product imports.
  • GM products stance. India maintains a strict policy against genetically modified products, citing religious sensitivities, and has stated it will not allow GM products in the future.
  • US concerns on regulations. The US argues that India’s requirements for dairy products lack clear animal or human health justification, highlighting ongoing trade tensions.

Back to basics: Section 301 Tariffs and Trade Negotiations

Section 301 of the US Trade Act allows the United States to impose tariffs or trade restrictions on countries that engage in unfair trade practices. These tariffs are often used as leverage in trade negotiations to address issues like forced labor, subsidies, or market distortions. Trade deals can reduce these tariffs by resolving disputes and opening markets.

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about the new US tariffs on India under Section 301: 1. The US has proposed a 25% tariff on Indian goods, which can reduce to 17% after a trade deal. 2. India’s imports of manufacturing inputs from China increased by $44 billion between 2017 and 2025. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (c). Statement 2 is correct but the increase in imports from China is $44 billion, while the total increase from all non-market economies is $91 billion; the trap is confusing the two figures.
