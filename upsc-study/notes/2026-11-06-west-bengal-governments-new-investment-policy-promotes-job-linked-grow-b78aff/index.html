West Bengal’s 2026 Investment Policy Focuses on Job-Linked Growth and Incentives

West Bengal government announced a new investment promotion policy in November 2026 that offers incentives linked to employment and fixed capital investment in plant and machinery. This policy marks a shift from the previous regime’s scrapping of industrial incentives and aims to revive industry and promote self-reliance.

West Bengal’s 2026 investment policy promotes industrial growth through job-linked incentives and streamlined approvals to revive the state’s economy.

Why is this in the news?

On November 5, 2026, West Bengal’s BJP government unveiled a new investment promotion policy to revive industries and promote job-linked growth.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the key features of West Bengal’s new investment policy?

  • Job-linked incentives. The policy offers employer EPF contribution reimbursements with 100% reimbursement for women and transgender employees in MSMEs for 10 years, and 75% for women in eligible service units.
  • Assistance on fixed capital investment. Assistance ranges from 100% for regular units, 110% for thrust sector units in priority areas, and 120% for thrust-sector units located in priority areas, capped at 10 years or until the ceiling is reached.
  • Thrust sectors identified. Sectors such as semiconductors, electronics, green hydrogen, waste-to-energy, emerging digital technologies, EVs and batteries, defence, and food and marine processing are prioritized.
  • Project approval timelines. The policy promises a 30-day delivery of certificates of intent, 45-day timeline for certificates of eligibility, and dedicated relationship officers for large projects.
  • Additional support measures. Waiver of surcharges on renewable energy open access until March 2030 and special assistance for sick and closed industrial units that can be revived.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does this policy differ from the previous regime’s approach?

  • Reversal of incentive scrapping. The previous Trinamool Congress government had scrapped three decades of industrial incentive schemes retroactively, while the new policy reinstates incentives to attract investment.
  • Land acquisition approach. The new government rejects forcible land acquisition and flawed policies requiring industries to buy land without government support, aiming for a more industry-friendly land policy.
  • Focus on industrial revival. The policy aims to end extortion and create a positive industrial atmosphere, with the government handing over land to 33 business groups and preparing a GIS-mapped land inventory.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this policy matter for employment and industrial growth?

  • Promotes inclusive employment. By linking incentives to employment, especially for women and transgender employees, the policy encourages job creation in MSMEs and service sectors.
  • Supports strategic sectors. Targeting thrust sectors aligns with emerging technologies and sustainability goals, fostering long-term industrial competitiveness.
  • Streamlines investment process. Fixed timelines and dedicated officers reduce bureaucratic delays, encouraging faster project implementation and investor confidence.

Back to basics: Investment Promotion Policies

Investment promotion policies are state or central government initiatives designed to attract and facilitate industrial and economic investments. They often include incentives such as subsidies, tax benefits, and streamlined approval processes to encourage businesses to invest in specific sectors or regions. These policies aim to boost employment, industrial growth, and economic development by creating a favorable environment for investors.
