Static anchor

Occupational safety means preventing or reducing work-related hazards through safer processes, appropriate equipment, training, supervision and institutional safeguards. In sanitation, the concept extends beyond PPE to the organisation and mechanisation of hazardous work.

Background

  • The National Action for Mechanised Sanitation Ecosystem (NAMASTE) is associated with the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment and seeks safer and more dignified sanitation work through mechanisation and occupational protection.[3]

India implications

  • Worker safety should be treated as a social-justice and public-health responsibility, not merely as an equipment-distribution exercise.
  • Urban sanitation systems need convergence between worker identification, mechanised operations, occupational training, protective equipment, health protection and livelihood support.
  • Implementation should be assessed through safety compliance, reduction of hazardous manual cleaning, access to healthcare and durable income security rather than inputs alone.

Way forward

  • Prioritise mechanisation and safe operating procedures so that protective equipment complements, rather than substitutes for, hazard elimination.
  • Identify sanitation workers and ensure regular occupational-safety training, suitable PPE, supervision and access to health protection.
  • Create outcome-based municipal monitoring covering hazardous-work incidents, equipment use, mechanisation, worker health and livelihood resilience.
  • Strengthen worker participation and grievance redress so implementation reflects actual workplace risks.
