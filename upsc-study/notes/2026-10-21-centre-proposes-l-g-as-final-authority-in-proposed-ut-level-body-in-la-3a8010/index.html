Centre Proposes L-G as Final Authority in Ladakh UT-Level Body

The Centre's draft proposal for Ladakh's governance suggests the Lieutenant Governor (L-G) will have final authority over regulations made by a new Union Territory-level elected body. This development is crucial for understanding Ladakh's unique constitutional arrangement and federal governance implications.

The Centre's draft for Ladakh proposes the L-G as the final authority on laws made by the UT-level elected body, highlighting a distinctive governance framework.

Why is this in the news?

The Ministry of Home Affairs proposed a draft on October 20, 2026, outlining the constitutional and statutory framework for Ladakh's governance, including the L-G's final authority role.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the Centre's proposal for Ladakh's UT-level body?

  • L-G's final authority. The draft states that every regulation made by the UT-level elected body will require the Lieutenant Governor's assent to take effect, making the L-G the final authority.
  • Constitutional amendment. The proposal includes introducing a new constitutional provision under Article 371 in a proposed Chapter 'K' to provide constitutional status to the UT-wide elected body.
  • Scope of powers. The UT-level body would have regulation-making, executive, planning, and financial powers over land, culture, heritage, forests, environment, natural resources, and budgeting.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How will the electoral and institutional structure be determined?

  • Delimitation questions. The draft seeks views on whether delimitation of constituencies will be population-based or consider factors like area, panchayat boundaries, and accessibility.
  • Women’s representation. Options include reserving one-third to 50% of seats for women, possibly extending to nominated positions, to ensure meaningful participation.
  • Election and membership. The draft asks whether members should be directly elected by the public or indirectly through local bodies, and whether nominated members should include experts.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for federalism and governance?

  • Unique governance model. Ladakh's proposed framework creates a special governance structure distinct from other UTs, balancing local autonomy with central oversight.
  • Role of L-G. The L-G's final authority over legislation highlights the central government's control in the UT, impacting the federal balance.
  • Institutional coherence. Settling constitutional and institutional choices is key to ensuring a coherent governance framework that addresses Ladakh's unique needs.

Back to basics: Union Territory Governance

Union Territories (UTs) in India are administrative divisions governed directly by the Central Government through a Lieutenant Governor or Administrator. Unlike states, UTs may have limited or no elected legislative bodies, and the L-G often holds significant executive powers. Constitutional provisions allow for special arrangements in UTs to balance local governance with central control.
