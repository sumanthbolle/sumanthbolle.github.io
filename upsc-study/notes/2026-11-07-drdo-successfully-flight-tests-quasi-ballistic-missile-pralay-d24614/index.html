DRDO Successfully Flight-Tests Indigenous Quasi-Ballistic Missile Pralay

On November 5, 2026, DRDO conducted a successful flight-test of the quasi-ballistic missile Pralay from Odisha's Dr APJ Abdul Kalam Island. This test marks a significant step towards the missile's induction, enhancing India's defence technological capabilities and operational preparedness.

DRDO's successful test of the indigenous Pralay missile advances India's strategic defence capabilities against emerging threats.

Why is this in the news?

The Defence Research & Development Organisation (DRDO) successfully flight-tested the quasi-ballistic missile Pralay on November 5, 2026, confirming all test objectives were met and paving the way for its imminent induction.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What is the Pralay missile and who developed it?

  • Missile type. Pralay is a quasi-ballistic missile developed indigenously by DRDO's Research Centre Imarat, Hyderabad, along with other DRDO laboratories.
  • Production partner. Bharat Dynamics Limited, a Development-cum-Production-Partner, produced the missile system.

[1] Press Information Bureau

What happened during the flight-test?

  • Test details. The flight-test was conducted from Dr APJ Abdul Kalam Island off Odisha's coast at around 1110 hrs on November 5, 2026, with all test objectives fully met as confirmed by data from Integrated Test Range, Chandipur.
  • Test supervision. Senior officials from DRDO, industry representatives, and Defence Forces observed the user evaluation trials.

[1] Press Information Bureau

Why does the successful test matter for India’s defence?

  • Operational preparedness. The missile's advanced technologies will enhance the military's technological capabilities and operational readiness against emerging threats.
  • Imminent induction. With the successful test, the induction of Pralay into the armed forces has become imminent, strengthening India's strategic defence.

Back to basics: Quasi-Ballistic Missile

A quasi-ballistic missile follows a trajectory that is partly ballistic and partly guided, allowing it to maneuver during flight to evade missile defense systems. Such missiles combine the speed and range of ballistic missiles with enhanced accuracy and flexibility, making them strategically valuable in modern warfare.
