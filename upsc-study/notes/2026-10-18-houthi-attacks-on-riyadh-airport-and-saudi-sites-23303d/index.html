Houthi Attacks on Riyadh Airport and Saudi Sites Escalate Gulf Conflict

In October 2026, Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi group launched attacks on Riyadh's international airport, an oil refinery, and other strategic sites in Saudi Arabia. This escalation deepens the conflict in West Asia, impacting regional security and global energy supplies, which is crucial for UPSC aspirants studying India's foreign policy and security challenges.

The Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia's key infrastructure have intensified the West Asia conflict, affecting regional stability and energy security.

Why is this in the news?

The Houthi group claimed attacks on Riyadh airport and Saudi oil facilities in early October 2026, amid ongoing conflict in Yemen and heightened regional tensions.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What triggered the Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia?

  • Conflict context. The Houthis, backed by Iran, have been fighting in Yemen and controlling large parts of northern Yemen, including key coastal areas along the Red Sea and Bab al-Mandab Strait.
  • Recent escalation. In October 2026, the Houthis struck Riyadh’s international airport, an Aramco oil refinery, and other sites, intensifying the conflict and targeting Saudi infrastructure.
  • Saudi-led counteroffensive. Saudi Arabia and its Yemeni allies launched a military push to retake Houthi-held territories, including strikes on Houthi positions on the Red Sea coast.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why do these attacks matter for regional and global security?

  • Impact on energy security. Houthi attacks have disrupted Saudi oil infrastructure and pipelines, including halting pumping by the East-West pipeline, a vital export route.
  • Maritime security threat. Control over the Bab al-Mandab Strait affects global maritime traffic, and the Houthis’ maritime blockade threatens shipping in the Red Sea.
  • Regional military alliances. Pakistan and Turkiye pledged troop deployments to Saudi Arabia under a mutual defence pact to counter Houthi attacks, showing wider regional involvement.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does this situation relate to India’s interests?

  • Strategic location. Saudi Arabia’s stability affects West Asia, a region critical for India’s energy imports and diaspora security.
  • India’s diplomatic stance. India has condemned attacks on Saudi airports, reflecting its concern over regional security and energy supply disruptions.
  • Broader security challenges. The conflict exemplifies the complex security environment in West Asia that India must navigate in its foreign policy.

Back to basics: West Asia Conflict and Energy Security

West Asia, also known as the Middle East, is a region with significant geopolitical tensions involving state and non-state actors. Conflicts here often affect global energy supplies because the region holds major oil and gas reserves. Control over strategic maritime routes like the Bab al-Mandab Strait is crucial for international trade and energy exports. Understanding these dynamics helps explain why regional conflicts have global economic and security implications.

Answer and trap
