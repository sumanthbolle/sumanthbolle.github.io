MHA Extends Ban on Zakir Naik’s Islamic Research Foundation Under UAPA

The Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) has extended the ban on the Islamic Research Foundation (IRF) under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA) for five more years. This move highlights India's ongoing legal and security measures against organizations involved in terrorism and communal disharmony.

The MHA extended the ban on Zakir Naik’s Islamic Research Foundation as an unlawful association under UAPA due to its involvement in promoting terrorism and communal discord.

Why is this in the news?

On November 13, 2026, the Ministry of Home Affairs extended the ban on the Islamic Research Foundation under the UAPA, citing its involvement in anti-national activities.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the reason for the ban extension on the Islamic Research Foundation?

  • Anti-national activities. The IRF, led by fugitive preacher Dr. Zakir Naik, has been involved in inciting communal hatred, fostering hostility, promoting terrorism, and propagating separatism.
  • Threat to security. The organization encourages violent unrest, regrouping of absconding terrorists, and communal discord, which threaten India's integrity and secular fabric.
  • Legal basis. The ban extension was made under Section 3 (1)(3) of the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act.

[1] Press Information Bureau[2] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the history and current status of the ban on IRF?

  • Initial ban in 2016. The IRF was first declared an unlawful association in 2016 under the UAPA.
  • Previous extensions. The ban was extended for five years in 2021 and now again for another five years in 2026.
  • Zakir Naik’s status. Zakir Naik left India in 2016 and currently holds permanent residency in Malaysia, which has not complied with India's extradition request.

[2] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for India’s internal security framework?

  • Zero tolerance policy. The ban aligns with the Prime Minister’s vision of zero tolerance towards terrorism and anti-national activities.
  • Preventing communal disharmony. The government aims to curb the IRF’s activities to protect peace, communal harmony, and the secular fabric of the country.
  • Legal and enforcement challenges. Despite the ban, challenges remain in controlling the organization’s influence abroad and preventing regrouping of absconding terrorists.

Back to basics: Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA)

The UAPA is an Indian law aimed at preventing unlawful activities and associations that threaten the sovereignty and integrity of the country. It empowers the government to designate organizations as unlawful associations and take legal action against them to maintain internal security and public order. The Act is a key tool in counter-terrorism and anti-extremism efforts.
