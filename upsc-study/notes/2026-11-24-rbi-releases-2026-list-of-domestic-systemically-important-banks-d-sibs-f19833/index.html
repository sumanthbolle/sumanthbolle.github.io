RBI Releases 2026 List of Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs)

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) announced the 2026 list of Domestic Systemically Important Banks, maintaining State Bank of India, HDFC Bank, and ICICI Bank as D-SIBs with specific additional capital requirements. Understanding this list is important for UPSC aspirants as it highlights the RBI's role in financial stability and regulatory frameworks relevant to the banking sector.

RBI's 2026 D-SIB list identifies SBI, HDFC Bank, and ICICI Bank as systemically important banks with additional capital buffers to safeguard financial stability.

Why is this in the news?

On November 23, 2026, the RBI released the updated list of Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs) for 2026, specifying additional capital requirements for these banks.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

What are Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs)?

  • Definition. D-SIBs are banks whose failure could disrupt the domestic financial system due to their size, complexity, and interconnectedness.
  • RBI Framework. The RBI issued the Framework for D-SIBs in 2014 and updated it in 2023, requiring disclosure of D-SIBs and assigning them to buckets based on their Systemic Importance Scores.

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Which banks are identified as D-SIBs in 2026 and what are their capital requirements?

  • Banks Listed. State Bank of India, HDFC Bank, and ICICI Bank continue as D-SIBs under the same bucketing structure as in 2025.
  • Capital Buffer Requirements. These banks must maintain an additional Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) capital buffer over the capital conservation buffer, varying by bucket: SBI at 0.80%, HDFC Bank at 0.40%, and ICICI Bank at 0.20% of Risk Weighted Assets (RWAs).

[1] Reserve Bank of India

Why does the D-SIB framework matter for financial stability and regulation?

  • Systemic Risk Mitigation. Additional capital requirements for D-SIBs reduce the risk of their failure impacting the broader financial system.
  • Regulatory Transparency. The RBI's disclosure of D-SIBs and their capital buffers enhances market discipline and regulatory oversight.
  • Global Coordination. Foreign banks designated as Global Systemically Important Banks (G-SIBs) with Indian branches must maintain proportionate CET1 capital surcharges in India, aligning with global standards.

Back to basics: Systemically Important Banks and Capital Buffers

Systemically Important Banks are those whose failure could threaten the stability of the financial system. To mitigate this risk, regulators require these banks to maintain additional capital buffers beyond the minimum regulatory capital. The Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) capital is a key measure of a bank's financial strength, representing its core equity capital against risk-weighted assets. These buffers help ensure banks can absorb losses during financial stress, protecting depositors and the economy.

Mains question (10 marks)

Explain the significance of Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs) in India's financial stability framework.

Explain: Explain how D-SIBs are identified and regulated and their role in maintaining financial stability.

Intro: Domestic Systemically Important Banks are banks whose failure could disrupt the financial system, requiring special regulatory oversight.

  • The RBI's D-SIB framework identifies banks based on their systemic importance scores and assigns additional capital requirements.
  • These additional Common Equity Tier 1 capital buffers help absorb losses and reduce systemic risk.
  • Disclosure of D-SIBs enhances transparency and market discipline.
  • The framework aligns with global standards, including requirements for foreign G-SIBs operating in India.

Conclusion: Thus, the D-SIB framework strengthens the resilience of the banking sector and safeguards the economy.
