Supreme Court Examines Election Commission's SIR Decision and Electoral Roll Issues

The Supreme Court heard arguments on November 16, 2026, about the Election Commission of India's claim that the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls was a unanimous decision. Opposition leader Rahul Gandhi challenged the ECI's lack of documentary proof and raised concerns about electoral roll exclusions and opaque digital platforms.

The Supreme Court is scrutinizing the Election Commission's decision-making and transparency regarding the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls.

Why is this in the news?

On November 16, 2026, the Supreme Court heard a petition led by Rahul Gandhi challenging the Election Commission of India's claim that the Special Intensive Revision was a unanimous decision by its members.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the controversy over the SIR decision?

  • Unanimity questioned. Rahul Gandhi argued that the ECI failed to provide any minutes, file notings, or approvals proving that the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) was a unanimous decision by the Chief Election Commissioner and Election Commissioners.
  • Internal dissent. Two Election Commissioners reportedly wrote directly to the Cabinet Secretary expressing displeasure over the SIR process, contradicting the ECI's claim of unanimous functioning.
  • Lack of clarity on decision-making. The ECI did not specify when and where the decision to conduct the nationwide SIR was taken, raising questions about procedural transparency.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What issues were raised about electoral roll management?

  • Form 6 declaration concerns. The additional declaration form annexed to the statutory Form 6 was argued to be part of the online Form 6 process, requiring a single OTP and electronic signature, contrary to the ECI's claim that it was separate.
  • Large-scale exclusions. The petition highlighted the exclusion of approximately 20.66 lakh names from Punjab's electoral roll and about 12.8 lakh electors excluded elsewhere, with women constituting about 62% of excluded electors in West Bengal.
  • Opaque digital platforms. Concerns were raised about the centralized and opaque functioning of ECINET and ERONET, the digital platforms managing voter registration and electoral rolls, potentially leading to manipulation.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for electoral governance and judicial oversight?

  • Transparency and accountability. The case questions the Election Commission's transparency in decision-making and the integrity of electoral roll management, which are crucial for free and fair elections.
  • Judicial scrutiny of election processes. The Supreme Court's hearing reflects its role in ensuring constitutional bodies like the ECI function with accountability and uphold democratic principles.
  • Impact on voter rights. Exclusions from electoral rolls and opaque procedures can disenfranchise voters, affecting the democratic process and representation.

Back to basics: Election Commission of India and Electoral Rolls

The Election Commission of India (ECI) is a constitutional body responsible for administering elections and maintaining electoral rolls. Electoral rolls are official lists of eligible voters, periodically updated through processes like the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) to ensure accuracy. Transparency and consensus in ECI decisions are essential to uphold electoral integrity and voter confidence.
