Global Struggle Over 1.5°C Climate Target at UN Summit

At the COP31 UN Climate Summit in Antalya in November 2026, nations debated the imminent and unavoidable crossing of the 1.5°C global warming threshold above pre-industrial levels. This target, central to the Paris Agreement, has become contested as countries weigh climate risks, emission cuts, and financial support.

The 1.5°C warming limit, crucial under the Paris Agreement, is now seen as imminent to cross, sparking global debate on climate action and equity at COP31.

Why is this in the news?

The 2026 COP31 UN Climate Summit in Antalya highlighted the global debate on the 1.5°C warming target after the UN declared in September that crossing this threshold is imminent and unavoidable.

What is the significance of the 1.5°C target?

  • Central Paris goal. The 1.5°C target is the most ambitious threshold of the Paris Agreement, aimed at limiting global warming to avoid severe climate impacts.
  • Climate risks beyond 1.5°C. Scientific evidence shows risks rise sharply beyond 1.5°C, including severe floods, heatwaves, and irreversible damage like coral reef loss.
  • Current warming status. The world has already warmed about 1.4°C above pre-industrial times, making the crossing of 1.5°C imminent and unavoidable.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is the 1.5°C target contested at COP31?

  • Divergent national positions. Some nations, especially vulnerable ones like Palau, insist on maintaining the 1.5°C goal as an existential necessity, while others, including blocs like the Arab Group and Like-Minded Developing Countries, see an opportunity to move away from it.
  • Concerns over emission cuts and finance. Developing countries fear stronger 1.5°C commitments could increase pressure to cut emissions without sufficient financial support.
  • Debate on overshoot and reversal. There is discussion on whether global temperatures can be lowered back to 1.5°C after crossing it, with some pushing for language on minimizing and shortening any overshoot.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the challenges in achieving or maintaining the 1.5°C target?

  • Need for rapid emission cuts. Achieving or returning to 1.5°C requires rapid reductions in greenhouse gas emissions globally.
  • Role of carbon removal. Carbon must be removed from the atmosphere through natural methods like reforestation and emerging technologies at a massive scale over decades.
  • Technological and scale challenges. The deployment of new carbon removal technologies is extremely ambitious and uncertain, requiring enormous effort and investment.

Back to basics: Paris Agreement and Global Warming Limits

The Paris Agreement is an international treaty aiming to limit global warming to well below 2°C and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels. These limits are set to reduce the risks and impacts of climate change. Countries submit nationally determined contributions (NDCs) to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and adapt to climate impacts. The 1.5°C target is more ambitious and requires faster and deeper emission cuts than the 2°C goal.
