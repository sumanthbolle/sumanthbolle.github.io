Tamil Nadu Faces Water Crisis Risk Without Northeast Monsoon

Tamil Nadu's major reservoirs currently hold about one-third of their capacity, much lower than last year's 92%. This shortage threatens water availability for agriculture, especially in the Cauvery delta, making the arrival of the northeast monsoon crucial.

Tamil Nadu's low reservoir storage and uncertain northeast monsoon rainfall risk a water crisis and reduced rice production.

Why is this in the news?

On November 8, 2026, reports highlighted Tamil Nadu's water storage at about a third of reservoir capacity, raising concerns of a water crisis if the northeast monsoon does not bring sufficient rainfall.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the current water storage situation in Tamil Nadu?

  • Reservoir storage low. The aggregate storage in Tamil Nadu's 15 major irrigation reservoirs is about one-third of their combined capacity, compared to around 92% at the same time last year.
  • Mettur dam status. The state's largest dam, Mettur, holds about 23 thousand million cubic feet (tmcft), which is insufficient to sustain water release to farmers in the Cauvery delta beyond a week.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How does the northeast monsoon affect Tamil Nadu's water availability?

  • Monsoon rainfall importance. Tamil Nadu has received around 240 mm of rainfall so far during the northeast monsoon against an anticipated 236 mm, but it needs at least 260 mm more rainfall, mostly in November, to reach comfortable levels.
  • Rainfall forecasts. Tamil Nadu Agricultural University predicted a near-normal total monsoon rainfall of 438 mm, close to the normal 434 mm, while the Meteorological Department forecasted normal rainfall for the southern peninsular region.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the implications for agriculture in Tamil Nadu?

  • Paddy cultivation trends. Farmers have sown paddy on about 3.62 lakh hectares during the samba-thaladi season, with nearly 50% using direct sowing, a method preferred during water shortages.
  • Reduced paddy area outside delta. Paddy coverage in non-delta areas is about 1.3 lakh hectares less than last year, reflecting water availability challenges.
  • Rice production figures. In 2025-26, Tamil Nadu produced 77.5 lakh tonnes of rice, slightly lower than the previous high of 79.07 lakh tonnes in 2021-22.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What measures are being taken to manage water sharing?

  • Demand for water release. The Tamil Nadu government requested Karnataka to release water from the Cauvery river, but the Cauvery Water Management Authority approved only a limited release of 3.88 tmcft for 15 days starting November 7, 2026.

Back to basics: Monsoon and Water Resource Management

The northeast monsoon is a critical rainfall period for Tamil Nadu, supplying most of the state's annual precipitation. Reservoirs store this water to support agriculture and drinking needs during dry periods. Water management involves balancing reservoir releases and inter-state river water sharing to sustain farming and avoid shortages.

Conclusion: Effective water management in Tamil Nadu requires adaptive strategies to cope with monsoon variability and inter-state cooperation.
