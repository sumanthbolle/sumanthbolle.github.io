Controversy over J&K Bank's New Reservation Policy and Its Implications

J&K Bank implemented a new reservation policy introduced by ex-chairman R.K. Chhibber, now a VHP trustee, sparking political and social opposition. The policy's design and impact raise important questions on merit, reservation, and regional equity in Jammu and Kashmir.

J&K Bank's reservation policy, pushed by ex-chairman R.K. Chhibber, has triggered debate on fairness and representation in recruitment within Jammu and Kashmir.

Why is this in the news?

On November 9, 2026, J&K Bank began filling 1,400 vacancies under a new reservation policy introduced by ex-chairman R.K. Chhibber, leading to widespread opposition from political parties and social groups.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What changes did J&K Bank make in its reservation policy?

  • Reservation details. The bank decided to fill 1,400 vacancies with 60% reserved categories and 40% open merit, including 8% Scheduled Caste, 20% Scheduled Tribe, 8% Other Backward Classes, 4% Actual Line of Control/International Border, 10% Resident of Backward Area, and 10% Economically Weaker Section.
  • Age limit adjustments. The upper age limit was reduced from 32 to 30 years for Open Merit candidates and increased to 40 years for reserved categories.
  • Recruitment scope. Vacancies include 323 probationary officers and 1,033 banking associate posts.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Who influenced the policy and why is it controversial?

  • Role of R.K. Chhibber. Ex-chairman R.K. Chhibber introduced the policy during 2021-2022 and remained on the bank's board until 2026 while also serving as a Vishwa Hindu Parishad trustee.
  • Political and social opposition. Regional parties including the ruling National Conference, student groups, and religious leaders have opposed the policy, calling it exclusionary and unfair to local youth, especially in Kashmir where some reserved categories like Scheduled Castes have negligible population.
  • Concerns on meritocracy. Critics argue that the policy undermines meritocracy by allocating only 40% of vacancies to open merit and restricting age limits, disadvantaging many aspirants who have prepared for years.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How have different stakeholders reacted to the policy?

  • Student and youth concerns. The J&K Students Association urged the Lieutenant-Governor to review the policy, highlighting the limited open merit vacancies and the impact on aspirants' opportunities.
  • Political leaders' views. Opposition PDP legislator and CPI(M) leader expressed concerns over the age limit and reservation in a commercial institution, calling for reconsideration to provide fair chances.
  • Religious leadership. J&K grand mufti emphasized fairness, inclusivity, and the need to avoid disadvantaging open merit candidates.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the key issues raised by the reservation policy in J&K Bank?

  • Regional imbalance. There are allegations of regional imbalance with more candidates from Jammu selected over Kashmir during previous recruitments.
  • Exclusion in reservation. The policy allows vertical appointment where reserved category candidates can also compete in open merit, potentially reducing opportunities for others.
  • Employment challenges. The policy comes amid high unemployment and limited recruitment opportunities in J&K, intensifying the stakes for youth.

Back to basics: Reservation Policy in India

Reservation is a system of affirmative action in India designed to improve the representation of historically disadvantaged groups such as Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Other Backward Classes in education, employment, and politics. It aims to promote social equity by allocating a certain percentage of opportunities to these groups. However, reservation policies must balance social justice with meritocracy and regional demographics to ensure fairness and inclusivity.
