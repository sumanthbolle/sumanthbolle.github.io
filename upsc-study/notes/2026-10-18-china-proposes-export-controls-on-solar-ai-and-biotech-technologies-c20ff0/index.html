China Proposes Export Controls on Solar, AI, and Biotech Technologies

China announced a draft plan to restrict exports of over 40 advanced technologies including solar manufacturing, AI, and biotech. This affects India’s efforts to reduce dependence on Chinese technology and has implications for India-China trade relations and technology security.

China’s new export controls on key technologies could delay India’s solar manufacturing growth and restrict Chinese investments in critical sectors.

Why is this in the news?

On October 16, 2026, China’s Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology announced a draft plan titled 'Announcement No. 48' to restrict exports of key technologies in solar, AI, and biotechnology.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What technologies are affected by China’s export controls?

  • Scope of controls. The draft plan proposes a complete export ban on 14 technologies and restrictions on 26 others, covering next-generation solar manufacturing, AI models, and some biotech platforms.
  • Solar technology specifics. Technologies such as perovskite and tandem solar-cell module manufacturing face a complete export ban, while polysilicon engineering technologies with large production capacities face restricted exports.
  • AI and biotech controls. AI technologies that could generate or mutate malicious code and certain cell and gene therapy platforms are banned, while 23 key AI technologies including foundation models and computing clusters face export restrictions.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why is China imposing these export controls?

  • National security concerns. China cites the increasing use of photovoltaic technologies in aerospace, defense, and unmanned systems, raising national security risks from technology exports.
  • Preventing misuse. Controls aim to prevent loss of control over sensitive technologies and misuse by malicious actors that could threaten critical infrastructure and government networks.
  • International context. China follows global trends where major economies have implemented export controls on sensitive AI, biotech, and photovoltaic technologies.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How do these controls impact India and its technology ambitions?

  • Effect on solar manufacturing. India’s efforts to reduce dependence on China for solar technology may face delays due to export bans and licensing restrictions on key solar manufacturing technologies.
  • Investment and collaboration challenges. Chinese firms may face constraints in solo investments, joint ventures, or licensing deals in India’s polysilicon and ingot-wafer sectors, limiting technology transfer.
  • Broader trade implications. These controls could affect India-China trade relations by restricting access to advanced technologies critical for India’s clean energy and biotech sectors.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What exceptions and future outlook does China indicate?

  • Exemptions for open-source. Technologies already public through open-source projects or academic publications are generally exempt from export controls.
  • Commitment to innovation. China states it remains committed to building an open-source AI innovation ecosystem and facilitating overseas chip fabrication activities.

Back to basics: Export Controls on Technology

Export controls are government-imposed restrictions on the export of certain technologies to protect national security and economic interests. They regulate the transfer of sensitive technologies that could be used in military or strategic sectors. Countries use these controls to prevent misuse, maintain technological advantages, and comply with international security norms.

Practice: MCQ, Mains and revision

Prelims MCQ

Consider the following statements about China’s Announcement No. 48 on export controls: 1. It proposes a complete export ban on perovskite solar-cell manufacturing technology. 2. It restricts exports of polysilicon technologies only if production capacity exceeds 30,000 tonnes annually. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. Both 1 and 2
  2. 1 only
  3. 2 only
  4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer and trap

Answer: (a). The trap is confusing the scope of the polysilicon export restriction which applies only to production lines with annual output capacities of at least 30,000 tonnes, not all polysilicon technologies.
