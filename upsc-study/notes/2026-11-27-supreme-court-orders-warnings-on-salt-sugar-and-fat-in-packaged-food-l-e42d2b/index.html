Supreme Court mandates separate warnings on salt, sugar, fat in packaged foods

The Supreme Court ordered the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) to implement front-of-package nutrition labelling with separate hexagonal warnings for high salt, sugar, and fat content. This ruling emphasizes public health over industry objections and sets a precedent for regulatory governance and consumer protection.

The Supreme Court directed FSSAI to mandate clear, separate pictorial warnings on packaged foods high in total sugars, saturated fat, or salt to address non-communicable disease risks.

Why is this in the news?

On November 26, 2026, the Supreme Court directed FSSAI to introduce separate front-of-package warning labels for salt, sugar, and fat in packaged foods to protect public health.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

What did the Supreme Court order on packaged food labels?

  • Separate hexagonal warnings. The Court mandated that each nutrient of concern—total sugars, saturated fat, and salt—must have its own hexagonal warning label with pictorial representation and text such as 'High Sugar', 'High Fat', or 'High Salt'.
  • Focus on total sugars and saturated fat. The Court directed FSSAI to base thresholds on total sugars and saturated fat rather than added sugars or fats, to prevent industry manipulation and better reflect health risks.
  • Size and visibility. Warning labels must occupy at least 4-5% of the package display panel, ensuring conspicuousness, with font size determined by FSSAI.
  • Exemptions and additives. FSSAI must formulate a clear list of exempted foods, typically single-ingredient items, and monitor industry responses such as increased preservatives or additives that could undermine the labels' purpose.
  • Implementation timeline. FSSAI was given a transition period of at least four months to implement the regulations and to prevent misuse during this phase.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

How does this order differ from FSSAI’s earlier proposals?

  • Rejection of star-rating system. The Court rejected FSSAI’s earlier star-rating system that gave an overall nutritional score, as it could mislead consumers into perceiving unhealthy foods as acceptable.
  • Separate warnings vs single hexagon. FSSAI had proposed a single hexagon for multiple nutrients, but the Court mandated separate hexagons for each nutrient to improve clarity.
  • Pictorial warnings introduced. Unlike FSSAI’s proposals, the Court required pictorial representations alongside text to ensure accessibility across literacy levels.
  • Thresholds based on scientific evidence. The Court instructed FSSAI to reformulate nutrient thresholds based on scientific evidence, balancing sensitivity to avoid label fatigue or consumer desensitization.
  • No category labels for beverages without high sugar. The Court ruled that beverages without high sugar should not carry category labels similar to warning labels, preventing consumer confusion.

[1] The Indian Express · Secondary

Why do these warning labels matter for public health?

  • Rising non-communicable diseases. India faces high prevalence of diabetes, hypertension, and obesity, with non-communicable diseases causing nearly 63% of deaths annually, projected to rise to 75% by 2030.
  • Impact of HFSS and UPFs. Consumption of foods high in fats, sugars, and salt (HFSS) and ultra-processed foods (UPFs) is a significant risk factor for these diseases.
  • Global precedents. At least 44 countries have implemented front-of-pack labels, with warning labels used in countries like Chile and Mexico leading to reduced consumption of unhealthy foods.
  • Consumer behavior change. Interpretive labels with clear warnings help consumers quickly identify unhealthy products, steering them towards healthier choices.

Back to basics: Front-of-Pack Nutrition Labelling (FoPNL)

Front-of-Pack Nutrition Labelling is a regulatory approach to display key nutritional information prominently on the front of packaged food products. It helps consumers make informed choices quickly by highlighting nutrients of concern such as salt, sugar, and fat. FoPNL can be interpretive, providing evaluative symbols or warnings, or non-interpretive, showing only numerical data. Interpretive labels are considered more effective in influencing healthier consumer behavior.
