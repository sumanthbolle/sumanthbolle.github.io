INDIA Bloc Questions Election Commission on Special Intensive Revision

The INDIA bloc has posed 48 questions to the Election Commission of India (ECI) challenging the decision-making and conduct of the nationwide Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls. This raises important constitutional and electoral governance issues relevant for understanding the accountability of constitutional bodies.

The INDIA bloc's 48 interrogatories to the Election Commission question the authorization and collective decision-making behind the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls.

Why is this in the news?

On November 25, 2026, the INDIA bloc filed 48 questions to the Election Commission of India regarding the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls, seeking transparency on the decision-making process.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What is the controversy over the Special Intensive Revision (SIR)?

  • Opposition's challenge. The INDIA bloc leaders have challenged the manner in which the nationwide SIR was initiated and conducted, alleging lack of collective approval and transparency.
  • Key allegations. They allege that Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar acted without the knowledge or concurrence of the other Election Commissioners and questioned the absence of recorded decisions authorizing the SIR.
  • Specific concerns. The petitioners also questioned changes to voter registration procedures, the introduction of an additional declaration in online Form 6, and the functioning of electoral databases ECINET and ERONET.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

How has the Election Commission responded to the allegations?

  • Unanimous approval claim. In its counter-affidavit dated October 26, 2026, the ECI stated that the SIR orders and the decision to file the affidavit were approved unanimously by all three members.
  • Opposition's rebuttal. Rahul Gandhi questioned the absence of minutes, file notings, or other records supporting the claim of unanimous approval.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does this matter for electoral governance and constitutional accountability?

  • Transparency in decision-making. The case highlights the need for transparent and collective decision-making in constitutional bodies like the Election Commission.
  • Checks on authority. It raises questions about the exercise of powers by the Chief Election Commissioner versus the full Commission, impacting the credibility of electoral processes.
  • Legal scrutiny. The Supreme Court's decision to hear the application adds judicial oversight to electoral governance issues.

Back to basics: Election Commission of India and Electoral Rolls

The Election Commission of India is a constitutional body responsible for administering elections and maintaining electoral rolls. Electoral rolls are lists of eligible voters, which are periodically updated through processes like the Special Intensive Revision to ensure accuracy and inclusion of new voters. Decisions by the Commission are ideally collective to maintain transparency and trust in democratic processes.
