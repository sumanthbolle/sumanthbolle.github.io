National Medical Commission Withdraws 2023 MSMER Regulations

The National Medical Commission (NMC) has withdrawn the 2023 Maintenance of Standards of Medical Education Regulations (MSMER), which included penalties for false information by faculty and administrators. This move raises concerns about regulatory relaxation in medical education, a key topic for understanding governance and regulatory challenges in healthcare.

The NMC's withdrawal of MSMER reduces penalties on medical colleges, affecting regulation and transparency in medical education.

Why is this in the news?

On October 27, 2026, The Hindu editorial reported that the NMC has denotified and withdrawn the 2023 MSMER regulations, which had imposed fines on faculty and administrators for false information in medical colleges.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What changes did the NMC make to MSMER regulations?

  • Withdrawal of MSMER. The NMC denotified and withdrew the 2023 Maintenance of Standards of Medical Education Regulations (MSMER), removing the framework of penalties related to educational standards and infrastructure at medical colleges.
  • Penalty provisions removed. MSMER had provisions to fine faculty members and administrators up to ₹5 lakh for submitting false information, but these were eliminated with the withdrawal.
  • MARB's retained powers. The Medical Assessment and Rating Board (MARB) still retains the authority to fine institutions up to ₹1 crore under separate 2023 regulations.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

Why does the withdrawal of MSMER matter for medical education regulation?

  • Crackdown on corruption weakened. MSMER was praised for targeting corruption and malpractice such as 'ghost faculty' and 'ghost patients' in medical colleges, which is now undermined.
  • Reduced individual accountability. With MSMER withdrawn, penalties on individual faculty and administrators are diminished, potentially reducing the deterrent against malpractice.
  • Regulatory overlap cited. NMC justified the withdrawal by citing overlap with MARB's powers, but the wholesale scrapping of MSMER lacks clear explanation.
  • Transparency concerns. The withdrawal raises doubts about whether MARB's powers are actively exercised and if assessment results are publicly accessible as intended.

[1] The Hindu · Secondary

What are the procedural and legal issues involved?

  • Appeal timelines conflict. MSMER specified timelines for appealing penalties, but MARB regulations have a 15-day deadline that may conflict with the 60-day period under the NMC Act, risking legal disputes.
  • Lack of published assessments. The Central Information Commission revealed that MARB had not formally assessed or rated medical colleges as required, and relevant reports were missing from NMC records.

Back to basics: Regulatory bodies and medical education standards

The National Medical Commission (NMC) is the statutory body regulating medical education and practice in India. It sets standards for medical colleges and practitioners. The Medical Assessment and Rating Board (MARB) is a specialized body under the NMC that assesses and rates medical institutions, imposing penalties for non-compliance. Regulations like MSMER establish rules and penalties to maintain educational quality and integrity in medical colleges.
